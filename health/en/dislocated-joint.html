Dislocated joint

A joint where the ends of the bones have been forced out of their normal place

ConditionAny other bone or soft-tissue injuryBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

A dislocated joint happens when the bones of a joint are forced out of place, often after a fall or hard knock, and it is very painful and needs a doctor to put it back safely.

  • A joint, often the shoulder
  • After a fall or hard knock
  • Sharp pain, joint looks odd
Which doctor
Hospital emergency doctor, then a bone (orthopaedic) doctor
Common in
Anyone after a fall, sport or accident; the shoulder is the joint most often affected
Contagious
No
Course
Usually needs a doctor to put the joint back, then weeks of rest and gentle exercise

Common signs

More signs

  • Strong pain in the joint, worse when you try to move it
  • The joint looks out of shape, bumpy or in the wrong place
  • Cannot move the joint, or it feels locked or very weak
  • Numbness or tingling below the joint

Why it happens

Other causes

  • An earlier dislocation, which can leave the joint weaker
  • Rarely, a fit (seizure) or an electric shock

See a doctor first if

  • Injury to the head, neck or back, or a major accident: call 999 and do not move the person
  • A bone sticking through the skin, or heavy bleeding: call 999
  • Numbness, tingling, or a pale, blue or cold hand or foot below the joint: go to hospital now
  • A joint that looks out of place or badly bent, or that you cannot move: go to hospital now
  • Pain or swelling that gets worse after the joint has been put back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not try to push the joint back yourself; it can harm nerves and blood vessels
  • Keep the joint still and supported in the position you find it until you get help
  • After it is put back, follow your doctor's advice on the sling, rest and exercises

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Recent shoulder injury or surgery
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
