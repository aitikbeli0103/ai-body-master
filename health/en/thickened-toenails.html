Thickened toenails

Toenails that have become thicker, harder to cut, and often discoloured or uneven

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

One or more toenails slowly grow thicker than normal, may turn yellow, white or brown, and become hard to trim; a fungus is a common cause.

  • Toenails
  • More common with age
  • Thick, yellow, crumbly nail
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor (dermatologist) or foot doctor (podiatrist)
Common in
Older adults; people with diabetes, sweaty feet or tight shoes
Contagious
Some types can be: nail fungus can spread to other nails and to other people
Course
Slow: toenails grow slowly, so any change can take many months

Common signs

More signs

  • Colour turning yellow, white or brown
  • Crumbly bits under the nail, sometimes with a slight smell
  • Discomfort in shoes, especially when the nail presses on the toe

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A fungal infection of the nail is a common cause
  • Repeated pressure or injury, such as tight shoes or stubbing the toe
  • Skin conditions such as psoriasis
  • Diabetes or poor blood flow to the feet

See a doctor first if

  • Red, hot, swollen toe with pus, or redness spreading up the foot, especially with fever: see a doctor today
  • Any nail change, sore or wound on the foot, especially with diabetes or poor blood flow
  • A dark brown or black streak or patch under the nail that is not from a knock
  • A nail that bleeds, has a lump, or hurts so much that walking is hard
  • Nail changes that keep spreading to other nails or the skin around them

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep your feet clean and dry, and dry well between the toes after washing
  • Trim straight across after a wash, when the nail is softer, with clean clippers
  • Do not dig at a thick nail. With diabetes or poor feeling in the feet, ask a foot doctor to trim it

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
