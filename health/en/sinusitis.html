Sinusitis

Swelling of the lining of the sinuses, the air spaces in the face

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

The sinuses are air spaces around the nose and eyes. When their lining swells, mucus builds up and the face feels full or sore.

  • Face, around nose and eyes
  • Often after a cold
  • Face pressure, blocked nose
Which doctor
Family doctor, ear-nose-throat (ENT) doctor
Common in
Anyone; often after a cold or with allergies
Contagious
The sinus swelling itself is not, but a cold behind it can spread
Course
Often gets better in days to weeks; some cases last longer

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Allergies that swell the nose
  • Bacteria sometimes join in

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen, red skin around the eye, or trouble moving the eye or seeing: go to hospital now
  • Severe headache with fever, stiff neck or confusion: call 999
  • High fever or symptoms that get worse after a few days
  • Symptoms that last more than ten days, or keep coming back
  • A baby or small child who is unwell with these signs: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Sinusitis?

Swelling of the lining of the sinuses, the air spaces in the face. The sinuses are air spaces around the nose and eyes. When their lining swells, mucus builds up and the face feels full or sore.

What are the common signs of Sinusitis?

Blocked nose · Sharp pain in the face · Headache across the forehead

Why does Sinusitis happen?

Germs (infection) · Dry air and air-conditioning

How can I look after myself with Sinusitis?

Drink enough water · A warm cloth on the sore area
