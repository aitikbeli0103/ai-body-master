Lymphoma

A cancer that starts in the infection-fighting white blood cells of the lymph system

ConditionCancer & tumoursBlood

What it is

Lymphoma is a cancer of the lymph system, the network of glands and vessels that helps fight germs. There are many types, in two main groups: Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

  • Lymph glands and spleen
  • Children and adults
  • Swollen glands, tiredness
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a blood doctor (haematologist) or cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Any age; some types are more common in young adults, others in older adults
Contagious
No
Course
Varies widely by type; the care plan depends on the type and stage

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some infections, such as Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) or hepatitis C
  • Some autoimmune conditions, where the body attacks its own cells
  • A close family member who had lymphoma can slightly raise the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, a swollen face, neck or arm, or severe chest pain: call 999
  • Severe headache, confusion, sudden weakness or a fit: call 999
  • Fever or chills while having cancer treatment: go to hospital now
  • A painless lump in the neck, armpit or groin that lasts for weeks or keeps growing
  • Drenching night sweats, fever that keeps coming back, or losing weight without trying
  • Easy bruising or bleeding, or looking very pale and tired

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your team about tiredness, pain, food and emotional support

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
