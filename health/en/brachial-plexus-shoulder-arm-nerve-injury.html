Brachial plexus (shoulder-arm nerve) injury

Damage to the bundle of nerves that runs from the neck to the shoulder and arm

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

The brachial plexus is a network of nerves that carries messages between the neck and the shoulder, arm and hand. When it is stretched or hurt, the arm may feel weak, numb or painful.

  • Neck, shoulder and arm
  • Often after an accident
  • Weak, numb or painful arm
Which doctor
Nerve doctor (neurologist), bone doctor, rehabilitation doctor
Common in
People after road accidents, falls or sports injuries; some newborns
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on how bad the injury is; some recover over weeks to months, others need long care

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A heavy blow or pressure on the shoulder, for example from a heavy bag
  • A hard birth, when a baby's shoulder is stretched
  • Swelling or inflammation of the nerves, or a growth pressing on them (less common)

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, speech trouble or sudden weakness on one side: call 999
  • After a serious accident, a limp arm or no feeling in it: go to hospital now
  • A neck injury with severe neck pain, or weakness in both arms or legs
  • A hand that turns pale, cold or blue
  • A newborn who does not move one arm: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Get checked early; the sooner a doctor looks, the better the plan
  • Support the weak arm and move it gently as your doctor or physiotherapist advises
  • Protect numb skin from cuts, knocks and hot water

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Recent shoulder injury or surgery
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Weak bones
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
