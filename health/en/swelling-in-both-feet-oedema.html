Swelling in both feet (oedema)

Puffiness of the feet and ankles from extra fluid in the tissues

SymptomHeart & blood vesselsArms & legs

What it is

Both feet and ankles look puffy because extra fluid collects there, often worse at the end of the day. Shoes and socks may feel tight.

  • Both feet and ankles
  • Often worse by evening
  • Puffy, tight, heavy feet
Which doctor
Family doctor
Common in
Older adults, pregnancy, and people who stand or sit for long hours
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; often eases with rest and raising the legs

Common signs

More signs

  • Sock or shoe marks that stay on the skin
  • A dent that stays for a moment after pressing the skin

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Standing or sitting for a long time, or hot weather
  • Weak leg veins, such as varicose veins
  • Heart, kidney or liver problems
  • Too much salt in food

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen feet with chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Only one leg swollen, red, hot or painful: go to hospital, it may be a clot
  • Swelling that comes fast, or waking up short of breath
  • Swelling in pregnancy with a bad headache or blurry vision
  • Skin that is broken, weeping or shiny and sore, especially with diabetes

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Raise your feet above hip level when resting
  • Move your ankles and walk often; avoid sitting still for hours

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
