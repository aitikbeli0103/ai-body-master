Peeling skin on the hands and feet

The skin on the hands and feet dries and comes off in flakes or sheets

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

The outer skin of the hands and feet dries and peels off. Often it is mild and linked to dry skin or a skin infection, but a few causes need a doctor.

  • Hands and feet
  • Can happen at any age
  • Dry, flaky or loose skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it lasts
Common in
Anyone; common in dry weather, with wet work or sweaty feet
Contagious
Some types can be, such as athlete's foot or a viral illness
Course
Often settles over a few weeks once the cause is found and avoided

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Dry skin from weather, hot water, frequent washing or hand sanitiser
  • A fungal skin infection, such as athlete's foot
  • Eczema, or soaps, cleaners and materials that irritate the skin
  • Sunburn, heavy sweating, or an illness with fever that has just passed
  • Some medicines or skin conditions like psoriasis; sometimes the cause is not clear

See a doctor first if

  • Widespread blisters or peeling skin with fever, or sore eyes or mouth: go to hospital now
  • A child with fever for several days, rash, red eyes or swollen hands and feet: see a doctor today
  • Red, swollen, painful palms and soles after starting a new medicine: ask your doctor soon
  • Swelling, pus, warmth or red streaks, or fever: see a doctor today
  • Any broken skin on the feet if you have diabetes, or cracks that bleed or will not settle

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep feet clean and dry, especially between the toes; change damp socks
  • Wear gloves for wet work, and shoes that fit and let the feet breathe
  • Do not pull off the peeling skin, scrub hard or soak for long in hot water
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before using any cream or medicine on the skin

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
