Frostbite

Skin and tissue damage caused by freezing cold

ConditionAny other bone or soft-tissue injuryWhole body

What it is

In very cold weather, the skin and the tissue under it can freeze, most often on the fingers, toes, nose and ears. It is rare in Malaysia but can happen on trips abroad or on cold mountains.

  • Fingers, toes, nose, ears
  • Cold trips, mountains
  • Numb, pale, hard skin
Which doctor
Family doctor, or hospital emergency department
Common in
Travellers and climbers in freezing places; people outside too long
Contagious
No
Course
Mild cases ease over days; deep ones need a doctor and may take months

Common signs

More signs

  • Cold, numb skin with pins and needles
  • Skin that looks pale, white, grey or waxy and feels hard
  • Pain, burning or throbbing when the skin warms up

Why it happens

  • Staying out in freezing air or wind for a long time
  • Wet clothes, tight gloves or shoes, or not enough warm clothing
  • Touching very cold metal or ice for a long time
  • Poor blood flow, smoking, diabetes, tiredness or alcohol can raise the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Skin that is white, hard or waxy, or stays numb after warming: go to hospital
  • Shivering that will not stop, slurred speech, confusion or drowsiness: call 999
  • Blisters, black skin or a very swollen limb
  • Fever, pus or spreading redness after the injury
  • A child, an older person or a person with diabetes with any frostbite

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Get out of the cold into a warm place and take off wet clothes
  • Take off rings and tight items, and warm the area gently with warm, not hot, water
  • Do not rub the skin, and do not use direct heat such as a heater or hot pack
  • Dress in dry layers, with warm gloves, socks and a hat when travelling in the cold

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
