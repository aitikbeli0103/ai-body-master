Hives from cold

Itchy red welts that appear on the skin after it gets cold

ConditionAllergiesSkin

What it is

In cold hives, the skin reacts to cold air, cold water or cold food and drink. Itchy red welts, and sometimes swelling, often appear as the skin warms up.

  • Skin exposed to the cold
  • After cold air or cold water
  • Itchy, raised red welts
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) or allergy doctor
Common in
Can start at any age; more often in young adults
Contagious
No
Course
Often lasts months or years and may ease over time; a doctor can advise

Common signs

More signs

  • Burning or stinging of the skin where the welts appear
  • Swelling of the hands, lips or face, sometimes after cold drinks or ice
  • Welts usually fade within a few hours as the skin warms up

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Cells in the skin release chemicals when the skin cools and then warms up again
  • Sometimes it follows an infection or goes along with another health problem

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, trouble breathing or wheezing: call 999
  • Feeling faint, dizzy or confused after cold water or a cold shower: call 999
  • Welts that hurt or burn, last more than a day, or leave bruises
  • Fever, chills or joint pain along with the rash
  • Reactions that keep coming back or get stronger over time

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Dress warmly and cover your skin in cold wind, air-conditioning or cold rain
  • Avoid sudden cold water such as cold pools and waterfalls, and never swim alone
  • Avoid ice and very cold drinks if they make your mouth or lips itch or swell
  • After a strong reaction in the past, ask your doctor about an emergency plan

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
