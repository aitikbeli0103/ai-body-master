Male infertility

When a problem with a man's sperm makes it hard for a couple to have a baby

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

A couple may find it hard to have a baby when the man makes too few sperm, or the sperm move poorly, look different or cannot travel out. Usually both partners are checked.

  • Sperm and testicles
  • Men trying for a baby
  • Often no signs at all
Which doctor
Family doctor, urologist or fertility specialist; often checked as a couple
Common in
Men trying for a baby; the man's side is part of the cause in many couples
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor can explain the options for each person

Common signs

More signs

  • Often there are no other signs, and the man feels well
  • Sometimes a lump, pain or swelling in the scrotum
  • Sometimes low interest in sex, or trouble with erections or ejaculation
  • Sometimes less facial or body hair, or breast swelling, when hormones are involved

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Swollen veins in the scrotum (varicocele), a common cause
  • Infections, an injury, past surgery, or a testicle that did not come down in childhood
  • Hormone or inherited (genetic) problems, or a blockage in the tubes that carry sperm
  • Often no clear cause is found

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle: go to hospital now
  • Feeling very low or hopeless about having a baby, or thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • A lump, hard area or swelling in a testicle
  • Pain or swelling in the scrotum with fever
  • Loss of sex drive, erection trouble, breast swelling or loss of body hair
  • Past cancer treatment, an undescended testicle, or a testicle injury or infection: see a doctor sooner

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Both partners can see a doctor together; a semen test is a simple first step
  • Talk with your partner or someone you trust about how you feel; counselling can help

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
