Contact dermatitis

A skin rash that appears where the skin touched something that irritates it or that it is allergic to

ConditionAllergiesSkin

What it is

Touching soap, cleaning products, metal, plants or other substances can leave the skin red, itchy, dry or blistered at the spot that touched them.

  • Where the skin touched it
  • Children and adults
  • Red, itchy or blistered skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it is severe or keeps coming back
Common in
People in cleaning, wet work or hairdressing; anyone using new products
Contagious
No
Course
Often settles in days to a few weeks once the cause is avoided

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Irritants such as soap, detergent, cleaning products, solvents, or washing hands very often
  • Allergens such as nickel in jewellery, latex, perfume, hair dye, rubber or some plants
  • Some skin-care products, cosmetics and creams put on the skin
  • Wet work, heat and sweat make the skin easier to irritate
  • The reaction can appear hours to days after contact, so the cause can be hard to spot

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A chemical in the eyes or on a large area of skin: rinse well with running water and go to hospital now
  • Large or spreading blisters, or a severe rash on the face, eyelids, mouth or genitals: see a doctor the same day
  • Pain, pus, yellow crusts, spreading redness or fever: the skin may be infected
  • A rash that does not improve after several days away from the suspected cause, or keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Find and keep away from what caused it; read the labels on soaps, cosmetics and cleaning products
  • Wear gloves for wet work or cleaning, with cotton gloves under rubber ones

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Contact dermatitis?

A skin rash that appears where the skin touched something that irritates it or that it is allergic to. Touching soap, cleaning products, metal, plants or other substances can leave the skin red, itchy, dry or blistered at the spot that touched them.

What are the common signs of Contact dermatitis?

Red rash · Itchy skin · Dry, flaky skin

Why does Contact dermatitis happen?

Irritants such as soap, detergent, cleaning products, solvents, or washing hands very often · Allergens such as nickel in jewellery, latex, perfume, hair dye, rubber or some plants · Some skin-care products, cosmetics and creams put on the skin · Wet work, heat and sweat make the skin easier to irritate · The reaction can appear hours to days after contact, so the cause can be hard to spot

How can I look after myself with Contact dermatitis?

Wash gently and pat dry · Try not to scratch; keep nails short · Keep the skin moisturised
