Squamous cell carcinoma

A cancer that starts in thin, flat surface cells, most often seen on sun-exposed skin

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

Squamous cells line the skin and the inside of the mouth, throat and other organs; this cancer starts when they grow out of control.

  • Skin or the lining of organs
  • Older age, long sun exposure
  • A sore that does not go away
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist), family doctor, cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Older adults; people with long sun exposure or weak body defences
Contagious
No
Course
On the skin it often grows slowly, but some can spread; have changes checked

Common signs

More signs

  • A sore that bleeds, crusts or does not go away
  • Often on skin that gets a lot of sun: face, ears, lips, scalp, hands
  • Inside the body the signs depend on the place, such as a long-lasting cough or trouble swallowing

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak body defences, for example after an organ transplant or with some long-term illnesses
  • Old burn scars, long-lasting sores, or contact with some chemicals can add to the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Bleeding that will not stop, or coughing up blood: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, or you cannot swallow even liquids: go to hospital now
  • A sore, lump or scaly patch that is still there after a few weeks
  • A growth that is getting bigger, bleeds easily or becomes painful
  • Hard, swollen glands in the neck, armpit or groin
  • A mouth sore, hoarse voice or trouble swallowing that lasts for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have a new or changing spot or sore checked by a doctor; do not put home remedies on it
  • Check your own skin now and then, including scalp, ears, lips and hands
  • Ask your team about pain, wound care and emotional support

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
