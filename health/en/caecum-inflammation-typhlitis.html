Caecum inflammation (typhlitis)

Serious inflammation of the caecum, the first part of the large bowel, mostly in people with a weak immune system

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

The caecum, the first part of the large bowel, becomes inflamed and can be infected. It mostly affects people whose body defences are very low, for example during chemotherapy, and needs urgent care.

  • Start of the large bowel
  • During chemotherapy, mostly
  • Fever and belly pain
Which doctor
Hospital emergency team; your cancer doctor (oncologist) and a surgeon
Common in
People having chemotherapy, especially for blood cancers; a very weak immune system
Contagious
No
Course
A serious illness that can worsen quickly; it needs urgent hospital care

Common signs

Why it happens

  • A very low number of white blood cells, the cells that fight germs
  • Chemotherapy, most often for blood cancers such as leukaemia
  • Bone marrow problems or a bone marrow transplant
  • Other illnesses or medicines that weaken the body's defences
  • Germs can then get into the damaged wall of the bowel and cause inflammation

See a doctor first if

  • Fever when you are on chemotherapy or have a weak immune system: call your cancer team or go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain, a hard swollen belly, or repeated vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Fast heartbeat, feeling faint or confused, or cold clammy skin: call 999
  • Pain in the lower right belly that gets worse, with fever or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Watery or bloody diarrhoea, or signs of dehydration such as dizziness and very little urine

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • If you are on chemotherapy, keep your cancer team's phone number with you and call early
  • Ask your cancer team before taking any medicine for fever, pain or diarrhoea
  • Rest and drink fluids as your team allows
  • Keep your blood-test and clinic appointments, and follow your care team's advice

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
