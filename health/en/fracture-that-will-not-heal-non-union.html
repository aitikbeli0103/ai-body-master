Fracture that will not heal (non-union)

A broken bone that has not joined together after a long time

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & muscles

What it is

A broken bone normally joins up over weeks to months. In non-union it stays apart, so the area keeps hurting and may move when it should not.

  • At an old fracture site
  • After a broken bone
  • Ongoing pain, weak limb
Which doctor
Bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon)
Common in
People after a bad fracture, smokers, people with diabetes
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a bone doctor's care; the plan depends on the bone and the cause

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain when you put weight on or use the limb
  • Swelling or tenderness around the old break
  • Movement or a feeling of looseness where the bone broke
  • The limb feels weak or unstable

Why it happens

  • A very bad break, or the bone ends not held steady
  • Poor blood supply to the bone
  • Smoking, diabetes or poor nutrition
  • An infection at the break
  • Often more than one reason together

See a doctor first if

  • A limb that is bent, out of shape or cannot take weight: go to hospital
  • Fever, or the skin over the break is hot, red or leaking fluid
  • Numbness, pale or cold skin below the break: go to hospital now
  • Pain or swelling that is getting worse instead of better over time
  • A new fall or knock to the same bone

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow your bone doctor's plan and keep your clinic visits
  • Follow the advice on resting the limb or using a cast, sling or crutches

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
