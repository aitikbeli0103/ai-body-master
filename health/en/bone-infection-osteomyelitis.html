Bone infection (osteomyelitis)

An infection inside a bone, caused by germs that reach the bone

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

Bone infection (osteomyelitis) happens when germs, usually bacteria, get into a bone, and it can cause deep bone pain, swelling and fever that need a doctor's check.

  • Inside a bone
  • After a wound or surgery
  • Deep bone pain and fever
Which doctor
See a doctor promptly: bone (orthopaedic) doctor, infection specialist
Common in
Children, older adults, and people with diabetes, deep wounds or weak immunity
Contagious
No, but germs from an open wound can spread by touch; keep wounds clean and covered
Course
Usually needs weeks of medicine, sometimes an operation; early care matters

Common signs

More signs

  • Swelling, warmth and redness over the area
  • Sometimes pus or fluid draining from the skin near the bone

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Germs travel through the blood from an infection elsewhere in the body
  • A deep wound, a broken bone that breaks the skin, or bone surgery
  • A skin or soft-tissue infection nearby, such as a foot sore, that spreads to the bone

See a doctor first if

  • High fever with shaking chills, confusion or feeling very weak: go to hospital now
  • A baby or child with a fever who will not use an arm or leg: see a doctor promptly
  • Bone pain with swelling, redness or warmth: see a doctor the same day
  • A foot sore or wound that is not getting better, especially with diabetes
  • Pus or fluid draining from the skin near a bone, or a wound that smells bad

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor early; do not wait for the pain to pass
  • Take all the medicine your doctor gives, for as long as they say, even if you feel better
  • Rest the area, and keep weight off it if your doctor advises
  • Go to all follow-up visits, and do not smoke

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
