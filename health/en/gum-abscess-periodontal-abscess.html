Gum abscess (periodontal abscess)

A painful pocket of pus in the gum beside a tooth, caused by infection

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

Germs get trapped in a deep gum pocket beside a tooth and form a painful, swollen lump of pus; it needs a dentist soon because the infection can spread.

  • Gum beside a tooth
  • Mostly adults
  • Throbbing pain and swelling
Which doctor
Dentist as soon as possible; gum specialist (periodontist)
Common in
Adults, especially with gum disease, diabetes or smoking
Contagious
No
Course
Pain may ease if it bursts, but the infection usually stays; a dentist is still needed

Common signs

  • A red, swollen, shiny gum next to a tooth
  • Throbbing pain that can spread to the jaw or ear
  • Pain when biting or chewing; the tooth may feel loose
  • A bad taste, bad breath, or pus coming from the gum
  • Sometimes fever or tender glands under the jaw

Why it happens

  • Gum disease that leaves deep pockets between the gum and the tooth
  • Plaque and tartar building up because the teeth are not cleaned well
  • A bit of food or floss stuck under the gum
  • Diabetes that is not well controlled, or smoking
  • A knock to the tooth or gum, or a weak immune system

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, mouth or neck, or trouble swallowing or breathing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fever with a swollen face, or feeling very unwell, shaky or confused: go to hospital now
  • Cannot open the mouth properly, or swelling that spreads under the jaw or toward the eye: go to hospital
  • A swollen gum with pus or throbbing pain: see a dentist the same day or the next day
  • Diabetes, a heart condition or weak immunity: do not wait, see a dentist early

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • See a dentist soon; do not wait for it to go away
  • Rinse your mouth gently with warm salt water; do not swallow it
  • Keep brushing gently with a soft brush and clean between the teeth as best you can
  • Do not squeeze, cut or poke the swelling
  • For pain, ask your dentist or pharmacist what is safe for you

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
