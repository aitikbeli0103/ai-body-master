Chronic cor pulmonale (lung-related heart disease)

Strain on the right side of the heart caused by long-term lung disease

ConditionLungs & breathingChest

What it is

When the lungs are damaged for a long time, the right side of the heart has to work harder to push blood through them. Over time it can tire and swell.

  • Lungs and right heart
  • Long-term lung disease
  • Breathless, swollen legs
Which doctor
Chest doctor and heart doctor (cardiologist)
Common in
Smokers and people with long-term lung disease such as COPD
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; care aims to keep symptoms steady

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term lung disease such as COPD or chronic bronchitis
  • Scarring of the lungs, or blood clots in the lungs
  • Sleep problems with low oxygen at night, such as sleep apnoea

See a doctor first if

  • Severe breathlessness, chest pain or fainting: call 999
  • Blue lips, or confusion and unusual sleepiness: go to hospital now
  • Swelling of the legs or belly that is getting worse
  • Quick weight gain over a few days
  • Fever or more phlegm, which can mean a chest infection

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Chronic cor pulmonale (lung-related heart disease)?

Strain on the right side of the heart caused by long-term lung disease. When the lungs are damaged for a long time, the right side of the heart has to work harder to push blood through them. Over time it can tire and swell.

What are the common signs of Chronic cor pulmonale (lung-related heart disease)?

Short of breath on stairs · Swollen legs and ankles · Tired all the time · Feeling faint · Tight, heavy chest · Cough with phlegm

Why does Chronic cor pulmonale (lung-related heart disease) happen?

Smoking

How can I look after myself with Chronic cor pulmonale (lung-related heart disease)?

Take medicine as your doctor says · Stop smoking · Stay active every day · Get the recommended vaccines
