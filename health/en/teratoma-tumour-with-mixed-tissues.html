Teratoma (tumour with mixed tissues)

A growth made of different kinds of tissue, such as hair, fat or bone; many are not cancer

ConditionCancer & tumoursReproductive

What it is

A teratoma is a tumour that grows from very early cells and can hold several kinds of tissue; some are not cancer, and some are.

  • Ovary, testicle or tailbone
  • Babies, children, young adults
  • A growth with mixed tissues
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a gynaecologist, urologist or surgeon, by location
Common in
Babies, children and young adults; the ovary type is most common in young women
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type and place; a doctor can explain what to expect after tests

Common signs

More signs

  • In a testicle: a lump or swelling, often painless
  • In a baby: a lump near the tailbone, often seen at birth
  • Sudden strong pain if the growth twists or bursts

Why it happens

  • It grows from very early cells that can turn into many kinds of tissue
  • The reason it happens is usually not known
  • For the testicle type: a testicle that never came down, or a close relative with testicular cancer
  • It is not caused by anything the person ate or did

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe pain in the lower belly, especially with vomiting or fever: go to hospital now
  • Sudden severe pain or swelling in a testicle: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant with belly pain or bleeding: go to hospital now
  • A lump or swelling in a testicle, even if it does not hurt
  • A baby with a lump near the tailbone: see a doctor promptly
  • A swollen belly, bloating or lower belly pain that lasts for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow your doctor's plan, including scans and check-ups; never stop treatment on your own
  • Know how your body normally feels, and tell a doctor about any new lump or change
  • Ask your team about pain, food, and any worries about having children

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
