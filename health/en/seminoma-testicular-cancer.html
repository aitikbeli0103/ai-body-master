Seminoma (testicular cancer)

A cancer of the testicle that starts in the sperm-making cells

ConditionCancer & tumoursReproductive

What it is

A seminoma is a type of testicular cancer. It often shows as a lump or swelling in one testicle, and doctors can check it with a simple exam and scan.

  • One testicle
  • Young to middle-aged men
  • A lump or heaviness
Which doctor
Urologist, cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Men from young to middle age
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the stage; the cancer care team can explain what to expect

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump or hard spot in a testicle, often not painful
  • A testicle that feels bigger, heavier or firmer
  • Back ache, if it has spread

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A testicle that did not move down into the sac in childhood
  • Being young or middle-aged

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden strong pain in a testicle, especially with swelling or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain or coughing up blood: call 999
  • A new lump, swelling or change in a testicle that lasts more than a few days
  • Back pain that does not go away, or a swollen leg
  • Weight loss without trying, or tender breasts

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Never stop or change treatment on your own
  • Know how your testicles normally feel and tell a doctor about changes
  • Ask the team about fertility, pain, and emotional support
  • Talk to someone you trust; you do not have to cope alone

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
