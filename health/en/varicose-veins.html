Varicose veins

Swollen, twisted veins just under the skin, most often in the legs

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsArms & legs

What it is

Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins that show under the skin, usually in the legs, and form when tiny valves inside the veins stop working well so blood pools instead of flowing up to the heart.

  • Usually in the legs
  • More common with age
  • Bulging veins, heavy legs
Which doctor
Family doctor; vascular surgeon (blood vessel doctor) if needed
Common in
Adults; more common with age, in women, in pregnancy and in people who stand a lot
Contagious
No
Course
Tends to stay and may slowly get worse over time; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak or damaged valves in the veins let blood pool and push the veins out

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing blood: call 999 (a clot may have reached the lung)
  • A vein that bleeds: lie down, raise the leg, hold a clean cloth firmly on it and call 999 if it does not stop
  • A hot, red, painful or swollen leg, or one leg suddenly much more swollen than the other: go to hospital now
  • A hard, red, painful cord or lump along a vein: see a doctor soon
  • A sore on the leg or ankle that does not go away, or skin that turns dark, hard, itchy or weepy
  • Pain, heaviness or swelling that keeps getting worse or stops you from walking or working

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • When resting, raise your legs on pillows above the level of your heart
  • Avoid standing or sitting still for long; change position and move often
  • Ask your doctor whether compression stockings are right for you; the fit matters and they are not safe for everyone
  • Do not rub or massage a painful, hard or red part of the leg, and protect your legs from knocks

Tell your therapist

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Varicose veins?

Swollen, twisted veins just under the skin, most often in the legs. Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins that show under the skin, usually in the legs, and form when tiny valves inside the veins stop working well so blood pools instead of flowing up to the heart.

What are the common signs of Varicose veins?

Bulging veins in the legs · Heavy, tired legs · Swollen legs and ankles · Cramps in the calf · Itchy skin

Why does Varicose veins happen?

Runs in the family · Getting older · Pregnancy · Standing or sitting still for long · An injury or fall

How can I look after myself with Varicose veins?

Stay active every day · Keep a healthy weight
