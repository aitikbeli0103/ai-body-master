Red palms

Reddening of the palms of the hands, often warm and usually painless

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legs

What it is

Red palms are a sign, not a disease. Pregnancy and liver, thyroid or joint conditions can show up this way, and some people simply have it in the family.

  • Palms of the hands
  • Can be normal in pregnancy
  • Red, warm, rarely sore
Which doctor
Family doctor first; a specialist if the cause needs one
Common in
Pregnant women; people with liver, thyroid or joint problems; some families
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; in pregnancy it often fades after the birth

Common signs

More signs

  • Red palms, most often at the base of the thumb and the little-finger side
  • The skin may feel warm; it is usually not sore or itchy
  • The redness may turn pale when pressed and come back when you let go
  • Other signs depend on the cause: tiredness, yellow skin or eyes, sweating, a racing heart or swollen joints

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Liver disease, including damage from long-term heavy alcohol use
  • An overactive thyroid or diabetes
  • Joint and immune conditions, such as rheumatoid arthritis
  • Skin problems such as eczema, some medicines, or it simply runs in the family

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, black stools, or becoming confused or very sleepy: go to hospital now
  • A child with red hands or feet and fever, red eyes, a rash or red, cracked lips: see a doctor today
  • In pregnancy: very itchy hands and feet, bad headache, vision changes or sudden swelling: tell your doctor today
  • Red palms with yellow skin or eyes, dark urine, pale stools or a swollen belly: see a doctor soon
  • Red palms with a racing heart, sweating, weight loss, or swollen, stiff joints: see a doctor soon
  • Red palms that last for weeks and have no clear reason: see a doctor for a check-up

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • If you are pregnant, mention it at your next antenatal visit
  • Cut down on alcohol; if you drink heavily, ask your doctor for help
  • Do not stop or change any medicine yourself; if you think one is the cause, ask your doctor or pharmacist

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
