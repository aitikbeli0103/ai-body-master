White patches on the skin

Skin patches that have lost some of their colour and look lighter or whiter than the skin nearby

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Some areas of skin look paler or white. Causes range from mild dry skin to a fungal infection or vitiligo, so it is worth having a doctor look at it.

  • Any part of the skin
  • Can start at any age
  • Paler than the skin nearby
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Any age; the cause often depends on age, climate and sun
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as certain skin infections; most are not
Course
Depends on the cause; some patches fade, some stay, and a doctor can explain

Common signs

More signs

  • Skin that is paler than normal, with edges that can be sharp or soft
  • A few small spots, or larger patches that join together
  • Patches may slowly grow, stay the same, or fade and come back

Why it happens

  • Vitiligo, where parts of the skin lose their colour; often the reason is not known
  • A fungal skin infection, which is common in warm, humid weather
  • Skin that was inflamed before, for example by eczema, a rash, a burn or an injury
  • Dry, sensitive skin, with mild pale patches that are more common in children
  • Less often, an infection that affects skin and nerves, or another condition a doctor needs to check

See a doctor first if

  • Red, hot, swollen or painful skin, or sores and blisters, especially with fever: see a doctor today
  • A pale patch where you cannot feel touch, heat or pain, or numb, weak hands or feet: see a doctor soon
  • Patches that spread quickly, or keep growing over weeks
  • A patch that is raised, shiny, bleeding or a sore that will not go away
  • A baby or young child with white patches, especially with fits or slow development: see a doctor promptly
  • Patches together with weight loss, fever, tiredness or swollen glands that you cannot explain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before using any cream, oil or home mixture on the patches
  • Take a photo now and then, to show your doctor how the patches change

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A severe allergy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
