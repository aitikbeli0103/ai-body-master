Pimples on the cheeks

Small red or pus-topped bumps on the cheeks, usually from blocked skin pores

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Pimples on the cheeks are small, sometimes sore bumps that often start when oil and dead skin block the pores and germs on the skin add swelling.

  • Cheeks and face
  • Common in teens and adults
  • Red bumps, sometimes sore
Which doctor
Pharmacist or family doctor; a skin doctor (dermatologist) if it does not improve
Common in
Teens and young adults most often, but adults of any age too
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes; it can take weeks or months to improve with steady care

Types of acne spots

Acne grades

Why care for acne early

Most severe acne started as mild acne that was left alone. Marks and scars take longer to fade, so start caring early.

Marks left after acne

Ways that make acne worse

  • Squeezing pimples: it pushes germs deeper
  • Using steroid creams for a long time: acne comes back when you stop
  • Strong acid peels at home: skin becomes sensitive
  • Gua sha or scrubbing over pimples: it spreads the inflammation
Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

More signs

  • Blackheads or whiteheads (small plugged pores)
  • Tender or sore skin when touched
  • Oily or shiny skin on the cheeks
  • Dark or red marks that stay for a while after pimples fade, and sometimes small scars

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Pores blocked by oil and dead skin; germs on the skin can add swelling
  • Hormone changes, such as in puberty, before periods or in pregnancy
  • Rubbing or pressure on the cheeks from hands, phones, masks or helmets
  • Heavy make-up, thick creams or hair products that block pores

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, face or throat, or trouble breathing after a new product or medicine: call 999
  • A hot, red, very painful swelling on the face with fever, or swelling near the eye: see a doctor today
  • Deep, painful lumps that leave marks or scars
  • Pimples that suddenly get much worse, or come with irregular periods or extra hair growth
  • A bump that does not go away in a few weeks, bleeds or keeps growing, especially in older adults
  • Pimples that began after starting a new medicine: ask your doctor or pharmacist

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep hands, phones and hair off the cheeks; wash pillowcases, towels and phone screens often
  • Choose oil-free, non-clogging skincare and make-up, and remove make-up before sleep
  • Ask a pharmacist or skin doctor before using strong acne products; they can dry or sting the skin

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • A severe allergy
