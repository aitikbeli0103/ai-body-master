Fluid in the joint (joint effusion)

Extra fluid collecting inside a joint, making it swollen and stiff

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

Extra fluid builds up in or around a joint, often the knee. The joint looks puffy and can feel tight, warm or painful to bend.

  • In or around a joint
  • Any age, often after strain
  • Swollen, stiff, tight
Which doctor
Family doctor, bone and joint doctor (orthopaedic)
Common in
Active people, older adults, people with joint problems
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor can explain what to expect

Common signs

More signs

  • A tight or achy feeling around the joint

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Wear of the joint (osteoarthritis) or other types of arthritis
  • Gout or an infection in the joint

See a doctor first if

  • A swollen joint with fever or chills: see a doctor today, it may be an infection
  • A joint that is very hot, red and very painful
  • Swelling after a fall or injury, or you cannot put weight on the limb: go to hospital
  • A bent or out-of-shape limb, or numb, pale or cold skin: go to hospital now
  • Swelling that keeps coming back or does not go down

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Raise the limb on a pillow when sitting or lying down
  • Gentle movement as your doctor allows; ask before taking any medicine

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
