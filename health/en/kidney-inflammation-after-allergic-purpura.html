Kidney inflammation after allergic purpura

Kidney inflammation that can follow allergic purpura, a rash of purple-red spots on the skin

ConditionAllergiesAbdomen

What it is

Allergic purpura causes inflammation of tiny blood vessels. When the kidneys are affected, blood or protein can leak into the urine, and the face or legs may swell.

  • The kidneys
  • Often starts in children
  • Urine changes and swelling
Which doctor
Kidney doctor (nephrologist); child doctor (paediatrician) for children
Common in
Mostly children who have had allergic purpura; adults can be affected too
Contagious
No
Course
Varies; some people need check-ups for a long time, so keep every appointment

Common signs

More signs

  • Earlier or at the same time: purple-red spots on the legs, joint pain or belly pain

Why it happens

  • The body's defence system overreacts and inflames tiny blood vessels, including those in the kidneys
  • It often starts after an infection, such as a throat or chest infection
  • Sometimes a medicine or another trigger is found, but often it is not
  • The kidneys are affected only in some people who have allergic purpura

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, chest pain, a fit, confusion, or a very bad headache with vomiting: call 999
  • Severe belly pain, vomiting blood, or black or bloody stools: go to hospital now
  • Passing very little urine, or none at all: go to hospital now
  • Red, brown or cola-coloured urine, or swelling that is getting worse: see a doctor the same day
  • New purple-red spots, new joint pain or a fever: see a doctor soon
  • A child who is very unwell, very sleepy or feverish: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist before any pain medicine, supplement or herbal product; some can strain the kidneys
  • Follow the advice on salt, food and fluids that your doctor or dietitian gives you
