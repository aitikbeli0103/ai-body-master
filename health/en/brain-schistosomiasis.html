Brain schistosomiasis

A rare infection in which a parasite caught from fresh water sends its eggs to the brain

ConditionInfectionsHead

What it is

A parasite (a tiny worm) caught from infected fresh water can, rarely, send its eggs to the brain. The swelling around the eggs may cause headache, fits, weakness or trouble seeing.

  • In the brain
  • After wading in infected water
  • Headache, fits, weak limbs
Which doctor
Hospital doctor: infectious disease specialist or neurologist
Common in
People who swim or wade in fresh water in parts of Asia, Africa or South America
Contagious
Not from person to person; caught from fresh water with infected snails
Course
Needs hospital care; how it goes depends on how early it is found

Common signs

More signs

  • A headache that does not go away, sometimes with feeling sick or vomiting
  • Signs can start weeks, months or even years after the contact with the water

Why it happens

  • Swimming, wading, washing or playing in fresh water that has infected snails
  • The parasite's tiny young (larvae) go through the skin and travel in the blood
  • Some eggs reach the brain and cause swelling around them
  • Eggs passed in the stool or urine of infected people reach water and infect snails again

See a doctor first if

  • A fit, sudden weakness on one side, face drooping or trouble speaking: call 999
  • Sudden severe headache, confusion or being hard to wake: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fever with a stiff neck, or vomiting with a bad headache: go to hospital now
  • Headache that keeps getting worse, or new vision or walking problems, after fresh-water contact in an affected area
  • Blood in the urine or stool, or long-lasting belly pain, after time in an affected area: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • See a doctor early and tell them about any travel or fresh-water contact, even long ago
  • Follow your hospital team's advice and go to every follow-up visit
  • Do not drive or swim alone after a fit, until your doctor says it is safe
  • When visiting affected areas, avoid swimming, wading or washing in fresh lakes, rivers and ponds
  • Ask a travel clinic for advice before you travel to affected areas

Tell your therapist

  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Numb or tingling arm
