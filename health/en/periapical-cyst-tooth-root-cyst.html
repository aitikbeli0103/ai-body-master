Periapical cyst (tooth root cyst)

A fluid-filled sac at the tip of a tooth root, usually after a long tooth infection

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

A periapical cyst forms at the tip of a tooth root when the tooth nerve has died or the tooth has been infected for a long time. It often causes no pain, so it may be found only on an X-ray.

  • At the tip of a tooth root
  • Adults with old tooth problems
  • Often painless; may ache
Which doctor
Dentist; root canal specialist (endodontist) or mouth surgeon
Common in
Adults with a deep cavity, a past tooth knock or an old filling
Contagious
No
Course
Does not go away by itself; a dentist can explain how to deal with the tooth

Common signs

More signs

  • A tooth that is darker in colour or feels tender when you bite
  • A small swelling or bump on the gum above the tooth
  • A bad taste in the mouth if the cyst leaks

Why it happens

  • A deep cavity that reaches the nerve of the tooth
  • A tooth injury that damaged the nerve
  • A tooth that has been treated before but is infected again
  • Poor cleaning of the teeth and gums over a long time

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, mouth or neck with trouble swallowing or breathing: go to hospital now
  • Fever with a swollen face or a very painful tooth
  • Swelling around the eye, or you cannot open your mouth well
  • Pus or a bad taste from a bump on the gum
  • A tooth that is loose, or pain that lasts more than a few days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a dentist soon, even if there is no pain
  • Brush gently twice a day and clean between the teeth
  • Chew on the other side until the tooth is checked
