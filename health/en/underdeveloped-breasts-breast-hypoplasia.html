Underdeveloped breasts (breast hypoplasia)

Breasts that are smaller than expected or have not fully developed, often just a natural difference

ConditionWomen's healthChest

What it is

Breasts differ a lot in size and shape, and 'underdeveloped' means the breast tissue stayed small or grew only a little, which is often natural but sometimes linked to hormones.

  • The breasts
  • Girls and women
  • Often only a size difference
Which doctor
Family doctor; gynaecologist or hormone doctor if needed
Common in
Girls and women of any age; it often runs in the family
Contagious
No
Course
Often stays the same and is usually harmless; a doctor can check for hormone causes

Common signs

  • Breasts that stay small, with little breast tissue after puberty
  • One breast that is clearly smaller or shaped differently from the other
  • Breasts or nipples that develop late, or only a little
  • Sometimes other hormone signs, such as late or missed periods
  • Feeling shy or unhappy about how the body looks

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Often it is simply your natural size and shape, and may run in the family
  • Very low body weight, very hard training, or too little food for a long time can slow growth

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself, or feeling hopeless about your body: see a doctor now or call 999
  • A new lump, nipple discharge, skin dimpling or a change in only one breast
  • A girl whose breasts or periods have not started well into her teens
  • Breasts that shrink, together with missed periods, hot flushes or very low weight
  • One breast that is much smaller than the other and is still changing

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Remember that breast size varies a lot and does not show how healthy you are
  • Get to know how your breasts normally look and feel, and check them regularly
  • Be careful with creams, pills or products that promise bigger breasts; ask your doctor first
  • Talk to someone you trust if you feel upset about your body

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
