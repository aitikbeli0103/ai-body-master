Varicose vein ulcer

An open sore on the lower leg caused by weak leg veins

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

When leg veins cannot push blood back up well, pressure builds and the skin near the ankle can break down into a slow-healing sore. It needs proper wound care from a doctor or nurse.

  • Lower leg, near the ankle
  • More common in older adults
  • Open sore, swollen leg
Which doctor
Family doctor; vascular surgeon or wound nurse
Common in
Older adults; people with varicose veins, past leg clots or long standing
Contagious
No
Course
Often heals slowly over weeks to months with care, and can come back

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak valves in the leg veins, so blood pools in the lower leg
  • Varicose veins or an old blood clot in the leg
  • A small knock or scratch that does not heal well

See a doctor first if

  • A suddenly swollen, painful or warm leg: go to hospital now (possible blood clot)
  • Chest pain or sudden trouble breathing: call 999
  • Fever, spreading redness, pus or a bad smell from the sore
  • Heavy bleeding from a varicose vein: press firmly with a clean cloth, lie down, raise the leg and call 999
  • A sore that is getting bigger, very painful or not healing

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Varicose vein ulcer?

An open sore on the lower leg caused by weak leg veins. When leg veins cannot push blood back up well, pressure builds and the skin near the ankle can break down into a slow-healing sore. It needs proper wound care from a doctor or nurse.

What are the common signs of Varicose vein ulcer?

Bulging veins in the legs · Swollen legs and ankles · Heavy, tired legs · Itchy skin · Cuts heal slowly

Why does Varicose vein ulcer happen?

Standing or sitting still for long · Getting older

How can I look after myself with Varicose vein ulcer?

Keep wounds clean and covered · Rest with your legs raised · Take breaks from sitting · Keep the skin moisturised
