Bone cyst

A fluid-filled hollow, not a cancer, inside a bone

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & muscles

What it is

A bone cyst is a hollow space inside a bone that fills with fluid. It is usually not cancer and is often found by chance on an X-ray.

  • Inside a bone
  • Often children and teens
  • Often no pain at all
Which doctor
Bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon), family doctor
Common in
Children and teenagers, most often in long bones like the arm or thigh
Contagious
No
Course
Some stay the same or shrink with growth; the doctor watches it over time

Common signs

More signs

  • Mild swelling over the bone
  • Sudden pain after a small knock, because the bone has cracked

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It often shows up while a child is growing fast
  • It seems linked to how a growing bone develops
  • It is not caused by anything the child ate or did

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe pain after a knock or fall, or a limb that looks bent: go to hospital
  • Cannot move the limb or put weight on it
  • Pain that wakes the child at night or keeps getting worse
  • Fever with a hot, red, swollen area over the bone

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep your follow-up visits and X-rays as your doctor asks
  • Ask your doctor which sports or rough play are safe for now
  • Choose gentle activity and avoid hard knocks to the affected bone

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
