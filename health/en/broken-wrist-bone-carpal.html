Broken wrist bone (carpal)

A break in one of the small bones that make up the wrist

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legs

What it is

One of the small wrist bones cracks, often after a fall onto the hand. Pain may be mild at first, so it can be missed.

  • Small bones of the wrist
  • After a fall on the hand
  • Wrist pain that lingers
Which doctor
Family doctor or emergency department; bone doctor (orthopaedic)
Common in
Active young adults and people who fall; sports players
Contagious
No
Course
Often heals in weeks; some wrist bones heal slowly, so follow-up matters

Common signs

More signs

  • Swelling and tenderness when you press the wrist
  • Sometimes a "sprain" that does not get better

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak bones can break more easily

See a doctor first if

  • Bone through the skin or heavy bleeding: call 999
  • Numb, pale, cold or blue fingers: go to hospital now
  • A bent wrist or hand, or you cannot move it
  • Wrist pain that does not improve after a few days
  • Increasing swelling or pain even with rest

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Support the wrist in a splint or sling until you are seen
  • Raise the hand on a pillow to ease swelling
  • Go to your follow-up visits, even if it feels better

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
