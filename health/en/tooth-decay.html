Tooth decay

Damage to the hard outer layer of a tooth, which can wear a hole (a cavity) into it

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

Germs in plaque turn sugar into acid that slowly wears away the hard outer layer of a tooth, and over time it can make a hole and reach the nerve inside.

  • The hard surface of a tooth
  • Children and adults
  • Sensitive or aching tooth
Which doctor
Dentist (a check-up, and a filling if needed)
Common in
Very common in children and adults; more with sugary food and drinks
Contagious
No, though the germs can pass on in saliva, for example by sharing spoons
Course
Early spots may be kept in check with good care; a hole will not repair itself

Common signs

  • A white, brown or black spot on a tooth
  • Pain or sensitivity to hot, cold or sweet food and drinks
  • Pain when biting or chewing
  • A visible hole in the tooth, or food that keeps getting stuck there
  • Bad breath or a bad taste in the mouth

Why it happens

  • Plaque, a sticky film of germs, left on the teeth
  • Frequent sugary or starchy snacks and drinks, which feed the germs
  • Not brushing well, or not using fluoride toothpaste
  • A dry mouth, for example from some medicines or health problems
  • Babies who fall asleep with a bottle of milk or a sweet drink

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen face or neck, or trouble swallowing or breathing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fever with a toothache or a swollen cheek: see a doctor or go to hospital the same day
  • A toothache that throbs or keeps you awake at night: see a dentist soon, as the nerve may be involved
  • A swollen gum, pimple-like bump or pus near a tooth: see a dentist soon, it may be an abscess
  • A child with a toothache, fever or swollen cheek: see a doctor or dentist promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Brush twice a day, especially before bed, with fluoride toothpaste
  • Clean between the teeth every day with floss or small brushes
  • Cut down on sweets, sugary drinks and constant snacking; water is best between meals
  • See your dentist regularly; small holes are simpler to deal with when found early
  • Do not wait for pain; decay often does not hurt until it is deep

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
