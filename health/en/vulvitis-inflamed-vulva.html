Vulvitis (inflamed vulva)

Redness, itching or soreness of the outer part of the female genitals (the vulva)

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

The skin of the vulva becomes inflamed, so it can look red or swollen and feel itchy, burning or sore, often from irritation, allergy or infection.

  • The skin of the vulva
  • Women and girls of any age
  • Itching, burning, soreness
Which doctor
Family doctor, gynaecologist or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Women and girls of any age; more likely with sweating, irritants or low oestrogen
Contagious
Some types can be, such as infections passed on through sex
Course
Often improves when the cause is found and dealt with; it can come back

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Irritation from scented soap, wipes, detergents, pads, condoms or tight synthetic clothes
  • An allergy to a product that touches the skin
  • Sweat and damp, or skin that stays in contact with urine or stool
  • Skin conditions such as eczema, or low oestrogen after menopause

See a doctor first if

  • Fever, spreading redness, pus, or a hot, very painful swelling: go to hospital today
  • Painful blisters or open sores, or sores that do not go away: see a doctor soon
  • A lump, or a white, red or dark patch that stays or keeps growing
  • Bleeding that is not from your period, or any bleeding after menopause
  • A girl or child with soreness or discharge: see a doctor promptly
  • Symptoms that are no better after a week or two of gentle care, or that keep coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself
