Precancerous cervical lesions

Abnormal changes in the cells of the cervix (neck of the womb) that are not yet cancer

ConditionCancer & tumoursReproductive

What it is

Cells on the surface of the cervix change in an abnormal way, most often after a long-lasting HPV infection. They are not cancer, but some can slowly become cancer if not followed up.

  • Neck of the womb (cervix)
  • Found on screening tests
  • Usually no signs at all
Which doctor
Family doctor, or a gynaecologist (women's specialist)
Common in
Sexually active women; smoking or a weak immune system raise the chance
Contagious
No. But HPV, the usual cause, can spread through sexual contact
Course
Many mild changes go away by themselves; your doctor decides what follow-up is needed

Common signs

More signs

  • Found on a cervical screening test (Pap smear or HPV test)
  • These signs can also come from other, more common problems

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-lasting infection with certain types of HPV, a virus passed on through sex
  • Most HPV infections clear by themselves; trouble starts when the virus stays for years
  • More sexual partners, for you or your partner, raises the chance of catching HPV

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding with dizziness or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant, with bleeding or severe belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause
  • Bloody, watery or smelly vaginal discharge
  • Pelvic pain, or pain during sex, that does not go away
  • A screening result that was not normal and no follow-up visit yet

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

What are the common signs of Precancerous cervical lesions?
