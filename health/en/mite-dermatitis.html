Mite dermatitis

An itchy skin rash caused by bites or contact with tiny mites

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Tiny mites can bite or irritate the skin and cause red, very itchy bumps. They may come from straw, grain, animals, birds or dusty bedding.

  • Skin: arms, neck, trunk, legs
  • After contact with mites
  • Very itchy red bumps
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone in contact with mites, such as farm workers or pet and bird owners
Contagious
Some types can be: scabies mites spread by close skin contact
Course
Usually settles in days to weeks once contact with the mites stops

Common signs

More signs

  • Bumps often appear on the arms, neck, trunk or legs
  • Scratching can cause sores, crusts or an infection
  • Others at home may itch too if the source is shared

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Bites or contact with tiny mites from straw, grain, hay, plants or animals
  • Mites in dusty or damp bedding, mattresses, carpets or sofas
  • Some people react more strongly, such as those with sensitive skin or eczema

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face or lips, trouble breathing or feeling faint after a bite: call 999
  • Rash with fever, spreading redness, pus or growing pain: see a doctor promptly
  • A baby, young child or older person with a widespread, very itchy rash: see a doctor promptly
  • Itching between the fingers or on the wrists with others at home itching too: it may be scabies
  • A rash that is not better after about two weeks, keeps coming back or stops you sleeping

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash the skin gently with water and mild soap after contact with straw, grain, animals or dust
  • Wash bedding, towels and clothes in hot water if the fabric allows, and dry them in strong sun
  • Clean mattresses, sofas and carpets often, and have pets with mites checked by a vet

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
