Ganglion cyst of the hand

A soft, fluid-filled lump on the hand or wrist, often at the back of the wrist

ConditionBones, joints & spineArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

A smooth, round lump filled with jelly-like fluid that grows on the wrist, hand or finger. It is not cancer, and it can come and go.

  • Wrist, hand or finger
  • Often young to middle age
  • Soft lump, may ache
Which doctor
Family doctor, hand or bone doctor if painful
Common in
Often women and adults aged 20 to 40
Contagious
No
Course
May stay the same, shrink or go away by itself; it can also return

Common signs

More signs

  • The lump may change size, often bigger after hand use

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Fluid from a joint or tendon cover forms a small pouch
  • Repeated hand and wrist use, or a past wrist injury
  • Arthritis in nearby small joints

See a doctor first if

  • A lump that grows fast, is hard, or does not move at all
  • Redness, heat, pus or fever around the lump
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in the fingers, or a weak grip
  • Fingers that turn pale or cold, or a lump after a hand injury

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Do not hit or press the lump to burst it; this can cause injury
  • Rest the hand and wrist when they ache; avoid heavy repeated gripping
  • A loose, comfortable wrist support may help; ask your doctor
  • If it does not bother you, a doctor may advise just watching it

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Numb or tingling arm
