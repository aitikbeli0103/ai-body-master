Whooping cough (pertussis)

An airway infection that spreads easily and causes long coughing fits, sometimes with a 'whoop' sound

ConditionInfectionsChest

What it is

Whooping cough is a bacterial infection of the breathing tubes that starts like a cold, then brings hard fits of coughing, often with a 'whoop' when breathing in.

  • Airways and lungs
  • Most risky for young babies
  • Long fits of coughing
Which doctor
Family doctor; a paediatrician for babies and children
Common in
Anyone; most dangerous for young babies, who are too young to be fully vaccinated
Contagious
Yes, easily, through droplets from coughs and sneezes, mostly in the first weeks
Course
The cough often lasts for weeks, and sometimes for months

Common signs

  • At first, a runny nose, a mild cough and sometimes a low fever, like a cold
  • After a week or two, hard fits of coughing, one cough right after another
  • A high-pitched 'whoop' when breathing in after a fit (not everyone has this)
  • Vomiting, or feeling very tired and out of breath, after a coughing fit
  • In young babies there may be no whoop; they may struggle to breathe or turn pale or blue

Why it happens

  • Infection with a germ (bacteria) called Bordetella pertussis
  • Breathing in tiny droplets from an infected person's cough or sneeze
  • Close, long contact with someone who has it, such as living in the same home
  • Not having had the vaccine, or being too young to have had every dose
  • Protection from the vaccine can fade over the years, so teenagers and adults can catch it too

See a doctor first if

  • A baby or child who stops breathing for a moment, turns blue or pale, goes floppy or has a fit: call 999
  • Struggling to breathe, blue lips, or cannot catch your breath after coughing: call 999
  • A baby or young child with coughing fits, vomiting after coughing, or poor feeding: see a doctor promptly
  • Coughing so much that you cannot eat or drink, or signs of dehydration such as little urine
  • A high fever, fast breathing or chest pain on top of the cough: it may have spread to the lungs
  • Contact with someone who has whooping cough, especially if you live with a baby or are pregnant

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Stay home and keep away from babies, pregnant women and older people until your doctor says it is safe
  • Rest, take small sips of fluid often, and eat small meals if coughing makes you vomit
  • Avoid smoke, dust and strong smells, which can set off a fit of coughing
  • Cover your mouth when you cough, use tissues, and wash your hands often
  • Ask your doctor about the whooping cough vaccine for children, pregnant women and people close to babies

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
