Femoral (thigh) nerve injury

Damage to the big nerve at the front of the thigh

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legWhole body

What it is

The femoral nerve runs from the lower back down the front of the thigh. When it is squeezed, stretched or hurt, the thigh and knee can feel weak, numb or painful.

  • Front of the thigh
  • After injury, surgery, diabetes
  • Weak, numb or aching
Which doctor
Family doctor, nerve doctor (neurologist) or bone doctor
Common in
People after hip or pelvic injury or surgery, and people with diabetes
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause and how badly the nerve is hurt; often slow, over weeks to months

Common signs

More signs

  • Hard to climb stairs or get up from a chair
  • Numbness or tingling on the front of the thigh and inner leg
  • Aching or burning pain in the groin or thigh
  • The thigh muscle may look thinner over time

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Pressure on the nerve during hip, pelvic or belly surgery
  • Lying a long time in an awkward position, or a tight band or strap

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden leg weakness with back or belly pain, or after a hard fall: go to hospital now
  • Weakness or numbness in both legs, or trouble passing urine or stools: go to hospital now
  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Falls, or the knee keeps giving way
  • Numbness or weakness that keeps getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Use a stick or rail on stairs to lower the risk of falls
  • If you have diabetes, keep up regular checks with your doctor
  • Ask your doctor about gentle exercises that are right for you

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
