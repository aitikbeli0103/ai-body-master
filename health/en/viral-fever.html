Viral fever

A fever caused by a virus, often with body aches and tiredness

ConditionInfectionsHead

What it is

Many different viruses can make the body temperature rise. The fever is one way the body fights the virus, and it often goes away by itself.

  • Whole body, feels hot
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Fever, aches, tiredness
Which doctor
Family doctor; children see a paediatrician
Common in
Anyone, especially young children and during the flu season
Contagious
Often yes, by droplets from coughs and sneezes, and by touch
Course
Often settles within a few days; some viruses take longer

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Touching dirty surfaces and then your face
  • Crowded places and poor air flow
  • Being tired or run down, which makes it easier to catch

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, chest pain, or a fit (seizure): call 999
  • High fever with a stiff neck, confusion, or a rash that does not fade: go to hospital now
  • Fever with bad belly pain, repeated vomiting, bleeding, or drowsiness; dengue is common in Malaysia
  • Fever lasting more than three days, or signs of dehydration
  • A baby, young child or older person who is very unwell: see a doctor promptly

Questions and answers

What is Viral fever?

A fever caused by a virus, often with body aches and tiredness. Many different viruses can make the body temperature rise. The fever is one way the body fights the virus, and it often goes away by itself.

What are the common signs of Viral fever?

Fever, feeling hot · Shivering with chills · Aching all over · Headache across the forehead · Tired all the time · No appetite

Why does Viral fever happen?

Crowded places and droplets

How can I look after myself with Viral fever?

Ask your pharmacist · Wash your hands often
