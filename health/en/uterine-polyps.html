Uterine polyps

Small soft growths on the inner wall of the womb

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

A polyp is a small growth that sticks out from the womb lining. Most are not cancer, but a doctor should check them, especially if there is bleeding.

  • Inside the womb
  • Often women over 40
  • May cause odd bleeding
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's doctor)
Common in
Women near or after menopause; also younger women
Contagious
No
Course
They can stay the same, grow, or sometimes go away; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • Bleeding after menopause, or trouble becoming pregnant

Why it happens

Other causes

  • More likely with age, high blood pressure and being overweight
  • Some hormone medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Any bleeding after menopause
  • Very heavy bleeding, dizziness or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding between periods or after sex that keeps happening
  • Bleeding or severe belly pain in pregnancy
  • Feeling very tired, pale or short of breath from long-lasting bleeding

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Go for the scan or check your doctor suggests; do not ignore odd bleeding
  • Ask your doctor before taking any hormone medicine or supplement

Questions and answers

What is Uterine polyps?

Small soft growths on the inner wall of the womb. A polyp is a small growth that sticks out from the womb lining. Most are not cancer, but a doctor should check them, especially if there is bleeding.

What are the common signs of Uterine polyps?

Often no signs at first · Period comes late or early · Heavy or long periods

Why does Uterine polyps happen?

The cause is not fully known · Hormone changes

How can I look after myself with Uterine polyps?

Note down your symptoms · Check your blood pressure at home · Eat healthy, balanced meals
