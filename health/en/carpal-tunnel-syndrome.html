Carpal tunnel syndrome

A squeezed nerve in the wrist that causes tingling, numbness and pain in the hand

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

The median nerve runs through a narrow tunnel in the wrist, and when it is squeezed the thumb, index and middle fingers can tingle, go numb or hurt, often worse at night.

  • Wrist and hand
  • More common in women
  • Tingling, numbness, weak grip
Which doctor
Family doctor; nerve, bone or hand doctor; occupational therapist
Common in
Adults, more often women; people who use their hands a lot; pregnant women
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes at first; it may worsen if ignored, so get advice early

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Repeated or forceful hand and wrist work, or using tools that vibrate
  • Swelling in the wrist, for example after an injury or from arthritis
  • Pregnancy, diabetes, an underactive thyroid or being overweight
  • A narrow wrist tunnel that runs in some families, or an old wrist fracture

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or trouble speaking, or sudden numbness on one side: call 999
  • Arm or hand numbness or pain with chest pain, sweating or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Numbness that stays all the time, or a hand that is weak or drops things
  • The muscle at the base of the thumb getting thin or weak
  • Hand numbness after a wrist or neck injury, or together with neck pain
  • Tingling or numbness that lasts for weeks or wakes you most nights

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest your hands and wrists, and take short breaks from tasks that bring on the tingling
  • Keep your wrist straight and relaxed at work and when you sleep
  • Set up your desk, keyboard and tools so your wrists stay in a natural position
  • Shake or gently move your hands when they tingle; ask about a light wrist support for night

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
