No sperm in semen (azoospermia)

No sperm found in the semen when it is tested in a laboratory

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

Tests of the semen show no sperm at all. Either the testicles make little or no sperm, or sperm is made but a blockage stops it reaching the semen.

  • Testicles and sperm tubes
  • Found when trying for a baby
  • Usually no signs
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a urologist or fertility specialist
Common in
Men whose partner cannot get pregnant; found through a semen test
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a fertility specialist can explain which options may be possible

Common signs

More signs

  • Usually no signs; it is found when a semen test is done
  • Semen can look normal, because sperm are only a tiny part of it
  • When hormones are involved: low interest in sex, small or soft testicles, less body hair
  • Sometimes a lump, pain or swelling in the scrotum

Why it happens

  • A blockage in the tubes that carry sperm, from infection, injury, surgery or from birth
  • Hormone problems that stop the testicles making sperm
  • Testicles that make little or no sperm after mumps, an injury, cancer treatment or an undescended testicle
  • Inherited (genetic) conditions; sometimes no cause is found
  • A vasectomy, an operation that stops sperm reaching the semen, also gives no sperm

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle: go to hospital now
  • Feeling very low or hopeless about not having a baby, or thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • A lump, hard area or swelling in a testicle
  • Pain or swelling in the scrotum with fever
  • Loss of sex drive, erection trouble, breast swelling or loss of body hair (a hormone problem may be behind it)
  • Past cancer treatment, an undescended testicle, or a testicle injury or infection: see a doctor sooner

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your doctor to repeat the semen test, because one result alone may not be enough
  • Both partners can see a doctor together to talk about the cause and the options
  • Talk with your partner or someone you trust about how you feel; counselling can help

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
