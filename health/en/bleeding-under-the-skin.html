Bleeding under the skin

Blood that leaks from tiny blood vessels under the skin, showing as bruises or purple dots

SymptomSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Small blood vessels under the skin leak, so blue, purple or red patches or tiny dots appear. A bruise after a knock is common, but bruising with no clear reason needs checking.

  • Anywhere on the skin
  • Older age, some medicines
  • Blue, purple or red patches
Which doctor
Family doctor; a blood doctor (haematologist) if it keeps happening
Common in
Anyone; more often in older people and people on blood-thinning medicine
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as dengue (spread by mosquito bites)
Course
A bruise from a knock usually fades over a few weeks; other causes vary

Common signs

More signs

  • Tiny red or purple dots that look like pinpricks
  • A firm, tender lump under the skin after a knock
  • The colour changes over days: blue or purple, then green or yellow
  • Spots that stay the same colour when you press on them

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Blood-thinning medicine and some other medicines; ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • Infections, such as dengue fever
  • Blood or liver conditions, or a shortage of vitamin C or K

See a doctor first if

  • Purple or dark red spots that do not fade when pressed, with fever, stiff neck or feeling very ill: call 999
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or blood in vomit, stool or urine: go to hospital now
  • Fever with tiny red spots, strong belly pain, vomiting or bleeding gums: go to hospital today
  • A big, hard, very painful swelling, or a head knock with drowsiness, vomiting or confusion: go to hospital now
  • Easy bruising with no clear cause, or bruises with tiredness, a pale look or weight loss: see a doctor soon
  • You take blood-thinning medicine and bruise more than before: tell your doctor, and do not stop it yourself

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • For a fresh bruise, rest the area and hold a cool pack wrapped in cloth on it for short spells
  • Raise the limb if it is swollen
  • Do not rub, squeeze or cut a bruise or lump

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A kidney or liver condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
