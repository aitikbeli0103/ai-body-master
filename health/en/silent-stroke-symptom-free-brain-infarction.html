Silent stroke (symptom-free brain infarction)

A small area of brain damage from a blocked blood vessel that causes no clear signs at the time

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

A silent stroke is found on a brain scan, often by chance. You may not have noticed anything, but it shows the brain's blood vessels need care.

  • The brain
  • More common with age
  • Often found by chance
Which doctor
Brain and nerve doctor (neurologist), heart doctor, family doctor
Common in
Older adults; people with high blood pressure, diabetes or heart rhythm problems
Contagious
No
Course
The damage stays, but good care can lower the chance of more strokes

Common signs

More signs

  • Mood changes

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Diabetes and high cholesterol
  • Heart rhythm problems that can send small clots to the brain

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Sudden loss of vision, even for a short time: go to hospital now
  • Sudden dizziness with trouble walking, or a very bad headache
  • Memory or thinking that is getting worse
  • Symptoms that come and go: still go to hospital at once

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Silent stroke (symptom-free brain infarction)?

A small area of brain damage from a blocked blood vessel that causes no clear signs at the time. A silent stroke is found on a brain scan, often by chance. You may not have noticed anything, but it shows the brain's blood vessels need care.

What are the common signs of Silent stroke (symptom-free brain infarction)?

Often no signs at first · Forgetting things · Unsteady when walking

Why does Silent stroke (symptom-free brain infarction) happen?

High blood sugar or blood pressure · Smoking · Getting older

How can I look after myself with Silent stroke (symptom-free brain infarction)?

Take medicine as your doctor says · Keep your regular tests · Stop smoking
