Depression

A health condition where low mood and loss of interest last for weeks and affect daily life

ConditionTold by a doctor: moodHead

What it is

Depression is more than feeling sad for a day or two: low mood, loss of interest and low energy last for weeks and make daily life hard, and it is a health condition, not a weakness.

  • Mood and thinking
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Low mood, little interest
Which doctor
Family doctor; psychiatrist, counsellor or psychologist
Common in
Anyone at any age; can follow loss, long illness or big stress
Contagious
No
Course
Many people improve with the right support, but it can come back; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • There is usually no single cause; several things add up
  • Hard life events, such as losing a loved one or a job, money worries or problems at home
  • Body changes, such as after having a baby, or thyroid problems

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: tell someone you trust, then see a doctor now or call 999
  • Hearing or seeing things others do not, or strong beliefs that people want to harm you: see a doctor now
  • Not eating or drinking, or not able to look after yourself or your children for days: see a doctor soon
  • Low mood that lasts more than two weeks, keeps getting worse, or stops you working, studying or caring for family
  • Very high energy, hardly needing sleep, or risky behaviour: tell your doctor
  • Drinking more alcohol or using drugs to cope: see a doctor soon
  • A teenager, child or new mother who stays low, pulls away or talks about death: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Talk to someone you trust, such as family, a friend or a religious leader; you do not have to cope alone
  • Keep a regular routine: wake up, eat and sleep at similar times each day
  • Do some gentle activity you can manage, such as a short walk in daylight
  • Break jobs into small steps, be kind to yourself, and do not expect too much when you feel low
  • Ask your doctor about counselling and other support; if you take medicine, never stop it suddenly on your own

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • A heart condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
