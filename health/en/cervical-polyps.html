Cervical polyps

Small, soft, finger-like growths on the cervix, usually not cancer

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

A cervical polyp is a small, soft growth on the cervix or just inside it. Most are harmless and cause no problems, but any bleeding should be checked.

  • On the cervix
  • Mostly adult women
  • Soft growth, often painless
Which doctor
Women's doctor (gynaecologist); family doctor first
Common in
Adult women, especially those who have had children
Contagious
No
Course
Often harmless; a doctor can remove one if needed, and polyps can sometimes grow back

Common signs

More signs

  • Light bleeding after sex
  • A soft growth seen when a doctor looks at the cervix

Why it happens

Other causes

  • May be linked to long-lasting irritation or inflammation of the cervix
  • May be linked to blocked tiny blood vessels in the cervix

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy vaginal bleeding, or bleeding with dizziness or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding or bad lower belly pain in pregnancy: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding after sex or between periods, or any bleeding after menopause
  • Discharge that is bloody or bad-smelling, or fever with lower belly pain
  • Bleeding or discharge that comes back after a polyp was removed

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep up regular Pap smear or HPV screening as your doctor advises
  • Write down when bleeding or discharge happens and tell your doctor
  • Avoid douching and scented products in the vaginal area
  • If a polyp is removed, follow your doctor's advice on rest and sex afterwards

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
