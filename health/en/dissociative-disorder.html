Dissociative disorder

Feeling cut off from your thoughts, memories or surroundings, often after great stress

ConditionTold by a doctor: otherWhole body

What it is

The mind 'switches off' from thoughts, memories or the world around, often as a way to cope with great stress. A person may feel unreal or have gaps in memory.

  • Mind and sense of self
  • Often linked to past trauma
  • Feeling unreal or detached
Which doctor
Family doctor, psychiatrist or psychologist
Common in
People who have lived through trauma or long, heavy stress, often from childhood
Contagious
No
Course
Varies; it can come and go, and steady support from a doctor often helps

Common signs

More signs

  • Feeling cut off from your body or thoughts, as if watching yourself from outside
  • The world feels unreal, foggy or dreamlike
  • Feeling unsure who you are, or losing track of time
  • Often with low mood, worry or trouble sleeping

Why it happens

  • Often linked to great stress or trauma, especially in childhood
  • The mind may 'switch off' to cope with pain that is too hard to face
  • Accidents, disasters, war or other frightening events
  • Long-term stress, or growing up in a home that did not feel safe
  • Not everyone who goes through trauma gets it; the exact cause is not fully known

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Hurting yourself, or not being able to keep yourself safe
  • Sudden confusion or memory loss after a head injury, a fit or a new medicine: go to hospital now
  • Feeling detached or losing memory again and again, so daily life is hard
  • Using alcohol or drugs to cope with these feelings

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • When you feel detached, feel your feet on the floor and name what you see, hear or touch
  • Avoid alcohol and drugs, and tell a person you trust how you feel

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
