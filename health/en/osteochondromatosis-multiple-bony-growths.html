Osteochondromatosis (multiple bony growths)

An inherited condition with many bony growths on the bones

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

Several bony lumps, called osteochondromas, grow on the surface of bones, often near the joints. It usually runs in families and shows up in childhood.

  • Arms, legs, near joints
  • Often starts in childhood
  • Hard lumps on the bone
Which doctor
Bone doctor (orthopaedic), child doctor, genetic doctor
Common in
Children and young people; often runs in families
Contagious
No
Course
Growths often appear during childhood; a doctor follows them over time

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump that catches on muscle or clothes and becomes sore
  • One arm or leg that looks shorter or slightly bent
  • Shorter height than others in the family

Why it happens

  • Usually a change in a gene passed down in the family
  • Sometimes it appears for the first time in a person with no family history
  • It is not caused by food, injury or anything the parents did
  • Having a parent with the condition makes it more likely in a child

See a doctor first if

  • A lump that grows quickly, or that becomes painful without a knock
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or leg
  • A limb that turns pale, cold or blue, or a lump that presses on a blood vessel: go to hospital
  • A bone that seems to have broken after a small knock: go to hospital
  • A child with a new limp or bent limb: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
