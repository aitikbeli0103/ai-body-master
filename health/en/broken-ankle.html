Broken ankle

A crack or break in one or more of the bones that form the ankle joint

ConditionAnkle & footArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

A twist, fall or accident can crack or break the bones around the ankle, causing pain, swelling and bruising, and making it hard or impossible to put weight on the foot.

  • Ankle joint
  • After a twist, fall or accident
  • Pain, swelling, hard to walk
Which doctor
Hospital emergency doctor; bone doctor (orthopaedic doctor)
Common in
People after sports, falls or accidents; older adults with weak bones
Contagious
No
Course
Hospital care first; recovery takes weeks to months, and some need an operation

Common signs

More signs

  • The ankle may look bent or out of shape

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Twisting or rolling the ankle on uneven ground or on stairs
  • A direct blow to the ankle
  • Weak bones make a break more likely, even from a small fall

See a doctor first if

  • Bone through the skin, heavy bleeding, or a badly bent ankle: call 999
  • Sudden chest pain or trouble breathing (it can be a blood clot in the lung): call 999
  • Foot or toes numb, pale, blue or cold: go to hospital now
  • Cannot put weight on the foot, or severe pain and swelling after an injury: go to hospital now
  • Calf pain, swelling, warmth or redness, especially in a cast, may be a clot: see a doctor now
  • Fever, a bad smell, or red, hot or wet skin around a wound or cast: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay off the foot, keep the ankle still and do not try to straighten it
  • Raise the foot on pillows, and use a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for short times
  • Get an X-ray at the hospital; follow your doctor's advice on the cast, splint or boot, and keep it dry

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak bones
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
