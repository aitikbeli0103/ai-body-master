Dry, flaking skin

Dry skin that comes off in small flakes or scales

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

The top layer of dry skin lifts and falls off as fine flakes or scales. It is often harmless, but sometimes a skin condition is behind it.

  • Any part of the skin
  • More common with age
  • Flaky, rough, itchy skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more with age, dry air or air-conditioning
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as a fungal skin infection
Course
Often eases with gentle skin care; if it keeps coming back, get it checked

Common signs

More signs

  • Rough, tight or dull skin
  • Fine cracks that may sting
  • Flaking that gets worse in dry or air-conditioned rooms

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Harsh soaps, strong cleansers, or scrubbing too hard
  • Skin conditions such as eczema, psoriasis or a fungal infection
  • Other conditions such as diabetes, thyroid or kidney problems, or some medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Skin suddenly turns red and peels or blisters over large areas, with fever: go to hospital now
  • Flaking with pus, yellow crusts, warmth, spreading redness or fever
  • Round or ring-shaped scaly patches that spread, or thick, red, silvery scaly patches
  • Flaking skin together with yellow skin or eyes, weight loss or feeling very tired
  • No improvement after a few weeks of gentle care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Put on a thick, fragrance-free cream or ointment right after bathing
  • Use lukewarm water, short showers and a mild cleanser

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A severe allergy
