Fungal infection of the hand (tinea manuum)

A fungal skin infection of the palms and fingers that causes dry, scaly, sometimes itchy skin

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A fungus, often the same kind that causes athlete's foot, grows on the skin of the palm and fingers and can make it dry, thick and scaly, often on one hand only.

  • Palms and fingers
  • Often with athlete's foot
  • Dry, scaly, sometimes itchy
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Adults, especially those with athlete's foot or sweaty hands
Contagious
Yes, it can spread by touch, shared towels or from your own feet
Course
Can last for months and may come back; a doctor can advise on what helps

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A type of fungus that grows on the top layer of the skin, hair and nails
  • It often passes to the hands from athlete's foot or a nail infection on your own body
  • Touching someone's infected skin, shared towels, damp floors, or sometimes pets

See a doctor first if

  • Hot, swollen, very painful skin, pus, or red streaks up the arm, with or without fever: see a doctor today
  • If you have diabetes or a weak immune system and the rash is spreading, see a doctor early
  • A rash you are not sure about: eczema and psoriasis can look the same, so get it checked
  • It has not improved after a couple of weeks of careful hand care, or it keeps coming back
  • Your nails or feet are also affected: see a doctor so everything is looked after together

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep hands clean and dry, and dry well between the fingers
  • For wet work, wear gloves with a cotton lining and let your hands dry in between
