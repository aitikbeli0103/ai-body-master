Secondary liver tumour (metastases)

Cancer that started elsewhere in the body and has spread to the liver

ConditionCancer & tumoursChest

What it is

Cancer cells from another part of the body, such as the bowel, breast or lung, can travel in the blood and settle in the liver.

  • Liver, upper right belly
  • After another cancer
  • Tired, poor appetite
Which doctor
Cancer doctor (oncologist), liver specialist
Common in
People who have had cancer elsewhere in the body
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the first cancer and how far it has spread; the care team can explain

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Cancer cells from another organ travel to the liver in the blood
  • It is more often seen with cancers of the bowel, breast, lung or stomach
  • The liver filters a lot of blood, so it is a common place for spread
  • It is not caused by anything the person did

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, or black or bloody stools: go to hospital now
  • Confusion, very sleepy, or hard to wake: call 999
  • Yellow skin or eyes, especially with fever or shaking chills
  • Sudden strong pain in the upper belly, or a belly that swells fast
  • Weight loss, no appetite or itchy skin that does not go away

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Never stop or change treatment on your own
  • Eat small, light meals and avoid alcohol unless the team says it is fine
  • Ask the team about pain, food and emotional support

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • A kidney or liver condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
