Low blood protein (hypoproteinaemia)

Too little protein in the blood, which can lead to swelling and tiredness

ConditionBloodBlood

What it is

Proteins in the blood, such as albumin, help keep fluid inside the blood vessels. When they are low, fluid can leak out and cause swelling in the legs, face or belly.

  • Protein level in the blood
  • Often with another illness
  • Swelling and tiredness
Which doctor
Family doctor; kidney or liver doctor if needed
Common in
People with kidney, liver or gut illness, poor eating, or long illness
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; it often improves when the cause is looked after

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Kidney illness that lets protein leak into the urine
  • Liver illness, because the liver makes most blood proteins
  • Not eating enough protein, or poor absorption from the gut
  • Long illness, burns or serious inflammation

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or a swollen belly that makes breathing hard: go to hospital now
  • Chest pain or fainting: call 999
  • One leg that is suddenly swollen, red or painful
  • Very little urine, or foamy or dark urine
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or vomiting blood

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Low blood protein (hypoproteinaemia)?

Too little protein in the blood, which can lead to swelling and tiredness. Proteins in the blood, such as albumin, help keep fluid inside the blood vessels. When they are low, fluid can leak out and cause swelling in the legs, face or belly.

What are the common signs of Low blood protein (hypoproteinaemia)?

Swollen legs and ankles · Puffy face in the morning · Tired all the time · Hair coming out · Brittle, ridged nails · Cuts heal slowly

Why does Low blood protein (hypoproteinaemia) happen?

Kidney illness that lets protein leak into the urine · Liver illness, because the liver makes most blood proteins · Not eating enough protein, or poor absorption from the gut · Long illness, burns or serious inflammation

How can I look after myself with Low blood protein (hypoproteinaemia)?

See your doctor for check-ups · Eat healthy, balanced meals · Rest with your legs raised
