Haemangioma (blood vessel growth)

A non-cancerous growth made of extra tiny blood vessels, often seen as a red or bluish mark

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsBlood

What it is

A haemangioma is a growth made of extra tiny blood vessels. It often looks like a soft red or bluish bump or patch on the skin and is usually not cancer. It is most often seen in babies.

  • Blood vessels, often the skin
  • Most often seen in babies
  • Soft red or bluish mark
Which doctor
Family doctor, children's doctor (paediatrician), skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Babies, more often girls and those born early; small red dots can appear in adults
Contagious
No
Course
In babies it often grows for some months, then slowly shrinks over years

Common signs

More signs

  • A soft red, pink or bluish bump or patch on the skin
  • It may look like a strawberry mark, or sit deeper like a bluish swelling under the skin
  • Usually painless and soft to touch
  • In a baby it often appears in the first weeks of life and grows for a while

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Extra tiny blood vessels grow and clump together in one spot
  • In babies it is more common in girls, babies born early and those with a low birth weight
  • Small red dots in adults (cherry angiomas) become more common with age

See a doctor first if

  • A baby with a mark on the face or neck and noisy or hard breathing, or trouble feeding: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding that does not stop with gentle firm pressure: go to hospital now
  • A baby's mark near the eye, nose, mouth or ear, or one that grows fast: see a children's doctor promptly
  • An open sore, pus, a bad smell, or fever with the mark: it may be infected
  • A baby with several marks on the skin, or a mark over the lower back: ask a doctor to check
  • An adult's red or dark spot that grows, changes colour, itches or bleeds: see a doctor to have it checked
