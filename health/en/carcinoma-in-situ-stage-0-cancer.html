Carcinoma in situ (stage 0 cancer)

Cancer-like cells that are still in the layer where they started and have not spread deeper

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

Carcinoma in situ is sometimes called stage zero cancer. Abnormal cells are found only in the layer where they began and have not grown deeper. It can happen in many parts of the body.

  • Can start in many places
  • Often found on a check-up
  • Often no signs at all
Which doctor
The specialist for the body part involved, or your family doctor
Common in
Any age; often found during routine screening tests
Contagious
No
Course
It may spread deeper over time, so your doctor will advise the next steps

Common signs

More signs

  • Depending on the place, a patch, spot or lump that looks or feels different
  • Depending on the place, mild bleeding, discharge or discomfort

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The causes depend on where it is, and often the cause is not known
  • Long-lasting infection with some viruses, such as HPV for the cervix
  • Too much strong sun on the skin

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, trouble breathing, heavy bleeding or fainting: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • A new lump, or a sore or spot that does not go away or keeps changing
  • New bleeding from the vagina or bowel, or blood in your urine or phlegm
  • Weight loss, tiredness or a cough that lasts for weeks with no clear reason
  • A follow-up check you were told about has been missed: please book it soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your doctor to explain what was found and what the next steps are
  • Go for regular screening, such as a smear test or mammogram, as your doctor advises

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
