Cancer around the anus (perianal cancer)

Cancer that starts in the skin around the anus, or in the short canal just inside it

ConditionCancer & tumoursPelvis

What it is

This cancer starts when cells in the skin around the anus, or just inside it, grow out of control. Its signs are often mistaken for piles, so any bleeding or lump should be checked.

  • Around the anus
  • Adults, more often with age
  • Bleeding, itching or a lump
Which doctor
Family doctor first; bowel surgeon (colorectal); cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Adults, more often with age; long-term HPV infection, smoking or a weak immune system
Contagious
No, but the HPV virus linked to it can pass on by close skin contact, including sex
Course
Depends on the size and how far it has spread; the cancer team explains the plan

Common signs

More signs

  • Bleeding from the back passage, often mistaken for piles
  • Mucus or other fluid leaking from the anus, or a change in bowel habit
  • Swollen lumps (glands) in the groin

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term infection with HPV, a common virus spread by close skin contact, including sex
  • Past anal warts, or an earlier cancer linked to HPV, such as cervical cancer

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding from the back passage, or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • Cannot pass stool or wind, with a swollen belly or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Fever with severe pain and a hot, red, swollen area near the anus: see a doctor the same day
  • Any bleeding from the back passage, even a little; do not assume it is piles
  • A lump, sore or growth near the anus that stays or keeps growing
  • Itching, pain or discharge that lasts for weeks, or swollen lumps in the groin

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your team about pain, skin care around the anus, food and emotional support
  • Keep the area clean and dry: wash gently with water, pat dry, and try not to scratch
  • There is no shame in asking for help; talk to your care team or someone you trust

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
