Scalp injury

A cut, scrape, bruise or lump on the skin that covers the head

ConditionHead & neckHeadSkin

What it is

The scalp has many blood vessels, so even a small cut can bleed a lot. Most scalp injuries are minor, but a hard knock needs a check for head injury.

  • Scalp
  • Common in falls and play
  • Cut, bruise, bleeding
Which doctor
Family doctor or clinic; emergency doctor if deep
Common in
Anyone; children often get head bumps
Contagious
No
Course
Minor ones usually get better in days to weeks; deep cuts may need stitches

Common signs

  • A cut or scrape on the scalp
  • Bleeding, which can look heavy
  • A soft, painful lump (a 'goose egg')
  • Bruising that can spread to the forehead or around the eyes
  • Tender skin and a sore head

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Road or work accidents
  • Being hit by an object

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, a fit, confusion or repeated vomiting after the knock: call 999
  • Bleeding that does not stop after ten minutes of firm pressure
  • A dent in the skull, or a wound that gapes open
  • A wound that becomes red, hot, swollen or leaks pus
  • Any head knock in a baby or young child: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Press firmly on the cut with a clean cloth for several minutes
  • Watch for sleepiness or vomiting over the next day
  • Ask your doctor about a tetanus shot if the wound was dirty

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Recent neck injury or surgery

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
