Fasciitis (inflamed fascia)

Irritation or swelling of the thin tissue (fascia) that wraps muscles, causing ache and tightness

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

Fascia is the thin, tough layer around muscles. When it is strained or irritated, the area can feel sore, tight and stiff, especially after rest or heavy use.

  • Back, feet or other muscles
  • Active or desk-bound adults
  • Sore, tight, stiff
Which doctor
Family doctor, physiotherapist, sports or orthopaedic doctor
Common in
Adults who exercise a lot, stand long, or repeat the same movement
Contagious
No
Course
Often eases over weeks with rest and gentle care, but can return if the strain continues

Common signs

  • A sore, tight or burning ache in a muscle area
  • Stiffness that is worse after rest or first thing in the morning
  • Pain that increases with use or stretching
  • A tender spot or tight band you can feel with your fingers

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Overuse or doing the same movement again and again
  • Standing, sitting or lifting in a poor position for long
  • Tight muscles, little stretching or sudden hard exercise
  • Sometimes an illness that causes body-wide inflammation

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A red, hot, badly swollen area, especially with fever
  • Sudden severe pain, or you cannot move or use the limb
  • New numbness, weakness or a swollen, painful calf
  • Pain that keeps getting worse or lasts many weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest the sore area and cut back on the activity that brings it on
  • Do slow, gentle stretches that do not cause sharp pain
  • Warm up before exercise and build up slowly

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
