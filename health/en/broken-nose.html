Broken nose

A crack or break in the bone of the nose, often after a knock to the face

ConditionHead & neckHead

What it is

The bone or cartilage of the nose is cracked or broken, usually by a blow to the face. The nose may be sore, swollen and out of shape.

  • Nose
  • After a knock or fall
  • Pain, swelling, bleeding
Which doctor
Family doctor or emergency doctor; ear, nose and throat doctor
Common in
Anyone; often after sports, falls, fights or accidents
Contagious
No
Course
Swelling often settles over days to weeks; a doctor will check if the nose needs setting

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain and tenderness over the nose
  • Swelling and bruising around the nose and eyes
  • A nose that looks crooked or flattened

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Being hit by a ball or a hard object
  • Thin or weak bones can make it easier to break

See a doctor first if

  • A nosebleed that will not stop after fifteen minutes of pinching: go to hospital
  • Clear watery fluid dripping from the nose after a head injury
  • Fainting, confusion, repeated vomiting or a bad headache after the blow: call 999
  • Double vision or trouble moving the eyes
  • A dark, swollen lump inside the nose, or a very crooked nose

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Sit up and lean forward, then pinch the soft part of the nose to stop bleeding
  • Do not push or straighten the nose yourself
  • Avoid blowing your nose hard and avoid contact sports while it is sore

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Weak bones
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
