Red urine

Urine that looks pink, red or brownish

SymptomKidneys & urinaryAbdomen

What it is

Red urine may mean blood in the urine, but some foods and medicines can also change its colour. It is best to have it checked.

  • Kidneys, bladder, urine tubes
  • Any age
  • Pink, red or tea-coloured urine
Which doctor
Family doctor; urologist or kidney doctor
Common in
Anyone; more often with infections or stones
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor can find out what is behind it

Common signs

More signs

  • Sometimes small clots in the urine

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Urine infection of the bladder or kidney
  • Stones in the kidney or urine tubes
  • Kidney or prostate problems, or hard exercise
  • Sometimes a growth in the urine tubes

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot pass urine, or passing large clots: go to hospital now
  • Feeling faint, pale or very weak with red urine
  • Fever with chills, or severe pain in the side or back
  • Red urine that is not explained by food or medicine
  • Red urine while taking blood-thinning medicine, or in a child

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not ignore it, even if it stops: have it checked
  • Note what you ate and any medicine you took
  • Do not stop or change your medicine without asking your doctor
