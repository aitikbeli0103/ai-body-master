Dry gangrene

Dry, dark, shrivelled skin and tissue that has died because blood no longer reaches it

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

When blood cannot reach part of the body, often a toe, foot or finger, the tissue dries, turns dark and dies. It needs a doctor, because infection can follow.

  • Toes, feet or fingers
  • Diabetes or poor circulation
  • Dry, dark, cold skin
Which doctor
Family doctor first; vascular or foot surgeon
Common in
People with diabetes, narrowed leg arteries or who smoke
Contagious
No
Course
Dead tissue does not recover; early care by a doctor is important

Common signs

  • Skin that turns pale, then blue, brown or black
  • Dry, hard, shrivelled skin that looks like leather
  • The area feels cold and numb, or has long-lasting pain
  • A clear line between healthy skin and dark skin

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Narrowed or blocked arteries that cut off blood flow
  • Diabetes, which harms blood vessels and nerves

See a doctor first if

  • Any skin turning dark, black or blue: see a doctor today
  • Fever, chills, feeling very unwell or confused: go to hospital now
  • Redness, swelling, pus or a bad smell around the area: infection may be starting
  • Sudden severe pain, or a foot or hand that turns pale and cold
  • A foot sore or wound that does not heal, especially with diabetes

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • People with diabetes should check both feet every day
  • Keep feet clean and dry, and wear shoes that fit; do not walk barefoot
  • Do not cut corns or hard skin yourself

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • A heart condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Dry gangrene?

Dry, dark, shrivelled skin and tissue that has died because blood no longer reaches it. When blood cannot reach part of the body, often a toe, foot or finger, the tissue dries, turns dark and dies. It needs a doctor, because infection can follow.

What are the common signs of Dry gangrene?

Skin that turns pale, then blue, brown or black · Dry, hard, shrivelled skin that looks like leather · The area feels cold and numb, or has long-lasting pain · A clear line between healthy skin and dark skin

Why does Dry gangrene happen?

Smoking · An injury or fall · High blood sugar or blood pressure

How can I look after myself with Dry gangrene?

See your doctor for check-ups · Stop smoking
