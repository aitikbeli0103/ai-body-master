Dermoid cyst

A slow-growing, non-cancerous lump under the skin that forms before birth

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A dermoid cyst is a closed sac under the skin, present from birth. It holds skin material such as skin cells, oil and sometimes hair, and it usually grows slowly.

  • Often face, scalp or neck
  • Present from birth
  • A smooth, round lump
Which doctor
Family doctor first, then a skin doctor (dermatologist) or surgeon
Common in
Present from birth; often noticed in babies and children, sometimes only in adults
Contagious
No
Course
Grows slowly and does not go away by itself; a doctor can say if it needs removing

Common signs

More signs

  • Common places are near the eyebrow or eye, the nose bridge, the scalp and the neck
  • Usually painless, and the skin over it looks normal
  • It grows slowly over months or years and may look bigger as a child grows
  • Sometimes a tiny pit in the skin over it, with a hair growing out

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The trapped cells keep making skin cells and oil, so the lump slowly gets bigger
  • Many sit along lines where parts of the face or skull joined while the baby was growing
  • Less often, similar cysts form inside the ovary or near the spine

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe lower belly pain with vomiting or fainting (a cyst in the ovary can twist): go to hospital now
  • A red, swollen, painful lump near the eye with fever, or trouble moving the eye or seeing: go to hospital now
  • A lump that turns hot, red, painful or starts to leak: see a doctor the same day
  • A baby or child with a lump, pit or hair tuft on the nose, scalp or lower back: see a doctor promptly
  • A lump that grows fast, becomes hard or changes shape

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep the skin clean and dry, and avoid tight hats, glasses or clothes rubbing on it
  • Ask a doctor or surgeon whether it needs removing and what to expect
  • Watch it and tell your doctor if it changes in size, colour or comfort

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
