Leukaemia

Cancer of the blood and bone marrow, where abnormal white blood cells crowd out healthy blood cells

ConditionCancer & tumoursBlood

What it is

The bone marrow, where blood is made, makes too many abnormal white blood cells that crowd out healthy blood cells, so a person may feel tired, get infections or bruise easily.

  • Blood and bone marrow
  • Children and adults
  • Tired, pale, bruises easily
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a blood specialist (haematologist)
Common in
Children and adults; the type often depends on age
Contagious
No
Course
Some types grow fast and others slowly; your blood specialist explains the plan

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Earlier chemotherapy or radiation treatment for another cancer
  • Long contact with some industrial chemicals, and smoking
  • Very high radiation exposure, which is rare

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, chest pain, a severe headache, confusion or a fit: call 999
  • Bleeding that does not stop when you press on it, black stools or vomiting blood: go to hospital now
  • Fever or shaking chills with leukaemia or during cancer care: go to hospital now
  • Tiny red or purple skin spots, easy bruising, or nosebleeds or bleeding gums that keep coming: see a doctor promptly
  • Looking very pale, feeling very tired or dizzy, a fever that lasts, or bone pain that will not go away
  • A child who is pale, bruises easily, has a fever or is not playing as usual: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow the plan from your blood specialist and keep all appointments and blood tests
  • Ask your team about food, pain and emotional support
  • Never stop treatment on your own; talk to your team first

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Weak bones

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
