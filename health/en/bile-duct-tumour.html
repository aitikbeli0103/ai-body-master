Bile duct tumour

A growth in the tubes that carry bile from the liver; it may be non-cancerous or cancer, and only tests can tell

ConditionCancer & tumoursAbdomen

What it is

A bile duct tumour is an abnormal growth in the tubes that carry bile from the liver to the gut; it can be non-cancerous or cancer, and it may block the flow of bile.

  • Liver and bile ducts
  • Mostly adults
  • Belly pain or yellow skin
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a liver, digestive or cancer specialist at a hospital
Common in
Adults; people with long-term liver or bile duct problems
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type and where it is; tests and a specialist team guide the plan

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term inflammation of the bile ducts or liver
  • May be linked to bile duct stones or bile duct cysts present from birth
  • Liver fluke infection from raw or undercooked freshwater fish, in some parts of Asia

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with chills, yellow skin or eyes and severe upper belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Vomiting blood, black stools, fainting or confusion: call 999
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools: see a doctor without delay
  • Itchy skin all over with no rash that does not settle
  • Upper belly pain that keeps coming back or gets worse
  • Weight loss you cannot explain, or a swollen belly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow your doctor's plan for scans, tests and any operation; if it is cancer, follow your cancer care team
  • Never stop or change medicines on your own; ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • If your skin itches, keep your nails short and ask your doctor for help

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
