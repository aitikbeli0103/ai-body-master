Stable angina (heart-related chest pain)

Chest discomfort when the heart muscle gets too little blood during effort

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

When the heart works harder, such as on stairs or in stress, it may not get enough blood, causing a tight or heavy feeling in the chest that usually eases with rest.

  • Middle of the chest
  • Often in middle age and older
  • Tight, heavy feeling
Which doctor
Family doctor or heart doctor (cardiologist)
Common in
Older adults; people with high blood pressure, diabetes or who smoke
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; often set off by effort and eases with rest; follow your doctor's plan

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Narrowed blood vessels that feed the heart muscle

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain that does not ease after a few minutes of rest: call 999
  • Chest pain with sweating, feeling faint, vomiting or pain spreading to the arm, jaw or back: call 999
  • Pain that comes more often, more easily or at rest
  • New breathlessness or fainting

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep seeing your doctor and ask about the plan made for you
  • Stop and rest when you feel the tightness
  • Keep a gentle, regular walk if your doctor says it is fine

Questions and answers

What is Stable angina (heart-related chest pain)?

Chest discomfort when the heart muscle gets too little blood during effort. When the heart works harder, such as on stairs or in stress, it may not get enough blood, causing a tight or heavy feeling in the chest that usually eases with rest.

What are the common signs of Stable angina (heart-related chest pain)?

Tight, heavy chest · Short of breath on stairs · Sweating a lot · Feeling sick, nausea · Tired all the time

Why does Stable angina (heart-related chest pain) happen?

High blood sugar or blood pressure · Smoking · Runs in the family

How can I look after myself with Stable angina (heart-related chest pain)?

Stop smoking
