Red rash all over the body

A red rash over large areas of the body, which may itch, with many possible causes

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A red rash over much of the body shows the skin or body is reacting to something. Common reasons include viral infections, allergies, medicine reactions, heat and eczema.

  • Large areas of the skin
  • Any age, many causes
  • Red, may itch or feel warm
Which doctor
Family doctor; a children's doctor for a child; skin doctor if it lasts
Common in
Anyone; children often get rashes with viral infections
Contagious
Some types can be (measles, chickenpox); allergy, eczema and heat rash are not
Course
Depends on the cause; many mild rashes fade over days to a couple of weeks

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Viral infections, such as measles, chickenpox, dengue and many common childhood viruses
  • Allergic reactions to food, insect bites or things that touch the skin
  • A reaction to a new medicine, which can appear days to weeks after starting it
  • Heat rash (prickly heat) from sweating in hot, humid weather
  • Skin conditions such as eczema or psoriasis, or less often an immune or bacterial illness

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, face, tongue or throat, trouble breathing, or fainting with a rash: call 999
  • Blisters, peeling skin, or sores in the mouth, eyes or private parts: go to hospital now
  • Rash with high fever, stiff neck, bad headache, great drowsiness, or spots that stay purple when pressed: call 999
  • Rash and fever with bleeding, bad belly pain or repeated vomiting (may be dengue): go to hospital now
  • A rash that began days or weeks after starting a new medicine: ask your doctor or pharmacist today what to do
  • Rash with fever in a baby, a pregnant woman or an older person: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay cool, take lukewarm showers and pat the skin dry
  • Avoid new soaps, perfumes and strong skin products while the skin is red
  • If it could be an infection, stay home and away from babies and pregnant women until a doctor has checked

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
