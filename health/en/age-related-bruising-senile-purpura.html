Age-related bruising (senile purpura)

Dark purple bruises on the arms and hands of older people, from thin, fragile skin

ConditionBloodSkin

What it is

With age the skin gets thinner and the small blood vessels beneath it become fragile. Even a light knock can leave a flat purple bruise that takes weeks to fade.

  • Back of hands, forearms
  • Older adults
  • Flat purple bruises
Which doctor
Family doctor
Common in
Older adults, especially after long sun exposure
Contagious
No
Course
Each bruise usually fades in a few weeks, but new ones may keep appearing

Common signs

More signs

  • Flat, dark purple or red-purple patches on the hands and forearms
  • Thin, paper-like skin
  • Usually painless, and the bruise slowly turns brown or yellow as it fades

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Thinner skin and weaker small blood vessels with age

See a doctor first if

  • Bruises with bleeding from the gums or nose that will not stop
  • Heavy bleeding, black stools or blood in the urine: go to hospital now
  • A bruise after a fall with a hard hit to the head, or a swollen painful limb
  • Many new bruises with fever, tiredness or weight loss
  • A lot of bruising that you cannot explain, or bruises on the trunk or in a child

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
