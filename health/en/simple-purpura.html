Simple purpura

Small purple or red spots or patches on the skin from bleeding under the skin

ConditionBloodArms & legsSkin

What it is

Tiny blood vessels under the skin leak a little blood, leaving purple or red marks that do not fade when pressed. It is often mild, but a doctor should check the cause.

  • Often on arms and legs
  • More in women, older adults
  • Marks, usually painless
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor, blood doctor if needed
Common in
Women and older adults; people who bruise easily
Contagious
No
Course
Marks usually fade over days to weeks, but may come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Flat purple, red or brown spots or patches on the skin
  • Marks that do not fade when you press on them
  • Often on the arms and legs, usually without pain

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Often the exact cause is not known, and other causes need to be ruled out

See a doctor first if

  • Spots with fever, a stiff neck or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding from the nose or gums, blood in urine or stools
  • Spots that spread fast or appear all over the body
  • Belly pain, joint pain or swelling together with the spots
  • A baby or child with spots that look unwell: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not stop or change any medicine on your own; ask your doctor
