Bladder tumour

A growth of cells in the lining of the bladder; some are cancer, some are not

ConditionCancer & tumoursPelvis

What it is

A bladder tumour is an abnormal growth in the bladder, the organ that stores urine. It may be cancer or not, and only tests can tell, so it needs a doctor's check.

  • In the bladder
  • More common in older adults
  • Blood in the urine
Which doctor
Family doctor first; urologist (urinary specialist); cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Older adults, more often men; people who smoke or work with certain chemicals
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type and size; tests guide the plan, and regular check-ups are common

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain in the lower belly or lower back that does not go away

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term bladder irritation, such as a long-term catheter or repeated infections
  • Past cancer treatment in the pelvis; often the cause is not known

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot pass urine, passing large blood clots, or feeling faint after bleeding: go to hospital now
  • Fever with shivering, or strong pain in your side or back: go to hospital the same day
  • Blood in the urine, even once or if it stops by itself
  • Needing to pass urine often, or burning, that lasts for weeks or keeps returning
  • Losing weight without trying, bone pain, or feeling very tired for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow your doctors' plan and keep every check-up; never stop treatment on your own
  • Ask your care team about pain, food and emotional support
  • If you work with chemicals, follow safety rules and wear the protection provided

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A kidney or liver condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
