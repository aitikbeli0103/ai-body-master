Neck and shoulder syndrome

Tight, aching neck and shoulders, often from desk work or phone use

ConditionBones, joints & spineArms & legs

What it is

Long-lasting tightness and aching in the muscles of the neck, shoulders and upper back, often with a stiff neck and tension headaches.

  • Neck and shoulders
  • Common with desk work
  • Tight and aching
Which doctor
Family doctor, orthopaedic doctor, physiotherapist
Common in
Office workers, drivers and heavy phone users
Contagious
No
Course
Comes and goes, flares with stress and long sitting
Medical details

What happens in the neck and shoulders

The muscles that join the neck, shoulders and upper back (such as the upper trapezius, the levator scapulae and the muscles between the shoulder blades) hold up the head, which weighs about 5 kg. When the head leans forward over a screen or a phone, these muscles have to work much harder, so they tighten and tire. Tight bands and tender knots (myofascial trigger points) form and can send an ache into the head, the shoulder blade or the arm. Stress adds to the tension. The nerves and discs are usually not the main problem, but wear and tear of the neck can look similar.

Common patterns

  • Muscle tension: tight, tender muscles; the ache is worse at the end of the working day
  • Tension headache: a band-like pain at the back of the head or around the temples
  • With nerve irritation: tingling into the arm or hand needs a doctor's check

Outlook

Neck and shoulder tension usually improves with better posture, movement breaks, regular exercise and stress care. It comes back easily if the habits stay the same. Treatment decisions are made by your doctor.

Common signs

Medical details

Symptoms in more detail

  • Neck and shoulder ache: a tight, heavy ache across the neck and the tops of the shoulders, worse after long periods at a screen or phone
  • Stiffness: turning the head or reaching is limited by tightness
  • Tension headache: a dull band of pain at the back of the head or the temples, sometimes with tired eyes
  • Between the shoulder blades: an ache or a burning feeling in the upper back
  • Not typical: numbness, tingling or weakness in the arm or hand, or trouble with balance, need a doctor's check

Why it happens

Medical details

Causes and risk factors

  • Head forward over a screen or phone: the further the head leans forward, the more the neck and shoulder muscles have to work
  • Long sitting and repetitive work: hours in one position, a screen that is too low or too high, and shoulders held up while typing
  • Heavy bag on one shoulder: the load pulls the muscles on that side
  • Little exercise: weak supporting muscles tire early
  • Stress and poor sleep: they keep the shoulders tense and slow recovery
  • Earlier neck injury: an old whiplash or strain can leave the neck more sensitive

See a doctor first if

  • Neck pain after a fall or accident
  • Pain with a fever or a very stiff neck and severe headache
  • New weakness or numbness in arms or legs
  • Trouble with balance or walking
  • Pain with chest tightness, sweating or breathlessness: call 999
  • Pain not easing after a few weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Medical details

How doctors check it

Changes on scans are common in people without symptoms, so results are always read together with your symptoms and examination.

  • History and examination: your work, posture and habits, then neck and shoulder movement, tender muscles, arm strength, reflexes and feeling
  • X-ray or MRI: not needed for most people; used when there are warning signs, nerve symptoms or pain that does not improve

Problems that can feel similar

Wear and tear of the neck (cervical spondylosis), a frozen shoulder, a rotator cuff problem, carpal tunnel syndrome and, rarely, heart problems can feel like neck and shoulder tension, so a doctor checks to tell them apart.

Pain with chest tightness, sweating or breathlessness can be a heart problem: call emergency services (999).

Looking after yourself

Medical details

Daily care in more detail

  • Workstation: top of the screen at eye level, about an arm's length away; keyboard close; elbows at about 90 degrees; shoulders relaxed, not shrugged
  • Movement breaks: stand up and move every 30 to 45 minutes; roll the shoulders and gently turn the head from side to side
  • Exercise: regular walking, swimming and strengthening of the shoulder blade and upper back muscles
  • Sleep: a pillow that keeps the neck in a neutral line; avoid sleeping on your stomach
  • Carrying: use a bag on both shoulders or a trolley, and carry less
  • Warmth and relaxation: warmth can ease tight muscles; slow breathing and short pauses help the shoulders let go

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Dizzy when turning the head

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
