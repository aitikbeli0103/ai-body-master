Skin ulcers (open sores)

An open sore where the top layers of skin have broken down and are slow to close

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A skin ulcer is an open sore that does not get better like a normal cut. It can be shallow or deep, and often has red edges and some fluid.

  • Often legs and feet
  • Older age, diabetes, bed rest
  • Open, sore, slow to improve
Which doctor
Family doctor; wound nurse or specialist depending on the cause
Common in
Older adults; people with diabetes, poor circulation or limited movement
Contagious
Some types can be, if infected; wash your hands after touching a sore
Course
Depends on the cause; many need weeks or months of care, and a doctor should look

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Poor blood flow in the leg veins or arteries, a common cause on the legs
  • Diabetes, which can damage nerves and blood flow, especially in the feet
  • Pressure on one spot from lying or sitting too long (bedsores)
  • An infection, an injury that is slow to get better, or a skin condition
  • Less often, a skin cancer or another illness; a doctor can look for the cause

See a doctor first if

  • Fever, shaking chills, fast heartbeat or confusion with a sore: go to hospital now
  • A cold, pale or blue foot or leg with severe pain, or black dead-looking skin: go to hospital now
  • One leg that suddenly becomes swollen, hot, red and painful: see a doctor now
  • Spreading redness, heat, pus, a bad smell or more pain around the sore
  • Any foot sore if you have diabetes, even if it does not hurt: see a doctor promptly
  • A sore that is not getting better, keeps growing, or keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not put toothpaste, herbs, powder, oil or leftover medicine on an open sore
  • Change position often and keep pressure off the sore; if you have diabetes, check your feet daily

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
