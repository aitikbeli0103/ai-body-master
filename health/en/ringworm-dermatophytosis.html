Ringworm (dermatophytosis)

A common fungal skin infection (not a worm) that causes itchy, scaly, often ring-shaped patches

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Ringworm is caused by a fungus that lives on skin, hair and nails; it often makes a red, itchy, scaly ring with a clearer centre, and it can spread to other people.

  • Skin, feet, nails, scalp
  • Often in hot, humid weather
  • Itchy, scaly, ring-shaped patch
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more often in children, people who sweat a lot, and in hot, humid weather
Contagious
Yes, by touching infected skin or pets, or sharing towels and clothes
Course
Skin patches often settle in a few weeks with proper care; scalp and nails take longer

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A fungus (not a worm) that lives on skin, hair and nails
  • Walking barefoot in damp places such as pool sides, gyms and changing rooms
  • Sweating a lot, tight clothes, diabetes or a weak immune system make it more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Fever or feeling very unwell with a red, hot, swollen, spreading rash: go to hospital now
  • A baby or young child with a rash on the scalp or body: see a doctor promptly
  • Patchy hair loss, a scaly scalp, or a swollen, tender, oozing spot on the scalp: see a doctor promptly
  • Thick, yellow or crumbling nails: see a doctor, as nail infections take longer to get better
  • A rash that is not better after a couple of weeks, keeps spreading or keeps coming back: see a doctor
  • Diabetes or a weak immune system together with a rash like this: see a doctor early

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Ringworm (dermatophytosis)?

A common fungal skin infection (not a worm) that causes itchy, scaly, often ring-shaped patches. Ringworm is caused by a fungus that lives on skin, hair and nails; it often makes a red, itchy, scaly ring with a clearer centre, and it can spread to other people.

What are the common signs of Ringworm (dermatophytosis)?

Red rash · Itchy skin · Dry, flaky skin · Hair coming out

Why does Ringworm (dermatophytosis) happen?

Germs (infection) · Close contact or sharing items

How can I look after myself with Ringworm (dermatophytosis)?

Do not share towels, razors or cups · Wear loose cotton clothes · Wash your hands often · Ask your pharmacist
