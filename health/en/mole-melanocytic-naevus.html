Mole (melanocytic naevus)

A common, usually harmless spot made of a small cluster of pigment cells in the skin

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A mole is a small flat or raised spot of darker skin where pigment cells have grown in a cluster. Most are harmless, but a mole that changes should be checked.

  • Anywhere on the skin
  • Most appear by early adulthood
  • Brown, tan or pink spot
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if a mole changes
Common in
Most people have some; more in fair skin and with a lot of sun
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stays much the same for years; any change is worth checking

Common signs

  • A small round or oval spot, flat or slightly raised
  • Colour from tan to dark brown, sometimes pink or skin-coloured
  • Even colour, a smooth edge and a regular shape
  • Usually painless and not itchy
  • May have a few hairs growing from it

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Pigment cells in the skin grow in a small cluster; the exact reason is not fully known
  • People with fair skin tend to have more moles

See a doctor first if

  • A mole that changes in size, shape or colour, or has several colours: see a doctor soon
  • A mole with a blurry or uneven edge, or two halves that do not match
  • A mole that bleeds, oozes, forms a crust or sore, or becomes itchy or painful
  • A new dark spot in an adult, or a spot that looks very different from your other moles
  • A hard lump in the armpit, neck or groin together with a changing mole

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Check your whole skin about once a month, using a mirror or asking a family member to help
  • Take a clear photo of any mole that worries you and compare it later
  • Protect your skin from strong sun with shade, a hat, long sleeves and sunscreen
  • Never cut, scrape, burn or put acid or strong home remedies on a mole
  • If you want a mole removed, ask a doctor first; do not use home or beauty-shop methods

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
