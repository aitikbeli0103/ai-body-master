Stomach ulcer

An open sore on the inner lining of the stomach that can cause burning or gnawing pain

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

A stomach ulcer is a sore on the inside lining of the stomach. It can cause burning or gnawing pain in the upper belly, often linked to meals, and sometimes bleeding.

  • Inside the stomach
  • Adults, more often older
  • Burning or gnawing pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, or a stomach doctor (gastroenterologist)
Common in
Adults; more likely with H. pylori, smoking or frequent pain-relief medicines
Contagious
Some types can be: the germ H. pylori may spread through food, water or saliva
Course
May last weeks and can come back; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood or what looks like coffee grounds, or black, tarry stools: go to hospital now
  • Sudden, very bad belly pain, or a hard, rigid belly: call 999
  • Feeling faint, looking pale, cold sweats or a racing heart: call 999
  • Upper belly pain with chest pain, breathlessness or pain spreading to the arm or jaw: call 999
  • Repeated vomiting, or vomiting food eaten many hours before: see a doctor today
  • Losing weight without trying, trouble swallowing, or feeling full very quickly: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take any medicine your doctor gives you exactly as told
  • Eat regular meals, and avoid foods that you notice make the pain worse

What are the common signs of Stomach ulcer?

Pain in the upper belly · Bloated, gassy belly · Feeling sick, nausea · No appetite

Why does Stomach ulcer happen?

Germs (infection) · Long-term use of some medicines · Smoking · Stress · The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Stomach ulcer?

Ask your pharmacist · Stop smoking · Rest well
