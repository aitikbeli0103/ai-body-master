Concussion

A short-term change in how the brain works after a knock or shake to the head

ConditionHead & neckHead

What it is

The brain is shaken inside the skull by a knock to the head or body. It can cause headache, dizziness and confusion, and needs rest and a doctor's check.

  • Head
  • Sports, falls, accidents
  • Headache, dizzy, foggy
Which doctor
Family doctor or emergency doctor
Common in
Anyone; children and sports players are often affected
Contagious
No
Course
Many people feel better within days to weeks, but it differs from person to person

Common signs

More signs

  • Headache or pressure in the head
  • Dizziness, feeling foggy or slow

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Road accidents
  • A hard shake of the body that whips the head back and forth

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, a fit, or cannot be woken: call 999
  • Repeated vomiting, or a headache that keeps getting worse
  • Weakness or numbness, slurred speech, or face drooping: call 999
  • Growing confusion, strange behaviour or one pupil bigger than the other
  • Any head knock in a baby or young child: see a doctor promptly
  • Symptoms that last for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Return to work, school and sport slowly, as your doctor advises
  • Avoid alcohol and any activity with a risk of another knock
  • Limit long screen time if it makes the headache worse

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Dizzy when turning the head
  • Epilepsy or fits

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
