White spots on the lips

Small white dots or patches seen on or near the lips

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

White spots on the lips are often harmless, but they can also come from dryness, a small infection or a change in the lip skin, so they are worth watching.

  • On or near the lips
  • Can affect any age
  • Small white dots or patches
Which doctor
Family doctor, dentist, skin doctor
Common in
Any age; dry lips and sun exposure make it more likely
Contagious
Usually no, but some causes such as cold sores can be
Course
Depends on the cause; harmless spots may stay, others fade in days to weeks

Common signs

More signs

  • Tiny white or yellowish dots on the lip
  • A white patch that may or may not hurt
  • Sometimes a tingling or burning feeling

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Tiny harmless oil glands, which many people have
  • Dry lips, licking the lips or sun damage
  • Biting or injury to the lip
  • Sometimes a long-lasting patch from smoking or sun, which needs a check

See a doctor first if

  • A spot or patch that lasts more than three weeks
  • A sore, lump or patch that bleeds, hardens or keeps growing
  • Swelling of the lips or face with trouble breathing: call 999
  • Painful blisters with fever
  • White patches that you can wipe off, in a baby, or that spread inside the mouth

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep your lips moist and avoid licking or biting them
  • Have a lasting spot checked by a doctor
