Filiform wart

A thin, thread-like or finger-like skin growth caused by a common skin virus

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A filiform wart is a small, thin growth that sticks out of the skin like a thread or finger. It is caused by a common virus (HPV) and is usually painless.

  • Often face and neck
  • Spreads by touch
  • Thin, thread-like growth
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Adults and children; more likely if you shave or your immune system is weak
Contagious
Yes, by touch or by sharing towels and razors
Course
May go away by itself over months or longer; some stay or spread

Common signs

  • A thin, thread-like or finger-like growth sticking out of the skin
  • Same colour as the skin, or a little pink or brown
  • Often on the face, such as around the eyes, nose and mouth, or on the neck
  • Usually painless, but it can catch on clothes or when shaving
  • It can grow quickly, and more can appear close by

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The virus gets in through tiny cuts or scratches, including shaving nicks
  • It spreads by touching a wart, or things used by someone who has warts
  • Scratching, picking or shaving can spread it to nearby skin

See a doctor first if

  • A growth that bleeds, hurts, changes shape or colour, grows fast or does not look like a wart: see a doctor soon
  • A growth on the eyelid or close to the eye, nose or mouth: see a doctor, do not remove it yourself
  • Growths on the private parts or around the anus: see a doctor
  • Many warts, or warts spreading fast, especially with a weak immune system: see a doctor
  • Redness, swelling, pus or fever around a wart: see a doctor
