Epididymal tuberculosis (TB)

A TB germ infection of the small coiled tube behind the testicle

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

The epididymis, a small tube behind the testicle, is infected by TB germs. It often follows TB elsewhere in the body and usually develops slowly.

  • Scrotum, behind the testicle
  • Men, often with other TB
  • Slow swelling, dull ache
Which doctor
Family doctor, urologist, TB or infection doctor
Common in
Men who have or had TB in the lungs or elsewhere
Contagious
The testicle itself rarely spreads it, but TB in the lungs can; ask your doctor
Course
Slow; needs a doctor's care and months of follow-up

Common signs

More signs

  • Swelling or a lump behind the testicle, often with only a dull ache
  • A heavy, dragging feeling in the scrotum
  • Sometimes a sore or discharge on the scrotal skin

Why it happens

Other causes

  • TB germs spreading through the blood from the lungs or another part of the body
  • Spread from TB in the urinary tract or prostate

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in the testicle: go to hospital now
  • Cough for more than two weeks, or coughing up blood
  • Fever with chills, or a hot, red, very painful scrotum
  • A lump that keeps growing, or a sore that leaks
  • Blood in the urine, or weight loss you cannot explain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest, drink enough water and eat regular, balanced meals
  • Wear snug, supportive underwear for comfort
  • Cover your mouth when coughing and keep rooms airy
