Pattern baldness (androgenetic alopecia)

Gradual hair thinning in a typical pattern, often inherited

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsHeadSkin

What it is

Hair slowly gets thinner and shorter on the top of the head or at the hairline because the hair roots shrink over time; it is common and not your fault.

  • Top of the head and hairline
  • Often runs in families
  • Slow, painless thinning
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist) or family doctor
Common in
Men and women, more with age; often runs in families
Contagious
No
Course
Usually changes slowly over many years; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • The hairline slowly moves back at the temples (more common in men)
  • In women, the parting looks wider but the front hairline is usually kept
  • Hairs become finer and shorter over time, so more scalp shows
  • Usually no itching, pain or redness of the scalp

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Genes passed down in the family, from either your mother's or father's side
  • Hair roots that are sensitive to male-type hormones (androgens), which both men and women have
  • It is not caused by wearing hats, washing your hair often, or using shampoo

See a doctor first if

  • Feeling very low about your hair, or thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Hair falling out suddenly, in patches or in large amounts: it may be another cause
  • A painful, red, itchy or scaly scalp, or sores or pus
  • In women: irregular periods, new facial hair, bad acne or a deeper voice along with hair loss
  • Hair loss with tiredness, weight change, or feeling too hot or cold
  • Hair loss that began after a new medicine: ask your doctor, and do not stop the medicine on your own

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Be gentle with your hair: mild shampoo, no tight styles, and less heat and harsh chemicals
  • A shorter haircut, wig or hairpiece are fine choices; talk to someone you trust if you feel upset

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
