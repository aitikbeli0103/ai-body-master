Multiple sclerosis

A long-term condition in which the immune system damages the covering of nerves in the brain and spinal cord

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

In multiple sclerosis the immune system attacks the protective covering of nerves, so messages between the brain and body slow down or get blocked. Signs vary a lot and can come and go.

  • Brain and spinal cord
  • Often starts in young adults
  • Numbness, weakness, tiredness
Which doctor
Brain and nerve doctor (neurologist)
Common in
Young and middle-aged adults; more often in women
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; often comes and goes in flare-ups, and a neurologist can plan care

Common signs

Why it happens

  • The exact cause is not known; the immune system attacks the nerves' covering by mistake
  • It is thought to come from a mix of genes and things in the environment
  • A close family member with the same condition raises the chance a little
  • Low vitamin D or little sunlight may be linked
  • Smoking, and some past virus infections, may raise the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden weakness of the face, arm or leg on one side, or speech trouble: call 999
  • Sudden loss of vision, or severe eye pain with blurred vision: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, or choking that does not clear: call 999
  • Not able to pass urine, or sudden loss of control of the bladder or bowel: go to hospital now
  • New numbness, weakness, balance trouble or vision change that lasts a day or more: see a doctor soon
  • Fever, or burning when passing urine, as infections can set off a flare-up: see a doctor the same day

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Follow your neurologist's plan, take medicines as prescribed and keep regular check-ups
  • Pace yourself: rest when tired and plan harder tasks for when you feel best
  • Stay gently active; ask your doctor or a physiotherapist what is safe for you
  • Stay cool in hot weather, as heat can make signs feel worse for a while
  • Do not smoke, and ask your care team for support with mood, sleep or bladder problems

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Multiple sclerosis?

A long-term condition in which the immune system damages the covering of nerves in the brain and spinal cord. In multiple sclerosis the immune system attacks the protective covering of nerves, so messages between the brain and body slow down or get blocked. Signs vary a lot and can come and go.

What are the common signs of Multiple sclerosis?

Tired all the time · Blurry vision · Numb, tingling feet · Legs feel weak · Unsteady when walking · Needing the toilet often

Why does Multiple sclerosis happen?

The exact cause is not known; the immune system attacks the nerves' covering by mistake · It is thought to come from a mix of genes and things in the environment · A close family member with the same condition raises the chance a little · Low vitamin D or little sunlight may be linked · Smoking, and some past virus infections, may raise the risk
