Cluster of boils (carbuncle)

Several joined boils under the skin that form one large, painful, draining lump

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A carbuncle is a deeper skin infection where several boils join together, causing a large, painful, red lump that may drain pus from more than one spot and make a person feel unwell.

  • Skin: neck, back, thighs
  • Adults; more with diabetes
  • Large, painful, red lump
Which doctor
Family doctor soon; a surgeon may need to drain it
Common in
Adults, more often with diabetes or a weak immune system
Contagious
Yes, the germs in the pus can spread by touch or shared towels
Course
Slower than a single boil; it often needs a doctor to drain it and may leave a scar

Common signs

More signs

  • Several heads or openings that drain pus
  • Often on the back of the neck, shoulders, back or thighs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A boil that was squeezed, scratched or not looked after
  • Diabetes, a weak immune system, or a long-term illness
  • Skin that is often rubbed, sweaty or damaged, such as from shaving or eczema

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with confusion, fast breathing, a racing heart or fainting: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fever, chills or feeling unwell along with the lump: see a doctor today
  • Red streaks spreading from the lump, or redness and swelling that spread fast: see a doctor today
  • A lump on the face (near the nose, upper lip or eye) or inside the ear: see a doctor the same day
  • Any carbuncle: see a doctor soon, as it often needs to be drained safely
  • A baby, an older person, or someone with diabetes or a weak immune system: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • A clean, warm (not hot) damp cloth for a few minutes, several times a day, may ease the pain
  • Cover any draining fluid with a clean dry dressing and change it when it gets wet
  • Wash towels, clothes and bedding in hot water; keep blood sugar well controlled if you have diabetes

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
