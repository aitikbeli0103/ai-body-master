Red spots on the belly

Small red spots on the skin of the belly, which can have several causes

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsAbdomen

What it is

Small red spots can appear on the belly for many reasons. Many are mild, such as heat rash or insect bites, but some need a doctor.

  • Skin of the belly
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Small red spots, may itch
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it lasts
Common in
Anyone; more often in hot, sweaty weather or after new soaps or clothes
Contagious
Depends on the cause; some rashes can spread, many cannot
Course
Mild causes often fade in days to a few weeks; ask a doctor if it stays

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Insect bites, such as mosquitoes or bed bugs
  • Skin irritation or allergy from soap, detergent, clothes or a new medicine
  • Inflamed hair follicles, a fungal skin infection, or scabies
  • Infections with a rash (chickenpox, measles, dengue); harmless red dots can also appear with age

See a doctor first if

  • Spots with fever, a stiff neck, a bad headache or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • Spots that stay when you press a clear glass on them, or look purple or dark red: go to hospital now
  • Swelling of the lips, face or tongue, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Fever with spots, bad belly pain, vomiting, or bleeding gums or nose (this may be dengue)
  • A baby or child with spots who is very unwell: see a doctor promptly
  • Spots that spread fast, blister, ooze pus, itch badly, or last more than two weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stop any new soap, lotion or detergent you started just before the spots came
  • Ask a pharmacist or doctor before putting any cream or medicine on the spots
