ADHD

A condition that makes it hard to focus, sit still or think before acting

ConditionTold by a doctor: attention & developmentWhole body

What it is

ADHD is a difference in how the brain handles attention, energy and impulses. It starts in childhood and may continue in adults. It is not laziness or bad parenting.

  • Attention and self-control
  • Starts in childhood
  • Restless, easily distracted
Which doctor
Family doctor, paediatrician, psychiatrist or psychologist
Common in
Children and teens; often continues into adult life
Contagious
No
Course
Often continues into adult life; routines and support help many people do well

Common signs

More signs

  • Restless, fidgeting, finding it hard to sit still or wait
  • Talks a lot, interrupts, or acts before thinking
  • In adults: trouble with time, work and relationships, or feeling restless inside

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Differences in how the brain grows and handles attention
  • Being born very early or very small may raise the chance
  • Smoking or alcohol in pregnancy, or a head injury early in life, may add to the chance
  • It is not caused by bad parenting, laziness or too much sugar

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or feeling hopeless: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Chest pain, fainting or a racing heart, especially when taking ADHD medicine: go to hospital now
  • Dangerous behaviour, such as running into roads or repeated injuries: see a doctor soon
  • Strong sadness, worry or anger, or alcohol and drug use along with these signs: see a doctor
  • A child who is struggling at school or home for months: ask a doctor for an assessment

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • See a doctor for an assessment; support is available for children and adults
  • Use simple routines: the same times for sleep, meals, homework or work
  • Break big tasks into small steps; use lists, timers and reminders
  • Get daily physical activity and enough sleep
  • Be patient and praise effort; ask your doctor or pharmacist about any medicine

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • High blood pressure
  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
