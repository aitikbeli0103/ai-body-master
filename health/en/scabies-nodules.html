Scabies nodules

Firm, itchy lumps in the skin that can stay after a scabies infection (tiny skin mites)

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Scabies is caused by tiny mites that burrow in the skin and cause strong itching. Some people get firm, itchy lumps (nodules), often in the groin, armpits or buttocks, which can last for weeks.

  • Groin, armpits, buttocks
  • Spreads by close skin contact
  • Firm, very itchy lumps
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone; more in crowded homes, hostels and care homes
Contagious
Yes, scabies spreads by close skin contact and shared bedding or clothes
Course
Lumps can last weeks to months even after the mites are gone; a doctor can advise

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Scabies mites that burrow into the skin and lay eggs
  • The body's reaction to the mites or their waste, which leaves hard lumps
  • Scratching a lot, which irritates the skin and keeps the lumps itchy

See a doctor first if

  • Red streaks, spreading redness, pus or fever (a possible skin infection): see a doctor today
  • A baby, toddler, pregnant woman or older person with strong itching and a rash: see a doctor promptly
  • Thick, crusted, flaky skin over large areas, especially with a weak immune system
  • Itching that keeps you awake at night or does not ease after following a doctor's advice
  • Several people at home itching at the same time
  • Lumps that grow, bleed, or have not gone away after many months

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor so the right medicine is chosen, and follow the instructions exactly
  • Close family and partners may need to be checked and given medicine at the same time
  • Wash bedding, towels and clothes used in the last few days in hot water, then dry them well
  • Do not put creams or other remedies on the lumps without asking a doctor or pharmacist

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A severe allergy
