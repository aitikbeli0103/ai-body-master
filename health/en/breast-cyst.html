Breast cyst

A smooth, fluid-filled sac in the breast that can feel like a soft or firm lump

ConditionWomen's healthChest

What it is

A breast cyst is a round sac of fluid in the breast. Simple cysts are common and not cancer, but any new breast lump should be checked by a doctor.

  • Inside the breast
  • Most often before menopause
  • A lump, maybe tender
Which doctor
Family doctor; a breast specialist or breast surgeon if advised
Common in
Women before menopause; less often after menopause
Contagious
No
Course
May come and go with periods; a lump that stays or changes needs checking

Common signs

  • A round or oval lump in the breast that feels smooth and moves under the skin
  • The lump may feel soft like a water balloon, or firm
  • The breast may feel tender or sore, often more before a period
  • The lump may get bigger before a period and smaller afterwards
  • There can be more than one cyst, in one or both breasts

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Fluid builds up inside the tiny glands and ducts of the breast
  • Hormone medicine taken after menopause can sometimes lead to cysts
  • Often the exact reason is not known

See a doctor first if

  • A hot, red, very painful breast with fever or chills: see a doctor the same day
  • Any new breast lump: see a doctor soon, even if it feels like a harmless cyst
  • A lump that is hard, uneven, stuck in place or keeps growing
  • Skin dimpling or thickening, a nipple that turns inward, or blood-stained nipple fluid
  • A lump or swelling in the armpit, or one breast changing in size or shape
  • A lump that comes back after a doctor has drained it, or a new lump after menopause

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Know how your breasts normally look and feel, and go for breast screening when your doctor advises
  • Wear a well-fitting, supportive bra, which may ease soreness

  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
