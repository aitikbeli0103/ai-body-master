Adenomyosis

Womb lining tissue growing into the muscle wall of the womb, often causing heavy, painful periods

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

In adenomyosis, tissue like the lining of the womb grows into the womb's muscle wall. The womb can get a little bigger, and periods can become heavy, long and painful.

  • Lower belly, in the womb
  • Often before menopause
  • Heavy, painful periods
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's doctor), family doctor
Common in
Women before menopause; also linked to past childbirth or womb surgery
Contagious
No
Course
Often lasts until menopause, then usually settles; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • One idea is that womb lining tissue pushes into the muscle after childbirth or womb surgery
  • It often appears together with fibroids or endometriosis

See a doctor first if

  • Very heavy bleeding, or bleeding with dizziness, fainting or a racing heart: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Sudden, severe lower belly pain, or pain with fever or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant or might be pregnant, with bleeding or pain: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding between periods, after sex or after menopause: see a doctor soon
  • Periods so heavy or painful that daily life is hard, or feeling very tired and pale: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have heavy or painful periods checked by a doctor; other problems can feel the same
  • A warm (not hot) pack on the lower belly may ease cramps
  • If periods are very heavy and you feel tired, ask your doctor to check for anaemia

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine

What are the common signs of Adenomyosis?
