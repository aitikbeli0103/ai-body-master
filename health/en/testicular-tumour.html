Testicular tumour

A lump or growth in one testicle, which may or may not be cancer

ConditionCancer & tumoursReproductive

What it is

A testicular tumour is a growth in a testicle. Most often it shows as a lump or swelling, and a doctor needs to check it to find out what it is.

  • One testicle
  • Often young to middle-aged men
  • Lump, swelling or heaviness
Which doctor
Family doctor first; urologist, cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Young and middle-aged men
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type and stage; your care team can explain

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump or hard spot in a testicle, often painless
  • One testicle looks bigger or feels different from the other
  • A heavy or dragging feeling in the scrotum

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A testicle that did not move down into the scrotum as a baby is a known risk
  • A past testicular tumour in the other testicle

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle, with sickness: go to hospital now
  • Any new lump, hard spot or swelling in a testicle, even without pain
  • A testicle that changes in size, shape or feel
  • Back pain, cough, or tender or enlarged breasts together with a testicle lump
  • Fever with a red, hot, painful scrotum

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Never stop or change treatment on your own
  • Ask your team about having children (fertility) before treatment
  • Talk to someone you trust; many men find this hard to share

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
