Penicillin allergy

When the body's defence system reacts wrongly to penicillin or related antibiotics

ConditionAllergiesWhole body

What it is

The body sees penicillin as a threat. A reaction can be mild, like an itchy rash, or serious, like swelling and trouble breathing. Serious ones need urgent help.

  • Skin, breathing, whole body
  • Often soon after a dose
  • Rash, itch, swelling
Which doctor
Family doctor; allergy doctor (immunologist) to check the allergy
Common in
Anyone; it can appear even if you took penicillin safely before
Contagious
No
Course
May fade over time or stay; an allergy doctor can check if it is still there

Common signs

Why it happens

  • The body's defence system reacts wrongly to penicillin or related antibiotics
  • A reaction can appear even if you took the medicine safely before
  • An upset stomach or loose stools alone is usually a side effect, not an allergy
  • A rash during a virus illness may be wrongly blamed on the medicine

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, wheezing or trouble breathing after taking the medicine: call 999
  • Feeling faint, very weak or collapsing after taking the medicine: call 999
  • A rash with blisters, peeling skin, or sores in the mouth or eyes: go to hospital now
  • A new rash, itching or swelling after an antibiotic: tell a doctor or pharmacist before the next dose
  • Fever, joint pain or swollen glands with a rash days after starting the medicine: see a doctor soon
  • A baby or child with a rash, swelling or vomiting after the medicine: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Tell every doctor, dentist and pharmacist about the allergy before any new medicine
  • Write down the medicine, the date and what happened, and keep it with your health records
  • Keep a note or card about the allergy in your wallet or phone
  • Ask your doctor if an allergy check is right for you; many people who think they are allergic are not
  • Never try the medicine again yourself to test it; any check must be done by a doctor

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
