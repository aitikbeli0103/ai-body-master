Sun spots (solar lentigo)

Flat brown spots that appear on skin that has had a lot of sun over the years

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Flat spots, from tan to dark brown, show up on skin that has had a lot of sun, such as the face, hands, shoulders and arms, and they usually do not hurt or itch.

  • Skin that gets a lot of sun
  • More common with age
  • Flat brown spots
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist), family doctor
Common in
Adults past middle age; people with fair skin or a lot of sun
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stay and may slowly increase; more sun can make them darker

Common signs

Types of dark spots

How dark spots form

Spots form when the skin makes pigment faster than it can renew itself. Sun protection every day is the most important care.

Not a pigment spot: see a doctor

  • A mole that grows or changes colour
  • A spot with an uneven edge, that is raised or bleeds
Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

More signs

  • Round or oval shape with a clear edge
  • On the face, backs of the hands, shoulders, arms and upper back
  • Usually no pain, itching or bleeding
  • More spots may slowly appear over the years

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Sunburn in the past, especially when young
  • Lighter skin that burns easily in the sun

See a doctor first if

  • A spot that is new, growing, or changing in shape or colour
  • A spot with an uneven edge, several colours, or a very dark or black colour
  • A spot that bleeds, oozes, or becomes a sore that stays open for weeks
  • A spot that itches, hurts, or feels raised or lumpy
  • One spot that looks very different from the others

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wear a hat, sunglasses and long sleeves in strong sun
  • Stay in the shade when the sun is strongest, around midday
  • Avoid unregistered whitening creams; ask a skin doctor first
  • Look at your skin from time to time and note any spot that changes

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pregnancy
