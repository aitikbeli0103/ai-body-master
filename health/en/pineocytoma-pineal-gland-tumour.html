Pineocytoma (pineal gland tumour)

A usually slow-growing tumour of the pineal gland, a tiny gland deep in the brain

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

A tumour forms in the pineal gland, which helps control sleep. It usually grows slowly. It is rare, and a specialist team looks after it.

  • Deep in the brain
  • Rare; adults and children
  • Headache, sickness, vision
Which doctor
Brain and nerve doctor, brain surgeon, cancer doctor
Common in
Rare; found in adults and sometimes in children
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow; a specialist team plans care and follow-up scans

Common signs

More signs

  • Headache that keeps coming back, often worse in the morning

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It comes from the cells of the pineal gland growing abnormally
  • It is rare, and nothing a person did is known to cause it
  • A growing lump can block fluid flow in the brain and cause pressure

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe headache, repeated vomiting or fainting: go to hospital now
  • A fit (seizure), or confusion, or being hard to wake: call 999
  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Sudden change in vision, or double vision that lasts
  • A child who is very sleepy, vomiting or unwell: see a doctor now

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.
