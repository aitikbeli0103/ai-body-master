Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)

A cancer of the blood and bone marrow that makes too many white blood cells, often slowly at first

ConditionCancer & tumoursBlood

What it is

In CML, the bone marrow makes too many abnormal white blood cells. It often starts slowly and may be found on a routine blood test before any signs appear.

  • Bone marrow and blood
  • Mostly adults
  • Often no signs at first
Which doctor
Blood specialist (haematologist) or cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Mostly adults, more often in middle and older age; rare in children
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow at first; needs regular blood tests and long-term specialist care

Common signs

Why it happens

  • A gene change in blood-making cells in the bone marrow (called the Philadelphia chromosome)
  • The change happens during life; it is not passed down in families
  • Past exposure to very high levels of radiation is a known risk, but this is uncommon
  • It is not caused by food or stress; often no clear reason is found

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden bad headache, blurred vision, confusion or trouble breathing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or vomiting blood or black stools: go to hospital now
  • Sudden severe belly pain, especially on the upper left side: go to hospital now
  • Fever, shivering or feeling very unwell: see a doctor straight away, as infections can be serious
  • New bruises, tiredness, night sweats or weight loss that keep getting worse
  • Trouble taking your cancer medicine, or side effects that worry you: call your cancer team first

Questions and answers

What is Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)?

A cancer of the blood and bone marrow that makes too many white blood cells, often slowly at first. In CML, the bone marrow makes too many abnormal white blood cells. It often starts slowly and may be found on a routine blood test before any signs appear.

What are the common signs of Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)?

Tired all the time · Soaking sweats at night · Losing weight without trying · Bruises easily · Pale, tired look · Fever, feeling hot

Why does Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) happen?

A gene change in blood-making cells in the bone marrow (called the Philadelphia chromosome) · The change happens during life; it is not passed down in families · Past exposure to very high levels of radiation is a known risk, but this is uncommon · It is not caused by food or stress; often no clear reason is found

How can I look after myself with Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)?

Take medicine as your doctor says · Keep your regular tests · Ask your pharmacist · Wash your hands often · Rest well
