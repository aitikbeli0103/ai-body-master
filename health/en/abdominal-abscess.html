Abdominal abscess

A pocket of pus that builds up inside the belly, often after an infection or surgery

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

An abdominal abscess is a pocket of pus that forms inside the belly after an infection. It often causes belly pain and fever, and it needs a doctor's care.

  • Inside the belly
  • After infection or surgery
  • Belly pain and fever
Which doctor
Family doctor or hospital first; then a surgeon or physician
Common in
Adults after belly infection or surgery; people with diabetes or weak immunity
Contagious
No; wash your hands after touching any dressing or drain
Course
Needs medical care; recovery time depends on the cause and size

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A burst appendix, inflamed bowel pouches (diverticulitis) or other bowel inflammation
  • Infection after belly surgery or an injury to the belly
  • Infection in an organ such as the liver, gallbladder or pancreas
  • A hole or leak in the wall of the stomach or bowel

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with shivering, a fast heartbeat, fast breathing, confusion or feeling faint: call 999
  • Very bad belly pain, a hard belly, or pain that is quickly getting worse: go to hospital now
  • Vomiting that will not stop, vomiting blood, or black or bloody stools: go to hospital now
  • Fever or belly pain after belly surgery, an injury or an appendix problem: see a doctor today
  • Belly pain with fever, no appetite or weight loss that lasts more than a day: see a doctor today

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
