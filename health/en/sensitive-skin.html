Sensitive skin

Skin that stings, burns, itches or turns red easily with things most people can use

ConditionAllergiesSkin

What it is

Sensitive skin reacts quickly to skincare, soap, sun, wind, heat or stress. It may feel tight or sting, look red, or get dry and itchy.

  • Skin, often face and hands
  • Anyone, especially dry skin
  • Stinging, tight, easily red
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone; more common in people with dry skin or eczema
Contagious
No
Course
Often long-term, but gentle skin care and fewer triggers can calm it

Common signs

3 stages of sensitive skin

Sensitive skin or allergy?

  • Sensitive skin over-reacts to weather, cosmetics or cleansers
  • An allergy is the body reacting to one substance (food, medicine, pollen): find it and see a doctor

Barrier care tips

  • Wash with water or a gentle cleanser; pat dry, don't rub
  • Avoid soap, acids, alcohol and fragrance on the face
  • No scrubs or face massage while the skin is sensitive
  • Repair takes about 1 to 6 months; be patient
  • Use sunscreen and moisturiser daily; avoid saunas and very hot water

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A weak skin barrier, so the skin loses water and irritants get in easily
  • Harsh soap, scrubs, strong skincare, perfume or too many products at once
  • Sometimes a skin problem such as eczema, or an allergy to a product, is behind it

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, or trouble breathing after using a product: call 999
  • A product in the eyes with pain, bad redness or blurred vision: rinse with clean water, go to hospital now
  • Blisters, oozing, or a rash that swells or spreads fast after a product: see a doctor the same day
  • Yellow crusts, pus, a hot swollen area or fever, which may mean an infection
  • Redness or soreness that stays for weeks even with gentle care
  • A rash on the face with fever, joint pain or feeling very unwell

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Use a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser and moisturiser, and wash with lukewarm water
  • Keep your routine simple and add only one new product at a time
  • Try a new product on a small patch of skin, such as the inner arm, for a few days first
  • Pat the skin dry, avoid scrubbing, and skip very hot showers

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Pregnancy
