Optic neuritis

Inflammation of the optic nerve that can cause sudden blurry vision and eye pain

ConditionEyesHead

What it is

The optic nerve, which links the eye to the brain, becomes inflamed. Vision may suddenly blur or dim in one eye, often with pain when moving the eye.

  • Usually one eye
  • Often young adults
  • Dim vision, eye pain
Which doctor
Eye doctor (ophthalmologist) and neurologist, urgently
Common in
Young and middle-aged adults; sometimes linked to another illness
Contagious
No
Course
Vision often starts to improve over weeks, but it needs a doctor to check the cause

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain behind the eye, worse when moving it
  • Colours look faded or washed out
  • A dark or missing patch in the middle of vision

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Often the immune system attacks the nerve covering
  • It may be linked to nerve illnesses such as multiple sclerosis

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden loss or dimming of vision: see a doctor the same day or go to hospital
  • Vision loss with face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Numbness, weakness or trouble walking together with the eye problem
  • A severe headache or a very painful red eye
  • Vision that gets worse again after it improved

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See an eye doctor quickly so the cause can be found
  • Rest your eyes and use good light
  • Stay safe at home and avoid driving until your doctor says it is fine
