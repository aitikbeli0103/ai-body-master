Sebaceous gland hyperplasia

Small, soft, yellowish bumps on the face from oil glands that have grown larger

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Oil glands in the skin grow bigger and form small, soft, yellowish or skin-coloured bumps, often with a tiny dip in the middle, mostly on the forehead, cheeks and nose.

  • Forehead, cheeks and nose
  • More common with age
  • Small, soft, painless bumps
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist), family doctor
Common in
Middle-aged and older adults; people with oily skin or much sun exposure
Contagious
No
Course
Usually lasts for years and may slowly increase in number

Common signs

More signs

  • Often a small dip in the centre of each bump
  • Mostly on the forehead, cheeks, nose or chin
  • Usually painless and does not itch
  • The skin around them may look oily

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Oil glands in the skin grow bigger, mostly as we get older

See a doctor first if

  • A bump that bleeds easily, forms a sore or does not close up
  • A bump that grows quickly, looks pearly or shiny, or is different from the others
  • Pain, itching or redness around a bump, or pus
  • You are not sure what the bump is: have a doctor look at it
  • Bumps that bother you or upset you: talk to a doctor about the options

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash your face gently with a mild cleanser, once or twice a day
  • Take a photo now and then so you can see if any bump changes
