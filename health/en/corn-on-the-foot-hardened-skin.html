Corn on the foot (hardened skin)

A small, hard, round patch of thick skin, often on the toes or soles, from pressure

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A corn is a small, hard, round patch of thick skin, often with a harder centre, that forms on or between the toes or on the sole where shoes press or rub.

  • Toes and soles of the feet
  • Adults in tight shoes
  • Hard spot, sore when pressed
Which doctor
Family doctor; foot specialist (podiatrist) or skin doctor
Common in
Adults in tight shoes or high heels, or with bent toes or bunions
Contagious
No
Course
Often fades once the pressure stops; may return if shoes or foot shape stay the same

Common signs

More signs

  • A small, hard, round bump of thick skin, often with a harder centre
  • Pain or a sharp feeling when pressed or when wearing shoes
  • On the top or tip of a toe, the sole, or between the toes
  • Between the toes it can look soft, whitish and damp

Why it happens

  • Shoes that are too tight, narrow or pointed, or high heels
  • Shoes that are too loose, so the foot slides and rubs
  • Not wearing socks, or socks with thick seams
  • Bent toes, bunions or other changes in foot shape
  • Standing, walking or running a lot

See a doctor first if

  • A red, hot, swollen, painful foot with fever or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • An open sore, pus, bleeding or a bad smell at the corn: see a doctor today
  • Diabetes, numb feet or poor blood flow: see a doctor about any corn, and do not cut it yourself
  • Pain so bad that it is hard to walk: see a doctor or foot specialist
  • A corn that keeps coming back, or a dark spot, bleeding or a lump that does not look like a corn: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wear socks, and use soft cushion pads or soft insoles to take pressure off the spot
  • Check your feet every day, especially with diabetes, and tell your doctor about any sore

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
