Nabothian cyst (cervical gland cyst)

Small mucus-filled bumps on the surface of the cervix (neck of the womb); usually harmless

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

Small glands on the cervix can get blocked, so mucus builds up in a tiny sac. These cysts are common, usually cause no signs, and are often found by chance at a check-up.

  • Neck of the womb (cervix)
  • Women of childbearing age
  • Usually no signs
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's doctor) or family doctor
Common in
Women of childbearing age; often found by chance at a check-up or scan
Contagious
No
Course
Usually harmless; small ones often stay the same and the doctor may just keep watch

Common signs

More signs

  • The doctor may see small, smooth, whitish or yellowish bumps on the cervix
  • There may be one or several, and they are usually small
  • Rarely, a large cyst may cause discharge or discomfort

Why it happens

  • The small mucus glands on the cervix get blocked and mucus builds up inside
  • New tissue can grow over the gland openings, often as the cervix changes after childbirth
  • Long-lasting irritation or inflammation of the cervix may play a part
  • Often the exact reason is not known, and it is not caused by anything you did

See a doctor first if

  • Very heavy bleeding, fainting, or bleeding or severe pain in pregnancy: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding after sex, between periods or after the menopause
  • Smelly or bloody discharge, or pain in the lower belly
  • Fever with lower belly pain or unusual discharge
  • A cyst that keeps growing, or a cervix that your doctor says looks unusual

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep up regular cervical screening (Pap smear or HPV test) as your doctor advises
  • Do not use vaginal douches; wash the outside gently with plain water
  • Use condoms; safe sex lowers the chance of infections that can irritate the cervix
  • Tell your doctor about any new discharge, bleeding or pain
