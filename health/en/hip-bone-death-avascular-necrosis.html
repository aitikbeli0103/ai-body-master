Hip bone death (avascular necrosis)

Loss of blood supply to the top of the thigh bone, which can weaken the hip joint

ConditionBones, joints & spineArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

The ball-shaped top of the thigh bone slowly loses its blood supply, so the bone can weaken and break down, which may cause hip pain and a limp.

  • Hip joint and groin
  • Mostly working-age adults
  • Hip ache, worse when walking
Which doctor
Bone and joint doctor (orthopaedic surgeon); family doctor first
Common in
Working-age adults; heavy drinkers; people on long-term steroid medicine
Contagious
No
Course
Often gets worse over time without care; an early check gives more options

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain that is worse when walking or standing on that leg
  • A stiff hip that is hard to move or turn

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Steroid medicine taken for a long time or in large amounts (never stop it on your own; ask your doctor)
  • Some health problems, such as sickle cell disease, lupus or blood clotting problems
  • Sometimes the cause is not known

See a doctor first if

  • After a fall: severe hip pain, a bent or twisted leg, or being unable to put weight on it: go to hospital now
  • Sudden chest pain or trouble breathing, or a painful, swollen calf: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fever with a very painful hip that you cannot move: go to hospital now
  • Hip, groin or thigh pain that lasts for weeks, especially if you take steroid medicine or drink heavily
  • Pain that keeps getting worse, wakes you at night, or a limp that stays
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in the leg

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a bone doctor for a check; an X-ray or scan can show what is happening
  • Ask your doctor how much weight to put on the leg, and whether a walking stick or crutches would help
  • Avoid heavy drinking and smoking, which can harm the blood supply to bone; ask for help to cut down
  • If you take steroid medicine, never stop it on your own; ask your doctor or pharmacist about the risks

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
