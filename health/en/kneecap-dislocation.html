Kneecap dislocation

The kneecap slips out of its groove at the front of the knee

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legBones & muscles

What it is

The kneecap (patella) suddenly slides, usually to the outer side, after a twist or a knock. The knee looks out of shape and hurts, and it often slips back by itself.

  • Front of the knee
  • Often teens and young adults
  • Sudden pain, knee gives way
Which doctor
Emergency doctor or bone doctor (orthopaedic); physiotherapist later
Common in
Teenagers and young adults in sport, dance or after a twisting fall
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor to check it; often settles in weeks to months, but it can happen again

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden pain and the knee gives way after a twist
  • The kneecap looks out of place, usually to the outer side
  • Swelling and tenderness around the kneecap

Why it happens

  • A twist of the knee while the foot is planted, common in sport and dance
  • A direct knock to the inner side of the knee
  • A kneecap that sits a little loose or shallow in its groove
  • Weak thigh muscles, or having had a dislocation before

See a doctor first if

  • A foot that is numb, pale, cold or blue: go to hospital now
  • The knee is bent or out of shape and cannot be straightened: go to hospital
  • Cannot put weight on the leg, or heavy swelling
  • The kneecap keeps slipping out, or the knee locks
  • Fever with a hot, red knee

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not force the kneecap back yourself; let a doctor do it if it has not slipped back
  • Ask your doctor or physiotherapist about a knee support and exercises to strengthen the thigh

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
