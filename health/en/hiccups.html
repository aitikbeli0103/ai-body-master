Hiccups

Short, repeated jerks of the breathing muscle with a 'hic' sound

SymptomStomach & intestinesChest

What it is

The muscle under the lungs (the diaphragm) tightens suddenly, and the voice box closes with a short 'hic'. It is usually harmless and stops by itself.

  • Chest and throat
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Short, repeated jerks
Which doctor
Family doctor if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; often after eating fast or drinking fizzy drinks
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stops by itself within minutes to hours

Common signs

  • A sudden 'hic' sound again and again
  • A small jump in the chest or belly with each hiccup
  • Feeling a little tired or annoyed if it lasts
  • Sometimes it gets in the way of eating or talking

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Eating or drinking too fast, or a very full stomach
  • Sometimes a problem in the stomach, gullet, nerves or brain, or some medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Hiccups that last more than two days
  • Hiccups with chest pain, trouble breathing, or weakness on one side of the body: call 999
  • Hiccups so strong that you cannot eat, drink or sleep
  • Vomiting blood or black stools together with hiccups

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Eat and drink slowly, in smaller amounts
  • Take small sips of water or breathe slowly and calmly for a minute
  • Cut down on fizzy drinks, alcohol and very spicy food
  • If it keeps coming back, tell your doctor
