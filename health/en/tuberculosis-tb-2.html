Tuberculosis (TB)

A long-lasting bacterial infection that usually affects the lungs but can reach other parts of the body

ConditionInfectionsChestWhole body

What it is

TB germs spread through the air. Most often the lungs are affected, but TB can also reach the lymph glands, bones, spine, kidneys or brain lining. It needs a long course of medicine from a doctor.

  • Lungs or other body parts
  • Weak immunity, higher risk
  • Cough, fever, night sweats
Which doctor
Family doctor; TB clinic; lung or infectious disease doctor
Common in
Anyone who breathes in TB germs; risk is higher with weak immunity or crowded living
Contagious
Some types can be; TB in the lungs spreads through the air
Course
Needs many months of medicine; finishing the whole course really matters

Common signs

  • A cough that lasts weeks, sometimes with blood, when the lungs are affected
  • Fever, night sweats, tiredness and losing weight without trying
  • Swollen glands in the neck or armpit that do not go away
  • Back pain or a painful, swollen joint that slowly gets worse (bone or spine TB)
  • Other signs depend on the organ, such as belly pain, blood in the urine or a lasting headache

Why it happens

  • TB is caused by a germ that spreads when a person with active lung TB coughs
  • The germ can stay asleep in the body for years without making a person ill
  • It can wake up and spread to other organs when the body's defences are weak
  • Diabetes, HIV, smoking, heavy drinking and poor nutrition raise the risk
  • Living or working close to someone with active TB, or in crowded places

See a doctor first if

  • Coughing up blood, trouble breathing or chest pain: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Severe headache with a stiff neck, confusion, strong sleepiness or fits: go to hospital now
  • Back pain with weak or numb legs, or trouble passing urine: go to hospital now
  • A cough for more than two weeks, fever, night sweats or weight loss: see a doctor soon
  • A lump or swollen gland that keeps growing, or a painful swollen joint: see a doctor
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or repeated vomiting while on TB medicine: see your doctor at once

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Take all your TB medicine exactly as your doctor says and keep going until your doctor says you can finish
  • Go to every clinic visit and test, and tell your doctor about any side effects
  • Avoid alcohol and smoking, and eat regular, nourishing meals
  • Cover coughs, keep rooms airy, and ask your doctor when it is safe to be around others
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking any other medicine or herbal product

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
