Absent periods (amenorrhoea)

Not having periods when they would normally be expected, such as periods that never started or have stopped for months

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

Amenorrhoea means missing periods. Periods may never have started, or may have stopped for months. Pregnancy, breastfeeding and menopause are normal reasons for this.

  • Womb and ovaries
  • Girls and women
  • Period missing or stopped
Which doctor
Family doctor, or a women's doctor (gynaecologist)
Common in
Girls whose periods have not started; women of child-bearing age
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor can find the cause and explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • No period at all when one would be expected, or periods missing for months
  • Signs of pregnancy, such as sore breasts, feeling sick or feeling tired
  • Extra facial hair, pimples or thinning hair with some hormone problems
  • Milky fluid from the breasts when not pregnant or breastfeeding

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some contraceptives and other medicines can stop periods for a while
  • Problems with the ovaries or womb, some of which are there from birth

See a doctor first if

  • Missed period with sudden severe lower belly pain, dizziness or fainting, and pregnancy is possible: call 999
  • Missed periods with headaches that keep coming back, vision changes or milky breast fluid: see a doctor soon
  • A girl whose periods have not started by her mid-teens, or who has no breast growth: see a doctor
  • Periods have stopped for months and you are not pregnant or near menopause: see a doctor
  • Hot flushes with missed periods at a young age: see a doctor
  • Losing weight quickly, or feeling worried about food and your body: talk to a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • If you could be pregnant, do a pregnancy test and see a doctor
  • Keep a steady weight that suits your body, and exercise in moderation
  • Write down the date of your last period and any changes, and tell your doctor

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
