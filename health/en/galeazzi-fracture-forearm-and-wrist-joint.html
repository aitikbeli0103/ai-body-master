Galeazzi fracture (forearm and wrist joint)

A broken forearm bone together with an injury to the wrist joint between the two forearm bones

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

A fall or hard blow breaks one forearm bone and also damages the joint where the two forearm bones meet at the wrist, so the arm is painful and swollen and needs a doctor's care.

  • Forearm and wrist
  • After a fall or a hard blow
  • Pain, swelling, hard to turn
Which doctor
Hospital emergency doctor; bone doctor (orthopaedic doctor)
Common in
Adults after a fall onto the hand, a sports injury or a road accident
Contagious
No
Course
Hospital care first; an operation is often needed, and recovery takes weeks to months

Common signs

More signs

  • The forearm or wrist may look bent, or there is a bump near the wrist
  • Hard to turn the forearm or move the wrist
  • Bruising around the forearm and wrist

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A direct, hard blow to the forearm
  • Weak bones make a break more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Bone through the skin, heavy bleeding, or a very bent arm: call 999
  • After a big fall or road accident, or with a head, neck or chest injury: call 999
  • Hand or fingers numb, pale, blue or cold: go to hospital now
  • Forearm very swollen, tight and more and more painful: go to hospital now
  • Any suspected broken arm, an arm that looks out of shape, or pain that stops you moving it: go to hospital now
  • Cast or splint feels too tight, or you have a fever, a bad smell or red, hot skin: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Keep the arm still and supported in the position that is most comfortable until a doctor sees it
  • Do not try to straighten the arm or push the bone back
  • Take off rings, watches and bracelets early, before the hand swells
  • Raise the hand on pillows, and use a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for short times
  • Keep any cast or splint dry and follow your doctor's advice; ask your doctor or pharmacist about pain medicine

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Broken, red or sore skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
