Scalp folliculitis (inflamed hair follicles)

Small red, sore or pimple-like bumps on the scalp where hair follicles are inflamed

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsHeadSkin

What it is

Hair follicles are the tiny pockets that hold each hair. When they get irritated or infected, small red bumps or pus spots can form on the scalp, and they may itch or feel sore.

  • Scalp, around the hair roots
  • Teens and adults
  • Red, sore or itchy bumps
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps returning
Common in
Teens and adults; more with sweat, hot weather, oily hair or tight hats and helmets
Contagious
Some types can be; avoid sharing combs, towels and hats
Course
Mild cases often settle in days to weeks; some come back or last longer

Common signs

More signs

  • Spots with pus that may crust over
  • Larger, tender lumps in deeper or long-lasting cases

Why it happens

  • Germs, usually bacteria, getting into hair follicles through tiny breaks in the skin
  • Sweat, oil and dirt blocking the follicles, especially in hot, humid weather
  • Scratching, shaving the scalp, or tight hats, helmets and headscarves
  • Heavy oils, gels or hair products that clog the scalp
  • Diabetes or a weak immune system, or sometimes a fungus or yeast on the scalp

See a doctor first if

  • Fever, chills or feeling very unwell with a painful scalp: go to hospital now
  • A large, hot, painful lump or boil with pus, or swelling of the face or around the eyes
  • Redness spreading across the scalp, face or neck
  • Bumps that keep coming back, leave bald patches, or form scars
  • A child with scaly, patchy or bald spots on the scalp: see a doctor promptly
  • No better after one to two weeks of gentle care, or you have diabetes or a weak immune system

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash the scalp gently with a mild shampoo; do not scrub hard
  • Keep the scalp dry and cool; change sweaty hats and wash helmets and headscarves often
  • Use less heavy oil and gel, and ask a pharmacist or doctor before using any medicated shampoo or cream

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A severe allergy
