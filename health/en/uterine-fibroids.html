Uterine fibroids

Growths in the muscle wall of the womb that are not cancer; many cause no problems

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

Fibroids are growths in the muscle wall of the womb that are not cancer, and while some women have no signs, others have heavy periods, pressure in the lower belly or need the toilet often.

  • The womb (uterus)
  • Women of child-bearing age
  • Heavy periods, pelvic pressure
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's health doctor), family doctor
Common in
Women of child-bearing age, more often in the years before menopause
Contagious
No
Course
Often grow slowly; many cause no problems, and they may shrink after menopause

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • More common if a close relative has had fibroids

See a doctor first if

  • Very heavy bleeding with dizziness, fainting or a racing heart: call 999
  • Sudden severe belly pain, or belly pain with fever or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • You cannot pass urine: go to hospital now
  • Periods so heavy that you soak through pads quickly or pass large clots
  • Bleeding between periods, after sex, or after your periods have stopped
  • Constant tiredness, a pale look or breathlessness, which may mean low blood

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Note how long your periods last and how heavy they are, and show your doctor
  • If your periods are heavy, eat iron-rich foods such as leafy greens, beans and meat
  • A warm (not hot) pack on the lower belly and rest may ease aching
