Colitis (inflamed colon)

Inflammation of the inner lining of the large bowel (colon); it can be short-term or long-term

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Colitis means the lining of the large bowel is inflamed. Depending on the cause, it may bring cramps, loose stools, a sudden need to use the toilet, and sometimes blood or mucus in the stools.

  • Lower belly, large bowel
  • Short or long-lasting
  • Cramps and loose stools
Which doctor
Family doctor; stomach and gut doctor (gastroenterologist)
Common in
Any age; more likely after a bowel infection or with a family history of bowel disease
Contagious
Some types can be, if an infection is the cause (via food, water or dirty hands)
Course
Depends on the cause: some types settle quickly, others last long; a doctor can explain

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Bowel infections from germs or parasites in food or water
  • Some medicines, such as antibiotics, that upset the balance of germs in the bowel
  • The body's defence system attacking the bowel lining (such as ulcerative colitis)
  • Reduced blood flow to the bowel, more often in older people

See a doctor first if

  • Severe belly pain with a hard, swollen belly, fever or a fast heartbeat: go to hospital now
  • Lots of blood in the stools, black stools, or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • Signs of dehydration: very dry mouth, dizziness, or passing very little urine
  • Diarrhoea that starts during or after taking antibiotics: see a doctor promptly
  • A baby, young child or older person with bloody or watery diarrhoea: see a doctor promptly
  • Diarrhoea or cramps that last more than a week or two, or keep coming back

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest, and note any foods that make symptoms worse
