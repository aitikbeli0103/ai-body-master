Skin infection

Germs such as bacteria, fungi or viruses getting into the skin and causing redness, pain or pus

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Germs have got into the skin, often through a cut, scratch, insect bite or cracked skin, so the area becomes red, warm, swollen or sore.

  • Any part of the skin
  • Anyone, more with broken skin
  • Red, warm, painful skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more likely with cuts, skin problems, diabetes or a weak immune system
Contagious
Some types can be, through touch or shared towels and razors
Course
Depends on the cause; mild ones often ease in days or weeks, others need a doctor

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Bacteria, often getting in through a cut, scratch, insect bite or shaving nick
  • Fungus, such as ringworm or athlete's foot, in warm, damp places
  • Viruses, such as warts, cold sores or shingles
  • Dry, cracked skin or eczema lets germs in more easily
  • Diabetes, poor blood flow or a weak immune system make infections more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with shaking chills, fast heartbeat, confusion or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • Redness that spreads fast, or red streaks moving away from the area: see a doctor now
  • An infection on the face near the eyes or nose, or in a baby, an older person or someone with diabetes
  • Pus, a bad smell, blisters, or a sore that is getting bigger or very painful
  • No better after a few days, or it keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not put herbs, powder, toothpaste or leftover medicine on the skin; ask a doctor or pharmacist
  • Keep skin dry, especially in folds and between toes; if you have diabetes, check your feet daily

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A severe allergy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
