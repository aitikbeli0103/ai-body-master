Anal cryptitis (inflamed anal crypts)

Inflammation of the small pockets (crypts) inside the anal canal

ConditionStomach & intestinesPelvis

What it is

The anal crypts are tiny pockets just inside the anus. If germs or hard stool get into them, they can become swollen and sore, causing an ache, itching or a feeling of fullness.

  • Inside the anus
  • Adults of any age
  • Dull ache, burning, itching
Which doctor
Family doctor; colorectal surgeon (bowel and anus doctor)
Common in
Adults, especially with constipation or loose stools
Contagious
No
Course
Often settles with good bowel habits and care, but can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • A dull ache, burning or a heavy feeling in the anus
  • Feeling you still need to go, even after passing stool
  • Pain that is worse during or after passing stool

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Loose stools or diarrhoea that keep irritating the area
  • Germs getting into a crypt and causing an infection
  • Other problems nearby, such as piles or a small tear in the skin

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding from the anus, or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • Severe throbbing pain, a hot, red, swollen lump near the anus, or fever: see a doctor today
  • Pus or a sticky discharge that keeps coming from near the anus
  • Blood in the stool, black stools, or a bowel habit change that lasts for weeks
  • Losing weight without trying
  • Symptoms that do not ease after a week or two of care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Eat plenty of fibre (fruit, vegetables, whole grains) and drink enough water
  • Do not strain, and do not sit on the toilet for a long time
  • Sitting in a basin of warm (not hot) water for a few minutes may ease the soreness

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
