Atlantoaxial asymmetry (top two neck bones)

The top two neck bones look uneven or tilted against each other

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

This is a finding on a scan or X-ray where the top two neck bones sit unevenly. It is often harmless, and only a doctor can say whether it matters for you.

  • Top of the neck
  • Often found by chance
  • Neck stiffness, headache
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a spine or nerve specialist
Common in
Adults and children; often seen on neck X-rays
Contagious
No
Course
Often causes no problem; a doctor decides if follow-up is needed

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A natural difference in the shape of the bones
  • The head was turned or tilted when the X-ray was taken
  • Tight neck muscles or long-term poor posture

See a doctor first if

  • Weakness of the face, arm or leg, or trouble speaking: call 999
  • A neck injury or a fall with neck pain: go to hospital now
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in the arms or legs
  • Neck pain that keeps getting worse or does not settle
  • Fever together with a stiff neck and headache

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Ask your doctor what your scan result means for you
  • Keep your head and neck in a relaxed, neutral position
  • Take breaks from phones and screens, and move gently
  • Avoid hard neck twisting or cracking

Tell your therapist

  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Dizzy when turning the head
  • Weak bones

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
