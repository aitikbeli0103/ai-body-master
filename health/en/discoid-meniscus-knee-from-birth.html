Discoid meniscus (knee, from birth)

A knee cartilage cushion that is thicker or differently shaped than usual since birth

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

The meniscus is a C-shaped cushion in the knee. In a discoid meniscus it is shaped like a disc instead. Many people have no trouble, but it can tear or cause clicking and pain.

  • Inside the knee
  • Born with it; often in children
  • Clicking or knee pain
Which doctor
Bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon), child doctor if a child
Common in
Children and young people; often found by chance or after a knee injury
Contagious
No
Course
Many have no problem; if painful, a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • A click or snap in the knee when bending or straightening

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The thicker cushion can tear more easily with twisting or sport
  • It can run in some families, but this is not always so

See a doctor first if

  • Knee locked and cannot straighten or bend: see a doctor soon
  • Cannot put weight on the leg after a fall or twist: go to hospital
  • A hot, red, very swollen knee with fever
  • A child who limps, avoids walking or complains of knee pain for more than a few days
  • Knee giving way, or pain that keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Let the knee rest when it hurts, and ease back into play slowly
  • See a doctor if the clicking comes with pain or swelling
  • Follow your doctor's advice on sport and exercises

Tell your therapist

  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine
