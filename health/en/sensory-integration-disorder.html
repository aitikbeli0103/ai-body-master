Sensory integration disorder

Difficulty in handling information from the senses such as touch, sound and movement

ConditionTold by a doctor: attention & developmentWhole body

What it is

The brain may find it hard to sort out what the senses report, so touch, sounds, lights or movement can feel too strong or too weak. It is mostly talked about in children.

  • Brain and senses
  • Mostly children
  • Upset by touch or noise
Which doctor
Paediatrician, occupational therapist
Common in
Children, especially with developmental differences
Contagious
No
Course
Often changes as a child grows; support from therapists and family can help

Common signs

  • Upset by loud noises, bright lights, or the feel of clothes and food
  • Seems clumsy, bumps into things, or has poor balance
  • Always moving, spinning or seeking touch, or avoiding touch
  • Finds it hard to calm down, focus or change activities
  • Fussy about food, grooming or getting dressed

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It can come with developmental conditions, such as autism or attention problems
  • Being born early may play a part

See a doctor first if

  • A child who loses skills they had, such as speech or walking: see a doctor promptly
  • Delay in speech or development compared with other children
  • A child who hurts themselves or others when upset
  • Sudden changes in behaviour, a fit, or loss of balance
  • Problems that stop school, eating, sleeping or family life

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Keep a calm, predictable daily routine
  • Give a quiet corner for the child to rest when things feel too much
  • Choose soft, comfortable clothes and avoid forcing things the child dislikes
  • Play outdoors and do active games that the child enjoys
  • Talk to the teacher and a therapist so everyone helps in the same way

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Epilepsy or fits

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
