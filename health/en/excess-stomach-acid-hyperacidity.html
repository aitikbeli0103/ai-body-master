Excess stomach acid (hyperacidity)

Too much stomach acid, or acid that irritates the stomach and food pipe, causing burning and a sour taste

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

The stomach makes acid to digest food, and when there is too much, or it flows up into the food pipe, it can cause burning, a sour taste and an upset stomach.

  • Stomach and food pipe
  • Often after heavy meals
  • Burning and a sour taste
Which doctor
Family doctor; a gut specialist (gastroenterologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Adults; more often with irregular meals, stress or spicy food
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes; meals, stress and habits can make it better or worse

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Big, fatty or very spicy meals, or eating very late at night
  • Skipping meals or eating at irregular times
  • Coffee, fizzy drinks, alcohol and smoking
  • Stress, being overweight, or some medicines (ask your doctor or pharmacist)
  • The stomach germ H. pylori, or a weak valve between the stomach and food pipe

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or pressure with sweating, trouble breathing, or pain in the arm or jaw: call 999
  • Vomiting blood, or black, sticky stools: go to hospital now
  • Pain or trouble when swallowing, or food that feels stuck
  • Losing weight without trying, or vomiting that keeps happening
  • Burning that happens often, wakes you at night or lasts more than two weeks
  • Feeling tired, pale or short of breath, which can be a sign of slow bleeding

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
