Short bowel syndrome

Not enough working bowel left to take in food, water and nutrients well

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Part of the small bowel is missing or does not work, often after surgery. The body then takes in less food, water and nutrients, which can cause diarrhoea and weight loss.

  • Small bowel
  • After bowel surgery or illness
  • Diarrhoea and weight loss
Which doctor
Gastroenterologist, dietitian, surgeon
Common in
People who had a large part of the bowel removed; some babies born with a short bowel
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; the bowel can slowly adapt, and a care team guides food and support

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Bowel damaged by a blocked blood supply, a twist or an injury
  • Long-term bowel illness such as Crohn's disease, needing surgery

See a doctor first if

  • Signs of dehydration: very dry mouth, dizziness, little or no urine, or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain, a swollen belly, or repeated vomiting
  • Fever, or redness and pain around a feeding tube or catheter
  • Muscle cramps, weakness or a fast heartbeat
  • Weight loss that keeps going, or a baby or child who is not growing; see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow the food plan from your doctor or dietitian
  • Ask your doctor before using vitamins, supplements or any medicine
  • Ask your care team about emotional support; living with this can be tiring

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Weak bones
