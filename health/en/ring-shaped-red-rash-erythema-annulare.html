Ring-shaped red rash (erythema annulare)

Red rings or ring-like patches on the skin that slowly spread outward

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Red patches that grow outward and clear in the middle, so they look like rings. It is a skin sign with many possible causes, so a doctor may look for the reason.

  • Skin of the body, arms or legs
  • Adults and children
  • Red rings, may itch a little
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Adults and children; often linked to infections, medicines or other illnesses
Contagious
Depends on the cause; ringworm can spread, many other causes do not
Course
Can last weeks to months and may come and go; a doctor can look for the cause

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A skin reaction to an infection, such as a fungal infection (ringworm)
  • Some illnesses inside the body, such as autoimmune or thyroid problems
  • A tick bite (more in other countries), or a recent throat infection in children

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, face or tongue, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Blisters, painful peeling skin, or sores in the mouth or eyes: go to hospital now
  • Rash with fever, joint pain or feeling very unwell: see a doctor today
  • A child with the rash, a recent sore throat, fever or joint pain: see a doctor promptly
  • A rash that started soon after a new medicine: ask your doctor or pharmacist, and do not stop it yourself
  • Rings that keep spreading, itch badly, or last more than two weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not scratch or rub the rings, and keep nails short
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before using any cream; the wrong one can make some rashes worse
  • Take a photo every few days to show your doctor how it changes

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • A severe allergy
