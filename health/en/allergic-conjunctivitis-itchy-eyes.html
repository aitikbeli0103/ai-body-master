Allergic conjunctivitis (itchy eyes)

Red, itchy, watery eyes when the eyes react to an allergen such as dust, pollen or pet fur

ConditionAllergiesHead

What it is

The thin, clear layer covering the white of the eye reacts to something the body is allergic to, so the eyes feel itchy and look red and watery.

  • Usually both eyes
  • Often with hay fever
  • Itchy, red, watery eyes
Which doctor
Family doctor or eye doctor (ophthalmologist)
Common in
Children and adults, especially people with hay fever, asthma or eczema
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes with dust, pollen or other triggers

Common signs

More signs

  • A burning or gritty feeling in the eyes

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Contact lenses, lens solutions and some eye make-up or eye drops can set it off in some people
  • It tends to run in families who have allergies, asthma or eczema

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden loss of vision, or vision that suddenly gets worse: go to hospital now
  • Swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A chemical in the eye or an eye injury: rinse with clean running water and go to hospital now
  • Strong eye pain, or a red eye with headache, feeling sick or pain in bright light: go to hospital now
  • Thick yellow or green discharge, or eyelids stuck together: this may be an infection that can spread
  • Red eyes that last more than a few weeks, or red eyes in a baby: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Try not to rub your eyes; rubbing makes the itch and swelling worse
  • Rest a cool, clean, damp cloth over closed eyes for comfort
  • Find and avoid your triggers; keep windows closed on dusty or hazy days and wash bedding often

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
