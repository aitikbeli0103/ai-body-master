Huntington's disease

An inherited brain condition causing jerky movements, mood changes and memory problems

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

Parts of the brain slowly break down over many years. It runs in families and usually starts in adult life.

  • Brain
  • Runs in families
  • Jerky movements, mood change
Which doctor
Brain and nerve doctor (neurologist); genetic counsellor
Common in
Adults, usually showing first signs in middle age
Contagious
No, but it can be passed down in families
Course
Gets worse slowly over years; care and support can help daily life

Common signs

More signs

  • Small, jerky or twitchy movements the person cannot control

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A change in one gene that is passed from parent to child
  • A child of an affected parent may carry the gene

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Choking or coughing a lot when eating, or food going down the wrong way
  • A fall with a head injury or a new fit: go to hospital now
  • Fever, cough and breathlessness, which can be a chest infection
  • Sudden worsening, severe agitation or a carer who cannot cope

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay under a specialist doctor and keep every appointment
  • Ask your doctor about medicines that can ease movements or mood
  • Eat well, stay gently active and ask about speech and swallowing help

Tell your therapist

  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Huntington's disease?

An inherited brain condition causing jerky movements, mood changes and memory problems. Parts of the brain slowly break down over many years. It runs in families and usually starts in adult life.

What are the common signs of Huntington's disease?

Unsteady when walking · Low mood, no interest · Foggy, cannot focus · Hard or painful to swallow

Why does Huntington's disease happen?

Germs (infection) · Runs in the family

How can I look after myself with Huntington's disease?

Make the home safe from falls · Talk to family and friends

Can an AI Body Master session help with Huntington's disease?

A session is gentle, needle-free meridian support for your wellbeing. It does not treat or cure Huntington's disease and never replaces your medicine or your doctor. Tell your therapist about it before the session starts.
