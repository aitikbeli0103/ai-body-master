Allergic shock (anaphylactic shock)

A sudden, severe allergic reaction that is a medical emergency

ConditionAllergiesWhole body

What it is

Allergic shock can happen within minutes of contact with something you are allergic to. Breathing, blood pressure and the skin can all be affected. It needs emergency care at once.

  • Whole body
  • Food, stings, medicines
  • Sudden, within minutes
Which doctor
Emergency: call 999. Later an allergy doctor
Common in
People with a known allergy, but it can be a first-time reaction
Contagious
No
Course
Can get worse very fast; needs emergency care and a doctor's follow-up

Common signs

More signs

  • Swollen lips, tongue, face or throat

Why it happens

  • Foods such as nuts, seafood, eggs or milk
  • Insect stings such as bees or wasps
  • Some medicines, and latex
  • Sometimes the cause is not found

See a doctor first if

  • Call 999 at once for trouble breathing, a swollen throat or tongue, or fainting
  • If you have an emergency injector prescribed by your doctor, use it as taught and still call 999
  • Lie flat with legs raised if dizzy; do not make the person stand up
  • Symptoms can come back hours later, so stay under medical watch
  • A child or older person with these signs: call 999 now

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

  • After an emergency, see a doctor to find out what caused it
  • Avoid the known trigger and read food labels
  • Tell restaurants, family and friends about your allergy
  • Keep any emergency medicine your doctor gave you with you and in date

Questions and answers

What is Allergic shock (anaphylactic shock)?

A sudden, severe allergic reaction that is a medical emergency. Allergic shock can happen within minutes of contact with something you are allergic to. Breathing, blood pressure and the skin can all be affected. It needs emergency care at once.

What are the common signs of Allergic shock (anaphylactic shock)?

Red rash · Wheezing breath · Feeling faint · Vomiting

Why does Allergic shock (anaphylactic shock) happen?

Foods such as nuts, seafood, eggs or milk · Insect stings such as bees or wasps · Some medicines, and latex · Sometimes the cause is not found

How can I look after myself with Allergic shock (anaphylactic shock)?

After an emergency, see a doctor to find out what caused it · Avoid the known trigger and read food labels · Tell restaurants, family and friends about your allergy · Keep any emergency medicine your doctor gave you with you and in date
