Open sores on the feet

Breaks in the skin of the foot that stay open and may weep, bleed or get infected

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legs

What it is

An open sore is a break in the skin of the foot that stays open. Feet carry our weight and can have poor blood flow, so a sore needs careful attention.

  • Feet and ankles
  • Higher risk with diabetes
  • Broken skin, may not hurt
Which doctor
Family doctor first; a skin, foot or vascular specialist if needed
Common in
People with diabetes or poor blood flow; older adults
Contagious
Some types can be, if the sore is infected; wash hands after touching it
Course
Depends on the cause; can take weeks or months, and needs a doctor's advice

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Diabetes, which can dull feeling in the feet and slow the way skin repairs itself
  • Poor blood flow to the legs and feet
  • Pressure or rubbing from tight shoes, or an injury you did not feel
  • Long-standing swollen legs or varicose veins
  • An infection or a skin condition; sometimes the cause is not clear

See a doctor first if

  • Fever, chills or feeling very unwell with a foot sore: go to hospital now
  • Skin turning black, blue or very pale, a cold foot, or severe pain: go to hospital now
  • Redness, warmth, swelling, pus or a bad smell around the sore: see a doctor today
  • Any foot sore if you have diabetes, even if it does not hurt
  • A sore that is deep, getting bigger or not getting better after a few days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rinse gently with clean water and cover with a clean, dry dressing
  • Keep weight and rubbing off the sore; rest and raise your foot when sitting
  • Check both feet every day, using a mirror for the soles
  • Do not cut corns or hard skin yourself or put home remedies on the sore

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
