Supinator syndrome (radial tunnel syndrome)

A nerve in the forearm is squeezed near the elbow, causing forearm ache

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & muscles

What it is

A nerve passes through a muscle called the supinator near the elbow. When it is squeezed, the upper forearm may ache, especially when you twist the arm or use the hand a lot.

  • Forearm, just below the elbow
  • Adults who use their arms a lot
  • Deep ache in the forearm
Which doctor
Family doctor, orthopaedic or hand doctor
Common in
Adults who twist the forearm often, such as in sport or repeated work
Contagious
No
Course
Often eases when the arm is rested, but can last; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • A deep ache in the upper forearm, a little below the elbow
  • Pain that gets worse when twisting the forearm or lifting with the palm down
  • Tenderness when pressing on the upper forearm

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Repeated twisting or forceful use of the forearm
  • Sports or work with a lot of gripping and turning, such as racket sports or using tools
  • A tight muscle or band of tissue pressing on the nerve

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden weakness in the arm with a drooping face or trouble speaking: call 999
  • You cannot straighten your fingers or lift your wrist
  • Pain after a fall or injury to the arm
  • A hot, red, swollen arm, especially with fever
  • Numbness or weakness that keeps getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take breaks during repeated twisting or gripping work
  • Use tools and handles that need less force and less twisting
  • Do gentle forearm stretches as advised by a professional

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Recent shoulder injury or surgery
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
