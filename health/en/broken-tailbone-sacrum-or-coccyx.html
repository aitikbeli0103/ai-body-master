Broken tailbone (sacrum or coccyx)

A crack or break in the bones at the bottom of the spine

ConditionBack & spineBones & muscles

What it is

The sacrum and tailbone sit at the base of the spine. A hard fall onto the buttocks or a blow can crack or break them, causing pain when sitting.

  • Base of the spine
  • After a fall
  • Pain when sitting
Which doctor
Hospital emergency, orthopaedic doctor
Common in
People after a fall; older adults and women with weak bones
Contagious
No
Course
Usually takes weeks to months to settle; a doctor guides how to manage it

Common signs

  • Pain at the base of the spine, worse when sitting
  • Pain when standing up from a chair
  • Tenderness and sometimes bruising over the tailbone
  • Pain during bowel movements

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak bones (osteoporosis), so even a small fall can crack the bone

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot stand or walk after a fall, or severe pain: go to hospital now
  • Numbness around the groin or buttocks, or leg weakness: go to hospital now
  • Trouble passing urine or stools, or leaking of either: go to hospital now
  • Bruising, heavy swelling or bleeding at the base of the spine
  • Pain that is not easing after a few weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Get checked first after any hard fall before trying other care
  • Use a soft cushion with a hole or a ring when sitting, if your doctor agrees
  • Lean forward a little when sitting and change position often
  • Eat enough fibre and drink water to avoid straining on the toilet

Tell your therapist

  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
