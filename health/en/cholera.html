Cholera

A germ infection of the gut from dirty water or food that causes sudden, very watery diarrhoea

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

A germ in unclean water or food causes sudden, heavy watery diarrhoea and sometimes vomiting, and the body can lose dangerous amounts of water and salts within hours.

  • The gut and body fluids
  • Where water is unsafe
  • Heavy watery diarrhoea
Which doctor
Hospital emergency care; infectious disease doctor
Common in
People living in or visiting places with unsafe water or poor sanitation
Contagious
Yes, through water or food made unclean by an infected person's stool
Course
Can turn serious within hours from water loss; fast medical care matters

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Raw or undercooked seafood, and food that has been left out for a long time
  • Poor sanitation, such as no safe toilets or clean water supply
  • Some infected people have only mild signs or none, but can still pass the germ on

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy watery diarrhoea with fainting, a fast heartbeat or cold, clammy skin: call 999 now
  • Dehydration: very dry mouth, sunken eyes, little or no urine, or bad leg cramps: go to hospital now
  • Very sleepy, confused, or too weak to drink: call 999
  • A baby, young child, pregnant woman or older person with watery diarrhoea or vomiting: go to hospital early
  • Watery diarrhoea after being in a place with unsafe water or a cholera outbreak: see a doctor and say so

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Get medical help early if the diarrhoea is heavy and watery; do not wait it out at home
  • On the way, keep taking small sips of oral rehydration drink from a pharmacy, or clean boiled water
  • Drink only boiled or bottled water, and eat food that is well cooked and still hot
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine for diarrhoea
