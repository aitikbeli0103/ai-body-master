Pityriasis alba (pale scaly patches)

Harmless pale, dry patches of skin, most often on the face of children

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Pale, dry, slightly flaky round or oval patches appear on the skin; they are common, usually harmless and not an infection.

  • Often on the face; also arms
  • Children and teenagers
  • Pale, dry patches
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Mostly children and teenagers, especially with eczema or dry skin
Contagious
No
Course
Usually fades by itself over months, sometimes longer; colour often returns

Common signs

More signs

  • Round or oval patches paler than the skin around them
  • Often on the cheeks; also on the neck, shoulders or upper arms
  • Usually little or no itching
  • The patches stand out more after sun, because the skin around them tans

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It is often seen in people with eczema, dry skin or sensitive skin
  • Mild inflammation of the skin seems to make it lose some colour for a while
  • It is not an infection and is not caused by poor hygiene

See a doctor first if

  • A pale patch with less feeling, or numbness or weakness in a hand or foot: see a doctor soon
  • Bright white patches with sharp edges, or patches that keep spreading: ask a doctor to check
  • Patches that become red, very itchy, sore, weepy or crusted
  • Patches that do not fade after many months, or you are not sure what they are
  • A baby with these patches, or a child with fever or who seems unwell: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Use mild soap, lukewarm water and short showers
  • Do not scrub, rub or peel the patches

Tell your therapist
