Vascular headache

Throbbing headache linked to changes in the blood vessels of the head

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

A vascular headache is a pulsing or throbbing pain thought to be linked to changes in the blood vessels around the head. Migraine is the best-known kind.

  • One or both sides of the head
  • Can come in attacks
  • Throbbing pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, neurologist
Common in
Adults of any age; often runs in families
Contagious
No
Course
Attacks usually last hours to a day or two and can come back

Common signs

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • The worst, sudden headache of your life: go to hospital now
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck, or after a head injury: go to hospital now
  • Headache with confusion, fainting, a fit or loss of vision
  • A new headache after age fifty, or headaches that keep getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

Tell your therapist

  • High blood pressure
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

What are the common signs of Vascular headache?

Why does Vascular headache happen?

Stress · Smoke, haze and strong smells · Hormone changes · Drinking alcohol

Drink enough water · Get good sleep · Note down your symptoms · Ask your pharmacist
