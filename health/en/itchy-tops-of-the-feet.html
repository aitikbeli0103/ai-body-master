Itchy tops of the feet

An itch on the upper side of the foot, between the toes and the ankle

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

The skin on the top of the foot feels itchy. Insect bites, rubbing from shoe straps, dry skin and allergies are common reasons, and swollen legs can also make the feet itch.

  • Top of the foot and ankle
  • Often after insect bites
  • Itch, bumps or red patches
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more often where there are mosquitoes, or with swollen legs
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as scabies or a fungal infection
Course
Depends on the cause; bites often settle within days, others may need a doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • Red, dry or scaly patches where straps or shoes rub
  • A round, red, scaly patch with a clearer centre (ringworm)
  • Scratch marks or crusts from scratching

Why it happens

  • Insect bites (mosquitoes, fleas, mites), which are very common on the feet and ankles
  • Rubbing or a reaction to shoe straps, laces, sandals, socks or rubber
  • Dry skin, eczema or a fungal skin infection (ringworm)
  • Swollen legs from poor circulation or standing or sitting a long time
  • Sunburn, contact with plants or grass, or sometimes scabies

See a doctor first if

  • Itching with swelling of the face, lips or throat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • One leg suddenly swollen, red, warm or painful: go to hospital now
  • Spreading redness, warmth, pus, red streaks up the leg or fever (it may be infected)
  • A sore or ulcer on the foot or ankle that does not go away, especially if you have diabetes
  • Itching that lasts more than two weeks, keeps you awake, or starts in others at home too

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not scratch: hold a cool, damp cloth on the itch and keep nails short
  • Use insect repellent, and keep the home free of standing water to reduce mosquitoes
  • Choose soft, well-fitting shoes and sandals, and check that straps and laces do not rub
  • Rest with your feet up if they swell, and walk around often if you sit a long time

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
