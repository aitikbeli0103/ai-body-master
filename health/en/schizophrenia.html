Schizophrenia

A long-term brain condition that can change thinking, feelings and how a person sees what is real

ConditionTold by a doctor: otherWhole body

What it is

A long-term brain condition that can cause hearing voices, strong false beliefs and confused thinking. It is not 'split personality', and it is not anyone's fault.

  • Brain and thinking
  • Often starts in young adulthood
  • Voices and confused thoughts
Which doctor
Psychiatrist (mind doctor); a family doctor can refer you
Common in
Often begins in young adulthood; affects men and women
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; symptoms can come and go, and steady care helps many people stay well

Common signs

  • Hearing voices or seeing things that others do not
  • Strong beliefs that are not true, such as feeling watched or in danger
  • Confused thinking or speech that is hard for others to follow
  • Pulling away from family and friends, or losing interest in daily life
  • Poor self-care, low energy, or feelings that seem flat

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Differences in brain chemicals and in how the brain grows
  • Great stress and hard early-life events may add to the risk
  • Heavy use of cannabis or other drugs may raise the risk in some people

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or others, or voices telling you to: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Sudden severe confusion, extreme fear, or being so agitated that it is not safe: call 999
  • High fever, very stiff muscles or sudden confusion, especially after a medicine change: go to hospital now
  • New or worse voices, or fear that others want to harm you: see a doctor soon
  • Not eating, drinking or sleeping for days, or not able to look after yourself
  • Medicine stopped or missed and symptoms coming back: see the doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Keep seeing your doctor and taking medicine as prescribed; ask before changing anything
  • Keep a calm daily routine with regular sleep and meals
  • Avoid alcohol, cannabis and other drugs; they can make symptoms worse
  • Stay in touch with people who support you; a support group can help
  • Family and friends: stay calm, listen, do not argue about what is real, and help them reach a doctor

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • A heart condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
