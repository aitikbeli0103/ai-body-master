Avulsion fracture (bone pulled off)

A small piece of bone pulled off where a tendon or ligament attaches

ConditionAny other bone or soft-tissue injuryArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

A sudden, strong pull from a muscle or ligament can tear a small chip of bone away from the main bone. It often happens during sport or a fall.

  • Arms, legs, fingers, hip
  • Active teens and adults
  • Sudden sharp pain
Which doctor
Family doctor or bone doctor (orthopaedic); hospital for an X-ray
Common in
Young people in sport, and teens whose bones are still growing
Contagious
No
Course
Often mends over weeks; a doctor decides how long to rest

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden sharp pain at the moment of a twist, kick or fall
  • Pain when you move or use the muscle that attaches there
  • Tenderness when touched, and the area may feel weak

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A sudden, forceful muscle pull, such as sprinting, jumping or kicking
  • A twist or a fall onto a joint
  • Growing bones in teens are weaker where tendons attach

See a doctor first if

  • A bent or out-of-shape limb, or bone showing through the skin: go to hospital now
  • Cannot move the part or put any weight on it
  • Numbness, tingling, or a pale or cold hand, foot or finger
  • Heavy swelling or severe pain that keeps getting worse
  • A child with a painful, swollen limb: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Stop the activity and rest the part
  • Keep it still and raised if you can, and get it checked soon
  • Follow your doctor's advice on a splint or cast and on when to return to sport
  • Warm up and stretch before sport

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
