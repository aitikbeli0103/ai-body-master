Torn Achilles tendon

The strong cord at the back of the ankle tears or snaps

ConditionAnkle & footArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

The Achilles tendon joins the calf muscle to the heel bone and lets you push off when you walk or run. When it tears, there is often a sudden pop and pain at the back of the ankle.

  • Back of the ankle, lower calf
  • Sports, sudden push-off
  • Sudden pop, weak push-off
Which doctor
Emergency department; orthopaedic or sports doctor
Common in
Adults aged about thirty to fifty who play sport now and then; also older adults
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor's plan, with a boot, cast or surgery; recovery usually takes months

Common signs

More signs

  • A sudden pop or a feeling of being kicked at the back of the ankle
  • Sharp pain and swelling at the back of the ankle or lower calf
  • Cannot rise on tiptoe or push off when walking
  • A gap or dent that can be felt above the heel

Why it happens

  • A sudden push-off or jump, such as in badminton, football or running
  • A sudden trip, fall or stepping into a hole
  • A tendon weakened by age, overuse or earlier pain at the back of the heel
  • Some medicines can weaken tendons; ask your doctor or pharmacist

See a doctor first if

  • A sudden pop with pain and you cannot push off or stand on tiptoe: see a doctor promptly
  • A foot that is cold, pale, blue or numb: go to hospital now
  • A swollen, red, painful calf, or sudden shortness of breath or chest pain: call 999
  • An open wound at the back of the ankle, or redness and fever after surgery
  • Pain at the back of the heel that keeps coming back, which may be a warning

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stop the activity, rest, and avoid walking on the foot until you are seen
  • Follow your doctor's plan for the boot, cast, crutches and exercise
  • Warm up well and build up sport slowly once you are cleared to return

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Weak leg or foot

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
