Procrastination

Putting off tasks again and again, even when you know it will cause problems

ConditionFocus & overthinkingWhole body

What it is

A common habit of delaying things you mean to do, often with worry or guilt; it is not a sign of a weak person.

  • Thoughts and daily habits
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Putting off, then stress
Which doctor
Family doctor or a counsellor if it affects work, study or mood
Common in
Anyone, including students and busy workers
Contagious
No
Course
Often a long-time habit; small steps and good routines can help over time

Common signs

  • Leaving tasks until the last minute
  • Starting, then drifting to the phone or other easy things
  • Feeling worried or guilty about what is not done
  • Rushing at the end, missing deadlines or making careless mistakes
  • Feeling tired, stressed or short of sleep because of it

Why it happens

  • Feeling overwhelmed, or not knowing where to start
  • Fear of failing, or wanting everything to be perfect
  • Tiredness, poor sleep or stress
  • Easy distractions such as the phone and social media
  • Sometimes anxiety, low mood or a focus problem such as ADHD is behind it

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Feeling hopeless, very low or empty for weeks, or losing interest in everything
  • Panic, constant worry or not being able to sleep
  • Using alcohol or other substances to cope with the stress
  • Trouble focusing since childhood that keeps harming school or work
  • Delaying that puts your job, studies, health or money at serious risk

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Break a big task into very small steps and start with the first one
  • Start with just a few minutes; getting started is often the hardest part
  • Write a short list and pick one important task for each day
  • Put the phone away and keep a quiet, tidy place to work
  • Sleep and eat regularly, and be kind to yourself rather than harsh

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
