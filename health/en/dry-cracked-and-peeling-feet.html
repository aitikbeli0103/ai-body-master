Dry, cracked and peeling feet

Dry, rough skin on the feet that splits, cracks or peels

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legs

What it is

The skin on the feet loses moisture and gets rough, hard, cracked or peeling. It is common, but sometimes an infection or another condition is behind it.

  • Soles, sides and heels
  • Walking or standing a lot
  • Dry, rough, peeling skin
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Adults and older people; people who stand a lot or wear open shoes
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as a fungal infection of the foot
Course
Often eases with regular foot care; it may return if care stops

Common signs

More signs

  • Cracks or splits that can sting or bleed
  • Pain when walking if the cracks are deep

Why it happens

  • Dry skin and hard skin (callus) building up on the soles and heels
  • Standing or walking a lot, open-back sandals, or shoes that rub
  • A fungal infection of the foot (athlete's foot), often starting between the toes
  • Long hot showers, harsh soaps, or walking barefoot on rough floors
  • Conditions such as diabetes, thyroid problems or eczema, and getting older

See a doctor first if

  • A red, hot, swollen foot with pus, a bad smell, red streaks up the leg or fever: go to hospital now
  • Any crack, sore or blister on the foot if you have diabetes or less feeling in your feet: see a doctor soon
  • Deep cracks that bleed, hurt a lot, or do not get better
  • Wet, white, smelly skin between the toes, or very itchy peeling skin, which may be a fungal infection
  • Dry, cracked feet that are not better after a few weeks of regular care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash your feet every day with lukewarm water and mild soap, then dry well, including between the toes
  • Look at your feet every day, especially if you have diabetes

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Fever or an active infection
