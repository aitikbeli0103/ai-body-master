Pulmonary cryptococcosis (fungal lung infection)

A lung infection caused by a fungus found in soil and bird droppings

ConditionLungs & breathingChest

What it is

A fungus called Cryptococcus can be breathed in and infect the lungs. Some people have no signs; others get cough, chest pain and fever.

  • In the lungs
  • Most serious if immunity is weak
  • Cough, chest pain, fever
Which doctor
Lung doctor (respiratory physician), infection doctor
Common in
People with weak immunity; healthy people can also get it
Contagious
No, it does not spread from person to person
Course
Needs a doctor's care; they can explain what to expect

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Breathing in tiny spores of the fungus from soil
  • Dry bird droppings, especially from pigeons, are a known source
  • HIV infection, cancer treatment or an organ transplant

See a doctor first if

  • Severe headache with stiff neck, confusion or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Severe trouble breathing, or blue lips: call 999
  • Coughing up blood
  • Fever or cough that lasts more than a week or two
  • Chest pain, or losing weight without trying

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Finish any medicine your doctor gives, and never stop it on your own
  • Wear a mask when cleaning bird droppings or digging soil, if your immunity is weak
  • Rest and drink enough fluids
  • Keep all your follow-up appointments

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Pregnancy
