Vertebrobasilar insufficiency

Poor blood flow to the back part of the brain, causing dizziness and balance trouble

ConditionBrain & nervesBlood

What it is

The arteries at the back of the neck and head bring blood to the brain stem and balance centres. When blood flow drops, dizziness, vision change and unsteadiness can follow.

  • Arteries at the back of the neck
  • More common in older adults
  • Dizzy, unsteady
Which doctor
Family doctor, neurologist, heart and vessel doctor
Common in
Older adults, often with high blood pressure, diabetes or high cholesterol
Contagious
No
Course
Spells often come and go, but they can be an early warning; see a doctor

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Hardening or narrowing of the arteries that supply the back of the brain
  • Problems with the neck bones pressing on the arteries in some people
  • Heart rhythm problems or a low blood pressure

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Sudden double vision, loss of vision, or trouble swallowing: call 999
  • Sudden severe dizziness with a bad headache or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Fainting, chest pain or a very fast, uneven heartbeat
  • Dizzy spells that keep coming back, even if they pass quickly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
