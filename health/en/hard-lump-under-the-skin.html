Hard lump under the skin

A firm lump you can feel under the skin, with many possible causes

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A hard lump under the skin is a firm swelling you can feel with your fingers. Many are not dangerous, such as cysts or scar tissue, but a lump that stays or grows should be checked by a doctor.

  • Anywhere under the skin
  • Can happen at any age
  • Firm lump, painful or not
Which doctor
Family doctor; a skin doctor or surgeon if needed
Common in
Anyone at any age; the likely cause depends on the place and the person
Contagious
No, though a boil or abscess can spread germs if it bursts
Course
Depends on the cause: some stay the same for years, others grow, so get it checked

Common signs

More signs

  • It may move a little under your finger, or feel fixed in place
  • It can be painless, or tender and sore when pressed
  • The skin over it may look normal, or be red, warm or darker
  • It may stay the same size, or slowly get bigger or smaller

Why it happens

  • A cyst (a closed sac under the skin, often filled with oily material)
  • A boil or abscess from an infection, or a swollen gland (lymph node) fighting an infection
  • Scar tissue, an old injury or bruise, an insect bite, or a fatty lump (lipoma)
  • A harmless skin growth such as a dermatofibroma, or a wart
  • Less often, a growth that needs checking, including some cancers, which is why a new lump should be seen

See a doctor first if

  • Red, hot, spreading swelling with fever, chills or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • A lump that is growing fast, feels very hard, does not move, or looks uneven in shape
  • A new lump in the breast, testicle or neck, or lumps in several places
  • A lump with weight loss without trying, night sweats, or a fever that keeps coming back
  • A lump that has lasted more than a few weeks, keeps coming back, or bleeds or leaks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Note when you first saw it, its size and any changes; a photo every few weeks helps
  • Keep the skin clean and covered with a clean dressing if it is sore or leaking
  • Do not put herbs, oils or home remedies on it; ask your doctor or pharmacist about any medicine

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
