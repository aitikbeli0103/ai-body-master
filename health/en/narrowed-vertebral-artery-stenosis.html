Narrowed vertebral artery (stenosis)

Narrowing of the artery in the neck that brings blood to the back of the brain

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

The vertebral arteries run up the neck to the back of the brain. When one narrows, less blood gets through, which may cause dizziness or, at worst, a stroke.

  • Neck and back of the brain
  • More common with age
  • Dizzy spells
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a brain and nerve or blood vessel doctor
Common in
Older adults; people with high blood pressure, diabetes or who smoke
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow; a doctor can check the vessels and explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Less often, a neck injury or a tear in the artery

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999 at once
  • Sudden severe dizziness with double vision or trouble walking: go to hospital now
  • Sudden very bad headache or neck pain after a neck injury: go to hospital now
  • Fainting or a short blackout

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Avoid sudden, forceful twisting of the neck
  • Stand up slowly and sit down if you feel dizzy

Tell your therapist

Questions and answers

What is Narrowed vertebral artery (stenosis)?

Narrowing of the artery in the neck that brings blood to the back of the brain. The vertebral arteries run up the neck to the back of the brain. When one narrows, less blood gets through, which may cause dizziness or, at worst, a stroke.

What are the common signs of Narrowed vertebral artery (stenosis)?

Room feels like spinning · Blurry vision · Unsteady when walking · Headache at the back · Feeling sick, nausea

Why does Narrowed vertebral artery (stenosis) happen?

Getting older · High blood sugar or blood pressure · Smoking

How can I look after myself with Narrowed vertebral artery (stenosis)?

Check your blood pressure at home · Stop smoking · Eat less salt
