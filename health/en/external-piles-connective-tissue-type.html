External piles (connective tissue type)

A soft fold or lump of extra skin at the edge of the anus, usually painless

ConditionStomach & intestinesPelvisSkin

What it is

Extra skin and soft tissue forms a small fold or lump at the edge of the anus, often after earlier swelling there; it is usually soft and painless, but it can itch.

  • Edge of the anus
  • Often follows earlier piles
  • Soft lump, little pain
Which doctor
Family doctor; a bowel specialist (colorectal surgeon)
Common in
Adults; more often after earlier piles, straining or childbirth
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stays as a small skin fold; often painless but may itch

Common signs

More signs

  • A soft, small lump or fold of skin at the edge of the anus
  • Usually painless, with little or no bleeding
  • It can be hard to keep the area clean after passing stools
  • A feeling that something is there when you sit or wipe

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It often stays behind after an earlier swollen pile or small tear
  • Long-lasting diarrhoea, or rubbing and irritation of the skin there

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding from the anus, or bleeding with feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • Sudden severe pain with a hard, swollen or bluish lump: see a doctor today
  • A red, hot, swollen area with fever, or a lump that oozes pus
  • Blood in or on the stools, or black stools: it is not always piles, so get it checked
  • A lump that grows fast, bleeds, or changes colour or shape
  • A change in bowel habit lasting weeks, or losing weight without trying

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Eat more vegetables, fruit and whole grains, and drink enough water
  • Do not strain or sit long on the toilet; go when you feel the need
  • Do not cut, burn or pull at the lump yourself

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
