Age-related macular degeneration (AMD)

Damage to the centre of the retina that blurs the middle of your vision

ConditionEyesHead

What it is

The macula is the small centre of the retina that gives sharp detail. With age it can wear down, making reading, faces and straight lines harder to see clearly.

  • Back of the eye (retina)
  • Mostly older adults
  • Blurry or dark centre of sight
Which doctor
Eye doctor (ophthalmologist), retina specialist
Common in
Adults over middle age; smokers; family history
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow, but it can change faster in some people; regular eye checks matter

Common signs

More signs

  • Straight lines look wavy or bent
  • Hard to read or to recognise faces
  • A dark or empty spot in the middle of vision

Why it happens

Other causes

  • High blood pressure, heart disease and being overweight may add to the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden loss of vision in one eye: go to hospital now
  • Straight lines suddenly look wavy or bent, or a new dark spot appears
  • Flashes of light or many new floating spots
  • Vision getting worse over days or weeks
  • Trouble reading, driving or recognising faces

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have regular eye checks; ask your eye doctor how often
  • Use bright light and a magnifier for reading if it helps
