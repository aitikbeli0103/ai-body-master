Skin tag (soft fibroma)

A small, soft flap of skin that hangs off the surface, usually harmless and often in skin folds

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Skin tags are small, soft, skin-coloured or slightly darker flaps of skin, usually on a thin stalk, that grow where skin rubs on skin; they are common and cannot be passed to others.

  • Skin folds: neck, armpits
  • Common in adults
  • Soft flap, usually painless
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Many adults; more with age, extra weight, diabetes or pregnancy
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stay in place, and more may appear over time

Common signs

  • Small, soft flaps of skin, skin-coloured or slightly darker
  • Often hang from a thin stalk, or look like a small soft bump
  • Often in skin folds: neck, armpits, groin, eyelids or under the breasts
  • Usually painless, but can catch on clothes, jewellery or a razor
  • There may be one or several, and they can slowly grow in number

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Skin rubbing on skin or on clothes or jewellery

See a doctor first if

  • Bleeding that does not stop after firm pressure for several minutes: go to hospital now
  • A tag that turns dark purple or black, or becomes very painful: see a doctor soon
  • A growth that changes fast, bleeds, has uneven edges or many colours, or looks unlike your others: see a doctor
  • Red, hot, swollen or oozing skin around it: see a doctor
  • Many new tags, or being very thirsty, tired and passing urine often: ask your doctor to check your blood sugar

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Leave it alone; do not cut, tie off, burn or pull it at home, as bleeding, infection and scars can follow
  • If it rubs or bothers you, ask a doctor about safe ways to remove it
  • Keep skin folds clean and dry, and wear loose, soft clothes
  • If there are many, ask your doctor about a blood sugar check

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
