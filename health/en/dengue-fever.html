Dengue fever

A virus infection spread by Aedes mosquito bites that causes sudden high fever, headache and body aches

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

A virus carried by Aedes mosquitoes causes sudden high fever with headache, pain behind the eyes and aching muscles and joints, and it can turn serious around the time the fever goes down.

  • Whole body
  • Spread by mosquito bites
  • High fever and body aches
Which doctor
Family doctor or clinic; hospital if there are warning signs
Common in
Anyone of any age living in or visiting warm countries such as Malaysia
Contagious
No, not by touch or air; it spreads through bites of infected Aedes mosquitoes
Course
Fever lasts several days; the risky time is often when the fever goes down

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Aedes mosquitoes breed in clean, still water in pots, tyres, drains and containers
  • These mosquitoes bite mostly in the daytime, often at home or near it
  • Dengue can be caught more than once, and a later infection can be more serious

See a doctor first if

  • Severe belly pain, constant vomiting, or bleeding from the nose, gums, vomit or stool: go to hospital now
  • Cold, clammy skin, fast breathing, great weakness, dizziness or fainting: call 999
  • Very sleepy, restless or confused, or hard to wake: call 999
  • Little or no urine, or too unwell to drink: go to hospital
  • Sudden high fever in a baby, child, pregnant woman or older person: see a doctor the same day
  • Fever lasting more than two days, or fever with a rash, in a place with dengue: see a doctor for a blood test

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • While ill, rest under a mosquito net or in a screened room so mosquitoes cannot spread it to others
  • Empty standing water around the home every week and use mosquito repellent

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
