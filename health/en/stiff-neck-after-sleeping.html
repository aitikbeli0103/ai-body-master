Stiff neck after sleeping

Sudden one-sided neck pain and stiffness on waking, also called wry neck (acute torticollis)

Medical details

What happens in the neck

A stiff neck after sleeping (wry neck, or acute torticollis) is a sudden spasm of the muscles on one side of the neck. It most often involves the levator scapulae, which runs from the top of the neck to the shoulder blade, and the upper trapezius. Sometimes a small joint between two neck bones (a facet joint) is also strained. The muscle tightens to protect the neck, so the head is held tilted to one side and turning it hurts and feels blocked. It usually starts after a night in an awkward position, a cold draught on the neck or a sudden twist, and most people notice it first when they wake up. It is common, it is not dangerous in itself, and it is a different problem from long-term wear and tear of the neck.

Common patterns

  • After sleep: an awkward position, a pillow that does not suit you, or cold air on the neck during the night
  • After a movement: a sudden turn of the head, a heavy day of carrying, or a long time with the neck bent over a phone or laptop
  • Comes back again and again: repeated stiff necks can mean the neck needs a check for an underlying problem, such as wear and tear or a disc problem

Outlook

Most stiff necks ease within 2 to 7 days, even without treatment, and many are much better after a day or two. Do not force the neck or try to crack it, because that can make the spasm worse. Gentle movement within what feels comfortable helps more than keeping the neck still. If it is not improving after a week or two, or it keeps coming back, see a doctor.

Common signs

Medical details

Symptoms in more detail

  • Pain on one side of the neck: a sharp or pulling pain, often from the base of the skull or the side of the neck down to the top of the shoulder, usually noticed on waking
  • Stiffness: the head is held tilted or turned to one side, and turning it the other way is limited and painful
  • A tight muscle: the muscle on the sore side feels hard and tender, sometimes with a knot
  • Spreading ache: a dull ache into the shoulder, the upper back or the back of the head, sometimes a mild headache
  • Not typical: numbness, tingling or weakness in the arm or hand, fever, or a severe headache need a doctor's check

Why it happens

Medical details

Causes and risk factors

  • Sleeping position: sleeping sitting up on a sofa, in a car or on a plane, or on your stomach with the head turned, keeps the neck twisted for hours
  • Pillow: one that is too high, too low or too firm bends the neck sideways or forward
  • Cold air: an air-conditioner or fan blowing on a bare neck and shoulders can make the muscles tighten
  • A sudden movement: a quick turn of the head, a jerk, or a strain while carrying something heavy
  • Tired muscles: a long, tiring day, a poor night's sleep, stress, or long hours bent over a screen make the neck muscles more likely to spasm
  • Risk factors: desk work, an earlier neck injury or wear and tear of the neck, and having had a stiff neck before

See a doctor first if

  • Neck pain after a fall, accident or sports injury
  • A very stiff neck with fever, severe headache or a rash: go to hospital now
  • New weakness, numbness or tingling in an arm or leg
  • Trouble swallowing or breathing, or severe dizziness
  • Pain with chest tightness, sweating or breathlessness: call 999
  • Not easing after a week or two, or keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Medical details

How doctors check it

Changes on scans are common in people without symptoms, so results are always read together with your symptoms and examination.

  • History and examination: how it started, your sleep and work habits, then neck movement, tender muscles, arm strength, reflexes and feeling
  • X-ray or MRI: usually not needed for a typical stiff neck; used after an injury, with warning signs or nerve symptoms, or when the problem does not settle or keeps returning

Problems that can feel similar

Wear and tear of the neck (cervical spondylosis), a muscle strain, a shoulder problem and, rarely, an infection or a heart problem can feel like a stiff neck, so a doctor checks to tell them apart.

Pain with chest tightness, sweating or breathlessness can be a heart problem: call emergency services (999).

Looking after yourself

Medical details

Daily care in more detail

  • Gentle warmth: a warm towel or warm pack on the sore side for a short time can ease the tight muscle; check that it is comfortably warm, not hot
  • Keep moving gently: turn and tilt the head slowly within a comfortable range several times a day, and take short walks; avoid forcing, jerking or cracking the neck
  • A pillow that suits you: one that keeps the neck in a straight line with the spine when you lie on your back or on your side; not too high, not too low, not too firm
  • Sleep position: sleeping on your back or side is easier on the neck than on your stomach; avoid sleeping sitting up on a sofa or in a car without neck support
  • Keep the neck covered: do not let an air-conditioner or fan blow straight on your neck and shoulders; a light scarf or a shirt with a collar helps
  • Pain relief: ask your doctor or pharmacist what is safe for you; do not start new medicine on your own

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Dizzy when turning the head
