Sudden swelling under the skin (angioedema)

Sudden swelling under the skin, often of the lips, eyelids, face, hands or feet

ConditionAllergiesSkin

What it is

Angioedema is sudden swelling under the skin, most often of the lips, eyelids, face, hands or feet. It can come with hives. Swelling of the tongue or throat is an emergency.

  • Lips, eyelids, face, hands
  • Allergy, medicine or inherited
  • Sudden, tight or puffy swelling
Which doctor
Family doctor; allergy doctor to find the cause; hospital if breathing is affected
Common in
Anyone; more likely with allergies, some medicines or a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Swelling usually goes down in a day or a few days, but it can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden swelling of the lips, eyelids, face, hands, feet or private parts
  • The skin can feel tight or warm, and it may not itch
  • The swelling can build up over minutes to hours and may last a day or more
  • Itchy hives may appear at the same time

Why it happens

Other causes

  • An allergic reaction to a food, insect sting, latex or medicine
  • Some medicines, such as certain blood pressure or pain medicines, even without an allergy
  • Infection, stress or a small injury can set off swelling in some people
  • Often the cause is not found

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the tongue, mouth or throat, a tight throat, noisy breathing, or trouble swallowing: call 999
  • Trouble breathing, wheezing, or feeling faint or dizzy: call 999
  • Swelling that starts fast after a sting, a food or a new medicine: go to hospital now
  • Strong belly pain with vomiting or diarrhoea together with swelling: go to hospital
  • A baby or child with sudden swelling of the face or lips: see a doctor or go to hospital promptly
  • Swelling that keeps coming back, has no clear reason, or runs in the family: see a doctor to find out why

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay away from foods, stings or products you already know cause a reaction
  • If a doctor gave you an emergency plan for severe allergy, keep it with you and follow it
  • Tell family and friends the warning signs, and that they should call 999

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • High blood pressure
