Adnexitis (inflamed ovary and tube)

Infection and swelling of the ovary and fallopian tube, usually from germs that travel up from the vagina

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

Adnexitis means the ovary and fallopian tube are inflamed, most often by an infection. It is part of pelvic inflammatory disease and needs a doctor's care.

  • Ovary and fallopian tube
  • Women of child-bearing age
  • Lower belly pain or fever
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's doctor), family doctor
Common in
Women of child-bearing age; also after childbirth, miscarriage or womb procedures
Contagious
Some types can be, when the germs pass between partners during sex
Course
Needs a doctor's care; if care is late, scarring of the tubes can follow

Common signs

More signs

  • Sometimes only mild signs, so it can go unnoticed

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Infections passed on during sex, such as chlamydia or gonorrhoea, are common causes
  • Childbirth, miscarriage, abortion or a procedure in the womb can let germs in
  • A new partner, a partner with an infection, or sex without a condom

See a doctor first if

  • Severe lower belly pain with a high fever, vomiting or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant or might be pregnant, with pain or bleeding: go to hospital now
  • Pain or fever after childbirth, a miscarriage, an abortion or a womb procedure: see a doctor the same day
  • Unusual discharge, bleeding between periods or pain during sex: see a doctor soon
  • Signs that do not get better, or come back, after seeing a doctor
  • Pelvic pain that lasts for weeks, or trouble getting pregnant

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor early; do not wait for it to go away
  • Take any medicine your doctor gives exactly as told
  • Ask your doctor if your partner needs checking; condoms can lower the chance of infection
  • Do not use vaginal douches or strong washes inside the vagina

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Fever or an active infection
