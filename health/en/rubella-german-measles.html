Rubella (German measles)

A mild viral infection with a light rash and swollen glands, but risky for the baby in pregnancy

ConditionInfectionsSkin

What it is

A virus causes a pink rash that starts on the face, a mild fever and swollen glands behind the ears. It is usually mild, but it can seriously harm an unborn baby if a pregnant woman catches it.

  • Skin, starting on the face
  • Take care in pregnancy
  • Light rash, mild fever
Which doctor
Family doctor (call first); see your doctor urgently if pregnant
Common in
People who are not vaccinated; children, teenagers and young adults
Contagious
Yes, spreads through coughs, sneezes and close contact, even before the rash
Course
Usually mild and over in a week or two; joint aches can last a little longer

Common signs

Why it happens

  • It is caused by the rubella virus
  • It spreads in droplets from coughs and sneezes, and by close contact
  • Not having the MMR vaccine, or not completing the vaccine schedule, raises the risk
  • A mother who catches it in pregnancy can pass it to her unborn baby
  • Crowded places such as schools, colleges and hostels help it spread

See a doctor first if

  • Severe headache, stiff neck, confusion, strong sleepiness or fits: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, or a baby or child who is very unwell or hard to wake: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant, or may be pregnant, with a rash or after contact with rubella: see your doctor urgently
  • Bleeding or bruising easily, nosebleeds, or blood in the urine or stool: see a doctor today
  • A high fever that does not settle: see a doctor today

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Rest at home and drink plenty of fluids
  • Stay home until your doctor says it is safe, and keep away from pregnant women and babies
  • Wash hands often, cover coughs and sneezes, and do not share cups
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist about medicine for fever and aches
  • Ask your doctor about the MMR vaccine, especially before planning a pregnancy
