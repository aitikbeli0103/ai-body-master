Epicanthal fold

A small fold of skin at the inner corner of the eye that covers part of it

ConditionEyesHead

What it is

A fold of skin runs from the upper eyelid down over the inner corner of the eye. It is a normal feature in many people and is usually not a health problem.

  • Inner corner of the eye
  • Seen from birth
  • Usually painless
Which doctor
Family doctor; eye doctor if you have vision concerns
Common in
Common in many East Asian families; also seen in babies and children
Contagious
No
Course
Often stays the same; in children it may become less obvious as the face grows

Common signs

  • A skin fold covering the inner corner of the eye
  • The eyes may look wider apart
  • Usually on both sides
  • Normally no pain, redness or change in vision

Why it happens

  • It is usually passed down in families and is part of normal eye shape
  • Common in babies because the nose bridge is still flat
  • Sometimes it comes with other conditions seen from birth
  • It is not caused by anything the parents did

See a doctor first if

  • Eye pain, redness or discharge: see a doctor
  • A child whose eyes look crossed or turned
  • Vision that seems poorer, or the child avoids looking at things
  • Droopy eyelids or other unusual features of the face in a baby: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • No care is needed when there are no problems
  • Keep the eye area clean and avoid rubbing it
  • Mention it at routine eye or child health checks
  • Ask your doctor if you are worried about how it looks

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
