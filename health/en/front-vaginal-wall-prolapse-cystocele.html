Front vaginal wall prolapse (cystocele)

The bladder sags and bulges into the front wall of the vagina because the support muscles have weakened

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

The muscles that hold up the bladder weaken, so it sags into the front wall of the vagina and can cause a bulge, a heavy feeling or urine leaks.

  • Bladder and vagina
  • After childbirth or menopause
  • Heavy feeling, a bulge
Which doctor
Gynaecologist or urologist; family doctor first
Common in
Women after childbirth or menopause; also with long-term straining
Contagious
No
Course
May stay mild or slowly get worse; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • A soft bulge or lump felt at or inside the vaginal opening

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot pass urine, or a painful, swollen lower belly: go to hospital now
  • A bulge that is very painful, bleeding or turning dark: go to hospital now
  • Fever or chills with back or side pain, or burning when passing urine: see a doctor today
  • Blood in the urine, or bladder infections that keep coming back
  • A bulge or heavy feeling that is getting worse or stopping you from daily life

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Pelvic floor exercises may help; ask a doctor or physiotherapist to show you how
  • Keep your weight in a normal range, and ask a doctor about a cough that will not go away
  • Take your time on the toilet so the bladder can empty well

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Cancer or cancer treatment

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Front vaginal wall prolapse (cystocele)?

The bladder sags and bulges into the front wall of the vagina because the support muscles have weakened. The muscles that hold up the bladder weaken, so it sags into the front wall of the vagina and can cause a bulge, a heavy feeling or urine leaks.

What are the common signs of Front vaginal wall prolapse (cystocele)?

Dragging ache in the lower belly · Leaking when coughing · Needing the toilet often · Pain during or after sex

Why does Front vaginal wall prolapse (cystocele) happen?

Pregnancy · Hormone changes · Straining on the toilet · Extra body weight · Getting older

How can I look after myself with Front vaginal wall prolapse (cystocele)?

Drink enough water · Avoid heavy lifting
