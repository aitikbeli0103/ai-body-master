Cherry angioma (red mole)

A small, round, bright red spot on the skin, often called a red mole

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A red mole is a small, bright red spot on the skin, most often a cherry angioma (a bunch of tiny blood vessels), but any spot that changes should be checked.

  • A red dot on the skin
  • Can show up from adulthood
  • Smooth, round, painless
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Adults, more often with age; may run in families
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stays and causes no trouble; more spots may appear slowly over time

Common signs

  • More red spots appearing slowly over the years
  • Bleeding if the spot is scratched, rubbed or cut
  • The spot catching on clothes, a watch strap or a razor
  • Colour that deepens from bright red to purplish
  • Usually no pain or itching

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Most often a cherry angioma, a bunch of tiny blood vessels in the skin that is usually harmless
  • Sometimes a different red spot, such as a small spider-like cluster of vessels, a pimple or an insect bite
  • Rarely, a red spot that changes could be something else, such as a skin cancer, so have changing spots checked

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding that does not stop with firm pressure: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • A red spot that grows fast, changes shape, or turns dark brown or black: see a doctor soon
  • A spot that is painful, itchy, crusty, has an uneven edge or has several colours: see a doctor
  • A sudden crop of many new red spots: see a doctor to check
  • A red spot or lump on a baby or child: see a doctor promptly
  • A spot that keeps bleeding or does not look like the others: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Most need no care; check your skin now and then for any change in size, shape or colour
  • If it bleeds, press firmly with a clean cloth for about ten minutes
  • If it bothers you, ask a doctor about safe ways to remove it; never cut, burn or dig at it yourself

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
