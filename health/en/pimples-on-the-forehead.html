Pimples on the forehead

Small bumps, whiteheads or red pimples across the forehead and along the hairline

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsHead

What it is

Pimples on the forehead form when pores near the brow and hairline get blocked with oil and dead skin. Hair products, hats and sweat often play a part.

  • Forehead and hairline
  • Teens and adults
  • Small bumps, sometimes sore
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Teens and adults; people who use oily hair products or wear hats and helmets
Contagious
No
Course
Often settles over weeks if the triggers are reduced, but it can come back

Why care for acne early

Most severe acne started as mild acne that was left alone. Marks and scars take longer to fade, so start caring early.

Ways that make acne worse

  • Squeezing pimples: it pushes germs deeper
  • Using steroid creams for a long time: acne comes back when you stop
  • Strong acid peels at home: skin becomes sensitive
  • Gua sha or scrubbing over pimples: it spreads the inflammation

More signs

  • Skin that feels rough or bumpy to the touch
  • Skin that looks shiny or feels oily
  • A dark or red mark may stay for a while after a bump goes

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The forehead is an oily area, so pores block easily
  • Hair oils, pomades, gels or other hair products that touch the forehead
  • Hats, headbands, helmets or a fringe or bangs that rub or trap sweat
  • Heavy make-up or sunscreen that blocks the pores

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, or trouble breathing after a new product: call 999
  • A puffy, swollen, tender forehead with fever or a bad headache: go to hospital now
  • Redness and swelling that spread quickly, with heat, pain or fever
  • Deep, painful lumps or pus that keep coming back or leave scars
  • A sore or bump that stays for weeks, bleeds, crusts or does not close
  • No improvement after several weeks of gentle care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash your forehead and hairline gently with a mild cleanser, especially after sweating
  • Choose water-based, oil-free hair products and keep them off the forehead
  • Wash hats, headbands and helmet pads often, and gently wipe sweat away with a clean cloth
  • If it does not settle, ask a pharmacist or doctor about skin products
