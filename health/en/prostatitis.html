Prostatitis

Swelling and soreness of the prostate gland, with or without an infection

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

The prostate, a small gland below the bladder, becomes swollen and sore. It can cause pain in the pelvis, pain when passing urine and, at times, fever.

  • Prostate, below the bladder
  • Men of any adult age
  • Pelvic pain, painful urination
Which doctor
Urologist, family doctor
Common in
Adult men of any age; more likely after urine infections or a urine tube (catheter)
Contagious
Usually no; some germs behind it can be passed on by sex
Course
Sudden infections need a doctor quickly; long-lasting types can come and go for months

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Germs (bacteria) getting into the prostate, often from the urine
  • Some infections that are passed on by sex
  • A urine tube (catheter) or a recent test or procedure on the bladder or prostate
  • Often no germ is found and the cause is not clear

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot pass urine, or a very painful, swollen lower belly: go to hospital now
  • Fever with shaking chills, confusion, fast breathing or fainting: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fever or chills with pain in the lower belly, groin or back and burning when passing urine: see a doctor today
  • Blood in the urine or semen
  • Symptoms that last for weeks, or come back after feeling better

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor to find the cause, and ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine
  • A warm bath may ease the pelvic ache
  • Avoid sitting for long; a soft cushion may help

What are the common signs of Prostatitis?
