Itching all over the body

An itch felt over most or all of the skin, not just one spot

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Itching all over is the urge to scratch across a large part of the body. It is common and often comes from dry skin, but sometimes it points to an illness inside the body.

  • Skin all over the body
  • Common in older adults
  • Itchy, worse at night
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone; more often older adults and people with dry skin
Contagious
Some causes can spread, such as scabies
Course
Depends on the cause; dry-skin itch often eases with skin care

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Skin problems such as eczema, hives, or reactions to soap, plants or food
  • Scabies or other tiny skin pests, and insect bites
  • Some illnesses, such as liver, kidney, thyroid or blood problems, and diabetes

See a doctor first if

  • Itching with swelling of the lips, tongue or face, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools
  • Itching with weight loss, night sweats, fever or lumps
  • Itching that lasts more than a couple of weeks or stops you sleeping
  • Itching with a rash that spreads, blisters, or signs of infection
  • Itching in pregnancy, or several people at home itching at the same time: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

What is Itching all over the body?

An itch felt over most or all of the skin, not just one spot. Itching all over is the urge to scratch across a large part of the body. It is common and often comes from dry skin, but sometimes it points to an illness inside the body.

What are the common signs of Itching all over the body?

Why does Itching all over the body happen?

Getting older · A side effect of some medicines

How can I look after myself with Itching all over the body?

Keep the skin moisturised · Wear loose cotton clothes · Try not to scratch; keep nails short · Ask your pharmacist
