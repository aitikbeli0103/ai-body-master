Bacterial dysentery (shigellosis)

A bowel infection from Shigella bacteria that causes diarrhoea, often with blood, plus fever and belly cramps

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Shigella bacteria infect the large bowel and cause diarrhoea with belly cramps and fever. The stool may contain blood or mucus. It spreads very easily and can cause dehydration.

  • Bowel and belly
  • Caused by Shigella bacteria
  • Diarrhoea, often with blood
Which doctor
Family doctor, or a hospital doctor if very ill
Common in
Young children, crowded places, and areas with limited clean water
Contagious
Yes, through food, water and dirty hands; it spreads very easily
Course
Often settles in about a week, but can be severe in young children and older people

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Touching dirty surfaces, then the mouth, or not washing hands after the toilet or changing nappies
  • Swallowing water from a pool, lake or river that is not clean

See a doctor first if

  • Severe dehydration (very dry mouth, no urine for many hours, sunken eyes, fainting): go to hospital now
  • A baby or child who has a fit, is very sleepy, will not drink, or has bloody diarrhoea: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain, a hard swollen belly, or vomiting so much that you cannot keep fluids down: go to hospital
  • Blood or mucus in the stool, black stool, or a high fever: see a doctor the same day
  • Diarrhoea that is not getting better after a few days, or comes back: see a doctor
  • Older people, pregnant women, or people with diabetes or a weak immune system: see a doctor early

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Drink fluids often in small sips; ask a doctor or pharmacist about rehydration drinks
  • Do not take medicine to stop the diarrhoea unless a doctor or pharmacist says it is safe
  • Stay home and do not cook for others until the diarrhoea has stopped and a doctor says it is fine

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A kidney or liver condition
