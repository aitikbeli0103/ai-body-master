Allergic rhinitis (hay fever)

Sneezing, a runny or blocked nose and itchy eyes when the nose reacts to dust, pollen or pet fur

ConditionAllergiesHead

What it is

The lining of the nose reacts to things in the air such as dust mites, pollen, mould or pet fur, so the nose itches, runs or blocks and you may sneeze a lot.

  • Nose, eyes and throat
  • Year-round or seasonal
  • Sneezing and a runny nose
Which doctor
Family doctor; ear-nose-throat (ENT) or allergy specialist if needed
Common in
Children and adults; often with asthma, eczema or allergies in the family
Contagious
No
Course
Often long-term; symptoms come and go with triggers such as dust or pollen

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Dusty bedding, soft toys, carpets and damp, mouldy corners can hold allergens
  • Why one person gets it and another does not is not fully known

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the lips, tongue, face or throat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Wheezing, a tight chest or shortness of breath: see a doctor soon; if it is severe, call 999
  • Fever, thick yellow-green mucus, or face pain that lasts more than a week: it may be a sinus infection
  • A nosebleed that does not stop: go to hospital now
  • A nose that is blocked on one side only for weeks, or keeps bleeding
  • Symptoms that spoil sleep, school or work, or a child with ear pain, trouble hearing or loud snoring

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Find your triggers and keep away from them as much as you can
  • Wash bedding often, air the room, and keep soft toys and carpets clean and dry
  • Keep pets out of the bedroom and have air-con filters cleaned regularly
  • On hazy days, keep windows closed and wear a mask outside

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Fever or an active infection
