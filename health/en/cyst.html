Cyst

A closed sac in the body, often filled with fluid, that is usually harmless

ConditionWhole body & generalChestAbdomenLower backReproductive

What it is

A cyst is a small closed pocket that can form in many parts of the body. Most are soft, slow-growing and harmless, but a doctor should check any new lump.

  • Can form in many places
  • Any age, adults more often
  • A soft, round lump
Which doctor
Family doctor; a specialist for the body part
Common in
Adults of any age; women for ovary or breast cysts
Contagious
No
Course
Many stay the same or go away; a doctor can advise on checks

Common signs

More signs

  • Often painless
  • A dull ache or feeling of pressure if it is large
  • Redness, warmth or pain if it becomes sore or infected

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A blocked gland or duct that fills with fluid

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe belly or pelvic pain, fainting or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • A lump that grows fast, feels hard or does not move
  • Redness, heat, pus or fever with a lump
  • A new breast lump, abnormal bleeding or weight loss you cannot explain
  • Pregnant and having belly pain or bleeding: see a doctor now

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Tell your doctor if it changes in size or feel

Questions and answers

What is Cyst?

A closed sac in the body, often filled with fluid, that is usually harmless. A cyst is a small closed pocket that can form in many parts of the body. Most are soft, slow-growing and harmless, but a doctor should check any new lump.

What are the common signs of Cyst?

A lump under the skin

Why does Cyst happen?

The cause is not fully known · Hormone changes · An injury or fall

How can I look after myself with Cyst?

Check for new lumps and changes · Do not squeeze or pick at it · Keep your regular tests
