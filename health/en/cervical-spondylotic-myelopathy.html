Cervical spondylotic myelopathy

Neck bone changes that press on the spinal cord and can affect the hands, legs and walking

ConditionBones, joints & spineNeckBones & muscles

What it is

Wear in the neck bones narrows the canal and presses on the spinal cord itself, which can make the hands clumsy and the legs stiff or unsteady.

  • Neck part of the spine
  • Mostly older adults
  • Clumsy hands, stiff legs
Which doctor
Spine or bone doctor (orthopaedic), spine surgeon, nerve doctor
Common in
Older adults with long-standing neck wear and tear
Contagious
No
Course
Can stay the same for a long time or slowly worsen; it needs regular check-ups

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Bone spurs or a bulging disc pressing on the spinal cord

See a doctor first if

  • New trouble holding your urine or stools: go to hospital now
  • Sudden weakness or numbness in the arms or legs, or being unable to walk: go to hospital now
  • A fall or neck injury followed by new weakness or numbness: go to hospital now
  • Walking that is getting more unsteady, or frequent falls
  • Hands getting clumsier, trouble with buttons or writing

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Cervical spondylotic myelopathy?

Neck bone changes that press on the spinal cord and can affect the hands, legs and walking. Wear in the neck bones narrows the canal and presses on the spinal cord itself, which can make the hands clumsy and the legs stiff or unsteady.

What are the common signs of Cervical spondylotic myelopathy?

Weak, clumsy grip · Tingling in the hand · Unsteady when walking · Legs feel weak · Stiff, aching neck

Why does Cervical spondylotic myelopathy happen?

Getting older · Present from birth · An injury or fall

How can I look after myself with Cervical spondylotic myelopathy?

See your doctor for check-ups · Avoid heavy lifting · Make the home safe from falls
