Melanoma (skin cancer)

A serious type of skin cancer that starts in the colour-making cells of the skin

ConditionCancer & tumoursSkin

What it is

Colour-making cells in the skin grow out of control, often showing as a new or changing dark spot or mole.

  • Skin, anywhere on the body
  • Can happen at any age
  • New or changing mole or spot
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist) first; then a cancer specialist
Common in
Adults; more often with sunburn, many moles or a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Finding it early matters; your doctors can explain what to expect

Common signs

  • A new dark spot, or a mole that changes in size, shape or colour
  • A mole with an uneven edge, uneven colours, or one half not matching the other
  • A spot that itches, bleeds, turns crusty or does not go away
  • A dark streak under a nail, or a dark spot on the palm or sole of the foot
  • A spot that looks different from your other moles

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Having many moles, or moles of an unusual shape
  • Melanoma in a close family member, or earlier in your own life
  • Sometimes the cause is not known; it can also appear on skin that rarely sees the sun

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden weakness on one side, a fit (seizure), severe headache with confusion, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A mole or spot that changes, grows, itches, bleeds or becomes sore: see a doctor soon
  • A dark streak under a nail, or a new dark spot on the palm or sole
  • A new lump under the skin, or hard swollen glands in the neck, armpit or groin
  • Weight loss without trying, constant tiredness, or a new long-lasting cough or headache
  • If you are having cancer treatment and get a fever, chills or severe pain: call your care team or go to hospital

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Check your skin often, and ask someone to look at places you cannot see
  • Do not use tanning beds, and take photos of your moles so you can notice changes
  • Ask your care team about pain, tiredness, food and emotional support

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

A serious type of skin cancer that starts in the colour-making cells of the skin. Colour-making cells in the skin grow out of control, often showing as a new or changing dark spot or mole.

Why does Melanoma (skin cancer) happen?
