Gum inflammation (gingivitis)

Mild redness and swelling of the gums, usually from plaque on the teeth

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

The gums around the teeth turn red, puffy and sometimes bleed when you brush; it is an early, mild stage of gum disease, and careful daily cleaning can help.

  • Gums around the teeth
  • Can happen at any age
  • Red, puffy, bleeding gums
Which doctor
Dentist (check-up and a proper clean)
Common in
Very common; any age, more with poor cleaning, smoking or pregnancy
Contagious
No
Course
Often settles in days to weeks with good cleaning; it can come back

Common signs

  • Gums that look red, shiny or puffy
  • Bleeding when you brush or floss
  • Bad breath that does not go away
  • Tender gums, but usually little or no pain
  • A soft, sticky film (plaque) on the teeth near the gum line

Why it happens

  • Plaque, a sticky film of germs, left on the teeth near the gums
  • Not brushing or cleaning between the teeth often or well enough
  • Smoking or chewing tobacco
  • Hormone changes, for example in puberty or pregnancy
  • Diabetes, a dry mouth, or some medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen face or neck, or trouble swallowing or breathing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Very painful, bleeding gums with grey-coated sores, a foul smell and fever: see a dentist the same day
  • Gums that bleed a lot, bleed without brushing, or will not stop bleeding
  • Bleeding gums with easy bruising, nosebleeds or constant tiredness: see a doctor
  • Gums that are still red and sore after weeks of careful cleaning: see a dentist

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Brush gently twice a day with a soft brush and fluoride toothpaste
  • Clean between the teeth every day; a little bleeding at first is common and often lessens
  • See a dentist for a check-up and a proper clean to remove hard tartar
  • Cut down on sugary snacks and drinks, and drink plenty of water
  • Stop smoking; it makes gum problems worse

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
