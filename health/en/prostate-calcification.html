Prostate calcification

Tiny hard calcium spots in the prostate, usually found by chance on a scan

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

Small hard deposits of calcium can form inside the prostate. They are common, often cause no trouble, and are mostly found by chance on a scan done for another reason.

  • Prostate gland
  • More common with age
  • Found by chance on a scan
Which doctor
Family doctor or urologist (urine and male health doctor)
Common in
Men, more often with age; usually found by chance on a scan
Contagious
No
Course
The spots usually stay, and many men have no problems from them

Common signs

More signs

  • Any signs usually come from a related problem, such as prostate inflammation or an enlarged prostate
  • Pain when ejaculating, or blood in the semen

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term inflammation or a past infection of the prostate (prostatitis)

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot pass urine, or only a few drops come out with pain: go to hospital now
  • Fever with shaking chills and burning urine, or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • Blood in the urine or semen: see a doctor promptly
  • A weak or slow flow, needing the toilet very often, or waking many times at night to pass urine
  • New back or bone pain that will not go away, or weight loss you cannot explain
  • Pain in the lower belly, groin or between the legs that keeps getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Let your doctor look at the whole picture; the calcium spots on their own often need no action

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Related conditions

  • Epididymal tuberculosis (TB)
  • Epididymitis
  • Inflamed seminal vesicles (vesiculitis)
  • Varicocele
  • Semen that does not liquefy
  • Acute epididymitis (inflamed tube behind testicle)
  • Abnormally shaped sperm (teratozoospermia)
  • Acute prostate infection (bacterial prostatitis)
  • Absent periods (amenorrhoea)
  • Difficulty getting pregnant (infertility)
  • Bladder irritation symptoms
  • PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome)
