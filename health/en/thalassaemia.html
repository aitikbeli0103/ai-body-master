Thalassaemia

An inherited blood condition in which the body makes less healthy haemoglobin

ConditionBloodBlood

What it is

Haemoglobin carries oxygen in red blood cells. In thalassaemia the body makes less of it, so red cells are fewer or break down early, and a person may feel tired.

  • Red blood cells
  • Inherited from parents
  • Tired and pale
Which doctor
Blood doctor (haematologist), children's doctor, family doctor
Common in
Families in Malaysia and Asia; found in babies and children
Contagious
No, it is inherited and cannot be caught
Course
Lifelong; mild forms may need little care, others need regular care

Common signs

More signs

  • In children, slow growth or poor appetite

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A person can carry the gene without being unwell
  • When both parents carry the gene, a child may have the condition
  • It is not caused by food, lifestyle or anything a person did

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting: call 999
  • A baby or child who is very pale, very sleepy or not feeding: see a doctor promptly
  • Fever, especially with chills
  • Dark urine, or yellow skin getting worse
  • A fast, pounding heartbeat or swollen belly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your doctor before taking iron or vitamin supplements
  • Eat a balanced diet and rest when you feel tired
  • Wash hands often and keep vaccines up to date to avoid infections
  • Couples planning a family can ask a doctor about genetic counselling
