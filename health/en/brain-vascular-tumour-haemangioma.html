Brain vascular tumour (haemangioma)

A cluster of abnormal blood vessels in the brain, usually not cancer

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

A haemangioma is a tangle or lump of unusual blood vessels in the brain. Many cause no trouble, but some can leak or cause fits, so a doctor should follow it.

  • Blood vessels in the brain
  • Often found by chance
  • Headache or fits, or none
Which doctor
Brain specialist (neurologist) or brain surgeon (neurosurgeon)
Common in
Found at any age, often in adults; some run in families
Contagious
No
Course
Many stay quiet for years; a doctor decides on watching or other options

Common signs

More signs

  • Headaches that keep coming back
  • Weakness, numbness or trouble with sight or speech

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The vessels form unusually before birth or in early life
  • Some types are passed down in families
  • Past radiation to the head can be a risk

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe headache, weakness, drooping face or trouble speaking: call 999 now
  • A first fit (seizure) or a fit that does not stop: call 999
  • Fainting or sudden confusion: go to hospital now
  • New or changing headaches, or a change in sight: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
