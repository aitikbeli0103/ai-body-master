Liposarcoma

A rare cancer that starts in fat cells, usually as a growing lump

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

Liposarcoma is a cancer that starts in fat cells. It can grow in soft tissue anywhere in the body, most often in the thigh or deep in the belly.

  • Fat tissue anywhere in body
  • Mostly adults
  • A lump that slowly grows
Which doctor
Family doctor first, then a cancer doctor (oncologist) or surgeon
Common in
Mostly adults, often middle-aged or older; uncommon overall
Contagious
No
Course
Varies a lot with the type and place; follow-up visits are important

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump or swelling, often deep in the thigh, arm or belly
  • The lump is often painless at first and slowly gets bigger
  • Pain, numbness or weakness if it presses on a nerve or muscle
  • Early on there may be no other signs, so a new lump should be checked

Why it happens

Other causes

  • In most people the cause is not known
  • Changes in the genes of fat cells make them grow out of control
  • Radiation to the same area years ago can raise the risk of some sarcomas

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden trouble breathing or chest pain: call 999
  • A leg or arm that suddenly swells, turns red or hurts (maybe a blood clot): go to hospital now
  • Very bad belly pain, repeated vomiting or a hard swollen belly: go to hospital now
  • Fever or chills while having cancer treatment: call your cancer team or go to hospital
  • A new lump that keeps growing, feels hard or deep, or becomes painful: see a doctor soon
  • A lump that comes back after surgery, or new numbness or weakness in a limb

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
