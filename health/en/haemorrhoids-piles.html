Haemorrhoids (piles)

Swollen blood vessels in or around the back passage (anus) that can itch, hurt or bleed

ConditionStomach & intestinesPelvis

What it is

Piles are soft swellings of the blood vessels inside or around the back passage. They can itch, feel sore or bleed a little when you pass stools.

  • Back passage (anus)
  • Adults and pregnant women
  • Itching, soreness, bleeding
Which doctor
Family doctor, or a bowel surgeon (colorectal surgeon)
Common in
Adults; more likely with constipation, pregnancy or long sitting
Contagious
No
Course
Mild piles often ease in days or weeks, but can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Itching or irritation around the back passage
  • Soreness or a dull ache near the back passage, especially when sitting
  • A soft lump you can feel near the opening of the back passage
  • Mucus leaking, or a feeling that the bowel is not fully empty

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Sitting for a long time, especially on the toilet
  • A low-fibre diet, long-lasting diarrhoea, and getting older

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding that does not stop, or feeling faint, weak or dizzy: go to hospital now
  • Black, tarry stools, or blood mixed through the stools: see a doctor straight away
  • New or repeated bleeding, or a change in bowel habit: see a doctor, as it is not always piles
  • A very painful, hard lump at the back passage that does not go down: see a doctor soon
  • Fever with severe pain, swelling or pus near the back passage: see a doctor today
  • Losing weight without trying, or looking pale and tired: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Eat more fibre, such as vegetables, fruit and whole grains, and drink enough water
  • Go to the toilet when you feel the urge, and do not strain or sit there for long
  • Sit in a shallow warm bath for a few minutes to ease soreness

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
