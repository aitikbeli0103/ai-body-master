Autoimmune encephalitis

The body's defence system attacks the brain and causes swelling

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

The immune system, which normally fights germs, mistakenly attacks parts of the brain. This can change memory, behaviour and thinking, and it needs a doctor's care in hospital.

  • The brain
  • Children and adults
  • Confusion, memory trouble
Which doctor
Nerve doctor (neurologist); go to hospital first
Common in
Rare; can affect children and adults
Contagious
No
Course
Often builds over days to weeks; needs specialist care and follow-up

Common signs

More signs

  • Changes in mood or behaviour, or seeing things that are not there

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The immune system makes antibodies that act against brain cells
  • Often the trigger is not found
  • It is not caused by anything the person did

See a doctor first if

  • A fit (seizure), or very hard to wake: call 999
  • Sudden confusion, severe headache, or stroke signs like face drooping: call 999
  • Fast change in behaviour, hearing or seeing things that are not there
  • Thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Trouble breathing or swallowing

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

What is Autoimmune encephalitis?

What are the common signs of Autoimmune encephalitis?

Forgetting things · Fits (seizures) · Cannot fall asleep · Fever, feeling hot

Why does Autoimmune encephalitis happen?

Germs (infection)

How can I look after myself with Autoimmune encephalitis?

See your doctor for check-ups · Rest well · Make the home safe from falls
