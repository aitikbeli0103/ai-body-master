Malaria

A fever illness caused by a parasite that mosquitoes pass on when they bite

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Malaria is caused by tiny parasites in the blood, passed on by the bite of infected mosquitoes. It causes fever with chills and sweats, and it can become serious fast if care is late.

  • Whole body (blood)
  • Forest visits or foreign travel
  • Fever, chills and sweats
Which doctor
Family doctor or hospital the same day; infectious disease doctor
Common in
People who go into forests or farms, and travellers to malaria areas
Contagious
No, not by daily contact; mosquito bites spread it
Course
Can turn serious quickly if care is late; early medical care is important

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A bite from an infected mosquito, often between dusk and dawn
  • In Malaysia, some cases come from monkey malaria parasites spread by mosquitoes near forests and farms
  • Travel to places where malaria is common, such as parts of Africa, Asia and South America

See a doctor first if

  • Confusion, fits, fainting or being very sleepy and hard to wake: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, yellow skin or eyes, very dark urine or very little urine: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding from the nose or gums, or a very pale, weak look: go to hospital now
  • Any fever after a forest stay or a trip to a malaria area, even weeks later: see a doctor the same day
  • Vomiting so much that you cannot keep drink or medicine down: see a doctor now
  • A baby, child, pregnant woman or older person with fever in a malaria area: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Prevent bites: wear long sleeves and trousers at dusk and night, and use insect repellent as the label says
  • Sleep under a mosquito net or in a screened room when you are in a risk area
  • Before travelling to a malaria area, ask your doctor about protection for your trip
  • If you get a fever after a forest or travel trip, get a blood test quickly instead of waiting

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • A kidney or liver condition
