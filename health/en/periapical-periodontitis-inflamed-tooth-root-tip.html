Periapical periodontitis (inflamed tooth root tip)

Inflammation or infection around the tip of a tooth root, in the bone below the tooth

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

Germs from a badly decayed or injured tooth reach the tip of its root, so the bone and tissue around it become inflamed or infected, which often causes pain on biting and can form an abscess.

  • Tip of a tooth root
  • After deep decay or injury
  • Pain on biting, swollen gum
Which doctor
Dentist; a tooth-nerve specialist (endodontist) if needed
Common in
Anyone with deep decay, a cracked tooth or an old tooth injury
Contagious
No
Course
Does not usually go away by itself and can flare up with swelling

Common signs

More signs

  • Throbbing toothache; the tooth may feel higher or looser than the others
  • A bad taste or bad breath from pus draining into the mouth

Why it happens

  • Deep tooth decay that has infected the nerve inside the tooth
  • A tooth whose nerve has died, for example after an injury
  • A cracked or broken tooth that lets germs inside
  • An old filling or earlier tooth work that has leaked or failed
  • Gum disease that reaches deep along the root

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen face or neck with trouble breathing or swallowing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Swelling that spreads towards the eye or under the jaw: go to hospital now
  • Fever, a swollen cheek or face, or pus and a bad taste in the mouth: see a dentist or doctor today
  • Severe or throbbing tooth pain that keeps you awake or will not ease: see a dentist soon
  • Tooth pain in a child, or in a person with diabetes or a weak immune system: see a dentist promptly
  • Pain that suddenly stops but the tooth stays dark, loose or tender: still see a dentist

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a dentist as soon as you can, even if the pain eases
  • Chew on the other side, and avoid biting hard food on that tooth
  • Brush gently around the tooth and clean between your teeth; rinse with plain lukewarm water

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A heart condition
  • Pregnancy
