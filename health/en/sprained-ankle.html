Sprained ankle

Stretched or torn ligaments around the ankle after the foot twists

ConditionAnkle & footArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

When the foot twists suddenly, the tough bands (ligaments) that steady the ankle can be stretched or torn, so the ankle becomes painful, swollen and hard to stand on.

  • Around the ankle
  • After a twist or a fall
  • Pain, swelling, bruising
Which doctor
Family doctor; bone doctor (orthopaedic) if severe
Common in
Anyone; common in sport and on uneven ground
Contagious
No
Course
Mild sprains often improve over days to weeks; worse ones take longer

Common signs

More signs

  • Swelling that comes up within hours
  • A stiff ankle that feels weak or unstable
  • Hearing a pop or feeling a tear at the moment of injury

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The foot rolls inwards suddenly, often on uneven ground
  • Landing badly after a jump, or missing a step on the stairs
  • High heels, loose shoes or slippery floors
  • A past sprain, weak ankle muscles or poor balance make it more likely

See a doctor first if

  • The foot is numb, pale, blue or cold: go to hospital now
  • You cannot put any weight on the foot, or the ankle looks bent or out of shape: go to hospital
  • Strong pain when touching the ankle bone or the outer foot bone, a cracking sound, or very heavy swelling
  • Pins and needles, or you cannot move your toes or foot
  • Pain or swelling that is not getting better after several days, or an ankle that keeps giving way

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • A snug, not tight, bandage and raising the foot on a pillow can ease swelling
  • Move gently as pain allows, wear snug flat shoes, and go back to sport slowly
  • For pain relief, ask your doctor or pharmacist what is safe for you

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
