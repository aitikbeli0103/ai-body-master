Geographic tongue

Smooth red patches with pale edges on the tongue that change shape and place over days

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

Some parts of the tongue lose their tiny bumps and look smooth and red, with pale edges like a map. The patches move around, and it is usually harmless.

  • On the tongue
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Often painless, may sting
Which doctor
Dentist or family doctor, if you want it checked
Common in
Anyone, at any age; it may run in families
Contagious
No
Course
Patches come, go and move around; it can last for months or years

Common signs

  • Smooth, red patches on the tongue, often with a white or pale edge
  • The patches change shape and move to a new place over days or weeks
  • Usually no pain, but some people feel burning or stinging
  • Spicy, salty, sour or very hot food may sting more
  • Patches may fade for a while and then come back

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It is seen more often in people with some skin conditions, such as psoriasis
  • Some foods can make it sting more, but this does not mean food caused it

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the tongue, lips or face, or trouble breathing or swallowing: call 999
  • A patch, sore or lump that does not move or fade and lasts more than two weeks: see a dentist or doctor
  • A white or red patch that feels hard or thick, or bleeds easily
  • Fever, or pain so strong that you cannot eat or drink properly
  • A lump in the neck, or weight loss with no clear reason

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Brush your teeth and tongue gently with a soft brush
  • When it stings, avoid spicy, salty, sour or very hot food and drinks
  • If it stings a lot, ask your dentist or pharmacist what you can use

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • A severe allergy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
