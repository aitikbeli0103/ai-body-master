High blood pressure

When the force of blood against the artery walls stays too high for too long

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

Blood pressure is the force of blood against the walls of your arteries. When it stays too high, it can strain the heart and blood vessels over time, often with no signs.

  • Heart and blood vessels
  • More common with age
  • Often no signs at all
Which doctor
Family doctor; heart doctor (cardiologist) if needed
Common in
Many adults, especially older people, or with extra weight or a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Usually long-lasting; a doctor's care can often keep it under control

Common signs

  • Most people feel nothing at all, so it is often found only by measuring
  • Sometimes a headache, often at the back of the head, or feeling dizzy
  • Sometimes a pounding heartbeat or feeling tired
  • A very high reading can come with blurry vision, chest pain or trouble breathing
  • Only a blood pressure reading can tell, so have it checked regularly

Why it happens

  • Often the exact cause is not known
  • Too much salt, extra weight, little exercise or too much alcohol
  • Getting older and a family history of high blood pressure
  • Long-term stress or poor sleep, including loud snoring with pauses in breathing
  • Sometimes other conditions, such as kidney disease or hormone problems, or some medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999 at once
  • Chest pain, a crushing feeling in the chest or sudden trouble breathing: call 999
  • A very high reading with a severe headache, confusion, blurred vision or a fit: go to hospital now
  • Sudden loss of vision, or the worst headache of your life: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant with a high reading, swelling or a bad headache: see a doctor the same day
  • Readings that stay high even when you take your medicine as prescribed: see your doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Have your blood pressure checked regularly and write down your readings
  • Eat less salt and salty sauces, and more vegetables and fruit
  • Keep to a steady weight, move your body on most days and limit alcohol
  • Do not smoke, and try to sleep well and make time to relax
  • Take your medicine as prescribed and never stop it on your own; ask your doctor if you are worried

Tell your therapist

  • High blood pressure
  • A heart condition
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Pregnancy
