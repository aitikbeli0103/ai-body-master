Vestibular neuritis

Inflammation of the balance nerve of the inner ear that causes sudden dizziness

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

The vestibular nerve sends balance signals from the inner ear to the brain. When it is inflamed, often after a virus, sudden strong spinning and unsteadiness can start.

  • Balance nerve of the inner ear
  • Often after a cold or flu
  • Sudden spinning, unsteady
Which doctor
Family doctor, ear-nose-throat doctor, neurologist
Common in
Adults, often after a virus infection
Contagious
No, but the virus behind it may spread
Course
The worst is often in the first days; balance usually improves over weeks

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The hearing is usually normal, which helps tell it from other ear problems
  • It is not caused by something you did

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Dizziness with a sudden severe headache, double vision or cannot stand: go to hospital now
  • Hearing loss or ringing in the ear along with the dizziness
  • Vomiting so much that you cannot keep fluids down
  • Dizziness that is not getting better after a few days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest in a safe place at first, then start moving gently as your doctor advises
  • Sip water often if you feel sick
  • Get up slowly and hold on to something steady when walking

Tell your therapist

  • Dizzy when turning the head
  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
