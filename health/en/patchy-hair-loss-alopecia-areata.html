Patchy hair loss (alopecia areata)

Hair falls out in round or oval patches, usually on the scalp

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

The body's defence system mistakenly attacks the hair roots, so hair falls out in smooth patches; it is usually painless and does not spread to other people.

  • Scalp, beard or eyebrows
  • Any age; may run in families
  • Smooth patches, often painless
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist); family doctor first
Common in
Any age, often starting young; more likely if family members have it
Contagious
No
Course
Hard to predict; hair may grow back by itself, but it can fall out again

Common signs

More signs

  • The skin in the patch usually looks normal, not red or scaly
  • Hair may also fall out from the beard, eyebrows or eyelashes
  • Sometimes tiny pits or ridges in the nails
  • In some people the hair loss spreads over the whole scalp or body

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The body's defence system mistakenly attacks the hair roots (follicles); the exact reason is not known
  • More likely with other autoimmune or allergic problems, such as thyroid disease, vitiligo or eczema
  • It is not caused by dirt, shampoo, hair dye or poor hair care

See a doctor first if

  • Feeling very low or hopeless about the hair loss, or thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • A painful, red, scaly or sore scalp, or pus or sores
  • Hair falling out quickly, in large amounts, or all over the head
  • Hair loss with tiredness, weight change, or feeling too hot or cold
  • Hair loss in a child: see a doctor promptly so other causes can be checked

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Be gentle with your hair: mild shampoo, loose styles, and less heat and harsh chemicals
  • A wig, scarf or cap is fine if it makes you feel more comfortable
  • Ask your doctor before using any hair-growth product, supplement or home remedy

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
