Swollen or enlarged testicle

One or both testicles look or feel bigger than usual, with or without pain

SymptomMen's healthReproductive

What it is

A swollen testicle means the sac holding it is larger, heavier or tender. It has many causes, and some need a doctor quickly.

  • Testicle and the sac
  • Boys and men of any age
  • Heavy, tender or sore
Which doctor
Family doctor or urologist; hospital at once if sudden and painful
Common in
Boys and men; after injury, infection or heavy strain
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as certain infections
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor needs to find it

Common signs

More signs

  • A heavy or dragging feeling in the sac
  • One side looks bigger or lower than the other
  • Tenderness, redness or warm skin on the sac

Why it happens

Other causes

  • An infection of the testicle or the tube next to it
  • Fluid collecting around the testicle, or a swollen vein
  • A twisted testicle, a hernia or, less often, a lump

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle: go to hospital now
  • Swelling after a hard knock or injury
  • Swelling with fever, vomiting or feeling very unwell
  • A hard lump you can feel, or a testicle that keeps getting bigger
  • A swollen testicle in a baby or boy: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wear snug, supportive underwear
  • Check yourself gently once a month and tell your doctor about any change

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Related conditions

  • Epididymal tuberculosis (TB)
  • Epididymitis
  • Inflamed seminal vesicles (vesiculitis)
  • Varicocele
  • Semen that does not liquefy
  • Acute epididymitis (inflamed tube behind testicle)
  • Abnormally shaped sperm (teratozoospermia)
  • Acute prostate infection (bacterial prostatitis)
