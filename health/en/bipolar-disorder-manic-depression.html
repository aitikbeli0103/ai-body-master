Bipolar disorder (manic depression)

An older name for bipolar disorder: mood that swings between very high and very low

ConditionTold by a doctor: moodHead

What it is

'Manic depression' is the older name for bipolar disorder. Mood, energy and thinking swing between a 'high' (mania) and a deep low (depression), for days, weeks or longer.

  • Brain: mood and thinking
  • Men and women alike
  • Highs and lows in mood
Which doctor
Psychiatrist (mental health doctor); start with your family doctor
Common in
Men and women; often starts in the late teens or twenties
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; mood can settle with regular medical care, though episodes may come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Highs: lots of energy, talking fast, racing thoughts, feeling you need little sleep
  • Highs: feeling very confident, getting easily irritated, or spending and taking risks without thinking
  • Between episodes, many people feel close to their usual selves

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Alcohol, drugs or some medicines can bring on or worsen mood swings

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or others, or of ending your life: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Seeing or hearing things that are not there, or strong beliefs that are clearly untrue: go to hospital now
  • Hardly sleeping for several nights yet full of energy, or not eating or drinking: see a doctor today
  • Risky or dangerous behaviour, such as reckless driving or spending all your money: get help now
  • A low mood that lasts more than two weeks, or a high that is getting out of control: see a doctor soon
  • Feeling worse or having side effects on your medicine: ask your doctor or pharmacist what to do

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep regular times for sleep, waking and meals; a steady daily routine helps
  • Learn the early signs of a high or a low, and ask someone close to you to help you notice them
  • Avoid alcohol and drugs, which can make mood swings worse

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Epilepsy or fits

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
