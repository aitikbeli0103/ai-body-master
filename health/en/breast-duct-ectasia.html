Breast duct ectasia

A milk duct under the nipple becomes wider and may get blocked, sometimes with sticky nipple discharge

ConditionWomen's healthChest

What it is

In breast duct ectasia, a milk duct near the nipple widens and its wall thickens, and it may fill with sticky fluid. It is not cancer, but the signs can look like other problems, so get it checked.

  • Behind the nipple
  • Often women near menopause
  • Nipple discharge, soreness
Which doctor
Family doctor; a breast specialist or breast surgeon if advised
Common in
Women near menopause; smoking may raise the chance
Contagious
No
Course
Often mild and may settle on its own; an infected duct needs a doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • Thick, sticky nipple discharge that may be white, green, brown or black
  • A nipple that turns inward or looks different from before
  • A tender, sore or red area around the nipple
  • A lump behind or near the nipple, which may feel thick or tender

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • A hot, red, swollen, painful breast with fever or chills: see a doctor the same day
  • Bloody nipple discharge, or discharge from one nipple that keeps coming on its own
  • Any new breast lump: see a doctor soon
  • A nipple that newly turns inward, or skin that looks dimpled like an orange peel
  • A lump or swelling in the armpit, or one breast changing in size or shape
  • Signs that keep coming back or do not settle

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have any nipple discharge, lump or nipple change checked by a doctor
  • Wear a comfortable, well-fitting bra; a warm (not hot) pack may ease soreness
  • If you smoke, ask your doctor or pharmacist about quitting

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
