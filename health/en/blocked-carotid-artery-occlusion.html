Blocked carotid artery (occlusion)

Narrowing or blockage of the artery in the neck that carries blood to the brain

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsNeck

What it is

The carotid arteries in the neck bring blood to the brain. When one is badly narrowed or blocked, less blood reaches the brain, which can raise the risk of a stroke.

  • Sides of the neck
  • More common with age
  • Often no signs at first
Which doctor
Family doctor, vascular surgeon, neurologist
Common in
Older adults; people with high blood pressure, diabetes or who smoke
Contagious
No
Course
Often builds up slowly over years; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • Brief loss of sight in one eye, like a curtain coming down

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Fatty build-up on the artery wall, which grows over many years
  • Diabetes

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999 now, even if it passes
  • Sudden loss of vision in one eye: go to hospital now
  • Sudden severe headache, confusion or loss of balance: call 999
  • Brief dizziness or fainting spells that keep coming back
  • Your doctor hears a whooshing sound in your neck and you have not had it checked

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
