Erythema multiforme

A skin reaction with round, target-like red patches, often set off by an infection or a medicine

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A skin reaction that causes round red patches, often with a darker middle like a target. An infection is a common trigger, and it often settles in weeks.

  • Hands, arms, feet and legs
  • Young adults and children
  • Target-like red patches
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it is severe
Common in
Mostly young adults and children; can happen at any age
Contagious
No, but an infection that sets it off (such as a cold sore) can spread
Course
Often settles by itself in a few weeks; it can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Often starts on the backs of the hands and feet, then spreads to the arms and legs
  • Usually the same places on both sides of the body
  • May itch or burn a little

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some other infections, such as a chest infection
  • Sometimes a reaction to a medicine
  • The body's defence system over-reacts to the trigger

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, or swelling of the face, lips or tongue: call 999
  • Blisters, peeling skin or a fast-spreading rash with fever: go to hospital now
  • Painful sores in the mouth or private parts, or red, painful eyes: see a doctor today
  • A rash that starts soon after a new medicine: tell a doctor the same day
  • A baby or child with a rash like this: see a doctor promptly
  • A rash that keeps coming back or lasts longer than a few weeks: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor to check the rash and look for what set it off
  • If your mouth is sore, choose soft, cool foods and avoid hot or spicy food

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A severe allergy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
