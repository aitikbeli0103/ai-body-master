Intestinal TB (tuberculosis)

A slow TB infection of the gut that can cause belly pain and weight loss

ConditionInfectionsAbdomen

What it is

TB germs can infect the bowel wall, often after swallowing germs from the lungs or from spread in the blood. It can cause ongoing belly pain and weight loss.

  • Bowel in the belly
  • Adults, weak defences
  • Belly pain, weight loss
Which doctor
Gut doctor (gastroenterologist), infection or chest doctor
Common in
Adults, people with weak body defences, people who live with active TB
Contagious
The bowel itself rarely spreads, but TB in the lungs can
Course
Slow over months; it needs a long treatment plan from a doctor

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • TB germs that reach the gut from the lungs or the blood
  • Swallowing coughed-up germs from TB in the lungs
  • Living closely with someone who has active TB

See a doctor first if

  • Severe belly pain with a hard, swollen belly and vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Cannot pass stools or wind, or vomiting that will not stop
  • Blood in the stools or black stools
  • Coughing blood, or a long cough with fever and night sweats
  • Weight loss you cannot explain, or belly pain that lasts for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Intestinal TB (tuberculosis)?

A slow TB infection of the gut that can cause belly pain and weight loss. TB germs can infect the bowel wall, often after swallowing germs from the lungs or from spread in the blood. It can cause ongoing belly pain and weight loss.

What are the common signs of Intestinal TB (tuberculosis)?

Cramping belly pain · Losing weight without trying · Fever, feeling hot · Soaking sweats at night · Loose, frequent stools · No appetite

Why does Intestinal TB (tuberculosis) happen?

A weak immune system

How can I look after myself with Intestinal TB (tuberculosis)?

Take medicine as your doctor says · Eat healthy, balanced meals · Drink enough water · Wash your hands often
