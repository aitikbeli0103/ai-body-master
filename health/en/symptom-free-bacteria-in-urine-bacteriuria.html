Symptom-free bacteria in urine (bacteriuria)

Bacteria found in the urine in a person who has no urinary symptoms

ConditionKidneys & urinaryReproductive

What it is

Bacteria are found in a urine test, but the person feels well: no burning, no fever, no pain. It is often found by chance.

  • Bladder and urine
  • More often in women
  • No symptoms
Which doctor
Family doctor; urologist if needed
Common in
Older adults, women, people with diabetes or a urine catheter
Contagious
No
Course
Often causes no problem; your doctor decides if anything is needed

Common signs

More signs

  • Bacteria are seen only on a urine test
  • Urine may look a little cloudy or smell strong, but this alone is not a sign
  • You feel well, with no fever and no pain when passing urine

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Bacteria can live in the bladder without causing harm
  • Women's shorter urinary tube makes it easier for bacteria to enter
  • Not emptying the bladder fully

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with shaking chills, or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • Pain in the side or lower back with fever
  • Burning, needing to pass urine often, or blood in the urine
  • Confusion in an older person
  • Pregnancy: tell your doctor if bacteria are found in the urine

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not hold your urine for long
  • Keep the private area clean and dry
  • Do not take medicine on your own; ask your doctor or pharmacist

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Fever or an active infection
