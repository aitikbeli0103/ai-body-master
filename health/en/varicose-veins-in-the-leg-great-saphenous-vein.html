Varicose veins in the leg (great saphenous vein)

Swollen, twisted veins along the inner leg from the groin to the ankle

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

The great saphenous vein is a long vein on the inner leg. When its valves weaken, blood pools and the vein bulges under the skin, making the leg feel heavy or tired.

  • Inner leg, thigh to ankle
  • Common in adults, often women
  • Bulging veins, heavy legs
Which doctor
Family doctor; vascular surgeon
Common in
Adults who stand long, are older or overweight; during and after pregnancy
Contagious
No
Course
Usually slowly gets more noticeable; a doctor can explain the choices

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak valves in the vein, so blood flows backwards and pools
  • Often there is no single clear cause

See a doctor first if

  • A suddenly swollen, painful, red or warm leg: go to hospital now (possible blood clot)
  • Chest pain or sudden trouble breathing: call 999
  • Bleeding from a vein: press firmly with a clean cloth, lie down, raise the leg and call 999
  • An open sore, or skin near the ankle turning dark, hard or itchy
  • Pain or swelling that keeps getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Walk often and move your ankles when you sit for long
  • Raise your legs above heart level when resting
  • Ask your doctor if compression stockings are suitable for you
  • Avoid sitting with crossed legs for hours

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
