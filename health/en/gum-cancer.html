Gum cancer

A cancer that starts in the gum tissue inside the mouth

ConditionCancer & tumoursHead

What it is

Abnormal cells in the gums grow out of control and may form a sore, a lump or a patch that does not go away.

  • Gums inside the mouth
  • More common in older adults
  • A sore or lump that stays
Which doctor
Dentist or family doctor first; then a head and neck or cancer specialist
Common in
Older adults; people who smoke, chew tobacco or betel, or drink a lot
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on how early it is found; your doctors can explain what to expect

Common signs

More signs

  • A sore on the gum that does not go away in a few weeks
  • A lump, a thick area, or a red or white patch on the gum
  • Bleeding, pain or numbness in the gum or jaw
  • Loose teeth, or dentures that no longer fit well

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Sometimes there is no clear cause, and often several things add up

See a doctor first if

  • Bleeding in the mouth that will not stop, or trouble breathing or swallowing: go to hospital now
  • If you are having cancer treatment and get a fever, chills or severe pain: call your care team or go to hospital
  • A sore, lump or patch on the gum that has not gone away in a few weeks
  • Loose teeth, or numbness or pain in the gum or jaw with no clear cause
  • A new lump in the neck, or a hoarse voice that lasts
  • Losing weight without trying, or finding it harder and harder to eat

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your care team about pain, eating and mouth care
  • Keep your mouth clean with a soft toothbrush, and see a dentist as your team advises
  • Talk about your worries with family, friends or a counsellor; you do not have to cope alone

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
