Bartholin's cyst

A lump near the vaginal opening when a small gland that makes lubricating fluid becomes blocked

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

The Bartholin's glands, one on each side of the vaginal opening, make fluid that keeps the area moist. If one gets blocked, the fluid builds up into a lump, often painless unless infected.

  • Beside the vaginal opening
  • Women of child-bearing age
  • A lump, painful if infected
Which doctor
Family doctor or gynaecologist (women's doctor)
Common in
Women of child-bearing age; less common after menopause
Contagious
No
Course
Small painless ones may need no care; infected ones need a doctor soon

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump near the opening of the vagina, usually on one side
  • Often painless when small, but it may feel uncomfortable when sitting, walking or during sex
  • If infected: the lump becomes painful, red, hot and swollen
  • The lump may burst by itself and let out fluid

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The gland's opening becomes blocked, so the fluid cannot drain out
  • Often the reason for the blockage is not known
  • A small injury, swelling or irritation near the opening can block it

See a doctor first if

  • Spreading redness, high fever, shaking chills, confusion or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • A very painful, red, hot lump, with or without fever: see a doctor the same day
  • Pregnancy, diabetes or a weak immune system together with a painful lump: see a doctor the same day
  • A lump that grows quickly, or makes it hard to sit, walk or pass urine: see a doctor soon
  • A new lump near the vagina after menopause, or a lump that is hard, uneven or does not go away

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
