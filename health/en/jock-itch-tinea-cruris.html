Jock itch (tinea cruris)

A fungal skin infection of the groin and inner thighs that causes an itchy red rash

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A common fungus grows in the warm, damp skin folds of the groin and inner thighs. It causes an itchy, red rash that often has a raised, curved edge.

  • Groin and inner thighs
  • More often in men and teens
  • Itchy, red, ring-shaped rash
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Men and teens; people who sweat a lot or wear tight clothes
Contagious
Yes, through skin contact or shared towels and clothes
Course
Often improves over a few weeks with the right care, but it can return

Common signs

More signs

  • A raised, curved edge that spreads outward, with clearer skin in the middle
  • Worse after sweating, or when clothes rub the skin
  • Sometimes athlete's foot or fungal toenails too

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A common fungus (a type of ringworm) that likes warm, damp, covered skin
  • Athlete's foot or fungal nails on your own body, which can spread to the groin

See a doctor first if

  • A rash with fever, spreading redness, pus or severe pain: see a doctor today
  • A rash that spreads quickly, or blisters and open sores
  • No improvement after a few weeks of the care a doctor or pharmacist advised, or it keeps coming back
  • Diabetes or a weak immune system: see a doctor early
  • The rash is not itchy, is on the genitals, or you are not sure what it is: have it checked

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep the area clean and very dry; dry the groin well after bathing and sweating
  • Dry your feet well, and look after athlete's foot too so it does not spread

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Fever or an active infection
