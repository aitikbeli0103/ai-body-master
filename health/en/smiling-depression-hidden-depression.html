Smiling depression (hidden depression)

Depression that is hidden behind a smile and a normal-looking life

ConditionTold by a doctor: moodHead

What it is

A person may look cheerful and cope well in public, but feel sad, empty or worn out inside. This is sometimes called smiling depression. It is real, and talking to a doctor can help.

  • Mood and thinking
  • Can affect anyone
  • Smiling outside, low inside
Which doctor
Family doctor or psychiatrist
Common in
Any adult, often those who feel they must stay strong
Contagious
No
Course
Often improves once it is spoken about and cared for

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Pressure to look fine and not worry others
  • Loss, loneliness or a hard time that was never talked about

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Giving away things, saying goodbye, or talking about not being here
  • Using alcohol or other substances to cope
  • Not able to sleep or eat for many days
  • Low mood that lasts more than two weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Smiling depression (hidden depression)?

Depression that is hidden behind a smile and a normal-looking life. A person may look cheerful and cope well in public, but feel sad, empty or worn out inside. This is sometimes called smiling depression. It is real, and talking to a doctor can help.

What are the common signs of Smiling depression (hidden depression)?

Low mood, no interest · Tired all the time · Cannot fall asleep · No appetite · Worried and on edge

Why does Smiling depression (hidden depression) happen?

Stress · The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Smiling depression (hidden depression)?

Get good sleep · Stay active every day

A session is gentle, needle-free meridian support for your wellbeing. It does not treat or cure Smiling depression (hidden depression) and never replaces your medicine or your doctor. Tell your therapist about it before the session starts.
