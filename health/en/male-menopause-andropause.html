Male menopause (andropause)

A slow drop in the male hormone testosterone with age, which can bring tiredness, low mood and less interest in sex

ConditionMen's healthWhole body

What it is

Unlike menopause in women, there is no sudden stop. Testosterone falls slowly with age, and some men feel tired, low or less interested in sex. Other conditions can feel the same.

  • Whole body
  • Middle-aged and older men
  • Tiredness and low mood
Which doctor
Family doctor; a urologist or hormone doctor (endocrinologist) if needed
Common in
Middle-aged and older men; more often if overweight or with diabetes
Contagious
No
Course
Usually develops slowly and differs from man to man; a doctor can look for other causes

Common signs

More signs

  • Less interest in sex, or trouble getting or keeping an erection
  • Losing muscle strength, gaining belly fat, or feeling weaker

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Testosterone naturally falls slowly as men get older
  • Some medicines, or past injury, infection or treatment of the testicles
  • Other problems, such as low mood, sleep apnoea or thyroid trouble, can feel the same

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting: call 999
  • Thoughts of harming yourself, or feeling hopeless: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Low mood, worry or poor sleep that lasts or gets in the way of daily life
  • Tiredness, erection trouble or weight change can also come from diabetes, heart or thyroid problems: get checked
  • Swelling or tenderness of the breast, or a lump in the breast or testicle
  • Weight loss you cannot explain, night sweats or a fever that will not go away

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor first: a simple check and blood test can look for the cause
  • Talk about how you feel with your partner, family or friends; ask for help if your mood stays low
