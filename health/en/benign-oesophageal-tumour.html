Benign oesophageal tumour

A non-cancerous growth in the wall or lining of the food pipe (oesophagus)

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

A benign (non-cancerous) growth forms in or on the wall of the food pipe. Small ones often cause nothing, while bigger ones can make swallowing harder or cause chest discomfort.

  • Food pipe, in the chest
  • Adults; not common
  • Food may feel stuck
Which doctor
Gut doctor (gastroenterologist); a surgeon if removal is needed
Common in
Adults; these growths are not common and are often found by chance
Contagious
No
Course
Small ones may only be watched; troublesome ones may be removed. Your doctor decides

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The growth comes from the muscle, lining or other tissue in the food pipe wall
  • Several types exist, such as muscle growths, cysts, polyps and fatty lumps

See a doctor first if

  • Food stuck with choking, or you cannot swallow even water or saliva: go to hospital now
  • Chest pain with sweating, breathlessness, or pain spreading to the arm or jaw: call 999
  • Vomiting blood, or black, tarry stools: go to hospital now
  • Trouble swallowing that is new or getting worse
  • Losing weight without trying, or not being able to eat enough
  • Coughing, choking or breathlessness when eating or drinking

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Eat slowly, take small bites and chew food well
  • Sip water with meals, and choose softer foods if swallowing is hard
  • Sit upright while eating and for a while afterwards
  • Ask your doctor what type it is and whether it should be removed or just watched
