Blocked fallopian tubes

One or both tubes that carry the egg to the womb are blocked, which can make it harder to get pregnant

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

The fallopian tubes carry an egg from the ovary to the womb. When a tube is blocked, the egg and sperm may not meet, which can make it harder to get pregnant. It often causes no signs.

  • The tubes beside the womb
  • Found when trying for a baby
  • Often no signs at all
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's doctor) or fertility specialist
Common in
Women of child-bearing age; more likely after a pelvic infection or belly surgery
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause and how much is blocked; a specialist can explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Endometriosis, where womb lining tissue grows outside the womb
  • Scar tissue from past surgery in the belly or pelvis, or from a burst appendix
  • Sometimes the cause is not found

See a doctor first if

  • Positive pregnancy test with sudden belly pain, bleeding, shoulder pain or fainting: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Sudden, severe lower belly pain, or pain with fever or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Lower belly pain with fever or a bad-smelling discharge: see a doctor the same day
  • Pelvic pain that keeps coming back or getting worse: see a doctor soon
  • Not getting pregnant after trying for some time: see a doctor, as earlier checks can help

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your doctor about the tests that can show whether the tubes are open
  • Have any pelvic infection checked early, and use condoms with a new partner
  • If you become pregnant, see a doctor early to check the pregnancy is in the womb
  • Trouble getting pregnant can be stressful; talk with your partner or someone you trust

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Fever or an active infection
