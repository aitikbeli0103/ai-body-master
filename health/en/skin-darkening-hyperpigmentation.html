Skin darkening (hyperpigmentation)

Areas of skin that look darker than the skin around them

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Some patches of skin turn darker than the rest because the skin makes extra colour (melanin). It is common, and often the cause is simple, such as sun or an old skin injury.

  • Can be on any area of skin
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Darker patches or spots
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone; more often with sun, ageing, pregnancy or skin injury
Contagious
No
Course
Often fades slowly over months once the cause is dealt with; some patches stay

Common signs

Types of dark spots

How dark spots form

Spots form when the skin makes pigment faster than it can renew itself. Sun protection every day is the most important care.

Not a pigment spot: see a doctor

  • A mole that grows or changes colour
  • A spot with an uneven edge, that is raised or bleeds
Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

More signs

  • Often on the face, hands, neck or other places that get sun
  • The skin may feel normal, or a little rough or dry
  • Patches often look darker after time in the sun

Why it happens

Other causes

  • After acne, a rash, an insect bite, a burn or another skin injury
  • Sometimes a health condition, such as diabetes or a hormone problem

See a doctor first if

  • Darker skin with vomiting, severe belly pain, dizziness or fainting: go to hospital now
  • A new spot or mole, or one that changes size, shape or colour, bleeds or has an uneven edge: see a doctor soon
  • A dark streak in a nail, or a new dark spot on the palm, sole or inside the mouth: see a doctor soon
  • Darkening over much of the body, with tiredness, weight loss or poor appetite
  • Dark, velvety patches in the neck, armpits or groin: see a doctor, as it can be linked to blood sugar problems
  • Dark patches that appeared after starting a new medicine or skin cream: ask your doctor or pharmacist

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Avoid skin-lightening creams of unknown source; ask your doctor or pharmacist first
  • Use gentle, fragrance-free skin products
  • Take a photo now and then, and show your doctor any patch that changes

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
