Benign duodenal tumour

A non-cancerous growth in the first part of the small intestine, just after the stomach

ConditionStomach & intestinesArms & legs

What it is

A benign (non-cancerous) lump grows in the duodenum, the first part of the small intestine. Many cause no trouble and are found by chance, but some need to be watched or removed.

  • Start of the small intestine
  • Often found by chance
  • Often no signs
Which doctor
Stomach and gut doctor (gastroenterologist); a surgeon if removal is needed
Common in
Adults; often found by chance during a camera test of the stomach (endoscopy)
Contagious
No
Course
Many stay small and cause no trouble; your doctor decides whether to watch or remove it

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Overgrowth of normal tissue in the gut wall, such as small polyps, fatty lumps or gland growths
  • Some grow from the muscle or nerve layers of the gut wall
  • A few rare inherited conditions cause many polyps in the gut

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, or black, tarry stools: go to hospital now
  • Fainting, or feeling very faint with a pale, clammy look: go to hospital now
  • Severe or sudden belly pain, or a hard, swollen belly with repeated vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or dark urine with pale stools
  • Upper belly pain, vomiting or feeling full that keeps coming back
  • Losing weight without trying

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your doctor what type it is and how often it should be checked
  • Avoid alcohol and smoking, which can irritate the stomach and gut

Tell your therapist

  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
