Tapeworm infection

A long, flat worm that lives in the gut, usually picked up from raw or undercooked meat or fish

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

A tapeworm can live in the gut for a long time; many people have no signs, while others notice belly upset or small white worm pieces in the stool.

  • The gut
  • Raw or undercooked meat, fish
  • Often none; belly upset
Which doctor
Family doctor; a gut doctor (gastroenterologist) if needed
Common in
People who eat raw or undercooked beef, pork or fish; places with poor sanitation
Contagious
Some types can be, through tapeworm eggs in stool on unwashed hands
Course
Often responds well to medicine from a doctor; it can stay for a long time if not found

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Food or water made unsafe by stool that contains tapeworm eggs
  • Not washing hands after the toilet or before eating and cooking
  • Living in or travelling to places with poor sanitation

See a doctor first if

  • A fit, a sudden bad headache, confusion or weakness on one side of the body: call 999
  • Very bad belly pain, a hard swollen belly, or vomiting with no stool or wind passing: go to hospital now
  • Worm pieces in your stool or on your underwear: see a doctor for a stool test
  • Weight loss you cannot explain, ongoing belly pain, or feeling very tired
  • Vomiting that will not stop, or signs of dehydration such as a very dry mouth and little urine
  • A child or a pregnant woman with these signs: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor for a stool test; ask your doctor or pharmacist before using any worm medicine
  • Cook beef, pork and fish all the way through; avoid raw or half-cooked meat
  • Wash fruit and vegetables well and drink only clean, safe water
  • Ask your doctor whether the people you live with should be checked too

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
