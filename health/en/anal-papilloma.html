Anal papilloma

A small, soft growth on the skin or lining of the anus, usually not cancer

ConditionStomach & intestinesPelvis

What it is

A papilloma is a small, soft growth in or near the anus. Some come from long irritation, some from a wart virus (HPV). Any new growth should be checked by a doctor.

  • In or near the anus
  • Adults; may cause no signs
  • A small soft lump or bump
Which doctor
Family doctor; colorectal surgeon (bowel and anus doctor)
Common in
Adults; more often with long-term irritation of the anus or HPV
Contagious
Some types can be (those caused by the wart virus, HPV)
Course
Often stays small; a doctor can check it and explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • A small, soft lump or skin flap near or inside the anus
  • A feeling that something is there, or that the bowel is not fully empty

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Infection with a wart virus (HPV), which spreads by close skin contact, including sex

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • A growth that is hard, grows fast, bleeds or has an open sore
  • Blood in the stool, or black stools
  • Pain, swelling, pus or fever near the anus
  • Losing weight without trying, or a bowel habit change that lasts for weeks
  • Many small growths or warts spreading around the anus or private parts

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have any new growth checked by a doctor first
  • Keep the area clean and dry; wash gently with water and try not to scratch or rub it
  • Keep stool soft with fibre and water, and avoid straining
  • Ask your doctor about safer sex and the HPV vaccine

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
