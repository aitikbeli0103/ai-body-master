Brain injury

Damage to the brain from a blow, a fall or another cause

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

The brain can be hurt by a hit to the head, a fall or a crash. It can range from mild, such as a concussion, to very serious.

  • Head and brain
  • After a knock, fall or crash
  • Headache, dizzy, confused
Which doctor
Emergency doctor; later a neurologist or rehabilitation doctor
Common in
Children, young adults, older adults who fall; road and sports injuries
Contagious
No
Course
Mild cases often settle over days to weeks; serious ones may need long recovery

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Violence or a strong blast

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, a fit, or being hard to wake after a head injury: call 999
  • Repeated vomiting, a headache that gets worse or growing confusion: go to hospital now
  • Blood or clear fluid from the nose or ear: go to hospital now
  • Weakness, numbness, trouble speaking or uneven pupils: call 999
  • A baby or child who hit their head and is unwell: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • After any hard hit to the head, get checked by a doctor
  • Rest, and return to normal activity slowly as your doctor advises
  • Wear a helmet on a motorbike or bicycle and a seat belt in a car

Tell your therapist

  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Dizzy when turning the head
  • Weak leg or foot

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
