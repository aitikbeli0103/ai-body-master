Sciatica

Pain along the sciatic nerve, from the lower back through the buttock and down the leg

ConditionBones, joints & spinePelvisBones & muscles

What it is

Pain that travels along the sciatic nerve from the lower back through the buttock and down the leg, usually on one side.

  • Back to leg
  • Irritated nerve
  • Shooting pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, orthopaedic (spine) doctor, neurologist, physiotherapist
Common in
Adults aged 30 to 60
Contagious
No
Course
Often improves within weeks to months
Medical details

What happens

The sciatic nerve is the longest nerve in the body. It is formed from nerve roots in the lower back (L4 to S3), runs through the buttock and down the back of the thigh, and then splits into branches that supply the leg and foot. Sciatica is the name for pain along this path. It is a symptom, not a disease: the nerve root is usually irritated or squeezed in the lower back, most often by a slipped disc or by narrowing of the spaces around the nerve, and sometimes by a tight buttock muscle (the piriformis).

Common causes by type

  • Lumbar radiculopathy: the nerve root is irritated in the spine; the most common cause
  • Piriformis syndrome: a tight muscle in the buttock presses on the nerve; less common
  • Other causes: pregnancy, injury or, rarely, a growth or an infection pressing on the nerve

Outlook

Most people improve within 4 to 6 weeks, often without surgery. Staying gently active, simple care at home and guidance from a doctor or physiotherapist help. A few people have lasting nerve pain and need further care.

Common signs

Medical details

Symptoms in more detail

  • Shooting pain: sharp, burning or electric pain from the lower back or buttock down the back of the leg, sometimes to the calf or foot, usually on one side
  • Worse with: sitting, coughing, sneezing and bending forward
  • Pins and needles or numbness: in the leg or foot, following the path of the nerve
  • Weakness: a heavy or weak leg, or trouble lifting the foot
  • Warning signs: numbness around the groin, new trouble with urine or bowels, or weakness in both legs: go to hospital now

Why it happens

Medical details

Causes and risk factors

  • Slipped disc: the most common cause
  • Narrowing of the spine (stenosis): age-related wear and bone spurs can narrow the spaces around the nerve
  • Slipped vertebra or injury: changes in the lower spine can irritate the nerve root
  • Tight buttock muscle: the piriformis can press on the nerve
  • Risk factors: age, heavy lifting or twisting, long sitting or driving, extra body weight, and diabetes (nerves are more easily damaged)

See a doctor first if

  • Numb around the groin or inner thighs: go to hospital now
  • New trouble passing urine or controlling the bowels: go to hospital now
  • A weak leg or foot, or tripping
  • Leg pain after a fall or accident, or with a fever
  • Severe pain that wakes you at night
  • Leg pain not easing after about 6 weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Medical details

How doctors check it

Scan findings are always read together with your symptoms, because disc changes are common in people who have no pain.

  • Examination: walking, strength, reflexes and feeling in the legs, and the straight leg raise test (the leg is lifted while you lie down to see if the pain shoots down the leg)
  • MRI: shows the discs and nerves; used when symptoms are severe or last more than about 6 weeks
  • X-ray, CT and nerve tests (EMG): used in selected cases

Problems that can feel similar

Hip joint problems, sacroiliac joint pain, muscle strain, poor blood flow in the leg and nerve damage from diabetes can feel like sciatica, so a doctor checks to tell them apart. Leg pain with a swollen, red, warm calf needs urgent care: call 999 if you also feel short of breath.

Looking after yourself

Medical details

Daily care in more detail

  • Stay gently active: short walks several times a day usually help more than long bed rest; avoid movements that send pain down the leg
  • Warmth: a warm pack on the lower back or buttock can ease tight muscles
  • Gentle stretches: only within a comfortable range; a physiotherapist can show which ones suit your nerve and spine
  • Sit and lift well: a small cushion in the curve of the lower back, change position often, bend your knees to lift
  • Pain relief: ask your doctor or pharmacist what is safe for you; do not start new medicine on your own
  • Weight and smoking: a healthy weight and not smoking help the discs and nerves

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

What are the common signs of Sciatica?

Pain shooting down the leg · Lower back pain · Pain in the hip · Numb, tingling feet · Legs feel weak · Hard to walk far

Why does Sciatica happen?

Sitting for hours · Lifting the wrong way · Getting older · Long hours of driving · Slouching with a laptop · Carrying heavy loads on one side

How can I look after myself with Sciatica?

Walk a little every day · A warm pack on the lower back · A chair that supports your back · Gentle back stretches · Lift with your legs · Strengthen your core
