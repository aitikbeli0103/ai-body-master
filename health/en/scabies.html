Scabies

A very itchy skin problem caused by tiny mites that live in the skin

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Tiny mites burrow into the top layer of the skin, causing strong itching (often worse at night) and a rash that spreads easily to people close to you.

  • Skin, often hands and waist
  • Spreads by close contact
  • Very itchy, worse at night
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone; spreads easily in crowded homes, care homes and hostels
Contagious
Yes, through close skin contact and sometimes shared bedding or clothes
Course
Needs the right medicine from a doctor; itching can last weeks afterwards

Common signs

More signs

  • Thin, wavy lines on the skin (burrows)
  • Often between fingers, on wrists, elbows and waist (babies: palms and soles too)
  • Scratch marks and sore, broken skin from scratching

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Tiny mites that burrow into the top layer of the skin
  • Passed on by long, close skin contact with someone who has it
  • Sometimes spread through shared bedding, towels or clothes
  • It is not caused by being unclean; anyone can get it

See a doctor first if

  • Spreading red, hot, painful skin, pus or fever with scratched sores: see a doctor today
  • A baby, young child, pregnant woman or older person with an itchy rash: see a doctor promptly
  • Thick, crusty patches of skin, especially if your immune system is weak: see a doctor soon
  • An itchy rash that is worse at night, or others at home are itchy too: see a doctor to check
  • New spots, or itching that gets worse even after using the medicine as told: go back to your doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor to confirm it; the right medicated cream or lotion must be used exactly as told
  • People living with you often need to use the medicine at the same time; ask your doctor
  • Wash bedding, towels and recently worn clothes in hot water, then dry in strong sun or a hot dryer
  • Things you cannot wash can be sealed in a plastic bag for several days

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
