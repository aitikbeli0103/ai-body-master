Chronic kidney disease

Long-lasting damage to the kidneys that slowly lowers how well they clean the blood

ConditionKidneys & urinaryAbdomen

What it is

The kidneys filter waste and extra water out of the blood. In chronic kidney disease they slowly work less well over months or years, often with no signs at first.

  • Both kidneys
  • More common with age
  • Often no signs at first
Which doctor
Family doctor, kidney doctor (nephrologist)
Common in
Older adults; people with diabetes, high blood pressure or a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Usually slow and long-lasting; regular care may help slow it down

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Diabetes and high blood pressure are the two most common causes
  • Swelling (inflammation) of the tiny filters inside the kidneys
  • Kidney stones, a blocked flow of urine, or repeated kidney infections
  • Long-term use of some medicines, including some painkillers, without advice
  • Inherited kidney conditions, such as cysts in the kidneys, or a family history

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, chest pain or fainting: call 999
  • Passing very little or no urine, or fast swelling of the face, legs or belly: go to hospital now
  • Confusion, strong sleepiness or a fit: call 999
  • Severe weakness, a very slow or uneven heartbeat, or vomiting that will not stop: go to hospital now
  • Blood in the urine, foamy urine, or new pain in the back or side: see a doctor soon
  • Swelling, tiredness or itching that keeps getting worse: see your doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Keep to regular check-ups, including blood and urine tests, as your doctor advises
  • Keep blood pressure and blood sugar well controlled, as your doctor advises
  • Ask your doctor or dietitian how much salt, protein and fluid is right for you
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking any painkiller, herbal product or supplement
  • Do not smoke, stay gently active and keep to a steady weight

Tell your therapist

  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • High blood pressure
  • A heart condition
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Questions and answers

What is Chronic kidney disease?

Long-lasting damage to the kidneys that slowly lowers how well they clean the blood. The kidneys filter waste and extra water out of the blood. In chronic kidney disease they slowly work less well over months or years, often with no signs at first.

What are the common signs of Chronic kidney disease?

Tired all the time · Swollen legs and ankles · Puffy face in the morning · Waking at night to pass urine · Itchy skin · No appetite

Why does Chronic kidney disease happen?

Diabetes and high blood pressure are the two most common causes · Swelling (inflammation) of the tiny filters inside the kidneys · Kidney stones, a blocked flow of urine, or repeated kidney infections · Long-term use of some medicines, including some painkillers, without advice · Inherited kidney conditions, such as cysts in the kidneys, or a family history

How can I look after myself with Chronic kidney disease?

Keep to regular check-ups, including blood and urine tests, as your doctor advises · Keep blood pressure and blood sugar well controlled, as your doctor advises · Ask your doctor or dietitian how much salt, protein and fluid is right for you · Ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking any painkiller, herbal product or supplement · Do not smoke, stay gently active and keep to a steady weight
