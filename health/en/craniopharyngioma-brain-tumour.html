Craniopharyngioma (brain tumour)

A slow-growing tumour near the pituitary gland at the base of the brain

ConditionCancer & tumoursHead

What it is

A craniopharyngioma is a tumour near the pituitary gland. It is usually slow-growing and not a typical cancer, but it can press on nearby parts and needs specialist care.

  • Base of the brain
  • Children and adults
  • Headache, vision change
Which doctor
Brain surgeon (neurosurgeon), hormone doctor (endocrinologist)
Common in
Seen in children and in adults; rare overall
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow-growing, but it can come back; long-term follow-up is usual

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It grows from leftover cells from the way the brain develops before birth
  • It is not caused by anything a person ate or did
  • It is not passed on in most families

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden very bad headache, repeated vomiting, or becoming very sleepy or confused: call 999
  • A fit, or sudden loss of vision: go to hospital now
  • A child with headaches, vomiting or poor growth: see a doctor promptly
  • Very strong thirst and passing a lot of urine
  • Vision getting worse, or double vision

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.
