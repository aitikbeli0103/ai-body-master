Joint that keeps dislocating

A joint that slips out of place again and again, often after an earlier injury or because it is loose

ConditionAny other bone or soft-tissue injuryBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

A joint that keeps dislocating slips out of place again and again, sometimes with only a small movement, and it often feels loose or unstable.

  • A joint, often the shoulder
  • After an earlier dislocation
  • Loose, slips, gives way
Which doctor
Bone (orthopaedic) doctor; sports doctor and physiotherapist help too
Common in
People who have had a dislocation before; those with very loose joints
Contagious
No
Course
May keep happening if the joint stays loose; a specialist can explain the options

Common signs

  • The joint slips out of place again, even with a small movement
  • A feeling that the joint is loose, unstable or about to give way
  • Pain, clicking or catching when you move the joint
  • Aching or swelling after the joint slips
  • Weak muscles around the joint, or avoiding certain movements out of worry

Why it happens

Other causes

  • An earlier dislocation that stretched or tore the bands (ligaments) holding the joint
  • Weak muscles around the joint after an earlier injury
  • Naturally loose joints, which some people are born with
  • A joint socket that is shallow or shaped differently from usual

See a doctor first if

  • Injury to the head, neck or back, or a major accident: call 999 and do not move the person
  • Numbness, tingling, or a pale, blue or cold hand or foot after the joint slips: go to hospital now
  • A joint stuck out of place that you cannot move: go to hospital now
  • The joint slips out more and more often, or with smaller and smaller movements: see a doctor soon
  • Pain, swelling or weakness that keeps getting worse between episodes

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • If it slips out, do not force it back yourself; keep it still and get medical help
  • See a bone doctor to check the joint and talk about the options
  • A physiotherapist can teach exercises to strengthen the muscles around the joint
  • Avoid movements or sports that make the joint slip until your doctor says they are safe
  • Use any brace or support exactly as your doctor or physiotherapist advises

Tell your therapist

  • Recent shoulder injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
