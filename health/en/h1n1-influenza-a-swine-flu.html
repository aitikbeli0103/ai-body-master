H1N1 influenza A (swine flu)

A flu virus that spreads easily between people and causes fever, cough, sore throat and body aches

ConditionInfectionsChest

What it is

The virus spreads through coughs and sneezes and causes sudden fever, cough, sore throat and body aches, and in some people it can become serious.

  • Nose, throat and lungs
  • Can affect any age
  • Fever, cough and body aches
Which doctor
Family doctor; hospital if breathing is hard
Common in
Anyone; higher risk of serious illness in babies, pregnancy and older age
Contagious
Yes, by coughs, sneezes and touched surfaces; not caught from eating pork
Course
Usually gets better in about a week; cough and tiredness may last longer

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • An influenza A (H1N1) virus that came from pigs but now spreads between people
  • A sick person can spread it from a day before signs start until several days after
  • It spreads faster in crowded places such as schools, offices and public transport
  • Babies, older people, pregnant women and people with long-term illness are more likely to become seriously ill

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, fast breathing, blue lips or chest pain: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Confusion, very hard to wake, fits, or a stiff neck with severe headache: call 999
  • A baby or young child who breathes fast, will not feed, is very sleepy or has no wet nappies: go to hospital now
  • Cannot keep fluids down, very dry mouth, dizziness, or passing very little urine
  • Fever or cough that gets better and then comes back worse, or fever that lasts more than a few days
  • Pregnancy, older age, long-term illness (heart, lung, kidney, diabetes) or weak immunity: see a doctor early

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay home and rest until the fever has gone and you feel better, so you do not pass it on
  • Cover coughs and sneezes with a tissue or your elbow, and wash hands often with soap
  • Wear a mask around others if you must go out, and keep away from babies and older people
