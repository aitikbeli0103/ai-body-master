Agoraphobia

Strong fear or anxiety about places or situations where it feels hard to get away or get help

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressHead

What it is

A person feels very anxious in places such as crowds, queues, public transport or open spaces, and may start to avoid them. It is a recognised condition and help is available.

  • Mind and body
  • Often starts in young adults
  • Fear in crowds or far from home
Which doctor
Family doctor, psychiatrist or psychologist
Common in
Adults, often starting in the teens or twenties; more often in women
Contagious
No
Course
It can last long if ignored, but many people improve with the right support

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A frightening event, panic attack or long period of stress
  • Other anxiety problems, or anxiety running in the family
  • Low mood, or a long time of staying home

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Chest pain, or trouble breathing that does not ease: call 999
  • You cannot leave home, work or go to school because of fear
  • Low mood, hopeless feelings, or using alcohol to cope
  • Panic attacks that keep coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Talk to someone you trust about how you feel
  • Take small, planned steps outside, with a friend at first
  • Breathe slowly and gently when you feel panic rising
  • Ask your doctor about talking therapy and other support

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • High blood pressure
  • Pregnancy
  • Epilepsy or fits

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
