Yellow soles of the feet

The soles of the feet look yellow or orange-yellow

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

The soles take on a yellow or orange tint. It often comes from lots of orange fruit and vegetables or thick skin, but sometimes it points to a liver or other medical problem.

  • Soles of the feet
  • Any age, even babies
  • Colour change, usually painless
Which doctor
Family doctor; a liver or digestive specialist if needed
Common in
People who eat lots of orange foods; babies; people with liver or thyroid problems
Contagious
No, but some causes, such as viral hepatitis, can spread
Course
Colour from food may fade slowly over weeks; other causes need a doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • Soles, and often palms, look yellow or orange, while the whites of the eyes stay white
  • Thick, hard or dry skin on the soles can look yellowish
  • The colour itself does not hurt

Why it happens

  • Eating a lot of carrots, pumpkin, sweet potato, papaya or mango, which can tint the skin
  • Thick, hard skin (calluses) or very dry skin on the soles
  • Liver, gallbladder or bile problems (jaundice), when the whites of the eyes turn yellow too
  • Some medical conditions, such as an underactive thyroid, diabetes or kidney problems
  • Some supplements (such as carotene) or medicines can tint the skin or affect the liver

See a doctor first if

  • Yellow skin or eyes with confusion, great sleepiness, vomiting blood or black stools: call 999
  • Yellow skin or eyes with high fever, shivering or strong belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Yellow eyes, dark urine, pale stools or itching all over: see a doctor today
  • A newborn or young baby who looks yellow, is very sleepy or feeds badly: see a doctor promptly
  • Yellow soles that last, or come with weight loss, no appetite or constant tiredness: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Look at the whites of your eyes in daylight; if they are yellow too, see a doctor soon
  • If you eat lots of orange fruit and vegetables, enjoy a wider mix of foods; the tint may fade
  • Limit alcohol, because it puts extra strain on the liver
