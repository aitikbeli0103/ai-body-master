Giant intestinal fluke infection (fasciolopsiasis)

A gut infection with a large flatworm, caught from raw water plants

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

A large flatworm lives in the small bowel and can cause belly pain and loose stools. It often comes from eating raw water plants.

  • Small bowel
  • Raw water plants
  • Belly pain, loose stools
Which doctor
Family doctor, infectious disease doctor
Common in
People who eat raw water plants; areas with pigs and ponds
Contagious
Not directly from person to person; it spreads through food and water
Course
A doctor can confirm it with a stool test and advise the next steps

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Eating raw or half-cooked water plants such as water chestnuts or water caltrops
  • Peeling water plants with the teeth
  • Pond water polluted by pig or human waste
  • Poor handwashing and unsafe toilets

See a doctor first if

  • Severe belly pain or a hard, swollen belly: go to hospital now
  • Vomiting or loose stools that will not stop, or signs of dehydration
  • Black stools or blood in the stools
  • Swollen face or legs, or feeling faint or very pale
  • A child who is weak, very pale or not eating: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Wash water plants well and cook them fully before eating
  • Do not peel water plants with your teeth
  • Wash hands with soap after the toilet and before eating
  • Drink enough clean water; ask your doctor or pharmacist before any medicine
