Glossitis (tongue inflammation)

Swelling and soreness of the tongue, which may look red and smooth

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

The tongue becomes swollen and sore, and often looks red and smooth. It can make eating, talking and swallowing uncomfortable. There are many possible causes.

  • On the tongue
  • Can happen at any age
  • Sore, swollen, smooth tongue
Which doctor
Dentist or family doctor
Common in
Anyone; more likely with poor mouth care, smoking or low iron or vitamins
Contagious
Some types can be, such as those caused by an infection
Course
Often settles once the cause is found and dealt with; some causes need a doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • A tongue that is swollen, sore or tender
  • The tongue may look smooth and shiny, and red or pale
  • Taste that seems changed or dull
  • A burning or stinging feeling in the tongue

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Mouth infections caused by yeast (thrush), viruses or bacteria
  • Injury or irritation, such as a burn, a sharp tooth, rough dentures or tongue biting
  • An allergy to a food, a medicine or a dental product
  • Low iron or vitamin levels, a dry mouth, or other health conditions

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden swelling of the tongue, lips or face, or trouble breathing or swallowing: call 999
  • Fever, a swollen face or neck, or feeling very unwell
  • A sore, patch or lump on the tongue that does not go away within two weeks
  • So painful that you cannot eat or drink properly
  • Soreness that keeps coming back, or comes with tiredness, a pale look or weight loss

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Brush your teeth and tongue gently with a soft brush, and clean between your teeth daily
  • Keep dentures clean, and tell your dentist if they rub or hurt
  • See a dentist or doctor to look for the cause, and ask before using any mouth gel or rinse
