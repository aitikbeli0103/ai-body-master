Toe ulcers

Open sores on the toes that are slow to heal

SymptomHeart & blood vesselsArms & legs

What it is

A toe ulcer is an open sore on a toe that does not heal as it should. It is often a sign of poor blood flow, nerve damage or pressure on the foot.

  • Toes and feet
  • Often with diabetes
  • Sore, slow to heal
Which doctor
Family doctor; foot or vascular specialist
Common in
People with diabetes, poor circulation or numb feet; older adults
Contagious
No
Course
Healing can take weeks to months and needs a doctor's care

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Poor blood flow to the feet and toes
  • Tight shoes or constant rubbing and pressure
  • Swollen legs from weak veins or a heart problem

See a doctor first if

  • A toe that turns black, blue or very pale and cold: go to hospital now
  • Fever or chills with a sore toe or foot: see a doctor today
  • Redness spreading up the foot, pus or a bad smell
  • Very bad pain in the foot or toes, especially at rest or at night
  • Any sore that does not start to heal, especially if you have diabetes

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Check both feet every day, including between the toes
  • Wear soft, well-fitting shoes and clean socks; never walk barefoot
  • Keep the sore clean and covered as your doctor or nurse shows you
  • Do not cut corns or calluses yourself; ask a doctor or foot specialist

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
