Pulmonary embolism (lung blood clot)

A blood clot that travels to the lungs and blocks a blood vessel there

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

A blood clot, often from a deep vein in the leg, travels to the lungs and blocks blood flow. It can be serious and needs urgent medical care.

  • Blood vessels in the lungs
  • After long rest or surgery
  • Sudden breathlessness
Which doctor
Emergency doctor first; later a heart, lung or blood doctor
Common in
People who sit or lie still for long, after surgery, or with a leg clot
Contagious
No
Course
Can come on fast; needs hospital care, and recovery takes time

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A clot in a deep leg vein that breaks off and moves to the lungs

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden trouble breathing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Chest pain, especially sharp pain when breathing in: call 999
  • Fainting, collapse, or coughing up blood: call 999
  • A racing heart with dizziness or very pale, sweaty skin
  • One leg that is newly swollen, painful, warm or red

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Pulmonary embolism (lung blood clot)?

A blood clot that travels to the lungs and blocks a blood vessel there. A blood clot, often from a deep vein in the leg, travels to the lungs and blocks blood flow. It can be serious and needs urgent medical care.

What are the common signs of Pulmonary embolism (lung blood clot)?

Short of breath on stairs · Sharp pain when breathing in · Dry, tickly cough · Racing, pounding heart · Feeling faint

Why does Pulmonary embolism (lung blood clot) happen?

Standing or sitting still for long · An injury or fall · Hormone changes

How can I look after myself with Pulmonary embolism (lung blood clot)?

See your doctor for check-ups · Ask your pharmacist · Drink enough water
