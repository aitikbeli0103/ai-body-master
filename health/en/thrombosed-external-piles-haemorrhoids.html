Thrombosed external piles (haemorrhoids)

A painful, hard lump at the edge of the anus from a small blood clot

ConditionStomach & intestinesPelvis

What it is

A small blood clot forms in a vein just outside the anus, making a firm, tender, bluish lump that can hurt when sitting or passing stools.

  • Edge of the anus
  • Adults, often after straining
  • Sudden painful lump
Which doctor
Family doctor, colorectal surgeon
Common in
Adults; after straining, long sitting or pregnancy
Contagious
No
Course
Pain often eases over days to weeks; the lump may take longer

Common signs

More signs

  • A sudden, firm, tender lump at the anus
  • Pain that is worse when sitting or passing stools

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long periods of sitting, such as on the toilet

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding from the bottom or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • Very severe pain, or a lump that keeps getting bigger
  • Fever, or red, hot, swollen skin around the lump
  • Bleeding from the bottom with a change in bowel habit or weight loss
  • You take blood-thinning medicine and have bleeding

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Eat plenty of fruit, vegetables and fibre, and drink enough water
  • Do not strain or sit on the toilet for long
  • A warm bath may ease the pain
  • Keep the area clean and gently dry

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
