Eating disorder

Unhealthy eating habits linked to strong worries about food, weight or body shape

ConditionTold by a doctor: eatingWhole body

What it is

An eating disorder is a health problem in which eating habits and thoughts about food and body shape start to harm a person's health and life. Help is available.

  • Mind, eating and body
  • Often starts in teens
  • Fear of food or weight gain
Which doctor
Family doctor, psychiatrist, dietitian
Common in
Teenagers and young adults, but any age and any gender
Contagious
No
Course
It can last long without help; many people get better with steady support from a care team

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Often a mix of feelings, family history and pressure about body shape
  • Low self-esteem, stress, or a past hard experience
  • Strict dieting or being teased about weight

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, chest pain, a very slow, fast or uneven heartbeat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Not eating or drinking for days, or being too weak to stand
  • Fast weight loss, or periods stopping in a girl or woman
  • Vomiting often, or using laxatives or water pills to lose weight
  • A child or teenager who refuses to eat: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Tell a doctor or someone you trust; asking for help is a brave first step
  • Try to eat regular meals with family or friends if you can
  • Avoid strict diets, and talk to a doctor or dietitian about safe eating
  • Limit social media that makes you feel bad about your body
  • Family can listen kindly and avoid comments about weight or looks

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Pregnancy
  • High blood pressure

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Eating disorder?

Unhealthy eating habits linked to strong worries about food, weight or body shape. An eating disorder is a health problem in which eating habits and thoughts about food and body shape start to harm a person's health and life. Help is available.

What are the common signs of Eating disorder?

Losing weight without trying · Tired all the time · Feeling faint · Always feeling cold · Hair coming out · Low mood, no interest

Why does Eating disorder happen?

The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Eating disorder?

Tell a doctor or someone you trust; asking for help is a brave first step · Try to eat regular meals with family or friends if you can · Avoid strict diets, and talk to a doctor or dietitian about safe eating · Limit social media that makes you feel bad about your body · Family can listen kindly and avoid comments about weight or looks
