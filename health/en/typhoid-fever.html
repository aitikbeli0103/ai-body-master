Typhoid fever

A serious infection from germs in unclean food or water, with a fever that builds up over days

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Typhoid fever is caused by a germ called Salmonella Typhi that spreads through unclean food or water, and it brings a high fever, tiredness and belly pain.

  • Whole body, mainly the gut
  • Spread by unclean food or water
  • Long, high fever
Which doctor
Family doctor or hospital; an infection specialist if needed
Common in
Anyone; more likely where clean water or toilets are lacking, and after travel there
Contagious
Yes, through food or water that carries the germ from an infected person's stool
Course
Can last weeks and may become serious without a doctor's care

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Infection with a germ called Salmonella Typhi
  • Eating or drinking food or water made unsafe by an infected person's stool
  • Food handled by someone with the germ who has not washed their hands well after the toilet
  • Some people carry the germ for a long time without feeling ill, and can still pass it on
  • Raw or poorly kept food, and ice or drinks made with unsafe water

See a doctor first if

  • Confusion, being very drowsy or hard to wake, a fit (seizure) or fainting: call 999
  • Vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, or sudden severe belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Signs of dehydration: very dry mouth, hardly any urine, dizziness, or cannot keep fluids down
  • A high fever that lasts several days, especially after travel or risky food or water
  • A baby, young child, older person or pregnant woman with a fever: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Rest, and take small, frequent sips of safe (boiled or bottled) water
  • Take any medicine your doctor gives you exactly as told, and keep your check-ups
  • Wash your hands well with soap after the toilet and before touching food
  • Stay home from work, school or cooking for others until your doctor says it is safe
  • Choose freshly cooked food and safe water, and ask your doctor about the typhoid vaccine before travel

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Questions and answers

What is Typhoid fever?

A serious infection from germs in unclean food or water, with a fever that builds up over days. Typhoid fever is caused by a germ called Salmonella Typhi that spreads through unclean food or water, and it brings a high fever, tiredness and belly pain.

What are the common signs of Typhoid fever?

Fever, feeling hot · Headache across the forehead · Tired all the time · No appetite · Cramping belly pain · Loose, frequent stools

Why does Typhoid fever happen?

Infection with a germ called Salmonella Typhi · Eating or drinking food or water made unsafe by an infected person's stool · Food handled by someone with the germ who has not washed their hands well after the toilet · Some people carry the germ for a long time without feeling ill, and can still pass it on · Raw or poorly kept food, and ice or drinks made with unsafe water
