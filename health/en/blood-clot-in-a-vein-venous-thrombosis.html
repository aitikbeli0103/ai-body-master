Blood clot in a vein (venous thrombosis)

A blood clot that forms inside a vein and can slow the blood flowing back to the heart

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsBlood

What it is

Veins carry blood back to the heart. A clot in a vein can cause swelling and pain, and a piece can sometimes travel to the lungs.

  • Often in the leg
  • After long rest or surgery
  • Swollen, sore, warm limb
Which doctor
Family doctor, vascular surgeon, blood doctor (haematologist)
Common in
People after surgery or long bed rest, in pregnancy, and older adults
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor's care and follow-up; it can take weeks to months to settle

Common signs

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden trouble breathing, chest pain or coughing blood: call 999
  • Fainting or a very fast heartbeat with breathlessness: call 999
  • One leg or arm that is newly swollen, painful and warm: go to hospital the same day
  • A limb that turns blue, pale or cold
  • Swelling during pregnancy or after surgery that gets worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Walk and move often; on long trips stand and stretch your legs
  • Do not rub or massage a swollen, painful leg

Tell your therapist

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • A heart condition
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
