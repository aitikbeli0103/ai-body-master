Heatstroke

Illness from the body getting too hot, usually in hot weather; the severe form, heatstroke, is an emergency

ConditionWhole body & generalWhole body

What it is

The body gets too hot and cannot cool itself, usually after a long time in strong heat or after hard work or exercise in the heat. It can get serious quickly.

  • Whole body
  • Hot days, hard outdoor work
  • Hot, dizzy, very tired
Which doctor
Family doctor; emergency department if severe
Common in
Outdoor workers, athletes, older adults, babies and children; anyone in strong heat
Contagious
No
Course
Mild cases often ease with rest and cooling; severe cases need hospital care at once

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Staying or working a long time in strong heat or direct sun
  • Hard exercise or heavy work in hot, humid weather
  • Being left in a hot parked car or closed room, which is very dangerous for children

See a doctor first if

  • Confusion, fits, fainting, or cannot be woken: call 999 now
  • Very hot body, hot red or dry skin, or sweating has stopped: call 999 and keep cooling them
  • Vomiting that will not stop, or you cannot keep drinks down: go to hospital now
  • A baby, child or older person who is limp, very sleepy or not drinking: go to hospital now
  • Symptoms that do not ease after resting in a cool place for a while: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Move to a cool, shaded place, rest, and loosen or remove extra clothing
  • If awake and able to swallow, sip cool water slowly
  • Cool the skin with a damp cloth or a fan; do not wait if the person seems worse
  • Plan outdoor work and sport for cooler hours, wear light clothes and a hat, and take breaks
  • Never leave a child, older person or pet in a parked car or closed room

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • A heart condition
  • High blood pressure
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
