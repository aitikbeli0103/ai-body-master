Compartment syndrome

Dangerous pressure build-up inside a muscle space that can cut off blood flow

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & muscles

What it is

Swelling or bleeding inside a tight space around muscles raises the pressure, which can squeeze blood vessels and nerves. It is often an emergency.

  • Lower leg or forearm
  • Often after an injury
  • Severe, tight pain
Which doctor
Emergency doctor and bone doctor (orthopaedic); go to hospital
Common in
After a fracture, crush injury or very tight cast; also after hard exercise
Contagious
No
Course
Sudden type needs urgent hospital care; a slower type from exercise can ease with rest

Common signs

  • Pain that is much worse than expected for the injury
  • Pain that gets worse when the muscle is moved or stretched
  • A swollen, tight, shiny feeling in the arm or leg
  • Numbness or tingling in the limb
  • Weakness, or skin that looks pale or feels cold

Why it happens

  • A broken bone, especially in the lower leg or forearm
  • A crush injury or a hard blow to a muscle
  • A cast, bandage or splint that is too tight
  • Bleeding or swelling after surgery or after blood flow returns to a limb
  • Very hard exercise (a slower, milder type)

See a doctor first if

  • Severe pain in a limb after an injury, surgery or a cast: go to hospital now
  • Pain that keeps rising even after rest or raising the limb: go to hospital now
  • A limb that is very tight and swollen, or has numbness, pins and needles or weakness
  • Pale, cold or bluish skin, or no feeling in the fingers or toes
  • A cast or bandage that feels too tight: do not wait, get it checked now

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Treat severe limb pain after an injury as urgent and go to hospital
  • Keep still and do not massage or press hard on the limb while you go
  • If you have a cast, ask the hospital to check it when it feels tight
  • After recovery, return to hard exercise gradually and listen to your body

Tell your therapist

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
