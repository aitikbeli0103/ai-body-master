Allergic purpura (small bleeding spots)

Small red-purple spots on the skin from inflamed small blood vessels, sometimes with sore joints and belly pain

ConditionAllergiesSkinBlood

What it is

Small blood vessels in the skin, and sometimes in the joints, gut and kidneys, become inflamed and leak a little blood, causing red-purple spots that do not fade when pressed.

  • Small blood vessels in skin
  • Mostly children
  • Red-purple spots, sore joints
Which doctor
Family doctor or children's doctor (paediatrician); kidney specialist if needed
Common in
Mostly children, but adults can get it too; often follows a cold or sore throat
Contagious
No
Course
Often eases over weeks but can return; a doctor should follow it up

Common signs

More signs

  • Small red or purple spots, mostly on the legs, feet and bottom, that do not fade when pressed

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The body's defence system inflames small blood vessels, so a little blood leaks under the skin
  • It often starts after an infection such as a cold or sore throat
  • Sometimes it follows a medicine, an insect bite or a food, but often no clear trigger is found
  • It is most common in children, but adults can get it too

See a doctor first if

  • Spots that do not fade when pressed, with fever, stiff neck, drowsiness or feeling very unwell: call 999
  • Severe belly pain, vomiting, or blood in the stools or black stools: go to hospital now
  • Sudden pain or swelling in the testicles: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain, a severe headache or a nosebleed that does not stop: go to hospital now
  • Blood in the urine, very little urine, or puffy face, eyes or legs: see a doctor the same day
  • New red-purple spots on the skin, even if you feel well: see a doctor promptly
