Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)

Flat dark marks left on the skin after acne, a rash or an injury has calmed down

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

When the skin is inflamed or hurt, it can make extra colour (melanin) and leave flat brown or dark marks after the redness or spot has gone.

  • Anywhere on the skin
  • Often in deeper skin tones
  • Flat dark marks, no pain
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist), family doctor
Common in
People who have had acne, eczema or skin injuries; often in deeper skin tones
Contagious
No
Course
Often fades slowly over months, sometimes longer; strong sun can make it darker

Common signs

Marks left after acne

Types of dark spots

How dark spots form

Spots form when the skin makes pigment faster than it can renew itself. Sun protection every day is the most important care.

More signs

  • The mark is flat, not raised, and usually does not hurt
  • Marks look darker after time in the sun
  • Marks can stay for months after the original spot has gone

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Skin inflammation, such as acne, eczema, rashes, insect bites or irritation from harsh products
  • Picking, squeezing or scratching the skin
  • Deeper skin tones are more likely to get these marks, and they may last longer

See a doctor first if

  • A dark mark that is new, growing, changing colour or shape, has an uneven edge or bleeds: see a doctor soon
  • Dark patches that appear with no earlier spot, rash or injury: see a doctor
  • The skin is still red, sore, swollen or leaking pus: see a doctor, as the first problem may still be active
  • Skin that stings, burns or gets thinner after a cream or skin-lightening product: ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • Marks that do not fade after many months, keep getting darker, or upset you a lot: see a doctor for advice

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Work with your doctor on the first skin problem (such as acne or eczema) so new marks do not form
  • Avoid skin-lightening creams of unknown ingredients or from unregistered sellers; ask a doctor or pharmacist first

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • A severe allergy

What are the common signs of Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH)?

Why does Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation (PIH) happen?
