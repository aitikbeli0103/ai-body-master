Manic episode

A spell of very high mood or energy, or irritability, with racing thoughts and little need for sleep

ConditionTold by a doctor: moodWhole body

What it is

For days or longer, mood and energy rise far above normal. Thoughts race, sleep feels unneeded and judgement can slip. It needs a doctor's care, and getting help early makes things safer.

  • Energy, mood and thinking
  • Lasts days, weeks or longer
  • Full of energy, restless
Which doctor
Family doctor or psychiatrist; hospital if it is severe or unsafe
Common in
People with bipolar disorder; sometimes linked to drugs, some medicines or illness
Contagious
No
Course
Often lasts a week or more; getting help early can make it safer and easier to manage

Common signs

More signs

  • Much more energy than usual, and feeling you need little or no sleep
  • Talking very fast, jumping between ideas, thoughts racing
  • Feeling very sure of yourself, very happy, or very easily annoyed
  • Risky choices, such as spending a lot of money or driving fast

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Most often part of bipolar disorder
  • Alcohol and drugs can bring one on
  • Some medicines, or illnesses such as an overactive thyroid, can cause a similar state; tell your doctor
  • Not following your doctor's plan for mood medicine can make an episode more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or others, or of ending your life: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Seeing or hearing things that are not there, or strong beliefs that are clearly untrue: go to hospital now
  • Hardly sleeping for several nights, or not eating or drinking: see a doctor today
  • Dangerous behaviour, such as reckless driving, giving away savings or getting into fights: get help now
  • Very confused, or unable to look after yourself: go to hospital now
  • A first episode like this, even if it feels good: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Reach out to a doctor early; mania can feel good, but help keeps you safer
  • Put big decisions about money, work or relationships on hold until you have talked to someone you trust
  • Avoid alcohol and drugs, and try to keep regular times for sleep and meals
  • Family and friends can help by staying calm, not arguing, and encouraging a visit to the doctor

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Epilepsy or fits

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
