Haemolytic jaundice

Yellow skin and eyes caused by red blood cells breaking down faster than the liver can clear

ConditionBloodBlood

What it is

When red blood cells break down too fast, a yellow substance called bilirubin builds up in the blood and turns the skin and eyes yellow.

  • Blood and skin
  • Can affect babies and adults
  • Yellow skin and eyes
Which doctor
Family doctor, blood doctor; children's doctor for babies
Common in
Newborn babies, and people with blood conditions that break down red cells
Contagious
No, though an infection behind it may spread
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor will find out why it is happening

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The immune system attacking red blood cells
  • In newborns, a different blood group from the mother, or G6PD deficiency
  • Infections or some medicines

See a doctor first if

  • A baby who looks yellow in the first days or is feeding badly: see a doctor promptly
  • Chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting: call 999
  • Confusion or being very sleepy and hard to wake
  • Fever with shivering, or dark urine and pale stools
  • Severe pain in the belly, especially the upper left

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

What is Haemolytic jaundice?

What are the common signs of Haemolytic jaundice?

Yellow skin and eyes · Pale, tired look · Tired all the time · Short of breath on stairs
