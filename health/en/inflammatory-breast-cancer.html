Inflammatory breast cancer

A rare, fast-growing type of breast cancer that makes the breast look red, swollen and warm

ConditionCancer & tumoursChest

What it is

Cancer cells block tiny channels in the skin of the breast, so the breast can turn red, swollen and warm within weeks, often without a clear lump, and may look like an infection.

  • The breast and armpit
  • Mostly women, rarely men
  • Red, swollen, warm breast
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a breast specialist or cancer specialist (oncologist)
Common in
Mostly women; it is rare, and men can also be affected
Contagious
No
Course
Can change over weeks, so early checking matters; your care team explains the plan

Common signs

More signs

  • A breast that becomes red, swollen and heavy, often within weeks
  • Skin that looks pitted or dimpled, like an orange peel
  • The breast feels warm, tender, sore or itchy

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Most cases are in women, but men can also get it, though rarely
  • It is not an infection, and it is not caused by anything you did

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or chest pain: call 999
  • A breast that quickly turns red, swollen or warm, with or without a lump: see a doctor promptly
  • Breast skin that looks like an orange peel, or a nipple that turns inward
  • Redness or swelling that does not improve after the doctor's first treatment: go back to your doctor
  • A new lump in the armpit or above the collarbone
  • New bone pain, a cough that will not go away, or weight loss without trying

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Follow the plan from your cancer care team and keep all appointments
  • Ask your team about pain, skin care, food and emotional support
  • If the breast is sore, wear soft, loose clothes
  • Ask your team before using any cream, supplement or herbal product
  • Never stop treatment on your own; talk to your team first

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Weak bones

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
