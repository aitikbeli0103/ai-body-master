Lymphoblastic lymphoma

A fast-growing cancer of immature white blood cells in the lymph system

ConditionCancer & tumoursBlood

What it is

Lymphoblastic lymphoma is a cancer of young white blood cells (lymphoblasts). It often shows as swollen glands, and a doctor needs to look into it promptly.

  • Lymph glands and blood
  • Often children, young adults
  • Swollen glands, tiredness
Which doctor
Blood doctor (haematologist), cancer doctor
Common in
Children, teenagers and young adults; also seen in older adults
Contagious
No
Course
It can grow fast, so early review matters; the care team can explain it

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Changes happen in young white blood cells as they grow
  • A weak body defence, or some rare inherited conditions, can raise the risk
  • It is not caused by anything the person did

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, a cough that is getting worse, or a swollen face or neck: call 999
  • High fever or chills, especially during treatment: go to hospital now
  • Bleeding that will not stop, or many new bruises
  • A new lump in the neck, armpit or groin that does not go away
  • Night sweats and weight loss without trying
  • A child who is very pale, very tired or very unwell: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Lymphoblastic lymphoma?

A fast-growing cancer of immature white blood cells in the lymph system. Lymphoblastic lymphoma is a cancer of young white blood cells (lymphoblasts). It often shows as swollen glands, and a doctor needs to look into it promptly.

What are the common signs of Lymphoblastic lymphoma?

Swollen glands in the neck · Fever, feeling hot · Soaking sweats at night · Losing weight without trying · Tired all the time

Why does Lymphoblastic lymphoma happen?

The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Lymphoblastic lymphoma?

See your doctor for check-ups · Wash your hands often · Rest well
