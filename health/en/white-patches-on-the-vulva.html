White patches on the vulva

Pale, white or thickened patches of skin on the vulva, which need a doctor to find the cause

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

White or pale patches can appear on the skin of the vulva, often with itching or soreness, and a doctor needs to look at them to find the cause.

  • Skin of the vulva
  • Any age; often after menopause
  • Itching, soreness, pale skin
Which doctor
Family doctor first, then a gynaecologist or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Women, most often after menopause; sometimes girls and younger women
Contagious
No, the common causes do not spread to other people
Course
Often long-lasting and may come and go; regular check-ups are important

Common signs

More signs

  • White, pale or shiny patches of skin on the vulva
  • Thick, whitish skin that can follow long-term scratching

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some are long-term skin conditions, thought to be linked to the body's defence (immune) system
  • Long-term itching and scratching can thicken the skin and turn it white
  • Irritation from scented soaps, wipes or tight clothes can make it worse
  • Less often, changes in the skin cells that only a doctor can check

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding, or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • A lump, open sore or growth that does not go away, or a patch that bleeds or changes
  • Any new white, red or dark patch on the vulva that does not go away: see a doctor soon
  • Bleeding after menopause or after sex
  • Itching so strong that it stops sleep, or pain that makes sex, walking or sitting hard
  • A girl with white patches, soreness or bleeding: see a doctor promptly
