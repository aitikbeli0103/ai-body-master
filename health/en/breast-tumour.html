Breast tumour

A lump of extra cells in the breast; many are not cancer, but some are, so it needs checking

ConditionCancer & tumoursChest

What it is

A breast tumour is a lump of extra cells in the breast. Many are not cancer, but some are, so a doctor needs to check it with a scan or a tissue sample.

  • In the breast
  • Mostly women
  • A lump you can feel
Which doctor
Family doctor first; breast clinic or breast surgeon
Common in
Mostly women, at any age; men can be affected less often
Contagious
No
Course
Some are only watched with regular check-ups; others need an operation or cancer care

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain or tenderness in some people, though many lumps do not hurt
  • Fluid from the nipple, or dimpled or red skin on the breast

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Earlier radiotherapy to the chest, or some inherited gene changes
  • Alcohol, being overweight after menopause, and little exercise are linked to breast cancer

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Fever with a hot, red, very painful breast: see a doctor the same day
  • A new lump or thickening in the breast or armpit, even if it does not hurt
  • A lump that is hard, fixed or growing, or skin that is dimpled or red
  • Nipple turning inward, rash on the nipple, or blood-stained fluid
  • Bone pain, weight loss or a cough that will not go away

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • If it is only being watched, report any change in size, shape or feel without waiting
  • Ask your team about pain, food and emotional support, and talk to someone you trust

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
