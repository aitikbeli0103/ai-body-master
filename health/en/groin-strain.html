Groin strain

A pulled or torn muscle on the inner thigh near the groin

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legBones & muscles

What it is

The muscles on the inner thigh are stretched too far or torn a little. It often happens during sport or a sudden twist, and causes pain in the groin.

  • Groin and inner thigh
  • Often from sport
  • Sharp or pulling pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, sports doctor or physiotherapist
Common in
Active people, football and running players, and anyone after a sudden twist
Contagious
No
Course
A mild strain often settles in a few weeks; a bad one can take longer

Common signs

More signs

  • A sudden pull or snap felt during movement
  • Pain when bringing the legs together or lifting the knee

Why it happens

  • Sudden sprinting, kicking or changing direction
  • Not warming up before sport
  • Tight or weak thigh muscles
  • Slipping or doing the splits by accident
  • Tiredness, or going back to sport too soon after an earlier strain

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot put weight on the leg or walk: see a doctor first
  • A bulge, a deep gap or heavy bruising in the groin
  • Pain with fever, or a hot, red swollen groin
  • A swollen, painful calf, or sudden chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Pain that is not getting better after a few weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest from sport and anything that brings on the pain
  • Walk gently as pain allows, and slowly build up again
  • Warm up and stretch before sport

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
