Rotator cuff injury

Strain, irritation or a tear in the muscles and tendons that hold the shoulder joint steady

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legs

What it is

The rotator cuff is the group of muscles and tendons around the shoulder that lets you lift and turn the arm; when it is strained or torn, the shoulder aches and feels weak.

  • Deep in the shoulder
  • More common with age
  • Ache, weak when lifting the arm
Which doctor
Family doctor; bone and joint doctor (orthopaedic doctor)
Common in
Older adults; people who often lift or reach overhead
Contagious
No
Course
Often eases over weeks to months with care; a large tear may need a bone doctor's help

Common signs

More signs

  • Trouble with things like combing your hair or putting on a shirt
  • Clicking or crackling when you move the shoulder
  • After a sudden tear: sharp pain, a snap feeling and a very weak arm

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A shoulder that was injured before, or weak shoulder muscles
  • Sometimes there is no clear cause

See a doctor first if

  • Shoulder or arm pain with chest pain, trouble breathing, a cold sweat or feeling faint: call 999
  • Shoulder out of shape, or you cannot move the arm after a fall: go to hospital now
  • A sudden loss of strength or a snap feeling when lifting or after a fall: see a doctor quickly
  • Hot, red, swollen shoulder, especially with a fever: see a doctor now
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness spreading down the arm into the hand: see a doctor soon
  • Pain that wakes you every night, or is no better after a few weeks of rest: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest from the movements that hurt, but do not keep the arm completely still for long
  • Sleep on your back or on the other side, with a pillow to support the sore arm
  • Ask your doctor or physiotherapist for safe exercises, and your doctor or pharmacist about pain medicine

Tell your therapist

  • Recent shoulder injury or surgery
  • Hot, red or swollen shoulder
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Weak bones

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Rotator cuff injury?

Strain, irritation or a tear in the muscles and tendons that hold the shoulder joint steady. The rotator cuff is the group of muscles and tendons around the shoulder that lets you lift and turn the arm; when it is strained or torn, the shoulder aches and feels weak.

What are the common signs of Rotator cuff injury?

Pain in the shoulder · Cannot lift the arm

Why does Rotator cuff injury happen?

Wear and tear with age · Heavy or repeated work · An injury or fall

How can I look after myself with Rotator cuff injury?

Avoid heavy lifting · A cold pack on the sore spot
