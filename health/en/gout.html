Gout

Sudden, painful joint attacks caused by uric acid crystals in the joint

ConditionBlood sugar, fats, uric acid & thyroidArms & legs

What it is

Too much uric acid in the blood can form tiny sharp crystals in a joint, causing sudden attacks of strong pain, redness and swelling, often in the big toe.

  • Joints, often the big toe
  • Men, and women after menopause
  • Sudden, severe joint pain
Which doctor
Family doctor; joint doctor (rheumatologist)
Common in
Men, and women after menopause; people who are overweight or have kidney problems
Contagious
No
Course
Each attack usually settles in days to weeks, but it can come back without long-term care

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden, severe pain in one joint, most often the big toe, often starting at night
  • It can also affect the ankle, knee, wrist or fingers
  • Pain is usually worst in the first day or two, then slowly eases
  • Between attacks the joint can feel normal

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Too much uric acid in the blood forms tiny crystals inside a joint
  • The body makes too much uric acid, or the kidneys do not remove enough of it
  • Lots of red meat, organ meats, seafood, beer, other alcohol or sugary drinks

See a doctor first if

  • A hot, red, swollen joint with fever or chills: go to hospital the same day, as it may be an infection in the joint
  • Severe pain in the side or back that comes in waves, with vomiting or blood in the urine: go to hospital now
  • Your first-ever attack of sudden, severe joint pain and swelling: see a doctor to find the cause
  • You cannot move the joint or stand on it, or the pain is not easing after a few days
  • Attacks that keep coming back, or hard lumps under the skin near the joints: see a doctor about long-term care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Drink plenty of water through the day, and limit alcohol (especially beer) and sugary drinks
  • Cut down on red meat, organ meats and seafood, and avoid crash diets
  • If attacks keep coming, ask your doctor about blood tests and long-term medicine to help prevent them

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • High blood pressure
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
