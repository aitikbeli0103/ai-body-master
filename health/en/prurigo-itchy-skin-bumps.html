Prurigo (itchy skin bumps)

A long-lasting skin condition with very itchy bumps that get worse the more they are scratched

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

In prurigo, the skin comes up in very itchy bumps; scratching makes the bumps firmer and the itch stronger, so it turns into a cycle that is hard to stop.

  • Arms, legs and body
  • Adults, sometimes children
  • Very itchy, firm bumps
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist), family doctor
Common in
Adults, sometimes children; often with eczema, allergies or other long-term illness
Contagious
No
Course
Often long-lasting and may come and go; a doctor can help break the itch-scratch cycle

Common signs

More signs

  • Scratch marks, scabs and sometimes small bleeding spots
  • With scratching, bumps can turn rough, thick or darker than the skin around
  • Often on the arms, legs and body, where your hands can reach

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Dry skin, eczema or allergies can make the skin very itchy
  • Insect bites, especially in children who react strongly to them
  • Some long-term illnesses, such as kidney or liver problems, can cause itching all over

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden itchy rash with swelling of the lips, face or tongue, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Hot, swollen, oozing or crusted skin, or a fever: it may be infected, so see a doctor promptly
  • Itching all over with yellow skin or eyes, weight loss, or tiredness: see a doctor to look for other causes
  • Others at home are itchy too: see a doctor, as some itchy rashes (such as scabies) spread
  • A bump that grows quickly, bleeds easily or does not close: see a doctor to have it checked
  • Feeling very low or hopeless because of the itch, or thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
