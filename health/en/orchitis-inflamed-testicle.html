Orchitis (inflamed testicle)

Inflammation of one or both testicles, often caused by an infection

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

A testicle becomes swollen, sore and tender, usually from a virus such as mumps, or from germs that spread from the urine tract or the tube behind the testicle.

  • One or both testicles
  • Teens and adult men
  • Pain, swelling, often fever
Which doctor
Family doctor or urologist; go to hospital if the pain is sudden and severe
Common in
Teenage and adult men; people not vaccinated against mumps; sexually active men
Contagious
Some types can be: mumps spreads by coughs and sneezes; some germs pass during sex
Course
Often settles over days to weeks with a doctor's care; some swelling may last longer

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain and swelling in one or both testicles, which feel heavy and tender
  • The scrotum may look red and feel warm

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Germs that spread from the bladder, urine tube, prostate or the tube behind the testicle
  • Sexually passed infections such as chlamydia or gonorrhoea
  • A urine catheter, or a recent test or operation on the urine tract
  • Sometimes no cause is found

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle, especially in a boy or teen: go to hospital now (it may be twisted)
  • High fever with shaking chills, confusion or feeling faint: go to hospital now
  • Cannot pass urine, or only a few drops come out: go to hospital now
  • A hot, red, tight scrotum that is getting worse, or swelling that does not go down
  • Swollen cheeks or jaw with fever (possible mumps): see a doctor promptly and stay away from others
  • A hard lump in the testicle, or pain and swelling that are not better after a few days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor to find the cause, and take any medicine exactly as your doctor tells you
  • If mumps or a sexually passed infection is the cause, avoid close contact and sex until your doctor says it is safe

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
