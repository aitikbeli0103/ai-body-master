Dislocated jaw joint (temporomandibular)

The jaw joint in front of the ear slips out of place and may lock

ConditionMouth & teethBones & muscles

What it is

The joint that connects the jaw to the skull can slip forward out of its socket. The jaw may feel locked open, with pain near the ear.

  • Jaw joint near the ear
  • After a wide yawn or bite
  • Jaw locked open, sore
Which doctor
Hospital emergency doctor; dentist or jaw (oral) surgeon
Common in
Adults; people with clicking or loose jaw joints
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor to put the joint back; it may happen again

Common signs

More signs

  • The jaw feels locked and the mouth will not close
  • Teeth do not meet properly
  • Muscle spasm and soreness along the cheek

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Opening the mouth very wide, such as yawning or taking a big bite
  • Loose jaw joints or long-standing jaw joint problems
  • Long dental work with the mouth held open

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing: call 999
  • A blow to the head or face with fainting, vomiting or heavy bleeding: go to hospital now
  • Jaw locked open and cannot close: go to hospital
  • Numbness of the face, or a bite that suddenly feels wrong
  • Repeated slips of the jaw

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Do not force the jaw back yourself
  • Rest the jaw and keep the mouth gently supported
  • Eat soft food for a while after the doctor has treated it
  • Avoid yawning wide, shouting and chewing gum
  • Ask your doctor or dentist how to protect the joint

Tell your therapist

  • Recent neck injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
