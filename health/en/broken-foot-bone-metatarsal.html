Broken foot bone (metatarsal)

A crack or break in one of the long bones in the middle of the foot, just behind the toes

ConditionAnkle & footArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

A fall, a heavy object dropped on the foot, a twist, or repeated pressure can crack or break a long bone in the middle of the foot, causing pain, swelling and trouble walking.

  • Middle of the foot
  • Injury, or repeated pressure
  • Pain when walking, swelling
Which doctor
Hospital emergency doctor; bone doctor (orthopaedic doctor)
Common in
People after a foot injury; runners, dancers, and people who suddenly walk much more
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor's check; recovery often takes weeks to months, and some need an operation

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain on the top, middle or outer side of the foot, worse when you walk or stand
  • A very tender spot when you touch the bone
  • A stress crack: pain that builds up slowly with activity and eases with rest

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A heavy object dropped on the foot, or a crush injury
  • Repeated pressure from running, jumping, or a sudden rise in walking or standing
  • Shoes that are worn out, too tight or do not support the foot
  • Weak bones make a break more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Bone through the skin, heavy bleeding, or a foot crushed by a heavy object: call 999
  • Sudden chest pain or trouble breathing (it can be a blood clot in the lung): call 999
  • Toes or foot numb, pale, blue or cold: go to hospital now
  • Cannot put weight on the foot, it looks bent, or it is very swollen after an injury: go to hospital now
  • Calf pain, swelling, warmth or redness may be a blood clot: see a doctor now
  • Foot pain that does not ease with rest, or any foot injury or wound if you have diabetes: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Raise the foot on pillows, and use a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for short times
  • Follow your doctor's advice on the boot, stiff shoe or cast, and go back to walking slowly

Tell your therapist

  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Weak bones
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Broken, red or sore skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
