Haemorrhagic fever

A group of severe virus illnesses that cause high fever and bleeding problems

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Different viruses can cause sudden high fever and weakness, then bleeding from the gums or nose or under the skin; severe cases can be life-threatening and need hospital care at once.

  • Whole body
  • Bites, animals or travel
  • High fever, bleeding signs
Which doctor
Hospital emergency department; infectious disease doctor
Common in
People living in or back from outbreak areas, or near rodents, ticks or mosquitoes
Contagious
Some types can be, through blood or body fluids; others spread by bites or animals
Course
Can become life-threatening quickly and needs urgent hospital care

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Several different viruses, for example dengue, hantavirus and Ebola
  • Bites from infected mosquitoes or ticks, depending on the virus
  • In some types, contact with the blood or body fluids of a sick person
  • Living in or travelling to an area where the virus is found

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with bleeding from the nose or gums, red spots or bruises, or blood in vomit, stool or urine: call 999
  • Cold, clammy skin, fast heartbeat, dizziness, fainting, confusion or great weakness: call 999
  • Hard to breathe, severe belly pain, or vomiting that will not stop: go to hospital now
  • Little or no urine, or too unwell to drink: go to hospital now
  • High fever after visiting an outbreak area, or contact with a sick person, rodents or ticks: go to hospital

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Go to hospital early if the signs fit; do not try to manage this at home
  • On the way, take small sips of fluid if you can, and tell staff where you have been and who you were near
  • Ask a doctor before taking any painkiller, because some can make bleeding worse
  • Do not touch the blood, vomit or body fluids of a sick person with bare hands; wash hands with soap after any contact
  • Guard against mosquito and tick bites, and keep rats and their droppings away from food and the home
