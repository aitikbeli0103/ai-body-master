Body dysmorphic disorder

Being very worried about a small or unseen flaw in your looks, and it takes up much of your day

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressWhole body

What it is

A person spends a lot of time worrying about a part of their body, even when others hardly notice it. This can bring distress and affect work, school and friendships. Kind help is available.

  • Thoughts about how you look
  • Often starts in the teens
  • Distress about looks
Which doctor
Family doctor, psychiatrist or psychologist
Common in
Teens and adults, women and men
Contagious
No
Course
Often long-lasting without help, but support from a doctor can make it easier to live with

Common signs

More signs

  • Spending a lot of time thinking about a part of the body
  • Checking the mirror again and again, or avoiding mirrors
  • Hiding the area with clothes, make-up or hats
  • Avoiding people, photos or social events

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Teasing, bullying or hurtful comments about looks in the past
  • Other anxiety, low mood, or a family history of these
  • Pressure from social media or from people around you

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Hurting yourself, or picking at or cutting the skin to change how you look
  • Not going to work, school or out of the house because of worry about looks
  • Low mood or hopeless feelings that last for weeks
  • Looking for risky or repeated procedures to change your looks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Talk to someone you trust about how you feel
  • Try to limit mirror checking and comparing yourself on social media
  • Spend time on things you enjoy that are not about looks
  • Ask your doctor about talking therapy and support; think carefully before any cosmetic procedure

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • A heart condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
