Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)

A slow blood cancer of white blood cells called lymphocytes

ConditionCancer & tumoursBlood

What it is

CLL happens when the body makes too many abnormal lymphocytes, a kind of white blood cell. It often grows slowly and may cause no signs at first.

  • Blood and glands
  • Mostly older adults
  • Tired, swollen glands
Which doctor
Blood doctor (haematologist), cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Mostly older adults, often found by a routine blood test
Contagious
No
Course
Often very slow; some people need only regular check-ups for a long time

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Having a close family member with the same illness
  • Contact with some chemicals over many years may play a part

See a doctor first if

  • Bleeding that will not stop, or vomiting or coughing blood: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain or fainting: call 999
  • Fever, chills or an infection that keeps coming back
  • Glands in the neck, armpit or groin that keep getting bigger
  • Weight loss, drenching night sweats or new pain in the left upper belly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)?

A slow blood cancer of white blood cells called lymphocytes. CLL happens when the body makes too many abnormal lymphocytes, a kind of white blood cell. It often grows slowly and may cause no signs at first.

What are the common signs of Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)?

Tired all the time · Swollen glands in the neck · Soaking sweats at night · Losing weight without trying · Fever, feeling hot

Why does Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL) happen?

The cause is not fully known · Getting older

How can I look after myself with Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)?

See your doctor for check-ups · Wash your hands often · Rest well
