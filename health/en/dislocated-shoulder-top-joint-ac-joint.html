Dislocated shoulder-top joint (AC joint)

An injury where the collarbone slips away from the top of the shoulder blade

ConditionShoulder, arm & handChestArms & legs

What it is

The joint at the top of the shoulder, where the collarbone meets the shoulder blade, is pulled apart. It is often caused by a fall onto the shoulder.

  • Top of the shoulder
  • Falls, contact sports
  • Pain, a bump on top
Which doctor
Family doctor or emergency; bone doctor (orthopaedic)
Common in
Active people; cyclists and contact-sport players; after falls
Contagious
No
Course
Mild ones often settle in weeks; severe ones may need more care

Common signs

More signs

  • A bump or step on the top of the shoulder
  • Swelling and bruising near the collarbone

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A fall onto an outstretched arm

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or trouble breathing after a hard fall: call 999
  • A hand or arm that is numb, pale or cold: go to hospital now
  • A shoulder that looks very out of shape, or skin that is broken
  • Cannot lift the arm at all
  • Pain that is getting worse after some days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Avoid lifting, pushing and sleeping on that shoulder
  • Slowly return to movement when your doctor says it is safe

Tell your therapist

  • Recent shoulder injury or surgery
  • Hot, red or swollen shoulder
  • Weak bones
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Numb or tingling arm

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
