Thin, sparse hair

Hair that has become thinner, with less volume or the scalp showing through

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsHeadSkin

What it is

There is less hair than before, so the parting looks wider, the ponytail gets thinner or the scalp shows through, and there are many possible causes.

  • Scalp and hair
  • Both men and women
  • Less hair, wider parting
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) for hair and scalp problems
Common in
Adults of any age; more with age, after childbirth or around menopause
Contagious
No, though a scalp fungal infection (a less common cause) can spread
Course
Depends on the cause; some kinds improve once the cause is dealt with

Common signs

More signs

  • A wider parting, or a thinner ponytail than before
  • The scalp shows through more, on top or at the front
  • Hair that feels finer or weaker than before
  • A hairline that is moving back

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Stress, a serious illness, surgery or a high fever some months earlier
  • Tight hairstyles, hair colouring, heat, or some medicines (ask your doctor or pharmacist)

See a doctor first if

  • Hair that falls out suddenly or in clumps, or in round bald patches
  • A red, scaly, itchy, painful or crusted scalp
  • Thin hair with great tiredness, feeling cold, weight change or looking pale
  • Women: thin hair with irregular periods, acne or new hair on the face
  • Hair loss that began after a new medicine: ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • A child with patchy hair loss: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Be gentle: avoid tight hairstyles, rough brushing, and too much heat or colouring
  • Wash with a mild shampoo and keep the scalp clean and comfortable
  • Be careful with unproven hair products and supplements; ask your doctor or pharmacist first
  • Get enough sleep, and ask a doctor to look for a cause if it keeps going

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Broken, red or sore skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
