Erosive athlete's foot (tinea pedis)

A fungal skin infection between the toes with soggy, white, sore, raw skin

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

Erosive athlete's foot is a fungal infection that usually starts between the toes. The skin turns white and soggy, peels away and can leave red, raw, sore patches.

  • Between the toes
  • Warm, sweaty, damp feet
  • Itchy, soggy, sore skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist); pharmacist for advice
Common in
Adults; people with sweaty feet, tight shoes, shared showers or diabetes
Contagious
Yes, it can spread by touch, damp floors, shared towels or shoes
Course
Often needs medicine from a doctor or pharmacist; it can come back if feet stay damp

Common signs

More signs

  • Painful cracks between the toes
  • A bad smell from the feet
  • Redness or peeling that can spread to the sole or side of the foot

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A fungus that grows well on warm, damp skin, especially between the toes
  • Sweaty feet, tight shoes, or socks that stay damp
  • Walking barefoot in public showers, changing rooms and pools
  • Diabetes or a weak immune system can make it more likely

See a doctor first if

  • A red, hot, swollen foot or leg with pus, red streaks up the leg or fever: go to hospital now
  • Any foot sore, crack or infection if you have diabetes or less feeling in your feet: see a doctor soon
  • Very painful, oozing cracks, yellow crusts or a strong smell, which can mean a bacterial infection as well
  • The rash spreads to the nails, groin, hands or other parts of the body
  • A weak immune system (for example from another illness or medicine): see a doctor early
  • No improvement after using the medicine as advised, or it keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash your feet every day and dry carefully between the toes, then let them air
  • Wear clean cotton socks, change them when damp, and choose shoes or sandals that let your feet breathe
  • Do not scratch the skin, and do not use leftover strong creams without asking your doctor or pharmacist

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
