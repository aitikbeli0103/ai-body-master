Cold sores (herpes labialis)

Small, painful blisters on or around the lips, caused by the herpes simplex virus

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

A common virus can cause tingling, then small fluid-filled blisters on or near the lips, which crust over and clear up by themselves but may come back later.

  • On or around the lips
  • Flares up when unwell or tired
  • Tingling, then painful blisters
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Children and adults; many people carry the virus without knowing
Contagious
Yes, by kissing or sharing cups, towels or lip balm, especially when sores are present
Course
Usually clears up on its own in one to two weeks, but can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Tingling, itching or burning on the lip a day or so before the sore shows
  • The blisters burst, weep and then form a yellow-brown crust
  • Pain or tenderness around the sore

Why it happens

Other causes

  • After the first infection the virus stays in the body and can wake up again later
  • A weak immune system or a skin injury on the lip can make it more likely

See a doctor first if

  • High fever with severe headache, stiff neck or confusion: call 999
  • A baby with blisters or fever, or a baby who is not feeding well: go to hospital now
  • Eye pain, redness, tearing, light sensitivity or blurry vision, or blisters near the eye: go to hospital today
  • Blisters spreading over a wide area, especially with eczema or a weak immune system: see a doctor today
  • Sores that last more than two weeks, are very painful or large, or come back often
  • Cannot drink or eat because of the pain, or signs of dehydration

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • While sores are present, do not kiss anyone or share cups, towels, lip balm or razors
  • Keep away from babies, people with eczema and people with weak immunity until the sores have gone

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
