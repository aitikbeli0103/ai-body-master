Hip dysplasia

A hip socket that is too shallow, so the hip joint does not fit well

ConditionBones, joints & spinePelvis

What it is

In hip dysplasia the socket of the hip is shallow, so the thigh bone does not sit snugly. It can be found in babies, or only later in adults.

  • Hip joint
  • Babies, or adults later
  • Hip ache, limp
Which doctor
Family doctor, paediatrician for babies, bone doctor (orthopaedic)
Common in
Babies, more often girls; runs in some families
Contagious
No
Course
Best checked early; a bone doctor can explain what care is needed

Common signs

More signs

  • In a baby: one leg looks shorter, or skin folds on the thigh look uneven
  • In a baby: the hip feels stiff or the legs do not open out evenly

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The hip socket formed shallow while the baby grew in the womb
  • A baby lying in the breech (feet-first) position before birth
  • Tight swaddling with the legs held straight may add to the risk

See a doctor first if

  • A baby with one leg that looks shorter, a stiff hip, or uneven leg folds: see a doctor promptly
  • A child who limps or walks late, or a child with a swaying walk
  • Sudden hip pain after a fall, or you cannot put weight on the leg: go to hospital
  • Hip pain with fever, or a child who is very unwell
  • Hip pain in an adult that keeps getting worse or limits walking

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take your baby to all routine checks so the hips are looked at early
  • Let a baby's legs bend and move freely; ask a nurse about safe carrying and swaddling
  • Follow the bone doctor's plan and keep all visits

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Weak leg or foot
