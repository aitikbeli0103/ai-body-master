Breast lumpiness (fibrocystic changes)

Lumpy, tender breast tissue that often changes with the monthly cycle

ConditionWomen's healthChest

What it is

Fibrocystic changes are common, non-cancer changes in breast tissue. The breasts can feel lumpy, thick or sore, often more so before a period.

  • Often in both breasts
  • Women before menopause
  • Lumpy, sore, comes and goes
Which doctor
Family doctor, breast clinic or women's doctor (gynaecologist)
Common in
Women who still have periods, mostly before menopause
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes with the monthly cycle and usually eases after menopause

Common signs

  • Breasts that feel lumpy, thick or rope-like, often on both sides
  • Tenderness, aching or a heavy feeling in the breasts
  • Symptoms often get worse before a period and ease after it
  • Lumps that change in size over the month, some soft like small cysts

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Breast tissue reacts to these hormones by swelling and becoming tender
  • More common in the years before menopause
  • Some hormone medicines can make breasts more tender; ask your doctor or pharmacist

See a doctor first if

  • A hot, red, painful breast with fever and chills: see a doctor today, or go to hospital now if very unwell
  • A new lump that feels different, stays after your period or keeps growing
  • Blood or clear fluid coming from the nipple on its own
  • Dimpling, puckering or redness of the skin, or a nipple turning inward
  • A lump or swelling in the armpit

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wear a well-fitting, supportive bra, including when you exercise
  • Note when the soreness and lumps come and go, and tell your doctor
  • Learn how your breasts normally feel and report any change that is new

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
