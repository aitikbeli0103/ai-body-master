Fainting at the sight of blood (blood phobia)

Feeling faint or dizzy when seeing blood or injuries

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressBlood

What it is

For some people, seeing blood, a needle or an injury makes the heart rate and blood pressure drop suddenly, so they feel faint or may pass out.

  • Triggered by blood
  • Can run in families
  • Faint, pale, sweaty
Which doctor
Family doctor; psychologist if it limits daily life
Common in
Children and adults; often seen in several family members
Contagious
No
Course
Often long-lasting but can improve with gentle practice and support

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The body overreacts: heart rate and blood pressure drop suddenly
  • A frightening experience with blood or needles in the past
  • Standing still, hunger or heat can make it worse

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting with chest pain, trouble breathing or a racing heart: call 999
  • Fainting with no blood or fear around
  • A head injury, fits or confusion after fainting
  • You avoid needed blood tests, vaccines or dental care
  • Fainting again and again

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Sit or lie down at once when you feel faint
  • Look away and breathe slowly during blood tests or injections
  • Tell the nurse or doctor about your fear before you start
  • Eat and drink well before a medical visit
  • Ask a friend to go with you

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • High blood pressure
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
