Laryngitis (voice box inflammation)

Swelling of the voice box that makes the voice hoarse or weak

ConditionEars, nose & throatHeadNeck

What it is

The voice box (larynx) becomes irritated and swollen, so the voice turns hoarse, weak or may almost disappear. Most often it comes with a cold or from overusing the voice.

  • Voice box in the throat
  • Anyone, often with a cold
  • Hoarse or lost voice
Which doctor
Family doctor; ear-nose-throat doctor if it lasts
Common in
Anyone; singers, teachers, smokers and people with colds
Contagious
Some types can be, if caused by a cold or flu virus
Course
Short-term cases usually get better within a week or two

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Using the voice too much, such as shouting or singing

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, noisy breathing or blue lips: call 999 now
  • A child with a barking cough, noisy breathing or drooling: go to hospital now
  • Trouble swallowing or a very sore throat with high fever
  • Hoarse voice lasting more than two to three weeks
  • Coughing up blood, or a lump in the neck

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

What are the common signs of Laryngitis (voice box inflammation)?

Hoarse, weak voice · Sore, scratchy throat · Dry, tickly cough · Fever, feeling hot

Why does Laryngitis (voice box inflammation) happen?

Germs (infection) · Smoking · Acid coming up from the stomach

How can I look after myself with Laryngitis (voice box inflammation)?
