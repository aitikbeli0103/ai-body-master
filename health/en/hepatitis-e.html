Hepatitis E

Liver infection caused by the hepatitis E virus, usually from unclean food or water

ConditionLiver, gallbladder & pancreasAbdomen

What it is

The hepatitis E virus inflames the liver. It is often mild and may settle on its own, but it can be serious in pregnancy and in people with weak immunity.

  • Liver, upper right belly
  • Higher risk in pregnancy
  • Tired, sick, yellow skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; liver or infection doctor if needed
Common in
Anyone; more often where water is unclean or food is undercooked
Contagious
Yes, mainly via food or water with the virus; person to person is uncommon
Course
Many people get better within weeks; a doctor should check, especially in pregnancy

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Raw or undercooked meat such as pork or wild game, or raw shellfish
  • A weak immune system or an existing liver disease can make it more serious

See a doctor first if

  • Pregnant and feeling unwell, with yellow skin or eyes, vomiting or tiredness: go to hospital now
  • Confusion, very sleepy, or hard to wake: go to hospital now
  • Vomiting blood, black stools or bleeding that will not stop: call 999
  • Cannot keep fluids down, very dry mouth, or very little urine: go to hospital
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools: see a doctor today
  • A high fever, or a baby, child or older person who is unwell: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Hepatitis E?

Liver infection caused by the hepatitis E virus, usually from unclean food or water. The hepatitis E virus inflames the liver. It is often mild and may settle on its own, but it can be serious in pregnancy and in people with weak immunity.

What are the common signs of Hepatitis E?

Fever, feeling hot · Tired all the time · Feeling sick, nausea · No appetite · Pain in the upper belly · Yellow skin and eyes

Why does Hepatitis E happen?

Unsafe food or water · Contact with infected blood or needles

How can I look after myself with Hepatitis E?

Rest well · Wash your hands often · Cut down on alcohol · Ask your pharmacist
