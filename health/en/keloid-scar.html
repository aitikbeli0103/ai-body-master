Keloid scar

A raised, firm scar that grows bigger than the original cut or wound

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A keloid forms when the skin makes too much scar tissue after a cut, piercing, burn, pimple or surgery. The firm, raised lump grows past the edge of the original wound.

  • Skin; chest, shoulders, ears
  • Can run in families
  • Firm lump; may itch or hurt
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist), family doctor
Common in
Teens and young adults; darker skin tones; a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Often stays for years and may slowly grow; a doctor can explain options

Common signs

  • A smooth, firm, shiny lump or ridge that sticks out above the skin
  • Pink, red, purple or darker than the skin around it
  • It grows beyond the edge of the original cut or wound and may keep growing
  • It can itch or hurt when touched, and may feel tight
  • It may show up weeks or months after the skin was injured

Marks left after acne

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A skin injury: a cut, burn, pimple, piercing, insect bite or surgery
  • Some people's skin makes too much scar tissue; the exact reason is often not known
  • Places where the skin is stretched, like the chest, shoulders and upper back, are more at risk
  • A wound that gets infected or stays open for a long time

See a doctor first if

  • Redness spreading around it, heat, pus or fever: see a doctor promptly
  • A lump that grows fast, bleeds, or has a sore that does not close
  • A new lump that came without any earlier injury, or that you are unsure about
  • A scar that pulls the skin and limits movement of a joint, eyelid or mouth
  • Pain or itch that keeps getting worse, or a scar that upsets you

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Never cut, burn or freeze a keloid yourself: it may come back bigger
  • If you tend to get keloids, tell your doctor before any piercing, tattoo or surgery
  • If it itches, hurts or bothers you, ask a skin doctor about your options

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
