Filariasis

A slow, long-lasting infection with thread-like worms from mosquito bites that can cause very large swelling

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Thread-like worms passed on by mosquito bites live in the body's lymph vessels, and over many years they can block the flow of fluid, causing a leg, arm or other body part to swell.

  • Lymph vessels and glands
  • Spread by mosquito bites
  • Slowly swelling leg or arm
Which doctor
Family doctor first; an infectious disease doctor if needed
Common in
People who live for years in warm places where it occurs and mosquitoes are common
Contagious
No, not by touch or air; it spreads through bites of infected mosquitoes
Course
Often silent for years; swelling may slowly grow, so early checking matters

Common signs

More signs

  • Thick, hard, rough skin over the swollen part

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Thread-like worms (filarial worms) that live in the lymph vessels
  • Repeated mosquito bites over a long time in an affected area raise the chance of infection
  • The worms can damage the lymph vessels, so fluid builds up and the skin may thicken
  • Skin infections in a swollen limb can make the swelling worse over time

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or trouble breathing, especially with a suddenly swollen, painful leg: call 999
  • Fever with chills, confusion or feeling very unwell, with a hot, red, swollen limb: go to hospital now
  • Sudden pain or swelling in the testicles: go to hospital now
  • A hot, red, painful swelling or red streaks on the skin: see a doctor the same day
  • Skin that cracks, forms sores or smells bad, or swelling that keeps growing
  • New, slowly growing swelling of a leg, arm or genital area, or fever that keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • See a doctor early for a test and advice, even if the swelling is small
  • Wash the swollen area gently with soap and clean water every day, and dry it well, also between the toes
  • Raise the swollen leg or arm when resting, and move it gently as your doctor advises
  • Clean any cut, crack or bite in the skin straight away, and wear comfortable, protective shoes
  • Use mosquito nets and repellent, and clear standing water around the home

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection

What is Filariasis?
