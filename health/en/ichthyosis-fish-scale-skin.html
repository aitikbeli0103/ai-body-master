Ichthyosis (fish-scale skin)

A long-term skin condition with very dry, rough skin and scales that look like fish scales

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

In ichthyosis the skin cannot shed its dead cells or keep in water normally, so dry, thick scales build up that look like fish scales; most types are inherited and start in childhood.

  • Skin, mostly shins and arms
  • Often runs in families
  • Dry, rough, scaly skin
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist); children's doctor for a baby
Common in
Mostly inherited and seen in childhood; rarely starts in adults
Contagious
No
Course
Inherited types are usually lifelong; daily skin care can make life easier

Common signs

More signs

  • Scales are most often on the shins, arms and trunk
  • Thick, deeply lined palms and soles in some types
  • Skin often looks better in warm, humid weather and worse when it is cold and dry

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The skin sheds dead cells too slowly, so scales build up
  • The outer layer of skin loses water easily, so it becomes dry and rough
  • Rarely, it starts in adult life because of another medical problem, such as kidney or thyroid disease, or a medicine

See a doctor first if

  • A newborn baby with tight, shiny or cracked skin, or a skin-like covering: go to hospital now
  • Skin turning red and peeling over most of the body, with fever, chills or feeling very unwell: go to hospital now
  • Skin that is red, hot, very painful, weeping or crusted, which may be infected: see a doctor today
  • Dry, scaly skin that first appears in adult life, or comes with weight loss, night sweats or lumps: see a doctor
  • Deep, painful cracks that bleed, or dry, sore eyes or eyelids that turn outwards: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take short lukewarm baths or showers, not hot, and pat dry gently without scrubbing
  • Use a mild, soap-free cleanser, and avoid harsh soaps and rough scrubbing
  • Wear soft cotton clothes, protect your skin from strong sun, and keep cool in hot weather

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
