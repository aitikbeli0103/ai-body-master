Osgood-Schlatter disease

Painful bump below the kneecap in growing children and teens

ConditionBones, joints & spineArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

In a growing child or teen, the tendon pulls on the bone just below the kneecap. This can cause a sore, swollen bump, especially after running or jumping.

  • Just below the kneecap
  • Growing teens, sporty
  • Sore on running, jumping
Which doctor
Family doctor, child doctor, bone doctor (orthopaedic)
Common in
Active children and teens during growth spurts
Contagious
No
Course
Usually settles when growth slows down, but can take months

Common signs

More signs

  • A bony bump that may stick out below the knee
  • Pain that gets worse with running, jumping or kneeling
  • Pain that eases with rest
  • Mild swelling around the sore spot

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Fast growth in the bones during the teen years
  • Tight thigh muscles

See a doctor first if

  • Your child cannot put weight on the leg, or the knee looks bent or out of shape: go to hospital
  • Pain after a fall or hard knock to the knee
  • Fever with a red, hot, very swollen knee
  • Pain at night or at rest, or a limp that keeps getting worse
  • Pain that does not improve after some weeks: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Cut back on the running or jumping that brings the pain on
  • Choose gentler activities such as swimming or cycling if they do not hurt
  • Ask your doctor about safe stretches for the thigh muscles
  • Avoid kneeling directly on the sore spot

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
