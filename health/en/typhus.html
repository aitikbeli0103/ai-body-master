Typhus

Infection spread by lice, fleas or mites that causes fever and a rash

ConditionInfectionsSkin

What it is

Typhus is caused by germs carried by lice, fleas or tiny mites. A bite lets the germ in, and a fever and rash can follow.

  • Skin rash and whole body
  • Bites from lice, fleas, mites
  • Fever, headache, rash
Which doctor
Family doctor or infection doctor; see one early
Common in
People in bushy or rural areas, or crowded unclean living conditions
Contagious
Not directly between people; it spreads by lice, fleas or mite bites
Course
Gets better with the right medicine from a doctor; early care matters

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A bite from an infected louse, flea or mite
  • Walking in grass, scrub or plantations where mites live

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, confusion, or very drowsy: call 999
  • A fit (seizure), fainting, or bleeding: call 999
  • Fever with a rash after visiting bushy or rural areas
  • A dark, scabbed spot where you were bitten
  • Fever lasting more than a few days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor early, because the right medicine works best early
  • Rest and drink plenty of fluids
  • Wear long sleeves and trousers in bushy areas, and wash clothes after
  • Keep the home clean and away from rats; wash bedding and clothes in hot water

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • A severe allergy
