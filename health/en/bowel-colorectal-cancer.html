Bowel (colorectal) cancer

Cancer that starts in the large bowel (colon) or the back passage (rectum)

ConditionCancer & tumoursAbdomen

What it is

Bowel cancer starts when cells in the lining of the colon or rectum grow out of control, often from a small growth called a polyp. Early on it may cause no signs.

  • In the large bowel
  • More common with age
  • Bowel changes, belly pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, bowel specialist (colorectal surgeon), cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Adults, more often with age; people with bowel polyps or a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the stage and type; the cancer team explains the plan and follow-up

Common signs

More signs

  • Feeling that the bowel is still not empty after passing stool

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Polyps in the bowel, or long-term bowel inflammation such as colitis
  • Close relatives with bowel cancer, or certain inherited conditions
  • Little fibre, plenty of red or processed meat, being overweight, and little exercise

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding from the back passage, or feeling faint or collapsing: call 999
  • Severe belly pain with a swollen belly, vomiting, or cannot pass stool or wind: go to hospital now
  • Blood in the stool, red or black, even a small amount
  • A change in bowel habit that lasts for weeks, or stools that become thin
  • Losing weight without trying, or feeling very tired, pale or short of breath
  • A lump you can feel in the belly or back passage

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your care team about pain, food, bowel changes and emotional support
  • Ask a dietitian or your team which foods suit you during treatment
  • Close family members can ask their doctor about bowel screening

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
