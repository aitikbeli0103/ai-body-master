Itchy toes

An itch in or between the toes that makes you want to scratch or rub

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

The skin of the toes, often between them, feels itchy. A common reason is athlete's foot, but sweat, tight shoes, dry skin and reactions to socks or shoes can also cause it.

  • Toes and between the toes
  • Common in tight, sweaty shoes
  • Itch, redness or cracks
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more often with sweaty feet, tight shoes or public wet floors
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as athlete's foot or scabies
Course
Depends on the cause; often eases with dry, airy feet and gentle care

Common signs

More signs

  • Small cracks or splits in the skin that sting
  • Itch or burning that is worse after taking off shoes and socks
  • A strong smell from the feet
  • Dry, scaly skin on the soles or sides of the feet too

Why it happens

  • Athlete's foot, a fungal infection that thrives in warm, damp spaces between the toes
  • Tight, closed shoes or damp socks that trap sweat
  • A reaction to socks, shoe materials, nail polish or soap
  • Dry skin or eczema
  • Insect bites or scabies mites

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, lips or throat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Toes that turn pale, blue or black, or feel cold and numb: go to hospital now
  • If you have diabetes, any sore, blister, crack or colour change on the toes: see a doctor promptly
  • Spreading redness, warmth, swelling, pus or fever (the toes may be infected)
  • A toenail that turns thick, yellow or crumbly, or itching that lasts more than two weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Dry between the toes well after washing; pat gently with a clean towel
  • Wear breathable cotton socks and change them when damp
  • Choose shoes with room for the toes, and do not wear the same pair every day
  • Let the feet air at home in open sandals or slippers when you can

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
