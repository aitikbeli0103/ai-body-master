Atrophic gastritis

Long-lasting inflammation that thins the lining of the stomach over the years

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

In atrophic gastritis the stomach lining is inflamed for a long time and slowly gets thinner. It may cause few signs at first, so regular check-ups matter.

  • Lining of the stomach
  • More common with age
  • Fullness, upper belly ache
Which doctor
Stomach and bowel doctor (gastroenterologist); family doctor first
Common in
Older adults; people with long-term H. pylori or an autoimmune illness
Contagious
Some types can be (H. pylori may spread by food, water or close contact)
Course
Develops slowly over years; regular check-ups help watch for changes

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term infection with the stomach germ H. pylori is the most common cause
  • An autoimmune problem, where the body's defences attack the stomach lining

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, or black, sticky stools: go to hospital now
  • Sudden, very severe belly pain or a hard belly: call 999
  • Upper belly pain with chest tightness, sweating or pain spreading to the arm or jaw: call 999
  • Feeling faint, very tired, pale or short of breath (the blood may be low)
  • Weight loss without trying, trouble swallowing, or vomiting again and again: see a doctor soon
  • Numb or tingling hands and feet, or a sore tongue: tell your doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Atrophic gastritis?

Long-lasting inflammation that thins the lining of the stomach over the years. In atrophic gastritis the stomach lining is inflamed for a long time and slowly gets thinner. It may cause few signs at first, so regular check-ups matter.

What are the common signs of Atrophic gastritis?

Pain in the upper belly · Bloated, gassy belly · No appetite · Feeling sick, nausea · Pale, tired look

Why does Atrophic gastritis happen?

Smoking · Getting older · Runs in the family

How can I look after myself with Atrophic gastritis?

See your doctor for check-ups · Eat healthy, balanced meals · Stop smoking · Ask your pharmacist
