Sudden lower-back sprain

A sudden strain or sprain of the muscles or ligaments in the lower back

ConditionBack & spineLower backBones & muscles

What it is

A sudden twist, heavy lift or awkward movement overstretches the muscles or ligaments in the lower back, causing sharp pain and tightness that make it hard to bend or stand straight.

  • Lower back
  • After lifting or twisting
  • Sharp pain, stiff and tight
Which doctor
Family doctor; a bone and joint doctor if it does not improve
Common in
Anyone who lifts, bends or twists a lot, or sits for long hours
Contagious
No
Course
Often eases over days to a few weeks; it can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Tight, spasming muscles on one or both sides of the spine
  • Hard to stand up straight, turn over in bed or walk quickly
  • The pain may reach the buttock, but not usually below the knee

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A sudden twist or awkward movement, for example in sports or housework
  • Weak back and stomach muscles, being unfit or being overweight
  • Sitting or bending for a long time, then moving suddenly

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden severe back or belly pain with feeling faint, a cold sweat or chest pain: call 999
  • Trouble passing urine or stool, or losing control of them: go to hospital now
  • Numbness around the buttocks or groin, or weak or numb legs: go to hospital now
  • Back pain after a hard fall or accident, or in older people with weak bones: see a doctor now
  • Back pain with fever or chills, or with weight loss you cannot explain: see a doctor soon
  • Pain shooting down below the knee, or no better after a couple of weeks: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest briefly if you need to, but keep moving gently; long bed rest can slow recovery
  • A warm or cold pack wrapped in a cloth, for short times, can ease pain; keep it off numb or broken skin
  • Sleep on your back with a pillow under the knees, or on your side with a pillow between the knees
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking any pain medicine; go back to activity slowly

Tell your therapist

  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Weak bones
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
