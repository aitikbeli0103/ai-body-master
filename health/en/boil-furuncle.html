Boil (furuncle)

A painful, red, pus-filled lump in the skin from an infected hair root

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A boil starts when germs infect a hair root, and the skin becomes a red, painful lump that slowly fills with pus and may come to a head.

  • Skin, around a hair root
  • Anyone; more if diabetes
  • Red, sore lump with pus
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Any age; more often with diabetes or a weak immune system
Contagious
Yes, the germs in the pus can spread by touch or shared towels
Course
Many burst and drain by themselves in a week or two; some need a doctor's help

Common signs

More signs

  • The lump grows bigger and softer over a few days
  • A white or yellow centre (pus) that may burst and drain
  • Warm, tender skin around the lump

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Tiny cuts, shaving, or skin rubbed by tight clothes
  • Sweating a lot, or skin that stays damp
  • Diabetes, a weak immune system, or skin problems such as eczema

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with confusion, fast breathing, a racing heart or fainting: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Red streaks spreading from the boil, or redness and swelling that spread fast: see a doctor today
  • A boil on the face near the nose, upper lip or eye, or inside the ear: see a doctor the same day
  • A boil that is very painful, keeps growing, or has not drained after about two weeks: see a doctor
  • Boils that keep coming back, or several at once: see a doctor
  • A boil in a baby or young child, or in someone with diabetes or a weak immune system: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Hold a clean, warm (not hot) damp cloth on it for a few minutes, several times a day
  • When it drains, wash gently with mild soap and water and cover with a clean dry dressing

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
