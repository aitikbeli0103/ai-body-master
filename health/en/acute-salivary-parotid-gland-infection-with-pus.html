Acute salivary (parotid) gland infection with pus

A bacterial infection of the saliva gland in the cheek, with pus

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

The parotid gland sits in front of the ear and makes saliva. When bacteria infect it, the cheek becomes swollen, tender and warm, and pus can come into the mouth.

  • Cheek, in front of the ear
  • Older or unwell people
  • Swollen, painful cheek
Which doctor
Family doctor, ear-nose-throat doctor, or dentist
Common in
Older adults, people who are dehydrated or very unwell, and after surgery
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor's care; many people improve once the infection is looked after

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Low saliva from drinking too little or being unwell
  • A stone or blockage in the saliva duct
  • Poor mouth care, or some medicines that dry the mouth

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble swallowing or breathing, or swelling spreading to the neck: go to hospital now
  • High fever or shivering with a swollen cheek
  • A swollen cheek that is quickly getting bigger
  • Weakness or drooping of one side of the face
  • Being very drowsy, confused, or unable to drink

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor early; this infection needs medical care
  • Brush your teeth and rinse your mouth gently after meals
  • Ask your doctor about any medicine that dries your mouth

Tell your therapist

  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • A severe allergy
