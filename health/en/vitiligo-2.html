Vitiligo

A long-lasting skin condition in which patches of skin lose their colour and turn pale or white

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkinWhole body

What it is

The skin cells that make colour stop working in some areas, so smooth pale or white patches appear, which are usually painless and cannot be passed to other people.

  • Any part of the skin
  • Often starts when young
  • Pale or white patches
Which doctor
Skin doctor (dermatologist); a family doctor can refer you
Common in
Any age and skin colour; often starts when young; may run in families
Contagious
No; it cannot be caught by touching or sharing things
Course
Long-lasting and hard to predict; patches may stay, spread or partly regain colour

Common signs

More signs

  • Pale or white patches of skin, often with a clear edge
  • Often on the face, hands, feet, elbows, knees, or around the mouth and eyes
  • Hair in the patch may turn white or grey
  • Patches often appear on both sides of the body in a similar way

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The body's defence system may mistakenly attack the cells that make skin colour
  • It is more common in people with some immune-related conditions, such as thyroid problems
  • It is not caused by poor hygiene or by eating a certain food

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself, or feeling very low about your skin: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Pale patches that appear quickly or spread fast, especially in a child
  • A patch that blisters, burns badly in the sun, or becomes sore, red or crusted
  • A new lump, open sore or changing dark spot on or near a patch
  • Unusual tiredness, weight change, feeling very hot or cold, or being very thirsty

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Be gentle with your skin; avoid rubbing, scratching or injuring the patches
  • Make-up or skin-coloured cover-ups can hide the patches if you wish
  • Avoid home remedies and unregistered creams; ask a skin doctor about safe options
  • It can affect how you feel; talk to someone you trust and ask for support if you feel low

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
