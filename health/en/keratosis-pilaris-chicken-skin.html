Keratosis pilaris (chicken skin)

A common skin condition with small, rough bumps that feel like goosebumps

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Tiny rough bumps appear on the skin, most often on the upper arms and thighs, when hair openings get blocked by keratin, a hard skin protein. It is usually harmless.

  • Upper arms, thighs, cheeks
  • Often starts in childhood
  • Tiny rough bumps, rarely sore
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if you want advice
Common in
Children, teens and young adults; often runs in families and with dry skin
Contagious
No
Course
Often lasts for years and comes and goes; it usually improves with age

Common signs

More signs

  • Most often on the upper arms, thighs, buttocks or cheeks
  • Bumps may be skin-coloured, white, red or brownish
  • Often looks worse in dry air or air-conditioned rooms

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Dead skin cells and keratin (a skin protein) build up and block the hair openings
  • Dry skin, eczema, hay fever or asthma make it more likely
  • The exact reason is not fully known

See a doctor first if

  • A rash that spreads quickly, or comes with fever or feeling very unwell: see a doctor today
  • Bumps that become painful, hot, full of pus or oozing (they may be infected)
  • Strong itching, cracked skin, or eczema patches that keep coming back
  • A sore, lump or patch that bleeds, grows or does not go away: have it checked
  • Bumps with hair loss, scarring or redness on the face or eyebrows

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash with lukewarm water and a mild soap; avoid hot showers and hard scrubbing
  • If you want to improve how it looks, ask your doctor or pharmacist about safe options

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
