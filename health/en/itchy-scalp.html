Itchy scalp

An itch on the skin of the scalp, sometimes with flakes

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsHead

What it is

The skin under the hair feels itchy. It is very common and is often caused by dandruff, dry skin, hair products or sweat, and sometimes by lice or a skin condition.

  • Scalp and hairline
  • Worse with sweat or stress
  • Itch, sometimes with flakes
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more often in hot, humid weather and with oily or dry scalps
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as head lice or a fungal infection
Course
Often comes and goes; usually eases with gentle care, but may need a doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • A dry, tight scalp, or an oily, red scalp
  • Small scabs or sore spots from scratching

Why it happens

  • Dandruff, or oily, flaky skin on the scalp (seborrhoeic dermatitis)
  • A dry scalp, or washing the hair too little or too much
  • A reaction to shampoo, hair dye, gel or other hair products
  • Head lice, or a fungal infection of the scalp
  • Sweat trapped under a cap, helmet or headscarf, or conditions like eczema or psoriasis

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, eyes or throat, or trouble breathing, for example after hair dye: call 999
  • A painful, swollen, hot scalp with pus or fever (the skin may be infected)
  • Patches of hair loss, or a scalp sore or scaly spot that does not go away
  • Itch with fever, a spreading rash, or feeling very unwell
  • Itching that keeps coming back or lasts more than a few weeks despite gentle care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash the hair regularly with a gentle shampoo, rinse well, and dry it after sweating
  • Pat the itchy spot gently instead of scratching; keep nails short
  • Stop any new hair product or dye you think may have caused it
  • If flakes keep coming back, ask a pharmacist about a suitable shampoo

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
