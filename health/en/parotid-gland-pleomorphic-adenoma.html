Parotid gland pleomorphic adenoma

A usually harmless, slow-growing lump in the saliva gland in front of or below the ear

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

The parotid gland is a saliva gland on the cheek, next to the ear. A pleomorphic adenoma is a lump in it that is usually not cancer, but it keeps growing slowly, so a doctor should check it.

  • Cheek, near the ear
  • Often adults of middle age
  • A slow, painless lump
Which doctor
Ear, nose and throat (ENT) doctor, head and neck surgeon
Common in
Adults, more often women; it can happen at any age
Contagious
No
Course
Grows slowly over months to years and does not go away by itself

Common signs

  • A painless, firm, smooth lump in front of or below the ear, or at the angle of the jaw
  • The lump grows slowly and moves a little under the skin
  • The cheek may look uneven on one side
  • Usually no pain and no weakness of the face

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It comes from cells of the saliva gland that grow more than they should
  • Earlier radiation to the head or neck may raise the chance
  • It is not caused by something you ate or did

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Weakness or drooping of one side of the face, or trouble closing an eye
  • A lump that suddenly grows faster or becomes hard and fixed
  • Pain, numbness or skin changes over the lump
  • New swollen glands in the neck, or any new lump that lasts more than two weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
