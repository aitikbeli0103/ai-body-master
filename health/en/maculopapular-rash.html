Maculopapular rash

A rash of flat red patches mixed with small raised bumps

SymptomSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A maculopapular rash has flat red patches and small raised bumps that often join together. It is a sign of something else, such as a virus, a medicine or an allergy.

  • Skin of body, arms, legs
  • Any age; virus or medicine
  • Red patches and bumps, may itch
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist); paediatrician for a child
Common in
Anyone; very common in children with viral illness and after new medicines
Contagious
Some types can be; a rash from a virus can spread, one from medicine or allergy cannot
Course
Depends on the cause; many fade over days to weeks, but see a doctor to find out why

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Viral infections, such as measles, rubella (German measles), dengue and many common childhood viruses
  • A reaction to a new medicine, often starting days to weeks after it was started
  • An allergy, such as to a food, an insect bite or a skin product
  • Eczema, and some bacterial infections or immune conditions
  • Sometimes the cause is never found, and the rash fades by itself

See a doctor first if

  • Rash with swollen lips, face or tongue, wheezing, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Rash with stiff neck, confusion, severe headache or spots that do not fade when pressed: go to hospital now
  • Blisters, peeling skin, or sores in the mouth or eyes, especially after a new medicine: go to hospital now
  • Fever with rash plus bleeding, severe belly pain, repeated vomiting or great weakness: go to hospital now
  • A baby, young child or pregnant woman with a new rash and fever: see a doctor promptly
  • A rash that spreads fast, is very painful, or has not faded after a week or two

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not scratch; keep nails short, and wash with lukewarm water and a mild, fragrance-free soap
  • Note when it started and any new medicine, food or product, and take photos for your doctor
  • If it began after a new medicine, tell your doctor or pharmacist soon; do not stop prescribed medicine on your own
  • With fever and rash, stay home and away from babies and pregnant women until a doctor has seen you
