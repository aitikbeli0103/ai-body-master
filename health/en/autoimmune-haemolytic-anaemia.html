Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia

The body's defence system attacks its own red blood cells

ConditionBloodBloodWhole body

What it is

The body's defence system wrongly destroys red blood cells faster than they are made. This can lead to anaemia, tiredness and yellow skin.

  • In the blood
  • Immune system problem
  • Tired, pale, yellow
Which doctor
Blood doctor (haematologist)
Common in
Adults, more often women; can also happen in children
Contagious
No
Course
It can come on fast or slowly; it needs a doctor's care and follow-up

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Other immune conditions, such as lupus
  • Some infections
  • Some blood cancers or other cancers

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, severe trouble breathing or fainting: call 999
  • Dark, tea-coloured urine or quickly yellowing skin and eyes
  • Fever or severe belly or back pain
  • A swollen, painful leg or sudden breathlessness: call 999
  • Getting more tired or pale over days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia?

The body's defence system attacks its own red blood cells. The body's defence system wrongly destroys red blood cells faster than they are made. This can lead to anaemia, tiredness and yellow skin.

What are the common signs of Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia?

Tired all the time · Pale, tired look · Yellow skin and eyes · Short of breath on stairs · Racing, pounding heart

Why does Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia happen?

The cause is not fully known · A side effect of some medicines

How can I look after myself with Autoimmune haemolytic anaemia?

Rest well · Wash your hands often · Take medicine as your doctor says · See your doctor for check-ups
