Acute pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)

A sudden infection of the womb, tubes and ovaries that causes lower belly pain and needs a doctor's care

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

A sudden infection in the womb, tubes or ovaries, usually from germs that travel up from the vagina. It can cause lower belly pain, fever and an unusual discharge.

  • Lower belly, womb, tubes
  • Women who are sexually active
  • Lower belly pain, fever
Which doctor
Family doctor or women's doctor (gynaecologist), without delay
Common in
Women who are sexually active; also after childbirth, miscarriage or womb procedures
Contagious
Some types can be, as the germs are often passed on by sex
Course
Can get worse if ignored; seeing a doctor early lowers the risk of lasting problems

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Often infections passed on by sex, such as chlamydia or gonorrhoea
  • Sex without a condom, or a partner with an infection
  • Germs getting in after childbirth, a miscarriage or a womb procedure, such as putting in a coil (IUD)
  • Having had this infection before makes it more likely again

See a doctor first if

  • Severe lower belly pain with high fever, vomiting or a hard, tender belly: go to hospital now
  • Feeling faint, very weak, confused or with a racing heart: call 999
  • Lower belly pain or bleeding when you are or may be pregnant: go to hospital now
  • Lower belly pain with an unusual discharge or fever: see a doctor today, do not wait
  • Not feeling better soon after starting the medicine your doctor gave you: go back to your doctor
  • New pain during sex or bleeding between periods: see a doctor soon

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor promptly if you think this could be it; do not wait for it to go away
  • Ask your doctor whether your partner should be checked too
  • Using condoms can lower the chance of catching or passing on infections

What are the common signs of Acute pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)?

Dragging ache in the lower belly · Unusual discharge or itching down below · Fever, feeling hot · Pain during or after sex · Burning when passing urine
