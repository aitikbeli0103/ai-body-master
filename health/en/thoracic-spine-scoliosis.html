Thoracic spine scoliosis

A sideways curve of the spine in the middle of the back, often with a slight twist

ConditionBones, joints & spineBack

What it is

The spine in the chest area curves to the side instead of running straight. It is often mild and painless, but a doctor should check it, especially in children and teenagers.

  • Middle of the back
  • Often starts in the teen years
  • Uneven shoulders or ribs
Which doctor
Family doctor, child doctor, then bone or spine specialist
Common in
Children and teenagers, more often girls; sometimes older adults with spine wear
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the curve and age; a doctor may just watch it over time or suggest other care

Common signs

  • One shoulder or shoulder blade looks higher or sticks out more
  • The waist or hips look uneven, or clothes hang unevenly
  • One side of the ribs looks more raised when bending forward
  • Tiredness or a dull ache in the back after standing a long time

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some nerve or muscle conditions, or wear of the spine in older adults

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A child or teen with a curve that looks like it is getting worse: see a doctor promptly
  • Weak or numb legs, or trouble passing urine or stools: go to hospital now
  • Back pain that wakes a child at night or keeps getting worse
  • Feeling short of breath on light activity with a marked curve

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have a doctor check any curve you notice, and go to follow-up visits
  • Carry school bags evenly and not too heavy
  • Use a chair and desk that let you sit with a supported back
  • Use a brace only if your doctor prescribes it, and wear it as advised

Tell your therapist

  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
