PTSD (post-traumatic stress)

Lasting distress after a very frightening event, such as flashbacks, nightmares and feeling on edge

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressWhole body

What it is

Some people have strong reactions that do not fade after a very frightening event. Memories keep coming back, sleep is poor, and the body stays on guard.

  • The mind and body after trauma
  • After a frightening event
  • Flashbacks and nightmares
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a psychiatrist or psychologist
Common in
Can follow a very frightening event; not everyone who goes through one is affected
Contagious
No
Course
Can start soon or months later; support can help over time

Common signs

More signs

  • Upsetting memories, flashbacks or nightmares of the event
  • Staying away from people, places or talk that bring it back
  • Feeling numb, guilty, ashamed or cut off from others

Why it happens

  • A very frightening event, such as an accident, violence, abuse, war or a disaster
  • Losing a loved one suddenly, or a serious injury or illness
  • Not everyone who goes through such an event is affected
  • More likely after repeated or long-lasting trauma, or with little support afterwards
  • Earlier trauma or other mental health problems may raise the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or someone else: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • You or someone else is in danger or being hurt right now: call 999
  • Using alcohol or other substances to block out memories or to sleep
  • Flashbacks, nightmares or panic that happen often, or stop you working, sleeping or caring for yourself
  • Feeling numb, hopeless or cut off from everyone for weeks
  • A child with new fears, nightmares or behaviour changes after a scare: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep a steady routine for sleep, meals and gentle activity
  • When a memory feels too strong, look around, name what you see and hear, and breathe slowly
  • Avoid using alcohol or other substances to cope; they can make sleep and mood worse
  • Ask your doctor about talking therapy for trauma, and go at your own pace

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • High blood pressure
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is PTSD (post-traumatic stress)?

Lasting distress after a very frightening event, such as flashbacks, nightmares and feeling on edge. Some people have strong reactions that do not fade after a very frightening event. Memories keep coming back, sleep is poor, and the body stays on guard.

What are the common signs of PTSD (post-traumatic stress)?

Short-tempered, on edge · Cannot fall asleep

Why does PTSD (post-traumatic stress) happen?

A very frightening event, such as an accident, violence, abuse, war or a disaster · Losing a loved one suddenly, or a serious injury or illness · Not everyone who goes through such an event is affected · More likely after repeated or long-lasting trauma, or with little support afterwards · Earlier trauma or other mental health problems may raise the risk

How can I look after myself with PTSD (post-traumatic stress)?

Talk to family and friends
