Cracked sacrum

A fine crack in the sacrum, the triangle-shaped bone at the base of the spine

ConditionBack & spinePelvis

What it is

The sacrum joins the spine to the pelvis. A fall, a blow or weak bones can cause a small crack, often with deep pain in the lower back and buttocks.

  • Back of the pelvis
  • Older adults, after a fall
  • Deep ache, hurts to walk
Which doctor
Hospital emergency, orthopaedic doctor
Common in
Older adults with weak bones; people after a fall or accident
Contagious
No
Course
Often takes weeks to months to settle; a doctor guides activity

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak bones (osteoporosis), where a crack can come with little force
  • Long-term strain on the bone, in some people

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot stand or walk, or severe pain after a fall: go to hospital now
  • Numbness around the groin or buttocks, or a weak leg: go to hospital now
  • Trouble passing urine or stools, or leaking of either: go to hospital now
  • Heavy bruising or swelling over the lower back
  • Fever with back pain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Get checked first, as a crack needs a proper look by a doctor
  • Follow your doctor's advice on rest and how much to walk
  • Ask your doctor about bone health, calcium and vitamin D

Tell your therapist

  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
