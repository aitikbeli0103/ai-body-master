Leptospirosis (rat urine disease)

A bacterial infection caught from animal urine, usually in flood water, rivers or mud

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Leptospirosis is an infection caused by germs in the urine of rats and other animals. People can catch it when dirty water or mud gets into cuts, eyes, nose or mouth. It can be mild or serious.

  • Whole body
  • After floods or river contact
  • Fever, aches, red eyes
Which doctor
Family doctor first; hospital if very unwell
Common in
People in contact with flood water, rivers, farm animals or rats
Contagious
No, rarely between people; it comes from animal urine in water or mud
Course
Many cases are milder, but it can turn serious fast; get medical care early

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Touching water, mud or soil that has animal urine in it, such as flood water or rivers
  • The germs enter through cuts and sore skin, or through the eyes, nose or mouth
  • Higher risk for farm, plantation and clean-up workers, and people who swim or play in rivers
  • Heavy rain and floods, and rats near homes or food stalls

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, chest pain or coughing up blood: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Yellow skin or eyes, very little urine, or bleeding (nose, gums, vomit or stools): go to hospital now
  • High fever with a bad headache, stiff neck, confusion or drowsiness: go to hospital now
  • Fever, bad muscle aches or red eyes after flood water, river or rat contact: see a doctor soon and mention it
  • Vomiting that will not stop, or you cannot drink: see a doctor today
  • A child, a pregnant woman or an older person with these signs: see a doctor promptly

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Avoid wading or swimming in flood water and rivers; wear boots and gloves if you must go in
  • Keep rubbish covered, store food safely and keep rats away from your home
  • If you feel unwell after contact, see a doctor early; rest and drink enough fluids meanwhile
