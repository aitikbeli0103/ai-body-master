Adenocarcinoma (gland cell cancer)

A type of cancer that starts in gland cells, which line many organs such as the lung, bowel, breast and prostate

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

Adenocarcinoma is a group of cancers that start in gland cells, the cells that make mucus and other fluids; it can begin in many organs, so the signs depend on where it starts.

  • Can start in many organs
  • More common with age
  • Signs depend on the organ
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a cancer specialist (oncologist) at a hospital
Common in
Mostly adults; it becomes more common with age
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on where it starts and how early it is found; your cancer team can explain

Common signs

More signs

  • Bleeding with no clear reason, such as blood in stools, urine or phlegm
  • A change in bowel habits, or trouble swallowing, that lasts for weeks
  • A cough, hoarse voice, belly pain or back pain that does not settle

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A family history of cancer, or long-term inflammation in an organ, can raise the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Coughing or vomiting blood, black or bloody stools, or heavy bleeding: go to hospital now
  • Chest pain, severe trouble breathing, face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Sudden severe belly pain, or repeated vomiting with a swollen belly: go to hospital now
  • A new lump or swelling that does not go away or keeps growing
  • Unexplained weight loss, ongoing tiredness or loss of appetite
  • A cough, hoarse voice, trouble swallowing or change in bowel habits that lasts for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Never stop or change treatment or medicines on your own; ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • Ask your care team about pain, food and emotional support
  • Ask your doctor about screening checks that suit your age and family history

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
