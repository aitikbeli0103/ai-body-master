Neuroendocrine tumour

A tumour that starts in hormone-making cells, often in the gut, pancreas or lungs

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

Neuroendocrine cells make hormones and are found in many parts of the body. A tumour can start from them; some grow slowly and others grow faster.

  • Can start in many organs
  • May be found by chance
  • Signs depend on the place
Which doctor
Cancer doctor (oncologist) or hormone doctor (endocrinologist)
Common in
Mostly adults past middle age; can happen at any age
Contagious
No
Course
Differs a lot: some grow slowly, others faster; your cancer team can explain

Common signs

More signs

  • Belly pain, a lump, or a cough that does not go away, depending on where the tumour is
  • Some release extra hormones, causing flushing of the face and neck, loose stools or wheezing
  • Some cause shaky, sweaty spells with hunger or confusion, which can be low blood sugar

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A few are linked to rare inherited conditions that run in families
  • A close relative with a neuroendocrine tumour may raise the risk
  • Long-term inflammation of the stomach lining may raise the risk of tumours in the stomach
  • They are found more often in middle-aged and older people

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, fits, trouble breathing, or a racing heart with sudden flushing: call 999
  • Vomiting blood, black stools, or bleeding that does not stop: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain with vomiting and a swollen belly: go to hospital now
  • Fever or shivering during cancer treatment: go to hospital now
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or dark urine with pale stools: see a doctor soon
  • A new lump, weight loss without trying, or flushing, loose stools or wheezing that keep coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Note when flushing, loose stools or shaky spells happen, and tell your team
  • Ask your team about pain, food and emotional support
  • Keep as active as you feel able, and rest when you need to

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • A heart condition
  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • High blood pressure

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
