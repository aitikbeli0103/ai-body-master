Narcolepsy

A long-term sleep condition with sudden, strong sleepiness during the day

ConditionSleepWhole body

What it is

In narcolepsy the brain cannot keep the sleep and wake times steady. A person may fall asleep suddenly in the day, even while talking or eating. A sleep doctor can explain more.

  • Brain and sleep
  • Often starts in youth
  • Sudden strong sleepiness
Which doctor
Sleep doctor, neurologist or family doctor
Common in
Often starts in teens or young adults
Contagious
No
Course
Usually lasts a long time; a doctor can help you manage it

Common signs

More signs

  • Falling asleep suddenly during talking, eating or work
  • Sudden weak muscles with strong feelings such as laughing
  • Feeling unable to move for a short time when falling asleep or waking
  • Broken sleep at night

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A shortage of a brain chemical that helps keep us awake
  • It may start after an illness or big change in the body

See a doctor first if

  • Falling asleep while driving or doing something dangerous: stop and see a doctor soon
  • A fall or injury caused by sudden sleep or weak muscles: go to hospital if badly hurt
  • Sleepiness that is getting worse or affecting work or school
  • Feeling very low or having thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep a regular time for sleeping and waking, even on days off
  • Plan short naps in the day if your doctor agrees
  • Avoid driving or using machines when very sleepy
