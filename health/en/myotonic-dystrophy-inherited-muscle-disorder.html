Myotonic dystrophy (inherited muscle disorder)

An inherited condition where muscles are weak and slow to relax

ConditionBrain & nervesBones & muscles

What it is

A gene passed down in families makes muscles weak and stiff, so a grip can be slow to let go. It can also affect the heart, eyes and more.

  • Muscles of the body
  • Runs in families
  • Stiff, weak, tired
Which doctor
Brain and nerve doctor (neurologist); heart doctor too
Common in
Usually shows up in adulthood; passed down in families
Contagious
No, but it can be passed to children through genes
Course
Usually changes slowly over many years; regular check-ups help

Common signs

More signs

  • Hands that are slow to let go after gripping
  • Weak face, neck, hands or feet
  • Cloudy eyes (cataracts) at a younger age

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A gene change passed from a parent to a child
  • A person with the gene can pass it to each child
  • It is not caused by anything you did

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, chest pain or a racing or very slow heartbeat: call 999
  • Trouble breathing: call 999
  • Choking or food going down the wrong way: go to hospital now
  • Fever with a new cough or chest infection signs
  • Before any operation, tell the team you have this condition

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay gently active, such as walking, and rest when tired
  • Keep warm; cold can make muscles stiffer

Tell your therapist
