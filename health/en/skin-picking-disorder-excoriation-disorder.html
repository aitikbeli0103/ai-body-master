Skin-picking disorder (excoriation disorder)

Repeated picking at the skin until it is marked, sore or wounded

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressSkin

What it is

Some people pick, scratch or squeeze their skin again and again and find it hard to stop, even when it causes marks or sores. It often links with stress or anxiety, and help is available.

  • Skin of face, arms, hands
  • Often starts in the teen years
  • Marks, scabs and sore spots
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor or psychologist
Common in
Teens and adults; more often in women
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes with stress; support can help skin heal and urges ease

Common signs

  • Picking or scratching the skin again and again
  • Scabs, scars, sore spots or dark marks on the skin
  • Trying to stop but not being able to
  • Picking when stressed, bored, or while looking in a mirror
  • Feeling shame and hiding the skin with clothes or make-up

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Skin problems such as pimples or dry skin can start the habit

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Heavy bleeding that does not stop after firm pressure: go to hospital
  • A wound with pus, spreading redness, warmth or fever
  • Deep wounds, or wounds that do not heal
  • Feeling very low, ashamed or cut off from people

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Cover sore spots with a plaster so they can heal
  • Keep your hands busy with a stress ball or a small task
  • Notice when the urge comes, and breathe slowly
  • Talk to someone you trust and ask your doctor for support

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
