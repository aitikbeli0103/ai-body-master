Herpes (blistering skin infection)

Viral infections that cause clusters of painful or itchy small blisters on the skin

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A herpes virus infects the skin and nerves and causes clusters of small, fluid-filled blisters that can sting, burn or itch; the best known kinds are cold sores, genital herpes and shingles.

  • Skin, lips or one side of body
  • Anyone, at any age
  • Tingling, then small blisters
Which doctor
Family doctor; eye doctor if near the eye; skin doctor if it keeps returning
Common in
Anyone; shingles is more common in older adults and people with weak immunity
Contagious
Yes, through close contact with the blisters or their fluid
Course
Blisters usually crust over and clear within a few weeks; the virus can come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Tingling, burning or itching on a patch of skin before anything shows
  • The blisters burst, weep, and then form crusts
  • Shingles: a painful band-like rash on one side of the body or face

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Herpes simplex spreads by close contact, such as kissing, skin-to-skin touch or sex
  • After the first infection, the virus stays asleep in the body and can wake up again later
  • Shingles comes from the chickenpox virus waking up years later, and is more common in older adults

See a doctor first if

  • Severe headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion or fits: call 999
  • A newborn baby with blisters or sores, or a baby who is unwell: go to hospital now
  • A blistering rash near the eye or on the nose tip, or eye pain or blurry vision: see a doctor today
  • If you are pregnant or have a weak immune system and have blisters or sores, see a doctor promptly
  • A painful band-like rash on one side of the body, or pain that stays after the rash has gone: see a doctor early
  • Red, swollen, pus-filled skin or red streaks, as the blisters may be infected

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist about medicines that may help; they work best when started early
  • Until the blisters have dried up, avoid kissing, sex, and sharing towels or lip products
  • Cover the rash, and keep away from newborns, pregnant women and people with weak immunity until it has crusted

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
