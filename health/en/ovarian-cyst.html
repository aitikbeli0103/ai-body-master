Ovarian cyst

A fluid-filled sac on or inside an ovary; most are harmless and many go away on their own

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled sac on an ovary. Most are small, harmless and cause no signs, but a large one can cause aching, bloating or sudden pain.

  • On or in an ovary
  • Women of childbearing age
  • Dull ache or bloating
Which doctor
Gynaecologist (women's doctor) or family doctor
Common in
Women of childbearing age; can also be found after the menopause
Contagious
No
Course
Many small cysts go away on their own over weeks to months; scans may be used to watch

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A fluid sac that forms during the normal monthly cycle and does not shrink back
  • Endometriosis, where tissue like the womb lining grows on the ovary

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in the lower belly, especially with fainting, fever or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Pale, cold, clammy skin, a racing heart or feeling about to faint: call 999
  • Belly pain or bleeding when you could be pregnant: go to hospital now
  • Bloating that does not go away, feeling full quickly, or losing weight without trying
  • A cyst found after your periods have stopped for good, or one that keeps growing
  • Pain during sex, or pelvic pain that keeps coming back or stops you from daily activities

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

What are the common signs of Ovarian cyst?

Dragging ache in the lower belly · Bloated, gassy belly · Period comes late or early · Needing the toilet often

Why does Ovarian cyst happen?

Hormone changes · Pregnancy

How can I look after myself with Ovarian cyst?

See your doctor for check-ups · Rest well · Avoid heavy lifting
