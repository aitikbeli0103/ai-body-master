Coughing up blood (haemoptysis)

Blood in the phlegm or spit that comes up from the lungs or airways

SymptomLungs & breathingChest

What it is

Coughing up blood means blood comes up from the lungs or airways. It can be a few streaks or more, and it always needs to be checked by a doctor.

  • Lungs and airways
  • Any age, often smokers
  • Blood when you cough
Which doctor
Family doctor first; chest doctor (respirologist)
Common in
Any age; smokers and people with lung disease
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as certain lung infections
Course
Depends on the cause, so a doctor needs to find out why

Common signs

More signs

  • Pink, red or rust-coloured streaks in phlegm
  • A cough that has lasted a while

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Lung infections such as tuberculosis (TB)
  • A blood clot in the lung, or a growth in the lung
  • Blood from the nose or throat can look the same, and sometimes no cause is found

See a doctor first if

  • Coughing up more than a small streak of blood: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing, chest pain or fainting: call 999
  • Blood in your phlegm more than once, even if small, so see a doctor soon
  • Weight loss, night sweats or a cough lasting weeks
  • Swollen painful leg together with breathlessness: call 999

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor even if the blood was small
  • Stay calm, sit upright and rest until you are seen
  • Note how much blood, what colour and how often

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • A heart condition
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
