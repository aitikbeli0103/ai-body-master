Haemochromatosis (iron overload)

A condition where the body takes in too much iron from food and stores it in organs like the liver and heart

ConditionBloodBlood

What it is

The body cannot get rid of extra iron, so it slowly builds up over years. Too much iron can strain the liver, heart, joints and other organs.

  • Liver, heart and joints
  • Often shows in midlife
  • Tired, aching joints
Which doctor
Family doctor, blood doctor or liver doctor
Common in
Adults, often middle-aged; may run in families
Contagious
No
Course
Builds up slowly over years; checks and care can be planned with a doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • Skin that looks darker or bronze
  • Less interest in sex, or irregular periods

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Other blood conditions that need many blood transfusions
  • Taking iron pills for a long time when not needed

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, a racing or irregular heartbeat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Vomiting blood or black stools: go to hospital now
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or a swollen belly
  • Being very thirsty and passing a lot of urine
  • Confusion, or a fever with shivering

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not take iron pills or extra vitamin C without asking your doctor
  • Avoid raw shellfish, which can be risky with extra iron
  • Family members may ask their doctor about testing

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Questions and answers

What is Haemochromatosis (iron overload)?

A condition where the body takes in too much iron from food and stores it in organs like the liver and heart. The body cannot get rid of extra iron, so it slowly builds up over years. Too much iron can strain the liver, heart, joints and other organs.

What are the common signs of Haemochromatosis (iron overload)?

Tired all the time · Aching, swollen joints · Pain in the upper belly

Why does Haemochromatosis (iron overload) happen?

Runs in the family · Drinking alcohol

How can I look after myself with Haemochromatosis (iron overload)?

See your doctor for check-ups · Cut down on alcohol
