Mumps

A viral infection that makes the glands in the cheeks swollen and sore

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

A virus causes swelling of the saliva glands in front of the ears, giving a puffy, sore cheek and jaw. It also brings fever, headache and tiredness, and it spreads easily.

  • Cheeks and jaw
  • Children and young people
  • Puffy cheeks, fever
Which doctor
Family doctor (call the clinic first, as it spreads easily)
Common in
Children and young people, and anyone who is not vaccinated
Contagious
Yes, spreads through droplets from coughs and sneezes, and shared cups
Course
Swelling usually settles in a week or two; some people get complications

Common signs

  • Swollen, sore cheeks or jaw, on one or both sides, in front of the ears
  • Pain when chewing or swallowing; sour foods and drinks can make it worse
  • Fever, headache and feeling tired
  • Muscle aches and loss of appetite
  • Some people have only mild signs, or none at all

Why it happens

  • It is caused by the mumps virus
  • The virus spreads in droplets from the coughs and sneezes of an infected person
  • It can also pass on through saliva, shared cups, cutlery or toys
  • Not having the MMR vaccine, or not completing the vaccine schedule, makes it more likely
  • Crowded places such as schools, hostels and childcare centres help it spread

See a doctor first if

  • Severe headache with a stiff neck, vomiting, confusion, strong sleepiness or fits: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain with repeated vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing or swallowing: go to hospital now
  • Sudden hearing loss in one ear: see a doctor straight away
  • A painful, swollen testicle, or lower belly pain in a woman: see a doctor today
  • A pregnant woman, a baby or someone with weak immunity near a person with mumps: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Stay home and rest until your doctor says it is safe to go back to school or work
  • Drink plenty of fluids and eat soft foods; sour foods and drinks can make the glands hurt
  • A warm or cool cloth on the sore cheek may feel soothing
  • Wash hands often, cover coughs and sneezes, and do not share cups or cutlery
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist about medicine for fever or pain, and about the MMR vaccine

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
