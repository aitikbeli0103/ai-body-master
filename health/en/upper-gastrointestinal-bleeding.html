Upper gastrointestinal bleeding

Bleeding in the gullet, stomach or first part of the small bowel

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Bleeding can start in the food pipe, the stomach or the first part of the small bowel. It may show as vomiting blood or black stools, and it is an emergency.

  • Gullet, stomach, bowel
  • More common in older adults
  • Blood in vomit or stool
Which doctor
Go to hospital at once; gastroenterologist
Common in
Older adults; people with stomach ulcers or liver disease
Contagious
No
Course
Needs hospital care; the outlook depends on the cause

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Ulcers in the stomach or first part of the bowel
  • Some pain medicines, and regular alcohol
  • Swollen veins in the gullet, often with liver disease
  • Strong vomiting that tears the gullet lining

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, or vomit like coffee grounds: call 999
  • Black, sticky, bad-smelling stools: go to hospital now
  • Fainting, feeling very dizzy, a racing heart or pale cold skin
  • Severe belly pain
  • Any bleeding while taking blood-thinning medicine

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not wait at home; go to hospital or call 999
  • Do not eat or drink until a doctor says it is fine
  • Limit alcohol and avoid smoking
