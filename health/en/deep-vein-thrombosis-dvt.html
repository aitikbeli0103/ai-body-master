Deep vein thrombosis (DVT)

A blood clot in a deep vein, most often in the leg

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsArms & legsBones & muscles

What it is

A blood clot forms in a deep vein, usually in the calf or thigh. It can block blood flow and make the leg swell and hurt. A clot can travel to the lungs, which is dangerous.

  • Leg: calf or thigh
  • After surgery or long sitting
  • Swollen, sore, warm leg
Which doctor
Family doctor or emergency; vascular or blood doctor
Common in
People after surgery or long trips, in bed for long, or with cancer
Contagious
No
Course
Needs a doctor's care at once; with proper care it often improves

Common signs

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden shortness of breath, chest pain or coughing blood: call 999
  • Fast heartbeat or fainting together with a swollen leg: call 999
  • One leg suddenly swollen, red, warm or painful: see a doctor today
  • A leg that turns pale, blue or cold: go to hospital now
  • Swollen arm or face with pain after a drip or long illness

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Get a doctor's check first; do not treat a swollen leg on your own
  • Do not rub or massage a painful, swollen leg
  • Move your ankles often if you must sit for a long time
  • If you take blood-thinning medicine, take it as your doctor says

Tell your therapist

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • A heart condition
