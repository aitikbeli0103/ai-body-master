Facial allergy

Redness, itch or swelling of the face caused by an allergic reaction

ConditionAllergiesHeadSkin

What it is

Facial allergy is when the skin of the face reacts to something it touched, ate or breathed in, becoming red, itchy, dry or swollen.

  • Face and skin
  • Products, food, dust
  • Red, itchy, dry skin
Which doctor
Family doctor or skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone, especially people with sensitive skin or allergies
Contagious
No
Course
Often settles in days to a few weeks once the cause is avoided

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Skin care, make-up, hair dye or soap that does not suit your skin
  • Foods or drinks you are allergic to
  • Insect bites, sun or some medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen lips, tongue or throat, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Swelling around the eyes so that you cannot open them
  • Hives all over the body, dizziness or feeling faint
  • Blisters, oozing, pain, or fever with the rash
  • A rash that does not improve in a week or keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Stop using any new product you think caused it
  • Wash gently with plain water or a mild cleanser
  • Do not scratch or rub; a cool, clean cloth can soothe
  • Test new products on a small patch first and note what you used

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
