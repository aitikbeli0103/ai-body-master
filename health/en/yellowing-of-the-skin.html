Yellowing of the skin

Skin that looks yellow, sometimes with the whites of the eyes yellow too

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

The skin looks yellow. This can come from lots of orange foods, but yellow skin with yellow eyes may be jaundice, a sign of a liver, bile or blood problem that needs a check.

  • Skin and whites of the eyes
  • Newborn babies and adults
  • Yellow tint, sometimes itchy
Which doctor
Family doctor first; a liver or digestive specialist if needed
Common in
Newborn babies; adults with liver, bile or blood problems
Contagious
No, but some causes, such as viral hepatitis, can spread
Course
Depends on the cause; yellow skin with yellow eyes needs a doctor's check soon

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Eating lots of carrots, pumpkin, papaya or sweet potato; the eyes stay white
  • Liver problems, such as viral hepatitis or damage from alcohol
  • Gallstones or another blockage in the bile tubes (bile ducts)
  • Red blood cells breaking down faster than normal, in some blood conditions
  • Some medicines or herbal products that can affect the liver

See a doctor first if

  • Yellow skin or eyes with confusion, great sleepiness, vomiting blood or black stools: call 999
  • Yellow skin with high fever, shivering or strong belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Yellow skin or eyes with dark urine, pale stools or itching all over: see a doctor today
  • A newborn or young baby who looks yellow, is very sleepy or feeds badly: see a doctor promptly
  • Yellow skin with weight loss, a swollen belly or no appetite for weeks: see a doctor soon
  • Yellow skin or eyes that last more than a few days for no clear reason: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Have the yellow colour checked by a doctor; a simple blood test can help find the cause
  • Look at the whites of your eyes in daylight and tell the doctor if they look yellow
  • Cut down on alcohol until a doctor has checked you, because it strains the liver
