Head lice

Tiny insects that live in the hair and scalp and cause itching

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Head lice are tiny wingless insects that live on the scalp, feed on blood and glue their eggs (nits) to the hair, causing an itchy scalp; they have nothing to do with dirty hair.

  • Scalp and hair
  • Most common in children
  • Itchy scalp, tiny eggs
Which doctor
Family doctor or children's doctor (paediatrician); a pharmacist can also advise
Common in
School-age children and their families; any hair type
Contagious
Yes, by close head-to-head contact, and sometimes shared combs or hats
Course
Does not go away by itself, but can be cleared with the right steps

Common signs

More signs

  • A feeling of something moving in the hair
  • Tiny grey-brown insects, or small white-yellow eggs (nits) stuck close to the scalp
  • Small red bumps or sore spots on the scalp, neck or shoulders from scratching
  • Trouble sleeping, as the itch is often worse at night; some people have no itch at first

Why it happens

  • Lice crawl from one head to another during close head-to-head contact
  • Sharing combs, hats, hair ties, headphones or pillows can sometimes pass them on
  • It is not caused by dirty hair or poor cleanliness; clean hair can have lice too
  • Common in children at school or day care, and it spreads easily within a family
  • Lice cannot jump or fly, and pets do not carry head lice

See a doctor first if

  • Sores, crusts, pus, swollen glands in the neck or a fever: the scratched skin may be infected, see a doctor
  • A baby or very young child with lice: see a doctor promptly before using any product
  • If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask a doctor or pharmacist before using any lice product
  • If you have asthma, allergies, eczema or broken scalp skin, ask a doctor or pharmacist before using a product
  • Lice are still there after a careful try, or keep coming back: see a doctor or pharmacist

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Comb damp, conditioned hair with a fine lice comb from roots to tips, and repeat every few days
  • Check everyone at home, and clear lice from everyone who has them at the same time
  • Never put petrol, kerosene or other strong chemicals on the hair or scalp; it is dangerous

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
