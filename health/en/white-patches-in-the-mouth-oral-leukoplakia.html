White patches in the mouth (oral leukoplakia)

A white or greyish patch inside the mouth that cannot be wiped off

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

A white or greyish patch forms on the lining of the mouth or on the tongue and cannot be wiped off; it is often painless, but a dentist or doctor should always check it.

  • Cheek, gum or tongue
  • Often linked to tobacco
  • Usually painless
Which doctor
Dentist or family doctor, who may refer you to a mouth or throat specialist
Common in
Mostly adults who smoke, chew tobacco or betel quid (sirih), or drink much alcohol
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow; a few patches can turn into mouth cancer over time, so regular checks matter

Common signs

  • A white or greyish patch on the cheek, gum, tongue or floor of the mouth that does not wipe off
  • The patch may be flat, thick, rough or slightly raised
  • Often no pain, though the spot may feel rough or sensitive to hot or spicy food
  • Sometimes red spots mixed in with the white patch
  • A patch that slowly gets bigger or thicker over weeks to months

Why it happens

  • Smoking or chewing tobacco, which irritates the lining of the mouth
  • Chewing betel quid (sirih) or areca nut
  • Drinking a lot of alcohol, especially together with smoking
  • Constant rubbing from a rough tooth, a broken filling or loose dentures
  • Often the cause is not known

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, or bleeding in the mouth that will not stop: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Any white patch that stays for two weeks or more: have a dentist or doctor look at it
  • A patch that turns red, thick or rough, starts to bleed, or has an ulcer or lump in it
  • A mouth sore or lump that does not go away in a few weeks
  • A lump in the neck, numbness in the mouth, a hoarse voice or weight loss that lasts for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Have the patch checked by a dentist or doctor; a tiny sample (biopsy) may be needed to be sure
  • Stop smoking and using tobacco, and stop chewing betel quid; ask for help to quit if you need it
  • Cut down on alcohol, and eat plenty of fruit and vegetables
  • Ask your dentist to smooth any rough tooth or adjust dentures that rub the mouth
  • Look in your mouth now and then, keep up regular dental check-ups, and report any change

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
