Parathyroid tumour

A growth on one of the four tiny glands in the neck that control calcium in the blood

ConditionCancer & tumoursWhole body

What it is

The parathyroid glands sit behind the thyroid. A growth on one is usually not cancer, but it can make the body hold too much calcium.

  • Tiny glands in the neck
  • More often in women
  • Bones, kidneys, tiredness
Which doctor
Hormone doctor (endocrinologist); neck surgeon if needed
Common in
Adults, more often women after middle age
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow, and some are found only on a blood test; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Most often a small harmless lump on one gland
  • More common after menopause
  • Sometimes a family condition, or past radiation to the neck
  • Cancer of this gland is rare

See a doctor first if

  • Confusion, severe weakness, or being very drowsy: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain, vomiting or a lot of thirst with passing a lot of urine
  • A new lump in the neck, or a hoarse voice that lasts
  • A bone that breaks after a small fall, or new bone pain
  • Fast heartbeat or fainting: go to hospital

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Keep your blood test and check-up visits
  • Drink enough water through the day unless your doctor limits fluids
  • Ask your doctor about calcium, vitamin D and supplements before taking any
  • Stay gently active to keep your bones strong

Questions and answers

What is Parathyroid tumour?

A growth on one of the four tiny glands in the neck that control calcium in the blood. The parathyroid glands sit behind the thyroid. A growth on one is usually not cancer, but it can make the body hold too much calcium.

What are the common signs of Parathyroid tumour?

Often no signs at first · Tired all the time · Aching bones · Very thirsty, drinking a lot · Feeling sick, nausea

Why does Parathyroid tumour happen?

The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Parathyroid tumour?

Keep your blood test and check-up visits · Drink enough water through the day unless your doctor limits fluids · Ask your doctor about calcium, vitamin D and supplements before taking any · Stay gently active to keep your bones strong
