Rheumatic arthritis

Painful, swollen joints that can move from joint to joint, often linked to a past throat infection

ConditionAutoimmune & rheumaticArms & legs

What it is

Rheumatic arthritis is joint inflammation that brings pain, swelling and warmth, often moving from one joint to another, and it can follow a throat infection with strep germs.

  • Joints of the arms and legs
  • Can follow a sore throat
  • Painful, swollen, warm joints
Which doctor
Family doctor; a rheumatologist (joint specialist), or a paediatrician for a child
Common in
Most often children and young people; the word is also used for joint aches in adults
Contagious
No, but the throat infection that can come before it can spread to others
Course
Can ease in weeks or last longer, and may come back; your heart may need checking

Common signs

Why it happens

  • After a throat infection with strep germs, the body's defences can wrongly react and inflame the joints
  • This wider illness is called rheumatic fever, and it can also affect the heart
  • A sore throat that went unnoticed or was not checked by a doctor can still lead to it
  • Crowded homes or schools, where throat infections spread easily, make it more likely
  • 'Rheumatic' aches in older adults may be other joint problems, which a doctor can tell apart

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, trouble breathing, a racing or uneven heartbeat, or fainting: call 999
  • One joint suddenly very hot, red and swollen, with fever or too painful to move: go to hospital now
  • Painful joints after a sore throat or fever, especially in a child: see a doctor promptly
  • Jerky movements you cannot control, a new rash, or small lumps under the skin
  • Joint pain and swelling that lasts for weeks, or morning stiffness that takes long to ease

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Rest a sore joint while it is hot and painful, then move it gently as it eases
  • A cool pack wrapped in a cloth may soothe a hot, swollen joint; do not put ice straight on skin
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine for the pain
  • Follow your doctor's plan to stop it coming back, and keep your check-ups, including for the heart
  • Have a sore throat with fever checked early, especially in children

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
