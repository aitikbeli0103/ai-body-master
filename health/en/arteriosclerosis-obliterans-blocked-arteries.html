Arteriosclerosis obliterans (blocked arteries)

Narrowing and blocking of the arteries in the legs, which lowers blood flow to the legs and feet

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

Fatty build-up slowly narrows the arteries that carry blood to the legs, so the legs and feet may not get enough blood, especially when walking.

  • Legs and feet
  • Older adults, smokers
  • Calf pain when walking
Which doctor
Family doctor, vascular surgeon or heart doctor
Common in
Older adults; people who smoke or have diabetes or high blood pressure
Contagious
No
Course
Usually slow over years; a doctor can explain how to protect your legs

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Fatty build-up inside the arteries (hardening of the arteries)

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden pale, cold, very painful or numb leg: go to hospital now
  • Chest pain, trouble breathing, or face drooping and arm weakness: call 999
  • A sore or wound on the foot or toe that does not heal
  • Toes or skin turning dark, blue or black
  • Pain in the leg at rest, especially at night

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Arteriosclerosis obliterans (blocked arteries)?

Narrowing and blocking of the arteries in the legs, which lowers blood flow to the legs and feet. Fatty build-up slowly narrows the arteries that carry blood to the legs, so the legs and feet may not get enough blood, especially when walking.

What are the common signs of Arteriosclerosis obliterans (blocked arteries)?

Hard to walk far · Cramps in the calf · Cold hands and feet · Cuts heal slowly · Numb, tingling feet

Why does Arteriosclerosis obliterans (blocked arteries) happen?

Smoking · High blood sugar or blood pressure · Getting older

How can I look after myself with Arteriosclerosis obliterans (blocked arteries)?

Stop smoking · Wear comfortable, supportive shoes · Check your blood pressure at home · Take medicine as your doctor says
