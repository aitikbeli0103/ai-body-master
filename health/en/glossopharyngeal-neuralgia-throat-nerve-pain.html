Glossopharyngeal neuralgia (throat nerve pain)

Brief, sharp, stabbing pain at the back of the throat, tongue or ear

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

A nerve that serves the throat and back of the tongue sends sudden, sharp pain, often when swallowing, talking or coughing.

  • Throat, tongue and ear
  • More often in later life
  • Sudden, stabbing pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, ear-nose-throat doctor, neurologist
Common in
Mostly adults in middle or later life; it is uncommon
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes in attacks that may fade and return; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • Sharp, electric-like pain at the back of the throat or tongue
  • Attacks set off by swallowing, chewing, talking or coughing
  • Each attack lasts seconds to a few minutes

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A blood vessel pressing on the nerve
  • A growth, infection or injury near the throat or base of the skull
  • Sometimes other nerve conditions

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, a slow heartbeat or feeling about to collapse with the pain: go to hospital now
  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, or face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Pain so bad that you cannot eat or drink
  • A lump in the neck, weight loss, or pain that keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor to find the cause, as other problems can feel the same
  • Note what sets off the pain, such as cold drinks or hard food
  • Choose soft, lukewarm food and small bites
