Fluid in the lungs (pulmonary oedema)

Fluid filling the tiny air sacs of the lungs, making breathing difficult

ConditionLungs & breathingChest

What it is

Fluid leaks into the air sacs of the lungs, so less air can get in. It often comes from a heart problem and can be an emergency.

  • Lungs
  • Often with heart illness
  • Struggling to breathe
Which doctor
Heart doctor (cardiologist), lung doctor; hospital if severe
Common in
People with heart failure, heart disease, high blood pressure or kidney illness
Contagious
No
Course
Can start suddenly or slowly; it needs a doctor to find and look after the cause

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A weak heart that cannot pump well (heart failure)
  • A heart attack, or problems with the heart valves

See a doctor first if

  • Severe trouble breathing, feeling of drowning, or cannot lie down: call 999
  • Chest pain, fainting, or blue or grey lips: call 999
  • Coughing up pink or frothy phlegm: go to hospital now
  • Waking at night short of breath, or fast weight gain with swollen legs
  • Getting more tired and breathless over days

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take heart and water medicines exactly as your doctor said; ask before changing them
  • Weigh yourself each morning and tell your doctor if it rises fast
