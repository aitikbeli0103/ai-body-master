Functional ovarian cyst

A fluid-filled sac on an ovary that forms during the monthly cycle

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

Each month an ovary makes a small sac. Sometimes it does not open or shrink as usual and stays as a fluid-filled cyst, which often goes away by itself.

  • Ovary, lower belly
  • Women who still have periods
  • Often no signs at all
Which doctor
Gynaecologist, family doctor
Common in
Women in their childbearing years
Contagious
No
Course
Many shrink or go away within weeks to months; a doctor may check again

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It is part of the normal monthly cycle of the ovary
  • Some women are more likely to get them again

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in the lower belly, with fainting, vomiting or fever: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant, or may be pregnant, with belly pain or bleeding: go to hospital now
  • Heavy bleeding, or bleeding after the periods have stopped for good
  • A belly that keeps getting bigger, or pain that does not go away
  • A cyst that is found after menopause

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest when you are sore, and avoid hard, jerky exercise if it hurts

Questions and answers

What is Functional ovarian cyst?

A fluid-filled sac on an ovary that forms during the monthly cycle. Each month an ovary makes a small sac. Sometimes it does not open or shrink as usual and stays as a fluid-filled cyst, which often goes away by itself.

What are the common signs of Functional ovarian cyst?

Dragging ache in the lower belly · Bloated, gassy belly · Period comes late or early

Why does Functional ovarian cyst happen?

Hormone changes · The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Functional ovarian cyst?

See your doctor for check-ups · Ask your pharmacist
