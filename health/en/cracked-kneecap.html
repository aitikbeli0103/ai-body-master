Cracked kneecap

A crack in the small round bone at the front of the knee

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legBones & muscles

What it is

The kneecap (patella) protects the front of the knee. It can crack after a fall onto the knee or a hard knock. Some cracks stay in place, while others need more care.

  • Front of the knee
  • After a fall or a knock
  • Swollen, painful knee
Which doctor
Emergency department, bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon)
Common in
People after falls, sports knocks or road accidents
Contagious
No
Course
Healing usually takes weeks to months; the doctor guides when to bend and walk

Common signs

More signs

  • Hard or painful to straighten the knee or lift the leg
  • Pain on walking, stairs or kneeling
  • A gap or dent you can feel over the kneecap

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Sudden strong pull of the thigh muscle during sport
  • Weak bones, which crack more easily

See a doctor first if

  • Cannot straighten the knee or lift the leg: go to hospital
  • The kneecap looks out of place, or the leg looks deformed
  • A deep cut over the knee, bleeding, or bone showing: go to hospital now
  • Numb, pale, cold or bluish lower leg or foot
  • Fever, or a wound that turns red or leaks pus

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Follow your doctor's advice on a brace, walking aids and weight on the leg
  • Do the gentle exercises your care team shows you
  • Keep all follow-up visits

Tell your therapist
