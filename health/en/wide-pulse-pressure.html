Wide pulse pressure

A big gap between the top and bottom blood pressure numbers

SymptomHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

Pulse pressure is the gap between the top and the bottom blood pressure numbers. It is called wide when this gap is larger than usual, and a doctor may look for the reason.

  • Blood pressure reading
  • More common with age
  • Often found at a check-up
Which doctor
Family doctor, heart doctor (cardiologist)
Common in
Older adults; also with fever, anaemia or an overactive thyroid
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor can explain it and check it again

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Stiff arteries, which become more common as we get older
  • A leaking heart valve
  • An overactive thyroid, or low blood count (anaemia)
  • Fever or other illness that makes the heart work harder

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain or sudden trouble breathing: call 999
  • Fainting or nearly fainting: go to hospital now
  • Sudden weakness on one side, face drooping or speech trouble: call 999
  • A racing or uneven heartbeat that does not settle
  • Swollen legs or breathlessness that keeps getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

What are the common signs of Wide pulse pressure?

Often no signs at first · Tired all the time · Racing, pounding heart · Short of breath on stairs · Fever, feeling hot

Why does Wide pulse pressure happen?

Stiff arteries, which become more common as we get older · A leaking heart valve · An overactive thyroid, or low blood count (anaemia) · Fever or other illness that makes the heart work harder

How can I look after myself with Wide pulse pressure?

Check your blood pressure at home · Keep a healthy weight · Stop smoking
