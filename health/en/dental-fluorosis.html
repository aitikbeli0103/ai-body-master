Dental fluorosis

White or brown marks on the teeth caused by too much fluoride while the teeth were forming

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

When a child's teeth are still forming under the gums, too much fluoride can change the outer layer (enamel), leaving faint white lines or spots, or stains, usually without pain.

  • Surface of the teeth
  • Starts in childhood
  • White or brown marks
Which doctor
Dentist (a children's dentist for a child)
Common in
Seen in children and adults; it forms in childhood while the teeth grow
Contagious
No
Course
The marks usually stay but do not spread; a dentist can explain ways to improve the look

Common signs

  • Faint white lines or flecks on the teeth, often hard to notice
  • Chalky white spots or patches
  • Yellow or brown stains in more marked cases
  • Rough or pitted enamel in severe cases
  • Usually on several teeth, and usually no pain

Why it happens

  • Too much fluoride while the teeth are forming, in the early years of childhood
  • Young children swallowing fluoride toothpaste instead of spitting it out
  • Drinking water or other drinks that contain a lot of fluoride
  • Fluoride tablets, drops or rinses used more than advised

See a doctor first if

  • A child has swallowed a lot of toothpaste, mouth rinse or fluoride tablets: go to hospital now
  • Vomiting, belly pain, drooling, weakness or fits after swallowing fluoride products: call 999
  • Swelling of the face or gums, a pus bubble on the gum, or fever with tooth pain: see a dentist or doctor today
  • Tooth pain, or teeth that are very sensitive to hot, cold or sweet things
  • Teeth that are pitted, crumbling or breaking
  • A brown or black spot that is getting bigger or has a hole, which may be decay

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Help young children brush, use only a small amount of toothpaste, and teach them to spit it out
  • Keep toothpaste, mouth rinses and fluoride tablets or drops out of children's reach
  • Ask your dentist or doctor before giving a child any fluoride tablets or drops
  • If the look bothers you, ask your dentist about options; ask before using whitening products
