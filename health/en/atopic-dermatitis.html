Atopic dermatitis

A long-lasting skin condition (often called eczema) with dry, red, very itchy skin that flares up and settles

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

The skin does not hold water well, so it gets dry, red and very itchy. Flare-ups come and go, and it often starts in babies or young children.

  • Skin, often in body folds
  • Often starts in childhood
  • Very itchy, dry, red skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) or allergy doctor
Common in
Often starts in babies and children; adults can have it too
Contagious
No
Course
Long-lasting, with flare-ups and calmer times; a doctor can help plan daily care

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The skin barrier is weak, so skin dries out and reacts easily
  • The body's defence system reacts too strongly in the skin

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen lips, face or tongue, or trouble breathing after a food, medicine or product: call 999
  • Painful clusters of blisters or sores on eczema skin, with fever or feeling unwell: go to hospital now
  • Skin red and sore over most of the body, with fever or chills: go to hospital now
  • Yellow crusts, pus, or skin that is hot and very sore: see a doctor promptly (it may be infected)
  • A baby or young child with bad eczema who is feeding poorly or very unsettled: see a doctor promptly
  • Itching stops you sleeping or working, or moisturising is not enough: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take short lukewarm showers, use mild fragrance-free soap, and pat dry
  • Keep nails short and try not to scratch; a cool, damp cloth can calm itchy skin
  • Wear soft cotton clothes and avoid rough wool and tight clothes
  • Learn what sets off your flare-ups and avoid it; use any creams as your doctor advises

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Atopic dermatitis?

A long-lasting skin condition (often called eczema) with dry, red, very itchy skin that flares up and settles. The skin does not hold water well, so it gets dry, red and very itchy. Flare-ups come and go, and it often starts in babies or young children.

What are the common signs of Atopic dermatitis?

Itchy skin · Dry, flaky skin · Red rash · Cannot fall asleep

Why does Atopic dermatitis happen?

Runs in the family · Dry air and air-conditioning · The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Atopic dermatitis?

Keep the skin moisturised
