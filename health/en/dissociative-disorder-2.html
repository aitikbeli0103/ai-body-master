Dissociative disorder

Feeling cut off from your thoughts, memory, body or surroundings

ConditionTold by a doctor: otherWhole body

What it is

In dissociative disorder, the mind may feel detached or unreal, or have gaps in memory. It is often linked to very hard experiences, and help is available.

  • Mind and memory
  • After very hard experiences
  • Feeling detached
Which doctor
Psychiatrist, psychologist, family doctor
Common in
People who went through trauma or long stress
Contagious
No
Course
It can come and go; with support from a professional, many people cope better over time

Common signs

More signs

  • Feeling unreal, or as if watching yourself from outside
  • Feeling numb or distant from feelings and people
  • Surroundings seem foggy, dreamlike or strange
  • Feeling worried, low or confused about who you are

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Often linked to trauma or abuse, especially in childhood
  • Other mental health problems, such as anxiety or low mood

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Hurting yourself, or being at risk of harm from others
  • Losing time or waking up in places you do not remember
  • Feelings of being unreal that last or stop you from work, study or daily life
  • Using alcohol or drugs to cope

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • When you feel unreal, look around and name things you can see, hear and touch
  • Avoid alcohol and drugs, and keep seeing your doctor or counsellor

Tell your therapist

  • Epilepsy or fits
  • A heart condition
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
