Peeling skin on the soles of the feet

The top layer of skin on the bottom of the feet comes off in flakes, patches or thin sheets

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legs

What it is

The outer layer of skin on the soles comes off in flakes, patches or thin sheets, and the cause can be anything from dry skin to a fungal infection.

  • Soles of the feet
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Dry, flaky or itchy skin
Which doctor
Family doctor, or a skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Anyone; more often with sweaty feet, closed shoes or dry skin
Contagious
Some causes can spread to others, such as athlete's foot (a fungal infection)
Course
Depends on the cause; mild cases often get better with simple foot care

Common signs

More signs

  • Sore skin when walking, especially if cracks open
  • Soggy skin or a bad smell if the feet stay wet

Why it happens

  • Dry skin, especially in dry weather or after long hot showers
  • Athlete's foot, a fungal infection that likes warm, damp feet
  • Eczema, or a reaction to shoes, socks, soap or other things touching the feet
  • Long-term skin conditions such as psoriasis
  • Sometimes it follows an illness with fever, or a reaction to a medicine

See a doctor first if

  • Widespread peeling or blisters with fever or mouth sores, or after a new medicine: go to hospital now
  • A child with peeling skin plus a high fever for days, red eyes, red lips or a rash: see a doctor promptly
  • Skin that is red, hot, swollen, very painful or oozing pus, or red streaks up the leg: see a doctor today
  • Peeling, cracks or sores on the feet in someone with diabetes or poor blood flow: see a doctor promptly
  • Peeling that spreads to the hands or nails, keeps coming back, or has not improved after a few weeks of gentle care

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash your feet daily with mild soap and dry well, especially between the toes
  • Change damp socks; choose cotton socks and shoes that let feet breathe, and let shoes dry out

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
