Ovarian teratoma (dermoid cyst)

A slow-growing ovarian cyst with tissue such as skin, hair or fat; usually not cancer

ConditionWomen's healthReproductive

What it is

A dermoid cyst grows from egg-forming cells and can hold tissue such as hair, skin or fat. It is usually not cancer and often has no signs, but it can twist.

  • On or in an ovary
  • Women of childbearing age
  • Often no signs
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a gynaecologist (women's doctor)
Common in
Women of childbearing age; also found in girls and older women
Contagious
No
Course
Slow growing; the doctor may watch it with scans or suggest removing it by surgery

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden strong pain in the lower belly if the cyst twists: this needs urgent care

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The exact reason is usually not known
  • It usually grows slowly and quietly, so it is often found by chance

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in the lower belly, with vomiting or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Fever, a hard, very tender belly or feeling very unwell after sudden pain: go to hospital now
  • Pregnant, with new belly pain or bleeding: go to hospital now
  • A cyst that keeps growing on scans, or one found after the menopause
  • Bloating that does not go away, feeling full quickly, or losing weight without trying
  • Pain that keeps coming back, or a lump you can feel in the lower belly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
