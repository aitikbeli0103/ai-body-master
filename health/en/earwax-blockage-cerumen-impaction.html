Earwax blockage (cerumen impaction)

Earwax that builds up and blocks the ear canal

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

Earwax protects the ear and normally moves out by itself. Sometimes it gets packed in and blocks the canal, so hearing is muffled and the ear feels full.

  • Outer ear canal
  • Common in older adults
  • Blocked, muffled ear
Which doctor
Family doctor, ear, nose and throat doctor (ENT)
Common in
Older adults, hearing-aid users, and people who use cotton buds
Contagious
No
Course
A doctor can check and clear it safely; it may build up again

Common signs

More signs

  • Itching or mild discomfort in the ear
  • Sometimes mild dizziness or a cough from the ear

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Poking cotton buds or fingers into the ear, which can push wax in deeper
  • Using hearing aids, earphones or ear plugs a lot
  • Narrow or hairy ear canals

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden hearing loss in one ear
  • Ear pain with fever, pus or blood coming from the ear
  • Dizziness with spinning, or the face drooping on one side: call 999 if sudden
  • A known hole in the eardrum or past ear surgery: see a doctor before trying anything
  • A child with a blocked ear who also has pain or fever: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not poke the ear with cotton buds, hairpins or fingers
  • Do not use ear candles
  • Wipe only the outer ear with a soft cloth
  • Let a doctor or nurse clear the wax if it keeps blocking the ear

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
