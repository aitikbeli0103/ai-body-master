Fatty lump under the skin (lipoma)

A soft, rubbery lump of fat under the skin that is usually painless and grows slowly

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

A lipoma is a soft lump of fat cells that grows slowly under the skin. It is not cancer and is usually harmless, but any new lump should be checked by a doctor to be sure.

  • Just under the skin
  • Often adults in middle age
  • Soft, rubbery lump
Which doctor
Family doctor; a surgeon or skin doctor if you want it removed
Common in
Adults, most often in middle age; sometimes runs in families
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stays and grows slowly over time; a doctor can remove it if it bothers you

Common signs

More signs

  • Usually painless, and grows very slowly
  • The skin over the lump looks normal
  • Often on the neck, shoulders, back, arms, belly or thighs
  • Some people have more than one lump

Why it happens

Other causes

  • More common from middle age onwards
  • Fat cells grow together into a soft lump under the skin

See a doctor first if

  • A lump that grows fast, turns hard, or no longer moves when pressed: see a doctor soon
  • Pain, redness or warmth in the lump, or a change in the skin over it: see a doctor
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness near the lump: see a doctor
  • A lump together with weight loss you cannot explain, fever or night sweats: see a doctor soon
  • A lump that comes back after it was removed, or any new lump you are unsure about: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Look at it and feel it now and then, and note its size so you can tell if it grows
  • Wear loose clothing and keep bag straps off the lump if it rubs
  • If it bothers you or looks different, ask a doctor what can be done

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
