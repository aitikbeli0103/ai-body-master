Tetracycline-stained teeth

Grey, yellow or brown colour inside the teeth from a medicine taken while the teeth were forming

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

Teeth look grey, yellow or brown because a medicine called tetracycline was taken while the teeth were forming, and the colour is inside the tooth so brushing does not remove it.

  • Colour inside the teeth
  • Linked to childhood medicine
  • Colour change, rarely painful
Which doctor
Dentist (cosmetic dentistry) to talk about the options
Common in
People given tetracycline as a child, or whose mother took it in pregnancy
Contagious
No
Course
The colour stays; a dentist can explain ways to improve how the teeth look

Common signs

  • Teeth that look grey, yellow, brown or blue-grey
  • Stripes or bands of colour across the teeth
  • Many teeth are affected, usually on both sides of the mouth
  • The colour can slowly get darker over time
  • Usually no pain; the problem is mainly the colour

Why it happens

  • Tetracycline taken by a child while the teeth are still forming
  • Tetracycline taken by the mother in pregnancy, which can reach the baby's forming teeth
  • The medicine binds to the tooth as it forms, so the colour sits inside the tooth
  • It is not caused by poor brushing or by food and drink stains
  • Doctors now avoid this medicine in pregnancy and in young children

See a doctor first if

  • Swollen face or neck, or trouble swallowing or breathing: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Toothache, a swollen gum or a bad taste: see a dentist soon, as stained teeth can still decay
  • One tooth that turns darker than the rest, on its own or after a knock: see a dentist
  • Very sensitive teeth, or teeth that chip or wear down fast: see a dentist
  • Before using a whitening product, ask your dentist, as it may not work well on this kind of stain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Brush gently twice a day with fluoride toothpaste and clean between the teeth
  • See your dentist regularly; stained teeth can decay like any others
  • Ask your dentist about ways to improve the look, and which ones suit your teeth
  • Cut down on smoking, tea and coffee, which add stains on the surface
  • Do not scrub hard to remove the colour; it is inside the tooth and scrubbing can wear the teeth

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
