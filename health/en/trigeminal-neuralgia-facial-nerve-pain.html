Trigeminal neuralgia (facial nerve pain)

Sudden, sharp, electric-shock-like pain on one side of the face

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

The trigeminal nerve carries feeling from the face. When it is irritated, even a light touch or a breeze can set off brief, severe pain.

  • One side of the face
  • More common in older adults
  • Sharp, shock-like pain
Which doctor
Family doctor, neurologist (brain and nerve doctor)
Common in
Mostly adults over middle age; slightly more in women
Contagious
No
Course
Pain often comes and goes in attacks; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

More signs

  • Attacks last seconds to a couple of minutes
  • Pain set off by chewing, talking, brushing teeth, washing the face or wind
  • Fear of eating or touching the face because of the pain

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A blood vessel pressing on the trigeminal nerve is a common reason
  • Other nerve conditions, such as multiple sclerosis
  • Rarely, a growth pressing on the nerve

See a doctor first if

  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • Sudden severe headache or loss of vision: go to hospital now
  • Facial pain together with numbness, weakness or a new lump
  • Pain so strong that you cannot eat, drink or sleep
  • Pain after a face injury, or with fever or a rash on the face

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Note what sets off the pain and try to avoid it
  • Eat soft food and take small bites if chewing hurts

Tell your therapist

  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
