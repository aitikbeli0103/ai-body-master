Green urine

Urine that looks green or blue-green instead of the usual pale yellow

SymptomKidneys & urinaryAbdomen

What it is

Green urine is an unusual colour change. Most often it comes from food colouring or a medicine, but sometimes an infection or another problem is behind it.

  • Urine passing out
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Colour change, often no pain
Which doctor
Family doctor; urinary specialist (urologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; after some foods, drinks or medicines
Contagious
No
Course
Often passes within a day or two once the cause is gone

Common signs

More signs

  • Urine that looks green, blue or greenish-brown
  • Often no pain at all

Why it happens

  • Food colouring in sweets, drinks or some foods
  • Some medicines or medical dyes used in tests; ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • A urine infection with certain germs
  • Rarely, an unusual problem in the liver, bile or urinary system

See a doctor first if

  • Green urine with fever, shaking chills or pain in the side or back: see a doctor today
  • Burning when passing urine, or needing the toilet very often
  • Blood in the urine, or yellow skin and eyes
  • Green urine that lasts more than two days with no clear reason
  • A baby or child with green urine: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Think back to foods, drinks and medicines from the last day or two
  • Do not stop any medicine yourself; ask your doctor or pharmacist
  • Tell your doctor how long it has lasted and what else you notice

Tell your therapist

  • A kidney or liver condition
  • Pregnancy
  • Fever or an active infection
