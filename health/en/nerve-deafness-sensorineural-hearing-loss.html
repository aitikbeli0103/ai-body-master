Nerve deafness (sensorineural hearing loss)

Hearing loss from damage to the inner ear or the hearing nerve

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

The tiny hair cells in the inner ear, or the nerve that carries sound to the brain, are damaged. Sounds become softer and speech is harder to follow, especially in noise.

  • Inner ear and hearing nerve
  • More common with age
  • Hard to hear, ringing
Which doctor
ENT doctor, hearing specialist (audiologist)
Common in
Older adults; people exposed to loud noise
Contagious
No
Course
Often stays the same or slowly worsens; a hearing check shows what can help

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long exposure to loud noise, such as work noise or loud music
  • Some illnesses, head injuries, or certain medicines

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden hearing loss in one or both ears: go to hospital now, as early care matters
  • Face drooping, arm weakness or trouble speaking: call 999
  • Hearing loss with strong spinning dizziness or a severe headache
  • Hearing that is worse on one side only
  • A child who does not react to sounds or is slow to speak

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Get your hearing checked by a hearing specialist
  • Protect your ears from loud noise with ear protection
  • Ask people to face you and speak clearly; reduce background noise
  • Ask your doctor about hearing aids if everyday life is hard
  • Do not use cotton buds or sharp things in the ear

Tell your therapist

  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Dizzy when turning the head
  • High blood pressure
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
