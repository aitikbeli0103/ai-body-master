3D motion sickness (3D vertigo)

Feeling dizzy or sick after watching 3D films, games or virtual worlds

ConditionBrain & nervesHead

What it is

Your eyes see movement on the screen but your body stays still. When these two messages do not match, the brain gets confused and you can feel dizzy or sick.

  • Head, eyes and balance
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Dizzy, sweaty, queasy
Which doctor
Family doctor; ear, nose and throat doctor if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone, especially those who also get car or sea sickness
Contagious
No
Course
Usually eases within minutes to hours after you stop watching

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Eyes see motion while the inner ear feels none, so the brain gets mixed signals
  • Fast-moving scenes, big screens or sitting very close to the screen
  • Long viewing time without a break
  • Being tired, hungry, or someone who easily gets car sickness

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden weakness on one side, face drooping or speech trouble: call 999
  • A sudden, very bad headache, or double vision
  • Fainting, chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Dizziness or vomiting that does not ease after you stop
  • A fit, or flashing lights setting off strange feelings: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Stop watching and look at something still, like a far wall or window
  • Sit down, breathe slowly and get some fresh air
  • Take regular breaks and sit farther from the screen in a bright room
  • Sip water and avoid watching on an empty or very full stomach

Tell your therapist

  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Dizzy when turning the head

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
