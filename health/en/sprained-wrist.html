Sprained wrist

Overstretched or torn ligaments in the wrist, usually after a fall or twist

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legs

What it is

Ligaments are the tough bands that hold the wrist bones together. A fall onto the hand or a sudden twist can stretch or tear them, causing pain and swelling.

  • Wrist joint
  • After a fall or sport
  • Painful, swollen wrist
Which doctor
Family doctor, orthopaedic doctor or hospital emergency
Common in
Anyone; common after falls and in sports
Contagious
No
Course
Mild sprains often settle in a few weeks; worse ones take longer

Common signs

More signs

  • Tenderness when touched
  • A stiff wrist that is hard to bend

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A sudden twist or heavy lifting
  • Sports such as badminton, basketball or cycling
  • Weak wrist muscles or slippery floors

See a doctor first if

  • A bent or deformed wrist, or you cannot move the hand: go to hospital now
  • A numb, pale, cold or bluish hand or fingers: go to hospital now
  • Heavy swelling or severe pain after a fall, as it may be a broken bone
  • Pain that does not ease after a week or two

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Raise the hand on a pillow to help swelling
  • Ask your doctor about a wrist support and when to start gentle movement

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
