Retracted eardrum

The eardrum is pulled inward because of low pressure behind it

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

The small tube joining the ear to the back of the nose does not open well. Air pressure drops behind the eardrum and pulls it inward, so the ear feels blocked.

  • Middle ear and eardrum
  • Children and adults
  • Blocked, muffled ear
Which doctor
Ear doctor (ENT), family doctor
Common in
People with colds, allergies or blocked noses; children
Contagious
No
Course
Often improves when the nose and ear tube clear; some need follow-up

Common signs

More signs

  • Popping or crackling when you swallow or yawn

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Colds, allergies or a blocked nose
  • Sudden pressure changes, such as flying or diving
  • In children, large adenoids

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden hearing loss: see a doctor the same day
  • Weakness or drooping of one side of the face: go to hospital now
  • Strong spinning dizziness
  • Severe ear pain, fever, or fluid or blood from the ear
  • A child who pulls at the ear, has a fever or does not hear well: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Swallow, chew gum or yawn to help the ear tube open
  • Do not blow your nose hard; do it gently, one side at a time
  • Keep away from allergy triggers, smoke and dust
  • Do not poke the ear; see a doctor if it stays blocked

Tell your therapist

  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
