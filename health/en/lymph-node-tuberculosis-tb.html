Lymph node tuberculosis (TB)

TB infection in the lymph glands, often causing lumps that do not hurt

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

TB germs settle in the lymph glands (nodes), which slowly swell into lumps. Most often they are in the neck, but they can be elsewhere.

  • Lymph glands
  • Adults and children
  • Slow-growing lumps
Which doctor
Family doctor first; TB or infection doctor
Common in
People with weak body defences or in contact with TB
Contagious
Usually not; lung TB found with it can spread
Course
Slow over weeks to months; needs a doctor's long plan

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • TB germs breathed in that then reach the lymph glands
  • Weak body defences, such as HIV or diabetes

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, or a lump pressing on the throat: go to hospital now
  • A lump that keeps growing, turns red or leaks fluid
  • A cough lasting weeks, coughing blood or weight loss
  • Fever and night sweats that keep coming back
  • Any new lump that lasts for weeks, as other illnesses can look the same

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Lymph node tuberculosis (TB)?

TB infection in the lymph glands, often causing lumps that do not hurt. TB germs settle in the lymph glands (nodes), which slowly swell into lumps. Most often they are in the neck, but they can be elsewhere.

What are the common signs of Lymph node tuberculosis (TB)?

Swollen glands in the neck · Fever, feeling hot · Soaking sweats at night · Losing weight without trying · Tired all the time

Why does Lymph node tuberculosis (TB) happen?

Close contact or sharing items · Crowded places and droplets

How can I look after myself with Lymph node tuberculosis (TB)?

See your doctor for check-ups · Take medicine as your doctor says · Do not squeeze or pick at it
