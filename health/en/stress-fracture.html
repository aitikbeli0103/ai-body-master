Stress fracture

A tiny crack in a bone caused by repeated strain, not one big injury

ConditionAny other bone or soft-tissue injuryBones & muscles

What it is

A small crack forms in a bone after it is loaded again and again, such as from running or marching. Pain slowly builds up and eases with rest.

  • Often shin, foot or hip bone
  • Runners, athletes, soldiers
  • Pain that grows with activity
Which doctor
Family doctor, bone specialist (orthopaedic) or sports doctor
Common in
Runners, dancers, athletes, soldiers; people with weak bones
Contagious
No
Course
Usually needs weeks to months of rest from the activity that caused it

Common signs

  • Pain at one spot that starts mild and slowly gets worse
  • Pain during activity that eases with rest
  • A spot that is tender when pressed, with mild swelling
  • Later, pain even at rest or at night

Why it happens

  • Doing much more running, jumping or marching than usual, too fast
  • Hard surfaces or worn-out, poorly fitting shoes
  • Weak bones, low calcium or vitamin D, or very low body weight
  • In women, missed periods can weaken bones

See a doctor first if

  • You cannot put weight on the leg, or sudden severe pain after a small step or twist: go to hospital
  • Deep groin or hip pain when walking, as the hip bone can break fully
  • Pain at night or at rest that keeps getting worse
  • Numbness, or a foot that is pale or cold
  • Pain that does not ease after a couple of weeks of rest

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Stop the activity that causes the pain and rest the bone
  • See a doctor, because a scan may be needed to confirm it
  • Use crutches or a boot if your doctor advises
  • Go back to sport slowly and only when your doctor says it is safe
  • Wear good shoes and eat foods with calcium and vitamin D

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pregnancy
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
