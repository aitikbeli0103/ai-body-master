Corrosive gastritis

Injury to the stomach lining after swallowing a strong acid, alkali or other harsh chemical

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Corrosive gastritis is a burn-like injury to the stomach lining after swallowing a strong acid, alkali or cleaning chemical. The mouth and food pipe may be hurt too. Get help at once.

  • Mouth, food pipe and stomach
  • After swallowing a chemical
  • Burning pain, an emergency
Which doctor
Emergency doctor at a hospital; later a stomach and bowel specialist
Common in
Young children who swallow cleaners by mistake; sometimes adults
Contagious
No
Course
An emergency that needs hospital care at once; follow-up with a doctor is important

Common signs

More signs

  • Burning pain in the mouth, throat, chest or upper belly
  • Burns or white patches on the lips, tongue or mouth

Why it happens

  • Swallowing a strong cleaner, drain cleaner, bleach or acid by mistake
  • Chemicals kept in drink bottles or in containers with no label
  • Young children are curious and put things in their mouths
  • Sometimes swallowed on purpose, when a person is in deep distress

See a doctor first if

  • Swallowed a strong acid, alkali, bleach or cleaning chemical: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Do not make the person vomit, and give nothing to eat or drink unless a doctor says so
  • Trouble breathing, noisy breathing, a hoarse voice or heavy drooling: call 999
  • Vomiting blood, black stools, or strong chest or belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Thoughts of harming yourself, or the chemical was swallowed on purpose: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Trouble swallowing or vomiting food in the weeks and months after: see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Bring the container or label to the hospital, so the doctor knows what was swallowed
  • Follow your doctor's advice on eating and drinking while your throat and stomach recover
  • Keep chemicals in their original labelled containers, locked away from children
  • If you feel very low, talk to someone you trust or your doctor; you do not have to cope alone
