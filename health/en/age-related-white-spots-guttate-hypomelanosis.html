Age-related white spots (guttate hypomelanosis)

Small, flat, pale spots on sun-exposed skin that appear with age and are usually harmless

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

As skin ages, small, flat, white spots can appear, mostly on the arms and legs, often after many years of sun; they usually cause no pain or itching.

  • Arms, legs, sometimes face
  • More common with age
  • Small white flat spots
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) to check spots
Common in
Middle-aged and older adults, after many years of sun exposure
Contagious
No
Course
Spots usually stay, and new ones may appear slowly; they do not hurt

Common signs

  • Small, flat white spots that are round or oval
  • Spots mostly on the forearms and lower legs, sometimes on the face, neck or back
  • The skin around the spots looks normal
  • Spots feel smooth, not scaly, and do not itch or hurt
  • New spots can slowly appear over the years

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It is not caused by poor washing, and it is not an infection

See a doctor first if

  • A pale patch where the skin feels numb or has lost feeling
  • A spot that grows, changes colour or shape, bleeds, or becomes a sore that will not go away
  • White patches that are itchy, scaly or painful
  • Patches that spread quickly, join together, or appear on the lips or around the eyes
  • White spots in a child or young adult: it is more likely to be something else, so see a doctor soon

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not try strong creams or home products on the spots; ask your doctor or pharmacist first
  • Look over your skin now and then, and tell your doctor about any spot that changes
  • The spots are usually harmless; if they bother you, ask a skin doctor about your options

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
