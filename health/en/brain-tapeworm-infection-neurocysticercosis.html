Brain tapeworm infection (neurocysticercosis)

An infection of the brain by the young form of the pork tapeworm

ConditionInfectionsHead

What it is

Tiny cysts from the pork tapeworm settle in the brain. Some people have no signs for years; others get fits or headaches. A doctor needs to confirm it with a scan.

  • The brain
  • From tapeworm eggs
  • Headache or fits
Which doctor
Brain doctor (neurologist), infection doctor
Common in
Places with poor sanitation where pork is eaten
Contagious
Not from the brain cysts; a tapeworm carrier can pass eggs
Course
Varies a lot; needs a doctor's follow-up and regular scans

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Swallowing eggs of the pork tapeworm from food, water or hands with germs
  • Living with someone who carries the tapeworm in the gut
  • Poor hand washing and unwashed vegetables
  • Eating undercooked pork can cause the tapeworm in the gut, which is the source

See a doctor first if

  • A fit (seizure), or fainting suddenly: call 999
  • Face drooping, weak arm or leg, or trouble speaking: call 999
  • Sudden severe headache with vomiting or confusion: go to hospital now
  • New vision changes or a headache that will not go away
  • Lumps under the skin together with any of the above

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash hands well with soap before eating and after the toilet
  • Wash vegetables and fruit well and cook pork until fully done
  • Keep your follow-up visits and ask about driving and swimming safety if you had a fit

Tell your therapist

  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Fever or an active infection
