Hepatitis

Swelling (inflammation) of the liver, most often caused by a virus

ConditionLiver, gallbladder & pancreasAbdomen

What it is

Hepatitis means the liver is inflamed. It may be short-lived or long-lasting. Viruses are the most common cause, but alcohol, some medicines and other conditions can also cause it.

  • Liver, upper right belly
  • Often caused by a virus
  • Tired, sick, no appetite
Which doctor
Family doctor; liver or digestive doctor if needed
Common in
Anyone; risk depends on the cause, such as virus, alcohol or some medicines
Contagious
Some types can be: A and E by food or water; B, C and D by blood or body fluids
Course
Short-term types often settle within weeks; long-term types need regular check-ups

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some medicines, herbal products or supplements can harm the liver
  • The body's own defences attacking the liver (autoimmune hepatitis)
  • Fat build-up in the liver, certain poisons, or sometimes no cause is found

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, passing black stools, or fainting: call 999
  • Confusion, very sleepy, or hard to wake: go to hospital now
  • Cannot keep fluids down, very dry mouth, or passing very little urine: go to hospital
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools: see a doctor today
  • A high fever, a fever that lasts, or severe belly pain: see a doctor today
  • A baby, child, pregnant woman or older person with these signs: see a doctor promptly

Questions and answers

What is Hepatitis?

Swelling (inflammation) of the liver, most often caused by a virus. Hepatitis means the liver is inflamed. It may be short-lived or long-lasting. Viruses are the most common cause, but alcohol, some medicines and other conditions can also cause it.

What are the common signs of Hepatitis?

Tired all the time · Feeling sick, nausea · No appetite · Pain in the upper belly · Yellow skin and eyes · Fever, feeling hot

Why does Hepatitis happen?

Germs (infection) · Drinking alcohol

How can I look after myself with Hepatitis?

Rest well · Cut down on alcohol · Ask your pharmacist · Wash your hands often · Get the recommended vaccines
