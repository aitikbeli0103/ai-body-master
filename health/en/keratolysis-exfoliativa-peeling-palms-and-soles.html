Keratolysis exfoliativa (peeling palms and soles)

Usually harmless, repeated peeling of the skin on the palms and sometimes the soles

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Small round patches on the palms, and sometimes the soles, dry out and peel in thin sheets. It is usually painless and often comes and goes.

  • Palms and soles
  • Often in hot, humid weather
  • Peeling skin, rarely painful
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps returning
Common in
Teens and young adults; more often in warm, humid weather
Contagious
No
Course
Often comes and goes; a patch usually settles by itself within weeks

Common signs

More signs

  • Sometimes the soles of the feet or the fingers are affected too
  • Usually little or no pain or itching
  • Hands may feel dry or tight, and it often comes back in warm weather

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Frequent hand washing, strong soaps, cleaning products or other chemicals
  • Rubbing or friction, for example from tools, sport or tight shoes

See a doctor first if

  • Peeling skin with a high fever, red eyes, mouth sores or a rash over the body: go to hospital now
  • A child with a fever and peeling hands or feet: see a doctor promptly
  • Painful cracks, bleeding, blisters, pus, or skin that is red, hot and swollen
  • Peeling that began after a new medicine: tell your doctor soon, and do not stop any medicine on your own
  • Strong itching, peeling that spreads past the palms and soles, or that does not settle after some weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep hands and feet dry and cool, and wear breathable socks and shoes
