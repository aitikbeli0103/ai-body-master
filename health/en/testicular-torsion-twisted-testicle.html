Testicular torsion (twisted testicle)

A twist of the cord that holds a testicle, which cuts off its blood supply

ConditionMen's healthReproductive

What it is

The cord that supports a testicle twists and cuts off its blood supply. This causes sudden, severe pain and is an emergency: go to hospital at once.

  • One testicle, in the scrotum
  • Mostly teens and young men
  • Sudden, severe pain
Which doctor
Hospital emergency department now; then a urologist or surgeon
Common in
Most often teenage boys and young men, but it can happen at any age
Contagious
No
Course
An emergency: the sooner it is seen, the better the chance of saving the testicle

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden, severe pain in one testicle or the scrotum
  • Swelling, redness, or a hard, very tender testicle
  • One testicle sitting higher than usual or at an odd angle
  • Pain that eases by itself can still be a warning: do not wait

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The testicle is not fixed firmly inside the scrotum, often from birth, so it can twist
  • A testicle that did not move down properly as a baby
  • It is not caused by anything the person did wrong

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle or the scrotum: go to the emergency department now, or call 999
  • Do not wait for the pain to settle: every hour counts
  • Swelling, redness or a testicle that sits high, especially with vomiting: go to hospital now
  • A baby or boy who cries a lot, will not feed or has a swollen, red or dark scrotum: go to hospital now
  • Pain in a testicle that comes and goes: see a doctor the same day, even if it settles

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Go to hospital at once; do not wait or try to fix it at home
  • Ask someone to take you; do not drive yourself while in pain
  • Avoid eating or drinking until a doctor has seen you, as an operation may be needed
  • Teenage boys should speak up early if a testicle suddenly hurts; there is nothing to be shy about
  • Afterwards, follow the doctor's advice on rest and when to go back to sport
