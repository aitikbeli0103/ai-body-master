White patches on the legs

Patches of skin on the legs that look lighter or whiter than the skin around them

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

Parts of the skin on the legs look lighter or white. Small flat spots on the shins are common and often not serious, but a doctor can check the cause.

  • On the legs, often the shins
  • More common with age
  • Flat white spots or patches
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor; a vein (vascular) specialist if needed
Common in
Adults, more with age and sun; people with leg vein problems
Contagious
Some causes can be, such as certain skin infections; most are not
Course
Depends on the cause; small sun-related spots tend to stay, others can change

Common signs

More signs

  • Small, flat, pale or white spots, often on the shins or forearms
  • Larger pale patches with clear edges, which may slowly spread
  • Thin, smooth white patches near the ankles, sometimes with dark spots around them
  • The spots themselves are usually painless

Why it happens

  • Small flat spots from years of sun on the legs, which are more common as people get older
  • Vitiligo, where parts of the skin lose their colour; often the reason is not known
  • Skin that is recovering after eczema, insect bites, cuts, burns or scars
  • Poor blood flow in the leg veins, which can leave thin, pale patches near the ankles
  • A fungal skin infection, or another skin condition that a doctor needs to check

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden pain and swelling in one leg, with chest pain or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A leg that is red, hot, swollen and painful, especially with fever: see a doctor today
  • A white patch next to an open sore on the lower leg or ankle that will not go away: see a doctor soon
  • A pale patch that has lost feeling, or numb, weak feet: see a doctor soon
  • If you have diabetes, show any new patch, sore or numb area on your legs or feet to a doctor soon
  • A patch that is raised, bleeds or turns into a sore that will not go away

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep walking each day; if your legs swell, rest with them raised on a pillow
  • Check your legs and feet often; if you have diabetes, check them every day
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before using any cream or home mixture on the patches

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
