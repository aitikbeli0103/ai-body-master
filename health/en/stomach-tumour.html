Stomach tumour

A lump or abnormal growth in the stomach that needs tests to tell what it is

ConditionCancer & tumoursAbdomen

What it is

A stomach tumour is a growth in the stomach wall. Some are not cancer and some are, so a doctor needs tests, such as a scope and a small tissue sample, to know.

  • Stomach, upper belly
  • More common in older adults
  • Upper belly pain, no appetite
Which doctor
Family doctor first; stomach doctor (gastroenterologist), surgeon, cancer doctor
Common in
Adults, more often middle-aged and older
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type and stage; your care team can explain

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A long-lasting stomach infection with a germ called H. pylori

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, or black, sticky stools: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain, a hard swollen belly, or fainting: go to hospital now
  • Trouble swallowing, or vomiting that keeps happening
  • Weight loss without trying, or feeling full after a few bites
  • Upper belly pain or indigestion that lasts for weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Stomach tumour?

A lump or abnormal growth in the stomach that needs tests to tell what it is. A stomach tumour is a growth in the stomach wall. Some are not cancer and some are, so a doctor needs tests, such as a scope and a small tissue sample, to know.

What are the common signs of Stomach tumour?

Pain in the upper belly · Feeling sick, nausea · No appetite · Losing weight without trying · Bloated, gassy belly

Why does Stomach tumour happen?

Smoking · Salty, smoked or preserved food · Runs in the family

How can I look after myself with Stomach tumour?

See your doctor for check-ups · Eat less salt · Stop smoking
