Cat-scratch disease

A germ infection from a cat scratch or bite that often makes nearby glands swell

ConditionInfectionsSkinWhole body

What it is

Germs from a cat, often a kitten, can get into the skin through a scratch, bite or lick. A small bump may appear, and nearby glands swell and become tender, sometimes with fever.

  • Skin and nearby glands
  • Children and cat owners
  • Swollen glands, mild fever
Which doctor
Family doctor; an infectious disease doctor if needed
Common in
Children and young people; people with cats or kittens at home
Contagious
Not from person to person; caught from a cat's scratch, bite or lick on broken skin
Course
Usually mild; swollen glands can take weeks or months to settle

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A scratch, bite or lick on broken skin from a cat that carries the germ (Bartonella)
  • Kittens are more likely to carry it, and the cat usually looks healthy
  • Cats pick up the germ from fleas, and it can stay on their claws and mouth

See a doctor first if

  • Severe headache, confusion, fits or being hard to wake: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • A red, painful eye with swelling next to it, or any change in vision: see a doctor now
  • High fever, shaking chills or feeling very unwell: go to hospital
  • A gland that is very swollen, hot, red or leaking pus, or a bite or scratch that spreads redness
  • Swollen glands that last many weeks, keep growing or feel hard, or fever that goes on for days
  • People with a weak immune system: see a doctor early after any cat bite or scratch that looks infected

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash a cat scratch or bite right away with soap and running water
  • Do not let a cat lick open cuts, and wash your hands after playing with cats
  • Keep your cat's nails short and ask a vet about fleas; avoid rough play with kittens

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
