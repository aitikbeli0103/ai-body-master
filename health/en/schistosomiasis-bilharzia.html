Schistosomiasis (bilharzia)

A water-borne parasite infection that can slowly affect the bowel, liver or bladder

ConditionInfectionsBlood

What it is

Tiny parasite larvae in lakes and rivers can enter the skin of people who swim or wade there. Over time the worms' eggs can cause swelling and damage in the bowel, liver or bladder.

  • Blood vessels, bowel, liver
  • Swimming in lakes or rivers
  • Itchy skin, fever, belly pain
Which doctor
Family doctor; infectious disease or tropical medicine doctor
Common in
People who live in or travel to warm regions with infected fresh water
Contagious
No, not from person to person; caught from infected fresh water
Course
Often slow; can quietly harm the liver, bowel or bladder if not found

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Tiny parasite larvae live in some freshwater snails and are released into the water
  • They enter through the skin when a person swims, wades, washes or fishes in infected water
  • Infected people who pass urine or stool in or near the water keep the cycle going
  • Risk is higher in warm regions of Africa, Asia, the Middle East and South America
  • Children who play in water, farmers, fishermen and travellers are more at risk

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood, black stools or a hard, swollen belly: go to hospital now
  • Fits, sudden weakness or numbness in the legs, or severe back pain: go to hospital now
  • Blood in the urine or stool, or burning when passing urine: see a doctor soon
  • Fever, cough, rash or muscle aches after swimming in fresh water abroad: see a doctor promptly
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or long-lasting belly pain, diarrhoea or weight loss: see a doctor
  • You swam or waded in fresh water in an affected area, even if you feel well: tell your doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Avoid swimming, wading or washing in lakes, rivers or ponds where the infection is found
  • Drink safe water: boiled, filtered or bottled
  • Never pass urine or stool in or near open water; use a toilet
  • Tell your doctor if you were in fresh water in an affected area, even if you feel well
  • Follow your doctor's advice, take any medicine exactly as prescribed, and go to your check-ups

  • A kidney or liver condition

  • Broken, red or sore skin
