Breast lump

A swelling or hard spot that you can feel in or near the breast

SymptomWomen's healthChest

What it is

A breast lump is a spot that feels different from the rest of the breast. Most lumps are not cancer, but every new lump should be checked by a doctor.

  • Breast or armpit
  • Any age; men too
  • A lump you can feel
Which doctor
Family doctor first, then breast clinic or breast surgeon
Common in
Women of all ages; less often men
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the cause; a doctor can explain what the lump is

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump that feels firm, rubbery or different from the tissue around it
  • Breast pain or tenderness, though many lumps do not hurt
  • Changes in the skin or nipple over the lump

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Fluid-filled cysts or non-cancer lumps such as fibroadenoma
  • Infection or injury of the breast
  • Sometimes breast cancer, which is why every new lump needs a check

See a doctor first if

  • Any new lump that does not go away after one period: see a doctor soon
  • A lump that is hard, fixed, or growing
  • Blood-stained fluid from the nipple, a nipple turning inward, or dimpled skin
  • A lump in the armpit or near the collarbone
  • A red, hot, painful breast with fever: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not wait or hope it goes away; book a check
  • Get to know how your breasts normally look and feel
  • Go for breast screening as your doctor advises

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Broken, red or sore skin
