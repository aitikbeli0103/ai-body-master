Testicular cancer

Cancer that starts in a testicle, often first noticed as a lump

ConditionCancer & tumoursReproductive

What it is

Testicular cancer is when abnormal cells grow out of control in a testicle, often first showing as a lump or swelling that is not always painful.

  • In a testicle
  • Often younger men
  • A lump, often painless
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a urologist and a cancer doctor (oncologist)
Common in
Mostly younger men; higher risk after an undescended testicle or with family history
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type and how early it is found; the cancer team explains the plan

Common signs

More signs

  • A lump or swelling in one testicle, often without pain
  • One testicle feels bigger, firmer or heavier than before
  • Sometimes tender or enlarged breasts

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A testicle that did not move down into the scrotum in childhood
  • Age: it is found most often in young and middle-aged men

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in a testicle, with swelling or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Chest pain, coughing blood or trouble breathing: call 999
  • A new lump, swelling or hard area in a testicle: see a doctor soon, do not wait
  • A heavy feeling or dull ache in the groin, scrotum or lower belly that does not go away
  • Back pain that keeps getting worse, or weight loss you cannot explain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Learn how your testicles normally feel, and tell a doctor about any change
  • Go to all your appointments and follow your cancer care team's plan
  • Never stop or change treatment or medicine on your own; ask your team first
  • Ask your team about pain, fertility, food and emotional support
  • Talk with family, friends or a support group; you do not have to cope alone

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
