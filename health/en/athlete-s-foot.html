Athlete's foot

A fungal skin infection of the feet that causes itching, peeling and cracks, especially between the toes

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsArms & legsSkin

What it is

A fungus that likes warm, damp places grows on the skin of the feet, causing itchy, red, peeling or cracked skin, most often between the toes.

  • Feet, between the toes
  • Damp, sweaty shoes and socks
  • Itchy, burning, peeling skin
Which doctor
Family doctor; skin doctor (dermatologist) if it keeps coming back
Common in
Anyone; more often with sweaty feet, closed shoes, or shared pools and showers
Contagious
Yes, through touching the infected skin or sharing damp floors, towels or shoes
Course
Can last for weeks and often returns if the feet stay damp

Common signs

More signs

  • White, soggy skin and cracks between the toes that can sting
  • Burning or stinging, and sometimes a bad smell from the feet

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A fungus (tinea) that grows on the skin in warm, damp places
  • Walking barefoot in public showers, pools and changing rooms
  • Diabetes, a weak immune system, or small cuts on the feet can make it more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Red, hot, swollen, very painful foot, red streaks up the leg, or fever: go to hospital or see a doctor today
  • You have diabetes or numb feet: see a doctor early for any foot rash or sore
  • Pus, yellow crusts, open sores or a bad smell: see a doctor promptly
  • Not better after a couple of weeks of care, spreading to the nails, or keeps coming back: see a doctor
  • A baby or young child with a rash on the feet: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Wash your feet every day and dry them well, especially between the toes
  • Wear clean cotton socks, change them when damp, and let shoes air out between wears
  • Wear slippers or sandals in public showers, pools and changing rooms

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
