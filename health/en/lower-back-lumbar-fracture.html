Lower-back (lumbar) fracture

A crack or break in one of the lower-back bones (lumbar vertebrae)

ConditionBack & spineLower back

What it is

The lumbar bones carry much of your body weight. A fall, an accident or weak bones can crack one of them, causing sudden lower-back pain that is worse on moving.

  • Lower back
  • After a fall or with weak bones
  • Sudden strong back pain
Which doctor
Hospital emergency, orthopaedic or spine doctor
Common in
People after a fall or accident; older adults with weak bones
Contagious
No
Course
Usually takes weeks to months; a doctor decides the care needed

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A car or motorbike accident
  • Weak bones (osteoporosis), where even a small fall or lifting can crack a bone

See a doctor first if

  • Weak or numb legs, or numbness around the groin or buttocks: go to hospital now
  • Trouble passing urine or stools, or leaking of either: go to hospital now
  • Back pain after a serious fall or accident: go to hospital now and avoid moving
  • Severe pain that stops you standing or walking
  • Fever or weight loss with ongoing back pain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Get checked first; a back fracture needs a doctor's scan and advice
  • Move slowly, roll to the side to get out of bed and avoid twisting or bending

Tell your therapist

  • Recent back injury or surgery
  • Weak bones
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Weak leg or foot
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
