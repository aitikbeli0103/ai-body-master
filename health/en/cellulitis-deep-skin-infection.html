Cellulitis (deep skin infection)

A bacterial infection of the deeper skin that makes an area red, swollen, warm and painful

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

Germs get in through a break in the skin and infect its deeper layers, so the area turns red, swollen, warm and sore; it needs a doctor's care because it can spread.

  • Skin, often the lower leg
  • After a cut, bite or skin crack
  • Red, warm, swollen and sore
Which doctor
Family doctor the same day; emergency department if severe
Common in
Anyone; more often in adults with swollen legs, diabetes or broken skin
Contagious
No, not usually; cover any open or weeping skin and wash hands
Course
Often improves over days with a doctor's help, but it can spread fast if not seen early

Common signs

More signs

  • Often on one lower leg, but it can happen on the arm, face or anywhere
  • Skin that looks tight and shiny, sometimes with small blisters
  • Swollen glands near the red area

Why it happens

  • Germs (bacteria) on the skin get in through a break in the skin
  • Cuts, scrapes, insect bites, burns or surgical wounds
  • Cracked or broken skin from eczema, athlete's foot or very dry skin
  • Swollen legs or arms, varicose veins, or having had cellulitis before
  • Diabetes, a weak immune system or being overweight make it more likely

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with shaking chills, fast heartbeat, confusion, fainting or feeling very ill: go to hospital now
  • Redness spreading fast, or red streaks moving up the arm or leg: go to hospital now
  • Redness and swelling around the eye or on the face: go to hospital now
  • Very bad pain, blisters, or skin turning dark or purple: go to hospital now
  • A baby, a young child or an older person with a red, swollen, warm area of skin: see a doctor today
  • Any red, hot, swollen, painful area of skin, especially on the leg, needs a doctor's check the same day

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest, and raise the affected leg or arm when sitting or lying down
  • Drink enough water; ask your doctor or pharmacist about pain relief
  • Look after small cuts, dry skin and athlete's foot early, to lower the chance of it coming back

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
