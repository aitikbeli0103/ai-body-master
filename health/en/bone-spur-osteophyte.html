Bone spur (osteophyte)

A small, bony growth that forms along the edge of a bone, often near a joint

ConditionBones, joints & spineBones & musclesWhole body

What it is

A bone spur is an extra bit of bone that grows slowly at the edge of a bone. Many are painless, but some rub on nearby tissue and cause aching.

  • Near joints or heels
  • More common with age
  • Aching, stiff spot
Which doctor
Family doctor, bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon)
Common in
Middle-aged and older adults; people with worn joints
Contagious
No
Course
Spurs do not go away by themselves, but many cause no trouble

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Heavy work, sport or carrying extra body weight
  • Pull on a tendon or ligament, such as at the heel

See a doctor first if

  • Numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or leg that is getting worse
  • Pain after a fall or injury, or a joint that is bent or cannot move
  • A hot, red, swollen joint with fever
  • Pain at night or pain that keeps getting worse
  • Trouble walking, or losing control of urine or stools: go to hospital

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest the sore spot and avoid actions that bring on the pain
  • Do gentle movement and stretching; ask your doctor what suits you

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • Pregnancy
