Parotid gland tumour

A growth in the parotid gland, the large saliva gland in front of and below the ear; most are not cancer

ConditionCancer & tumoursHead

What it is

The parotid gland makes saliva. A tumour here usually shows as a slow, painless lump on the cheek or in front of the ear. Most are not cancer, but some are, so a doctor should check it.

  • Cheek, in front of the ear
  • Mostly adults
  • A slow-growing lump
Which doctor
Family doctor first, then an ear-nose-throat (ENT) or head-and-neck surgeon
Common in
Mostly adults in middle age or older
Contagious
No
Course
Many grow slowly; a specialist decides the best next step for each lump

Common signs

More signs

  • Usually painless and slow-growing
  • Sometimes pain, or numbness in part of the face

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Past radiation to the head or neck is a known risk for some types
  • Most are found in adults, more often in middle age or later

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden face drooping, arm weakness or trouble speaking: call 999 (could be a stroke)
  • A lump that grows fast, feels hard or fixed, or becomes painful
  • Numbness, weakness or a slowly drooping smile on one side of the face
  • Trouble opening your mouth, chewing or swallowing
  • A new lump in the neck, or swollen glands that stay for weeks
  • A hot, red, painful swollen cheek with fever: see a doctor today (it may be an infection)

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not press, squeeze or rub the lump
  • Ask your doctor what type it is and what the next step is

Tell your therapist
