Swollen veins in the oesophagus and stomach (varices)

Swollen veins in the lining of the food pipe or stomach that can bleed

ConditionLiver, gallbladder & pancreasAbdomen

What it is

When blood has trouble flowing through a diseased liver, it backs up into veins of the food pipe and stomach. These veins swell, and they can bleed suddenly.

  • Food pipe and stomach
  • Linked to liver disease
  • Often no signs until bleeding
Which doctor
Liver or stomach specialist (hepatologist, gastroenterologist)
Common in
People with cirrhosis or other serious liver disease
Contagious
No
Course
Needs regular check-ups; a specialist can explain how to lower the risk of bleeding

Common signs

More signs

  • Black, sticky stools

Why it happens

  • Cirrhosis, long-term scarring of the liver
  • High pressure in the liver's blood vessels (portal hypertension)
  • Causes of liver damage such as long-term hepatitis or heavy drinking
  • Sometimes a blood clot in the liver's veins

See a doctor first if

  • Vomiting blood or coffee-ground vomit: call 999
  • Black, tarry stools or blood from the bottom: go to hospital now
  • Fainting, a racing heart or very pale skin
  • Confusion or being very sleepy
  • Any liver disease that has not been checked for these veins

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
