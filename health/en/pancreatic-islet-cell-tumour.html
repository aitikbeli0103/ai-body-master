Pancreatic islet cell tumour

A tumour of the hormone-making islet cells of the pancreas; it may be non-cancerous or cancerous

ConditionCancer & tumoursAbdomen

What it is

Islet cells in the pancreas make hormones such as insulin. A tumour here may make too much hormone, or may cause no signs at all and be found by chance on a scan.

  • Behind the stomach
  • Often found by chance
  • Can cause shaky, sweaty spells
Which doctor
Family doctor first, then a hormone (endocrine) or digestive specialist
Common in
Adults, mostly middle-aged or older; a few are linked to inherited conditions
Contagious
No
Course
Many grow slowly, but it varies; a specialist team plans care for each person

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some are linked to rare inherited conditions that run in families
  • Most are found in adults, more often in middle age or later
  • The tumour may make extra hormone, such as insulin, and this causes the signs

See a doctor first if

  • Fainting, a fit, or confusion that does not clear: call 999
  • Severe belly pain, vomiting blood or black stools: go to hospital now
  • Repeated spells of shaking, sweating, hunger or confusion that ease after something sweet
  • Yellow skin or eyes, or weight loss you cannot explain
  • Watery diarrhoea for weeks, or stomach ulcers that keep coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Ask your team what to do if your blood sugar drops, and carry a sweet snack if they advise it
  • Eat regular meals, and ask your doctor if you should avoid long gaps without food
  • Do not drive or use machines if you get spells of confusion or faintness
  • Never stop or change any medicine on your own; ask your doctor or pharmacist

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Epilepsy or fits
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
