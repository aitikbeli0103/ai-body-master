Rheumatic fever

An illness that can follow a strep throat and may affect the joints, heart and skin

ConditionAutoimmune & rheumaticWhole body

What it is

A few weeks after a throat infection with strep germs, the body's defence system can react and cause fever, painful joints and sometimes heart trouble. It mostly affects children.

  • Joints, heart, skin
  • Mostly school-age children
  • Fever and sore joints
Which doctor
Family doctor or children's doctor; heart doctor if needed
Common in
Children and young people after a strep throat
Contagious
No, but the strep throat before it can spread
Course
Usually improves over weeks, but the heart may need long-term checks

Common signs

More signs

  • Jerky, uncontrolled movements in some children

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The body's defence system reacts to the germ and also hits the joints and heart
  • Only a small number of strep throats lead to it

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting: call 999
  • A child with fever and a sore throat: see a doctor promptly
  • Painful, swollen joints a few weeks after a sore throat
  • Racing heart, swollen legs or being unusually tired
  • Sudden jerky movements or a new rash

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Questions and answers

What is Rheumatic fever?

An illness that can follow a strep throat and may affect the joints, heart and skin. A few weeks after a throat infection with strep germs, the body's defence system can react and cause fever, painful joints and sometimes heart trouble. It mostly affects children.

What are the common signs of Rheumatic fever?

Fever, feeling hot · Aching, swollen joints · Red rash · Tight, heavy chest

Why does Rheumatic fever happen?

Germs (infection) · Crowded places and droplets

How can I look after myself with Rheumatic fever?

Rest well · Keep your regular tests
