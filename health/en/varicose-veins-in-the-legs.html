Varicose veins in the legs

Swollen, twisted veins under the skin of the legs, often dark blue or purple

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsArms & legs

What it is

Small valves inside the leg veins stop working well, so blood pools and the veins swell and twist under the skin. It often brings aching, heavy legs.

  • Legs, mostly the calves
  • More common in women
  • Aching, heavy legs
Which doctor
Family doctor; vein or vascular surgeon if needed
Common in
Women, older adults, people who stand a lot, or with a family history
Contagious
No
Course
Usually changes slowly; care can often ease the aching and swelling

Common signs

Why it happens

  • Weak or damaged valves in the leg veins, so blood flows back down and pools
  • Getting older, and a family history of varicose veins
  • Standing or sitting for many hours, and being overweight
  • Pregnancy and hormone changes
  • An earlier injury or blood clot in a leg vein that damaged the vein

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden swelling, pain, warmth and redness in one leg (maybe a blood clot): go to hospital now
  • Chest pain, coughing blood or sudden trouble breathing: call 999
  • A vein that bleeds: lie down, raise the leg, press on it with a clean cloth and call 999
  • A red, hot, hard, tender lump or cord along a vein: see a doctor the same day
  • A painful open sore, or skin near the ankle that turns dark, hard or weepy: see a doctor soon
  • Legs that keep swelling, or pain that stops you from walking or sleeping: see your doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Walk often and move your ankles and calves; avoid standing or sitting still for a long time
  • Rest with your legs raised when you can
  • Ask your doctor about support (compression) stockings before you buy them
  • Keep to a steady weight and wear comfortable shoes
  • Keep your skin clean and moisturised, and take care not to scratch or knock the veins

Tell your therapist

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
