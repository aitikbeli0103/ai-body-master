Cyst under the tongue (ranula)

A soft, fluid-filled swelling under the tongue

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

A ranula is a soft, often bluish, fluid-filled swelling on the floor of the mouth. It forms when a saliva gland or duct under the tongue is blocked or damaged.

  • Under the tongue
  • Can happen at any age
  • Soft, painless swelling
Which doctor
Family doctor, dentist, ear-nose-throat (ENT) doctor
Common in
Children and adults; not common
Contagious
No
Course
Often stays or slowly grows; a doctor can explain the options

Common signs

  • A soft, smooth bump under the tongue
  • Often looks pale blue or clear
  • Usually little or no pain
  • The tongue may feel pushed up, making speaking or eating awkward

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A blocked saliva duct under the tongue
  • A small injury or bite to the floor of the mouth

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing or swallowing, or a swelling that pushes the tongue up fast: call 999
  • Swelling that spreads to the neck
  • Pain, redness or fever, which may mean infection
  • A lump that grows, feels hard or bleeds
  • A child with a swelling in the mouth: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See a doctor to have the swelling checked
  • Keep your mouth clean with gentle brushing
  • Avoid biting the area; choose soft food if it is sore

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pregnancy
