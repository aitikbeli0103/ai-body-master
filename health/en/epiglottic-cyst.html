Epiglottic cyst

A smooth, fluid-filled sac on the epiglottis, the flap that covers the windpipe when you swallow

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

A small cyst can form on the epiglottis at the base of the tongue. Small ones may cause nothing, but larger ones can make the throat feel full or the voice sound muffled.

  • Base of the tongue, throat
  • Can affect any age
  • Lump feeling in the throat
Which doctor
Ear, nose and throat doctor (ENT)
Common in
Adults and sometimes babies; often found by chance
Contagious
No
Course
Small ones may stay the same; a doctor decides if it needs action

Common signs

More signs

  • A feeling of a lump or something stuck in the throat
  • Noisy breathing or snoring in larger cysts

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A blocked small mucus gland in the lining of the epiglottis

See a doctor first if

  • Trouble breathing, noisy breathing or choking: call 999
  • Cannot swallow your own saliva, or drooling
  • A baby with noisy breathing or trouble feeding: go to hospital now
  • Coughing up blood
  • A hoarse voice or throat lump lasting more than a few weeks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • See an ENT doctor to find out what it is
  • Do not poke at your throat with fingers or objects
  • Sip water often and rest your voice if it is hoarse
  • Eat soft food in small bites if swallowing is hard

Tell your therapist

  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Epiglottic cyst?

A smooth, fluid-filled sac on the epiglottis, the flap that covers the windpipe when you swallow. A small cyst can form on the epiglottis at the base of the tongue. Small ones may cause nothing, but larger ones can make the throat feel full or the voice sound muffled.

What are the common signs of Epiglottic cyst?

Hoarse, weak voice · Hard or painful to swallow · Dry, tickly cough

Why does Epiglottic cyst happen?

Germs (infection) · Present from birth · The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Epiglottic cyst?

Stop smoking
