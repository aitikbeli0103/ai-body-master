Broken thigh bone (shaft)

A break in the long middle part of the thigh bone, the biggest bone in the body

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legBones & muscles

What it is

The thigh bone (femur) is very strong, so a break in its shaft usually follows a major force such as a road accident or a bad fall. It is an emergency.

  • Thigh, between hip and knee
  • After a major accident
  • Severe pain, cannot walk
Which doctor
Emergency department, bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon)
Common in
Road accident and fall injuries; older people with weak bones
Contagious
No
Course
Healing usually takes months; the care team guides each step

Common signs

More signs

  • Very strong pain in the thigh after an injury
  • The leg looks shorter, bent or turned out

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A strong direct blow or crush injury to the thigh
  • In older people, even a simple fall when bones are weak
  • Some bone conditions or long-term medicines that weaken bone

See a doctor first if

  • Any suspected thigh bone break: call 999 and do not move the person
  • Heavy bleeding, or bone sticking out through the skin
  • Signs of shock: pale, cold, sweaty, fast heartbeat, faint
  • Numb, pale, cold or bluish foot
  • After treatment: sudden chest pain, trouble breathing, or a swollen painful calf

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Stay still and wait for help; do not try to stand
  • Follow your care team's advice on rest, walking aids and exercises
  • Ask your doctor about pain relief and about preventing blood clots
  • Eat well and do not smoke

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Weak leg or foot

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
