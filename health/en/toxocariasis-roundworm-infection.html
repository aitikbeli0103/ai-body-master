Toxocariasis (roundworm infection)

Infection from the eggs of dog or cat roundworms swallowed by people

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

People can swallow tiny roundworm eggs from soil or pets. The young worms then travel through the body and may affect the liver, lungs or eyes.

  • Liver, lungs or eyes
  • Often young children
  • Fever, cough, tiredness
Which doctor
Family doctor; infection or eye specialist if needed
Common in
Young children who play in soil; people with dogs or cats
Contagious
No, it does not pass from person to person
Course
Many have mild or no signs; a doctor can explain what to do

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Swallowing roundworm eggs from soil or sand where dogs or cats have passed stools
  • Putting dirty hands or objects in the mouth
  • Pets that have not been dewormed

See a doctor first if

  • Wheezing or trouble breathing: go to hospital now
  • Vision that is suddenly blurry or lost, or a painful red eye
  • A fit (seizure) or confusion: call 999
  • Fever, belly pain or a cough that lasts more than a week
  • A child with these signs who often plays in soil: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Wash hands with soap after touching soil, pets or sand, and before eating
  • Ask a vet about deworming your pets
  • Wash vegetables and fruit well
  • Keep children from eating soil, and clean up pet waste
  • If a doctor gives medicine, take it as told

  • Pregnancy
  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection
