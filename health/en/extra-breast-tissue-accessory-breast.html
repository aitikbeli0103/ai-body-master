Extra breast tissue (accessory breast)

Extra breast tissue, usually in the armpit or beside the breast, present from birth

ConditionWomen's healthChest

What it is

Some people have extra breast tissue, most often in the armpit or beside the breast; it is there from birth, is usually harmless, and may swell or feel sore when hormones change.

  • Armpit or beside the breast
  • Present from birth
  • Soft bulge, may feel tender
Which doctor
Family doctor; breast clinic or surgeon if a lump is new or growing
Common in
Women, and sometimes men; often first noticed at puberty or in pregnancy
Contagious
No
Course
Usually stays the same; may swell or feel sore around periods or pregnancy

Common signs

More signs

  • Sometimes a small extra nipple or dark spot along a line from the armpit to the groin
  • Swelling or tenderness before periods, or in pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • Sometimes a little milk leaks from it while breastfeeding
  • A rubbing or pulling feeling under the arm in tight clothes or a tight bra

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Before birth, breast tissue forms along a line from the armpit to the groin, and sometimes extra tissue stays
  • It often becomes noticeable at puberty, when hormones make breast tissue grow
  • It is not caused by anything you did

See a doctor first if

  • A new, hard lump, or a lump that keeps growing, in the armpit or the extra tissue: see a doctor soon
  • Dimpled or red skin, a changing nipple, or bloody discharge from the breast or extra tissue
  • A red, hot, painful, swollen area with fever, especially when breastfeeding: see a doctor today
  • Swollen glands in the armpit that last for weeks, or come with weight loss or night sweats
  • Pain that keeps getting worse, or a bulge that keeps getting bigger

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Have it checked by a doctor once, so you know what it is
  • Learn how your breasts and armpits normally feel, and tell your doctor about any change
  • Follow your doctor's advice about breast screening for your age
  • Wear a soft, well-fitting bra; a tight one can rub and make the bulge more obvious
  • If it bothers you, ask a doctor about your options
