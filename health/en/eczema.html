Eczema

A long-term skin condition that makes the skin dry, red and itchy

ConditionSkin, hair & nailsSkin

What it is

In eczema the skin loses moisture easily and reacts to things around it, so patches become dry, red and itchy, often coming and going in flare-ups.

  • Skin, any part of the body
  • Often starts in childhood
  • Dry, red, itchy patches
Which doctor
Family doctor, skin doctor (dermatologist)
Common in
Children most often; also adults, especially with allergies or asthma in the family
Contagious
No
Course
Long-term; it comes and goes, and many children improve as they grow

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The skin barrier is weak, so skin loses water easily and irritants get in

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, or trouble breathing, for example after a food or new product: call 999
  • Painful clusters of small blisters or sores on eczema skin, with fever or feeling very unwell: see a doctor the same day
  • Weeping, yellow crusts, pus, or skin that is getting more red, hot and sore: it may be infected, so see a doctor soon
  • Red, hot, peeling skin over most of the body, especially with fever or chills: go to hospital
  • A baby or child with eczema who has a fever, feeds poorly, or is very sleepy or unwell: see a doctor promptly
  • Itching so bad that it stops sleep or daily life, or the skin is not getting better with regular moisturising

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Take short, lukewarm baths or showers, use a gentle cleanser, and pat the skin dry
  • Avoid triggers you notice, such as harsh soap, perfume, dust, rough clothes, and getting very hot or sweaty
  • For creams or ointments during flare-ups, ask your doctor or pharmacist; do not put herbal pastes or unknown creams on broken skin
  • Do not cut foods out on your own, especially for a child; ask your doctor first

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • A severe allergy
  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Questions and answers

What is Eczema?

A long-term skin condition that makes the skin dry, red and itchy. In eczema the skin loses moisture easily and reacts to things around it, so patches become dry, red and itchy, often coming and going in flare-ups.

What are the common signs of Eczema?

Itchy skin · Red rash · Dry, flaky skin

Why does Eczema happen?

The cause is not fully known · Dry air and air-conditioning · Smoke, haze and strong smells · Dust, pollen or pets · Stress

How can I look after myself with Eczema?

Keep the skin moisturised · Wear loose cotton clothes
