Perforated eardrum (burst eardrum)

A hole or tear in the thin eardrum that separates the outer ear from the middle ear

ConditionEars, nose & throatHead

What it is

The eardrum is a thin skin inside the ear. If it tears, hearing may drop, and germs and water can get into the middle ear more easily.

  • Eardrum, in the ear
  • Any age
  • Less hearing, ear pain
Which doctor
Ear doctor (ENT), family doctor
Common in
Anyone; after ear infection, injury or loud blast
Contagious
No
Course
Small tears often close by themselves over weeks to months

Common signs

More signs

  • Clear, bloody or pus-like fluid from the ear
  • Some dizziness

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Poking the ear with cotton buds, pins or other objects
  • A very loud blast or a slap on the ear
  • Sudden pressure change, such as flying, diving or a bad blow of the nose

See a doctor first if

  • Ear pain or fluid after a head injury: go to hospital now
  • Weakness or drooping of one side of the face
  • Strong spinning dizziness, or sudden major hearing loss
  • Fever, bad ear pain or pus from the ear
  • A child with ear pain, fever or fluid from the ear: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Do not put anything in the ear, including drops, water or cotton buds
  • Blow your nose gently, one side at a time
  • Avoid loud noise and flying until your doctor has checked the ear

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
