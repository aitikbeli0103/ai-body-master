Breast duct tumour

A growth inside a milk duct of the breast; many are not cancer, but tests are needed

ConditionCancer & tumoursChest

What it is

A breast duct tumour is a growth that starts in a milk duct, the small tube that carries milk to the nipple. Many are not cancer, but a doctor needs tests to be sure.

  • In a milk duct
  • Mostly women
  • Nipple fluid or a small lump
Which doctor
Family doctor first; breast clinic or breast surgeon
Common in
Mostly women, often in their middle years
Contagious
No
Course
Many are not cancer; tests decide if it is watched or removed, and follow-up is common

Common signs

More signs

  • Fluid from the nipple, clear or blood-stained, often from one breast only
  • Mild breast pain or tenderness in some people

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Extra cells growing inside the lining of a milk duct
  • More common in women in their middle years
  • Some types, such as several growths or unusual cells, can raise the chance of breast cancer later

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with a hot, red, very painful breast: see a doctor the same day
  • Fluid from one nipple that is blood-stained, or that comes without any squeezing
  • A new lump in the breast or armpit, or a lump that is growing
  • Dimpled or red skin, a rash on the nipple, or a nipple turning inward
  • A lump or nipple fluid that comes back after it was removed

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • If there is fluid, wear a soft breast pad and keep the skin clean and dry
  • Do not put herbal creams or self-made mixtures on a breast lump; ask your doctor first
  • Get to know how your breasts normally look and feel, and tell a doctor about any change

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Pregnancy
  • Cancer or cancer treatment
