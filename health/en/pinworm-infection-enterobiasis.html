Pinworm infection (enterobiasis)

A common worm infection where tiny threadlike worms live in the bowel and cause itching around the bottom

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Tiny white worms live in the lower bowel. At night they come out to lay eggs near the bottom, which causes strong itching. It is common, easy to pass on, and not a sign of being dirty.

  • Bowel and around the bottom
  • Common in young children
  • Itchy bottom, restless nights
Which doctor
Family doctor; children can see a paediatrician
Common in
Children, especially school-age; also family members who live with them
Contagious
Yes, eggs spread easily by hands, bedding, clothes and shared items
Course
Often comes back unless the whole family follows the doctor's advice together

Common signs

  • Strong itching around the bottom (anus), worse at night
  • Restless sleep, waking in the night, and feeling tired or cranky by day
  • Tiny white threadlike worms seen near the bottom or in the stool
  • Sometimes a mild belly ache, feeling sick or less appetite
  • In girls, itching or soreness in the private area can also happen

Why it happens

  • Swallowing tiny pinworm eggs, which are too small to see
  • Eggs get on fingers when scratching the bottom, then reach the mouth or food
  • Eggs can also be on bedding, underwear, towels, toys and floors
  • It spreads easily in families, schools, kindergartens and childcare centres
  • Not washing hands after the toilet, biting nails and sucking fingers

See a doctor first if

  • Severe belly pain that does not go away, with fever or vomiting: go to hospital now
  • Skin around the bottom that is red, raw, oozing or very painful from scratching: see a doctor
  • Itching with a discharge, or pain when passing urine: see a doctor
  • Itching or worms that keep coming back despite good hygiene: see a doctor
  • A baby or a pregnant woman with these signs: see a doctor before taking any medicine

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Wash hands with soap and water after the toilet, before eating and after changing nappies
  • Keep fingernails short and clean, and try not to scratch the bottom
  • Shower in the morning and change underwear every day
  • Wash bedding, pyjamas and towels in hot water, and clean the bathroom and floors
  • Ask your doctor or pharmacist about medicine; the whole family may need to take it together

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Fever or an active infection

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
