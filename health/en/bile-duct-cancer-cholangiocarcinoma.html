Bile duct cancer (cholangiocarcinoma)

A cancer that starts in the bile ducts, the thin tubes that carry bile (a digestive fluid) from the liver to the gut

ConditionCancer & tumoursAbdomen

What it is

Bile duct cancer starts in the tubes that carry bile from the liver to the small intestine; it is often found late because early signs can be mild, and yellow skin or eyes is a common warning.

  • Liver and bile ducts
  • More common with age
  • Yellow skin or eyes
Which doctor
Family doctor first; then a liver, digestive or cancer specialist at a hospital
Common in
Older adults; people with long-term liver or bile duct disease
Contagious
No
Course
Often found late; care depends on the stage and location, so ask your cancer team

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Long-term inflammation of the bile ducts or liver, or bile duct stones
  • Liver fluke infection from raw or undercooked freshwater fish, in some parts of Asia
  • Long-term liver disease, such as cirrhosis or hepatitis B or C
  • Bile duct cysts from birth, older age and smoking may also raise the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with chills, yellow skin or eyes and severe upper belly pain: go to hospital now
  • Vomiting blood, black stools, fainting or confusion: call 999
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pale stools: see a doctor without delay
  • Itchy skin all over with no rash that does not settle
  • Upper belly pain that keeps coming back or gets worse
  • Weight loss or loss of appetite that you cannot explain

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.
