Broken hip just below the ball joint (intertrochanteric)

A break in the upper thigh bone, just below the ball of the hip joint

ConditionPelvis, hip, knee & legBones & muscles

What it is

The thigh bone breaks in the area just below the hip ball joint. It most often happens when an older person with weak bones falls, and it needs hospital care.

  • Hip and upper thigh
  • Older adults after a fall
  • Severe hip pain, cannot stand
Which doctor
Emergency doctor at hospital; bone specialist (orthopaedic surgeon)
Common in
Older adults, more often women, especially with weak bones
Contagious
No
Course
Usually needs an operation and a long recovery with help from a care team

Common signs

More signs

  • The leg looks shorter and the foot turns outward

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak, thin bones (osteoporosis) that break more easily
  • Poor balance, weak muscles, dizziness or poor eyesight

See a doctor first if

  • A fall followed by hip pain and being unable to stand: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • A leg that is shortened or turned outward
  • Signs of shock: faint, pale, cold sweat, fast pulse, or confusion
  • After an operation: chest pain, sudden breathlessness or a swollen painful calf: call 999
  • A wound that becomes red, hot or leaks pus, or fever

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • After a fall, do not move the person; keep them warm and still until help comes
  • Do not give food or drink, in case an operation is needed
  • After hospital care, follow the care team on walking aids and exercises
  • Ask your doctor about bone health and getting enough calcium and vitamin D

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
