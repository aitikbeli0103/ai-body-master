Infectious mononucleosis (glandular fever)

A virus infection passed on mainly through saliva that causes fever, sore throat, swollen glands and heavy tiredness

ConditionInfectionsWhole body

What it is

Usually caused by the Epstein-Barr virus, it brings fever, a sore throat, swollen neck glands and heavy tiredness that can last for weeks, and it is most common in teenagers and young adults.

  • Throat and neck glands
  • Teenagers and young adults
  • Very tired, sore throat
Which doctor
Family doctor; hospital if there is severe belly pain or trouble breathing
Common in
Teenagers and young adults; children often have only a mild illness
Contagious
Yes, mainly through saliva: kissing, shared cups, spoons and toothbrushes
Course
Usually gets better in a few weeks, but tiredness can last longer

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Most often the Epstein-Barr virus, and sometimes other viruses
  • It passes on through saliva, for example kissing or sharing cups, spoons or toothbrushes
  • Catching the virus for the first time as a teenager or adult more often brings the full illness

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden, severe pain in the upper left belly or left shoulder tip: call 999, as the spleen can burst
  • Hard to breathe or swallow, drooling, or a throat so swollen it feels like it is closing: call 999
  • Severe headache with a stiff neck, confusion, or being very drowsy: call 999
  • Yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or very pale stools
  • Cannot drink enough because of a sore throat, or passing very little urine
  • Fever lasting more than two weeks, or easy bruising or bleeding: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Return to normal activity slowly; tiredness can last for weeks
  • Do not kiss, or share cups, spoons or toothbrushes, while ill
  • Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine, and ask if it is safe to drink alcohol

  • A kidney or liver condition
