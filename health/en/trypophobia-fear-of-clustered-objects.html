Trypophobia (fear of clustered objects)

A strong fear or disgust at the sight of clusters of small holes or bumps

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressWhole body

What it is

Some people feel strong fear, disgust or a crawling feeling on the skin when they see tight clusters of small holes or bumps, such as a lotus seed pod or honeycomb.

  • Triggered by what you see
  • Can affect anyone
  • Fear, disgust, skin crawling
Which doctor
Family doctor; a psychologist or counsellor if it affects daily life
Common in
Anyone; some people feel it a little, others very strongly
Contagious
No
Course
Varies from person to person; support from a professional can make it easier to cope

Common signs

More signs

  • Strong fear or disgust when you see clusters of small holes or bumps
  • Skin that feels itchy or 'crawling', or comes up in goosebumps
  • Wanting to look away from, or avoid, certain pictures, foods or objects

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Some researchers think the brain may react to patterns that look like danger or illness
  • As with other fears, it may start after a scary experience, or for no clear reason
  • Worry, stress or tiredness can make the reaction stronger

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself, or feeling you cannot cope: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Chest pain or trouble breathing that does not ease, or fainting: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Fear that stops you from working, eating, going out or sleeping: see a doctor soon
  • Repeated panic attacks, or low mood that lasts more than two weeks: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Many people react this way, so you are not alone
  • When it hits, look away, breathe slowly, and remind yourself that it cannot hurt you
  • If it limits your life, ask a psychologist about gentle, step-by-step ways to cope

Tell your therapist

  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
