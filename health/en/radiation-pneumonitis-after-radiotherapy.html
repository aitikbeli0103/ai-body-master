Radiation pneumonitis (after radiotherapy)

Inflammation of the lung that can follow radiotherapy to the chest

ConditionLungs & breathingChest

What it is

After radiation to the chest, the lung tissue can become inflamed. It may cause a dry cough and breathlessness weeks to months later.

  • In the lungs
  • After chest radiotherapy
  • Dry cough, breathless
Which doctor
Your cancer doctor first; lung doctor if needed
Common in
People who had radiotherapy to the chest or breast
Contagious
No
Course
Varies; many people improve with medical care over weeks

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Radiation to the chest can irritate healthy lung tissue
  • A larger area of lung in the radiation field
  • Some cancer medicines given together with radiotherapy

See a doctor first if

  • Severe trouble breathing or blue lips: call 999
  • New or worse breathlessness after radiotherapy: see your cancer team soon
  • Fever with a cough, as this may be an infection
  • Chest pain or coughing up blood
  • A dry cough that keeps getting worse

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Tell your cancer team about any new cough or breathlessness early
  • Never stop or change cancer treatment on your own

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Fever or an active infection
  • A heart condition
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

What are the common signs of Radiation pneumonitis (after radiotherapy)?

Dry, tickly cough · Short of breath on stairs · Fever, feeling hot · Tight, heavy chest · Tired all the time
