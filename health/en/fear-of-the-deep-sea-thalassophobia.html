Fear of the deep sea (thalassophobia)

A strong fear of deep water or the deep sea

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressWhole body

What it is

Some people feel intense fear or panic when they think about or look at deep, dark water or what may be under it, even when they are safe.

  • Whole body reaction
  • Can happen at any age
  • Fear, racing heart
Which doctor
Family doctor, psychologist or psychiatrist
Common in
Adults and children; may start after a scare in water
Contagious
No
Course
Often improves with gentle, step-by-step support; it varies from person to person

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A frightening event in water, such as nearly drowning
  • Scary films or stories about the sea
  • Fear of the unknown or of being out of control

See a doctor first if

  • Chest pain, trouble breathing or fainting: call 999
  • Thoughts of harming yourself: see a doctor now or call 999
  • Panic attacks that keep coming back
  • Bad dreams or poor sleep that go on for weeks
  • The fear stops you from daily life, work or travel

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Breathe slowly: in through the nose, out gently through the mouth
  • Take small steps, such as looking at pictures of water first
  • Never go into deep water alone while you are afraid
  • Limit scary videos before bedtime
  • Talk to your doctor about support

Tell your therapist

  • A heart condition
  • High blood pressure
  • Pregnancy

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

What are the common signs of Fear of the deep sea (thalassophobia)?

The cause is not fully known
