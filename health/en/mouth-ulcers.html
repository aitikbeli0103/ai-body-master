Mouth ulcers

Small, painful sores inside the mouth, on the cheeks, lips, tongue or gums

ConditionMouth & teethHead

What it is

A mouth ulcer is a small round or oval sore with a white or yellow middle and a red edge; it stings when you eat or talk, and most go away by themselves.

  • Inside the mouth
  • Can happen to anyone
  • Sore, stings when eating
Which doctor
Family doctor or dentist
Common in
Anyone; very common and can come back from time to time
Contagious
Usually not; ulcers caused by some infections can spread
Course
Small ulcers usually go away by themselves in about one to two weeks; some come back

Common signs

More signs

  • Pain or stinging, especially with hot, spicy, salty or sour food
  • A tingling or burning feeling a day or so before the sore shows
  • Usually inside the cheeks and lips, on or under the tongue, or on the gums
  • It can be hard to eat, drink or talk when the sores are big or many

Why it happens

Other causes

  • A small injury, such as biting your cheek, a sharp tooth, hard brushing, braces or loose dentures
  • In some people, certain foods that do not agree with them
  • Not enough iron, vitamin B12 or folate in the body

See a doctor first if

  • Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, or trouble breathing: call 999
  • Mouth sores with a skin rash, blisters or red, sore eyes, especially after a new medicine: go to hospital now
  • You cannot eat, drink or swallow, or a baby or child will not drink: see a doctor promptly
  • An ulcer that lasts more than three weeks
  • A white or red patch, a hard lump, or a sore that does not go away, especially if you smoke or chew tobacco or betel quid
  • Ulcers that keep coming back, many ulcers at once, or ulcers with a fever

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rinse your mouth gently with lukewarm salt water; do not swallow it
  • Eat soft, plain food and avoid very hot, spicy, salty, sour or rough food while it hurts
  • Brush gently with a soft toothbrush and keep your mouth clean
  • Ask your dentist or pharmacist which mouth gel or rinse is safe for you

  • Broken, red or sore skin
