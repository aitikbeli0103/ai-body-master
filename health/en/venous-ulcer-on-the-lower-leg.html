Venous ulcer on the lower leg

An open sore on the lower leg, linked to weak leg veins

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsArms & legs

What it is

When the leg veins cannot push blood back up well, pressure builds in the lower leg and the skin can break down into a sore that is slow to close.

  • Lower leg, often near the ankle
  • More common with age
  • Aching, swollen, itchy leg
Which doctor
Family doctor, vascular surgeon, wound care nurse
Common in
Older adults; people with varicose veins or long standing jobs
Contagious
No
Course
Often slow to close and may come back; steady care and a doctor's advice help

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak vein valves, so blood pools in the lower leg
  • Varicose veins or an old blood clot in a leg vein

See a doctor first if

  • Fast spreading redness, heat, pus or a bad smell from the sore
  • Fever or chills together with a leg sore
  • A leg that suddenly swells, is painful and warm
  • Chest pain or sudden trouble breathing: call 999
  • A sore that is not getting better over weeks, or is getting bigger

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Walk gently every day to help blood flow
  • Ask your doctor before using compression stockings or bandages

Tell your therapist

  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • Blood-thinning medicine
