Broken wrist (distal radius fracture)

A break in the large forearm bone close to the wrist

ConditionShoulder, arm & handArms & legs

What it is

The end of the forearm bone nearest the wrist breaks, often when someone falls and lands on an open hand. The wrist is painful and swollen.

  • Wrist and forearm
  • Common after a fall
  • Painful, swollen wrist
Which doctor
Emergency department; bone doctor (orthopaedic surgeon)
Common in
Older adults, especially women with weak bones; active children
Contagious
No
Course
Usually heals over weeks; stiffness can last longer

Common signs

More signs

  • The wrist looks bent or out of shape

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Weak bones (osteoporosis) make a break more likely
  • A strong blow to the forearm

See a doctor first if

  • Bone through the skin or heavy bleeding: call 999
  • Fingers that are numb, pale, cold or blue: go to hospital now
  • A bent or deformed wrist: go to hospital
  • Cannot move the wrist or fingers, or severe swelling
  • Pain or swelling that keeps getting worse after treatment

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Raise the hand up on pillows to help swelling
  • Remove rings early before the fingers swell
  • Gently move the fingers if your doctor says it is safe

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Numb or tingling arm
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
