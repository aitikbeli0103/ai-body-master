Acute gastroenteritis (stomach and bowel infection)

A sudden infection of the stomach and bowel that causes vomiting and diarrhoea

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Germs, most often viruses, irritate the stomach and bowel and cause sudden vomiting, loose stools and belly cramps, and the main risk is losing too much body water.

  • Stomach and bowel
  • Babies, elderly most at risk
  • Vomiting, diarrhoea
Which doctor
Family doctor; hospital if very weak or dehydrated
Common in
Anyone; most serious in babies, young children and older adults
Contagious
Yes, through dirty hands, food, water and close contact
Course
Usually better in a few days; see a doctor if it lasts longer

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Drinking water that is not safe, or raw or unwashed food
  • Dirty hands, shared plates and touching things a sick person has touched

See a doctor first if

  • Fever with a stiff neck, confusion, fits or fainting: call 999
  • Blood in the vomit or stools, or black stools: go to hospital now
  • Severe belly pain, or a hard swollen belly: go to hospital now
  • Very dry mouth, very little or no urine, dizziness or sunken eyes (dehydration): see a doctor today
  • A baby, young child, older person or pregnant woman who is vomiting or has diarrhoea: see a doctor promptly
  • Cannot keep fluids down, a high fever, or illness lasting more than a few days: see a doctor

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Rest, and sip fluids often; ask a pharmacist about oral rehydration salts
  • Eat light, plain food such as rice porridge once you feel able
  • Stay home, do not prepare food for others, and clean the toilet and surfaces well

Tell your therapist

  • Fever or an active infection
  • Pregnancy
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin
  • A kidney or liver condition

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

Questions and answers

What is Acute gastroenteritis (stomach and bowel infection)?

A sudden infection of the stomach and bowel that causes vomiting and diarrhoea. Germs, most often viruses, irritate the stomach and bowel and cause sudden vomiting, loose stools and belly cramps, and the main risk is losing too much body water.

What are the common signs of Acute gastroenteritis (stomach and bowel infection)?

Feeling sick, nausea · Loose, frequent stools · Cramping belly pain · Fever, feeling hot · No appetite

Why does Acute gastroenteritis (stomach and bowel infection) happen?

Germs (infection) · Unsafe food or water

How can I look after myself with Acute gastroenteritis (stomach and bowel infection)?

Wash your hands often · Ask your pharmacist
