Blood clot (thrombosis)

A clump of blood that forms inside a blood vessel and can block the flow

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsBlood

What it is

Blood normally flows freely. Sometimes it clumps into a clot inside a vessel, which can slow or block blood flow and may break loose and travel.

  • Inside blood vessels
  • Higher risk after long rest
  • Depends where the clot is
Which doctor
Family doctor, blood doctor (haematologist), vascular surgeon
Common in
People who sit or lie still for long, after surgery, in pregnancy, and older adults
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on where it is; it needs a doctor's care and follow-up

Common signs

More signs

  • Sudden shortness of breath or sharp chest pain

Why it happens

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden trouble breathing, chest pain or coughing blood: call 999
  • Face drooping, arm weakness or speech trouble: call 999
  • A leg or arm that is swollen, painful and warm: go to hospital the same day
  • A limb that turns pale, cold or numb suddenly: go to hospital now
  • Sudden severe belly pain or fainting: go to hospital now

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

Questions and answers

What is Blood clot (thrombosis)?

A clump of blood that forms inside a blood vessel and can block the flow. Blood normally flows freely. Sometimes it clumps into a clot inside a vessel, which can slow or block blood flow and may break loose and travel.

What are the common signs of Blood clot (thrombosis)?

Red, warm, swollen skin · Slurred or unclear speech · Often no signs at first

Why does Blood clot (thrombosis) happen?

Standing or sitting still for long · An injury or fall · Smoking · Runs in the family

How can I look after myself with Blood clot (thrombosis)?

See your doctor for check-ups · Drink enough water · Stop smoking · Ask your pharmacist
