Colon (large bowel) polyps

Small growths on the inner wall of the large bowel

ConditionStomach & intestinesAbdomen

What it is

Colon polyps are small bumps that grow on the inside wall of the large bowel. Most cause no trouble, but doctors often check for them and take some out, because a few can change over time.

  • Inside the large bowel
  • More common with age
  • Often no signs at all
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a bowel specialist (gastroenterologist or colorectal surgeon)
Common in
Adults, more often after middle age; sometimes runs in families
Contagious
No
Course
Many grow slowly and cause no trouble; a doctor decides if one should be removed

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Polyps or bowel cancer in close family members
  • A diet low in fibre and high in processed meat may add to the risk

See a doctor first if

  • Heavy bleeding from the bottom, or feeling faint or weak: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Strong belly pain with a swollen belly, vomiting, or no stool or wind: go to hospital now
  • Blood in the stool, even a little, that is new or keeps coming back
  • A change in bowel habit that lasts for more than a few weeks
  • Losing weight without trying, or feeling very tired and pale
  • Bowel polyps or bowel cancer in your family: ask your doctor when to start bowel checks

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Eat more vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains
