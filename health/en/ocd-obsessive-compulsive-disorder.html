OCD (obsessive-compulsive disorder)

Unwanted thoughts that keep coming back, and actions repeated again and again to ease the distress

ConditionTold by a doctor: anxiety, fears & stressWhole body

What it is

OCD means unwanted thoughts or urges keep coming back (obsessions), and a person repeats actions like washing or checking to feel safe (compulsions). It takes up time and causes distress.

  • The mind and brain
  • Can affect any age
  • Repeated thoughts and actions
Which doctor
Family doctor, then a psychiatrist or clinical psychologist
Common in
Children, teens and adults; can run in families
Contagious
No
Course
Often long-lasting, with ups and downs; support can make it easier to cope

Common signs

  • Unwanted thoughts, images or urges that keep coming back and are hard to switch off
  • Washing or cleaning again and again, or fear of germs and dirt
  • Checking again and again, such as locks, switches or the stove
  • Counting, arranging or repeating things until it feels 'just right'
  • Feeling very upset if the actions are stopped, and losing a lot of time to them

Why it happens

Other causes

  • It can run in families
  • Differences in how some parts of the brain work may play a part
  • A very stressful time or life event can set it off or make it worse
  • It is not a sign of weakness, and it is nobody's fault

See a doctor first if

  • Thoughts of harming yourself or ending your life: call 999 or go to hospital now
  • Feeling hopeless, or very low for weeks
  • Skin that is cracked, bleeding or infected from washing or cleaning
  • Actions or fears that take up much of the day or stop you working, studying or leaving home
  • Not eating, drinking or sleeping properly because of the fears or repeated actions
  • A child who suddenly develops strong fears or repeated actions: see a doctor promptly

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Keep a regular routine for sleep, meals and gentle activity
  • Try to be kind to yourself; having these thoughts does not make you a bad person

Tell your therapist

  • Broken, red or sore skin
  • Pregnancy
  • Pacemaker or metal implant

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
