Congenital heart disease

A heart problem present from birth: the heart or its main blood vessels formed differently

ConditionHeart & blood vesselsChest

What it is

Before birth, the heart or the large blood vessels next to it did not form in the usual way. It can be mild or serious, and some people only find out as adults.

  • Heart and chest
  • Present from birth
  • Tired or breathless easily
Which doctor
Heart doctor (cardiologist); a children's heart specialist for babies and children
Common in
Babies born with it; some adults only find out later in life
Contagious
No
Course
Depends on the type; many people need regular check-ups with a heart doctor

Common signs

More signs

  • A bluish tint to the lips, tongue or skin

Why it happens

Other causes

  • The heart or its large blood vessels did not form fully before birth

See a doctor first if

  • A baby with blue lips or skin, fast or hard breathing, or poor feeding: go to hospital now
  • A baby or child who is very sleepy, floppy or hard to wake: go to hospital now
  • Fainting, chest pain, or severe trouble breathing: call 999
  • A racing or uneven heartbeat that does not settle, or comes with dizziness
  • Swollen feet, ankles or belly, or getting tired and breathless more easily
  • A fever that lasts for days in a person with a heart problem

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

Questions and answers

What is Congenital heart disease?

A heart problem present from birth: the heart or its main blood vessels formed differently. Before birth, the heart or the large blood vessels next to it did not form in the usual way. It can be mild or serious, and some people only find out as adults.

What are the common signs of Congenital heart disease?

Often no signs at first · A baby who feeds poorly · Short of breath on stairs · Swollen legs and ankles

Why does Congenital heart disease happen?

The cause is not fully known · Runs in the family · Germs (infection) · Smoking

How can I look after myself with Congenital heart disease?

See your doctor for check-ups · Regular dentist check-ups · Ask your pharmacist
