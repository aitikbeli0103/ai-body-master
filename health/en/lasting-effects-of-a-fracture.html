Lasting effects of a fracture

Ongoing pain, stiffness or weakness that stays after a broken bone has mended

ConditionAny other bone or soft-tissue injuryBones & muscles

What it is

After a fracture, some people still have pain, stiffness, weakness or a limb that looks or works differently. A doctor can look for the cause and suggest support.

  • The area of the old fracture
  • After a broken bone
  • Aching, stiff, weak
Which doctor
Bone doctor (orthopaedic); a physiotherapist for movement
Common in
People who had a bad break, a long cast or surgery; older adults
Contagious
No
Course
Varies; many people improve slowly with time and guided exercise

Common signs

More signs

  • Weak muscles or a smaller limb after a long time in a cast
  • Swelling, or a limb that looks bent or shorter

Why it happens

  • Stiff joints and weak muscles after a long time without moving the limb
  • A bone that mended in a slightly different position
  • Damage to nearby joints, nerves or soft tissue at the time of injury
  • Weak bones, smoking or diabetes can slow recovery

See a doctor first if

  • Sudden new pain, swelling or a limb that gives way
  • Numbness, tingling, or a pale, cold or blue hand, foot or finger
  • Redness, heat, fever or pus around a surgery scar or metal implant
  • A calf that is suddenly swollen, red and painful
  • Pain that keeps getting worse, or a bone that seems not to have mended

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

  • Go to your follow-up visits and ask your doctor what movement is safe
  • Ask about a physiotherapist and do the exercises you are shown
  • Build up activity slowly and stop if pain sharply increases
  • Eat a balanced diet, stay active, and do not smoke

Tell your therapist

  • Weak bones
  • Pacemaker or metal implant
  • Numb or tingling leg or foot
  • A blood clot or a new swollen leg
  • Diabetes or less feeling in the skin

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.
