Hereditary ovarian cancer

Ovarian cancer that runs in a family because of an inherited gene change

ConditionCancer & tumoursPelvisReproductive

What it is

Hereditary ovarian cancer is cancer of the ovaries linked to a gene change passed down in a family. Early signs are often mild and easy to miss.

  • Ovaries, lower belly
  • Runs in families
  • Bloating, belly ache
Which doctor
Women's cancer doctor (gynaecological oncologist), genetic counsellor
Common in
Women whose close relatives had ovarian or breast cancer
Contagious
No, but a gene change can be passed to children
Course
Depends on how early it is found; your care team can explain what to expect

Common signs

Why it happens

Other causes

  • Several close relatives with ovarian or breast cancer

See a doctor first if

  • Severe belly pain, fainting or trouble breathing: go to hospital now
  • Bloating or belly swelling that lasts more than a few weeks
  • Bleeding from the vagina after menopause, or heavy unusual bleeding
  • Losing weight without trying
  • Feeling full quickly, or new pelvic pain that keeps coming back

If any apply, please see a doctor before a session.

This page is general information. Please keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor.

Keep your normal care

Keep taking your normal medicine and keep seeing your doctor. Our session is a gentle support and does not replace medical care.

Looking after yourself

More ways to look after yourself

  • Never stop treatment on your own; ask your team about pain, food and tiredness
  • Ask about genetic counselling for yourself and your relatives
  • Ask about emotional support; you do not have to cope alone

Tell your therapist

  • Cancer or cancer treatment
  • Pregnancy
  • Blood-thinning medicine
  • Weak bones

Your therapist will go through this with you before starting.

What are the common signs of Hereditary ovarian cancer?

Swollen, tight belly · Dragging ache in the lower belly · No appetite · Needing the toilet often · Tired all the time

Why does Hereditary ovarian cancer happen?

Runs in the family · Getting older · The cause is not fully known

How can I look after myself with Hereditary ovarian cancer?

See your doctor for check-ups · Eat small, regular meals
